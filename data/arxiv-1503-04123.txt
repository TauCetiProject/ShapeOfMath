REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (18)

- Azuma–Hoeffding inequality: If a real martingale has increments bounded in absolute value by c_i, then P(|M_n−M_0|≥t)≤2exp(−t²/(2∑_{i=1}^nc_i²)) for t>0.
- Bayesian posterior density: For a prior density p(θ) and likelihood ℓ(y|θ), the posterior density given observation y is π(θ|y)=ℓ(y|θ)p(θ)/∫ℓ(y|u)p(u)du, when the denominator is positive and finite.
- Compact petite sets for a Lebesgue-irreducible weak Feller kernel: Every compact subset of ℝᵈ is petite for a weak Feller Markov kernel that is irreducible with respect to Lebesgue measure. The full support of Lebesgue measure is essential to this version; irreducibility and weak Feller alone do not imply it for arbitrary irreducibility measures.
- Detailed balance of the general Metropolis–Hastings kernel: For a target probabilityπ and proposal kernelQ, the general Metropolis–Hastings kernel with acceptance min(1,r), where r is the reversed/forward joint-proposal Radon–Nikodym ratio on their common part and zero on the forward-only part, satisfies detailed balance withπ and hence leavesπ invariant.
- Equivalent petite-set drift from weighted uniform ergodicity: If a ψ-irreducible Markov kernel is V-uniformly ergodic for finite V≥1, then there are a finite V₀ with c⁻¹V≤V₀≤cV, a petite set C on which V₀ is bounded, λ<1 and b<∞ such that PV₀≤λV₀+b1_C.
- Gibbs distribution on a finite configuration space: For a finite set Ω, energy H:Ω→ℝ and inverse temperature β≥0, the Gibbs distribution is μ(σ)=exp(−βH(σ))/Z, where Z=∑_{τ∈Ω}exp(−βH(τ)).
- Harris geometric ergodicity from petite-set drift: LetP be ψ-irreducible and aperiodic. If a finite measurableV≥1, a petite setC withsup_CV<∞,0≤a<1 andb<∞ satisfyPV≤aV+b1_C, thenP is positive Harris recurrent andV-uniformly ergodic.
- Invariant moment bound from geometric drift: IfP is a Markov kernel,πP=π and a finite measurableV≥0 satisfiesPV≤aV+b with0≤a<1,0≤b<∞, thenπ(V)≤b/(1−a); no prior integrability assumption onV is required.
- Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality for a lower semicontinuous metric: For probability measures μ,ν on a Polish space equipped with a finite lower semicontinuous metric d, W_d(μ,ν)=sup_f|∫f dμ−∫f dν|, where f ranges over bounded Borel functions with |f(x)−f(y)|≤d(x,y). The equality is understood in the extended nonnegative reals; finite first moments also permit arbitrary integrable1-Lipschitz tests.
- Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling: Use a Markov chain with a prescribed stationary distribution to generate dependent approximate samples from that distribution,with ergodic averages used to estimate expectations.
- Power interpolation of weighted uniform ergodicity: If ||P^n(x,·)−π||_V≤Cρ^nV(x), π(V)<∞ and V≥1, then for every 0<r<1 the chain is V^r-uniformly ergodic, with bound 2^(1−r)C^rρ^(nr)V(x)^r.
- Total variation bound by chi-squared divergence: For probability measures P,Q, the full total variation norm satisfies ||P−Q||_TV≤√χ²(P‖Q), with the inequality understood in the extended sense. The convention sup_A|P(A)−Q(A)| has the additional factor1/2.
- Translation bound for a bounded unimodal density: If a real probability density h is weakly unimodal and h≤H, then ∫|h(z)−h(z−a)|dz≤2H|a| for every a∈ℝ.
- Unadjusted Langevin algorithm: For a differentiable positive target density π on ℝ^d and step h>0, the unadjusted Langevin chain is X_(n+1)=X_n+h∇logπ(X_n)+√(2h)Z_(n+1), with independent standard Gaussian vectors Z_n. It is the Euler discretization of the Langevin diffusion and generally does not preserve π exactly.
- Wasserstein contraction and submultiplicativity for Markov kernels: For Markov kernels P,Q on the declared lower-semicontinuous metric state space, W_d(μP,νP)≤τ_d(P)W_d(μ,ν) and τ_d(PQ)≤τ_d(P)τ_d(Q), whenever the products are unambiguous; the zero-coefficient case means all rows coincide.
- Weak and strong weighted kernel perturbations for autoregressions: For AR(1) kernels P_α with any innovation density of finite first moment and V=1+|x|, ||P_β−P_α||_(B_1→B_V)→0 as β→α. For fixed 0<α<1, the stronger norm ||P_β−P_α||_(B_V→B_V) need not tend to0 as β↓α; in fact its liminf is positive for every such density.
- Weighted geometric ergodicity of an autoregression with a density: For a real AR(1) kernel with |α|<1 and iid innovations having a Lebesgue density and finite first absolute moment, V(x)=1+|x| gives V-uniform ergodicity. For every ρ with |α|<ρ<1 there is C_ρ<∞ such that ||P^n(x,·)−π||_V≤C_ρρ^nV(x). This statement does not assert that the endpoint rate |α| is attained.
- Weighted total variation as a Wasserstein distance: For a lower semicontinuous weight V≥1 on a Polish space, set d_V(x,y)=(V(x)+V(y))1_(x≠y). For probabilities μ,ν with finite V-moments, W_(d_V)(μ,ν)=‖μ−ν‖_V.

1. Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling
   Use a Markov chain with a prescribed stationary distribution to generate dependent approximate samples from that distribution,with ergodic averages used to estimate expectations.
   Source: relies_on, §1; §4
   Note: Standard MCMC motivation and algorithmic background.

2. Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality for a lower semicontinuous metric
   For probability measures μ,ν on a Polish space equipped with a finite lower semicontinuous metric d, W_d(μ,ν)=sup_f|∫f dμ−∫f dν|, where f ranges over bounded Borel functions with |f(x)−f(y)|≤d(x,y). The equality is understood in the extended nonnegative reals; finite first moments also permit arbitrary integrable1-Lipschitz tests.
   Source: relies_on, §2, (2.1)
   Note: Fundamental imported optimal-transport duality; no origin assigned to this paper.

3. Wasserstein contraction and submultiplicativity for Markov kernels
   For Markov kernels P,Q on the declared lower-semicontinuous metric state space, W_d(μP,νP)≤τ_d(P)W_d(μ,ν) and τ_d(PQ)≤τ_d(P)τ_d(Q), whenever the products are unambiguous; the zero-coefficient case means all rows coincide.
   Source: relies_on, Proposition2.1
   Note: Imported Dobrushin contraction/submultiplicativity properties.

4. Weighted total variation as a Wasserstein distance
   For a lower semicontinuous weight V≥1 on a Polish space, set d_V(x,y)=(V(x)+V(y))1_(x≠y). For probabilities μ,ν with finite V-moments, W_(d_V)(μ,ν)=‖μ−ν‖_V.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma3.1
   Note: The identity is explicitly credited to Hairer–Mattingly[17] and Mao–Zhang–Zhang[26].

5. Foster–Lyapunov drift condition for a Markov kernel
   A Markov kernel P satisfies a geometric Foster–Lyapunov condition with V≥1 if PV≤aV+b for constants0≤a<1 and b<∞. Compact sublevel sets of V make the resulting occupation-law bounds useful for tightness.
   Source: relies_on, Theorems3.1–3.2; §4
   Note: The approximate chain’s moment bound is a stability input, weaker than ergodicity.

6. V-uniform ergodicity of a discrete-time Markov kernel
   A Markov kernel P is V-uniformly ergodic if it has an invariant probability π with π(V)<∞ and there are C<∞ and 0≤ρ<1 such that ‖P^n(x,·)−π‖_V≤CV(x)ρ^n for all x and n≥0, for a measurable weight V≥1.
   Source: relies_on, §3.2; applications
   Note: A weighted convergence assumption is distinct from drift alone.

7. Metropolis–Hastings transition kernel
   For a target probability π and proposal kernel Q, let r(x,y) be the Radon–Nikodym ratio of the reversed joint proposal law π(dy)Q(y,dx) to the forward law π(dx)Q(x,dy) on their mutually absolutely continuous part, and set r=0 on the forward-only singular part. The acceptance probability a=min(1,r) gives P(x,dy)=a(x,y)Q(x,dy)+(1−∫a(x,z)Q(x,dz))δ_x(dy).
   Source: relies_on, §4.2
   Note: Standard acceptance/rejection kernel and Radon–Nikodym ratio.

8. Bayesian posterior density
   For a prior density p(θ) and likelihood ℓ(y|θ), the posterior density given observation y is π(θ|y)=ℓ(y|θ)p(θ)/∫ℓ(y|u)p(u)du, when the denominator is positive and finite.
   Source: relies_on, §4.3
   Note: Prior/likelihood/posterior calculation supplies the Langevin target.

9. Unadjusted Langevin algorithm
   For a differentiable positive target density π on ℝ^d and step h>0, the unadjusted Langevin chain is X_(n+1)=X_n+h∇logπ(X_n)+√(2h)Z_(n+1), with independent standard Gaussian vectors Z_n. It is the Euler discretization of the Langevin diffusion and generally does not preserve π exactly.
   Source: relies_on, §4.3
   Note: The discretized target differs from the invariant law in general.

10. Autoregressive process of order one
   A real AR(1) process satisfies X_n=aX_(n−1)+Z_n, where (Z_n) are i.i.d. real innovations independent of X₀; the stable parameter range is |a|<1.
   Source: relies_on, §4.1
   Note: AR(1) examples show linear error sensitivity.

11. Radon–Nikodym theorem for finite measures
   If finite positive measuresν,μ on one measurable space satisfyν≪μ, there is a nonnegative integrable f withν(A)=∫_Af,dμ for every measurableA, unique up toμ-null sets.
   Source: relies_on, §4.2, (4.5)

- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Monotone real function [definition]: A real function on a linearly ordered set is nondecreasing if x≤y impliesf(x)≤f(y), and nonincreasing if the reverse inequality holds; monotone means either.
- Multivariate normal distribution [definition]: A distribution of a real vector X for which every real linear combination of coordinates is normally distributed; it is specified by its mean vector and positive semidefinite covariance matrix.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normal distribution [definition]: The real probability distribution N(m,σ²) with density (2πσ²)⁻¹ᐟ²exp(−(x−m)²/(2σ²)) for σ>0; variance zero gives a point mass at m.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Polish space [definition]: A topological space admitting a complete separable metric that induces its topology.
- Positive Harris recurrence [definition]: A ψ-irreducible Markov chain is Harris recurrent if every ψ-positive measurable set is visited infinitely often almost surely from every initial state; it is positive Harris recurrent if it also admits an invariant probability.
- Positive semidefinite real matrix [definition]: A symmetric real matrix A such that xᵀAx≥0 for every real column vector x.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Probability density relative to a measure [definition]: A nonnegative measurable function P with ∫P dμ=1; the measure P dμ is the corresponding probability measure.
- Probability kernel between measurable spaces [definition]: A probability kernel from(S,Σ) to(E,ℰ) assigns a probability measure K(s,·) onE to each s∈S, with s↦K(s,A) Σ-measurable for every A∈ℰ. A random probability measure is such a kernel with a probability space as its domain.
- Probability mass function on a finite set [definition]: A function p from a finite set Ω to [0,1] satisfying ∑_{ω∈Ω}p(ω)=1; the probability of A⊆Ω is ∑_{ω∈A}p(ω).
- Probability space [definition]: A probability space is (Ω,𝔽,P), where 𝔽 is a σ-algebra on Ω and P is a measure on 𝔽 with P(Ω)=1.
- Product measure [construction]: For σ-finite measure spaces, the product measure on the product σ-algebra assigns a measurable rectangle A×B the value μ(A)ν(B); its completion also includes subsets of product-null sets.
- Radon–Nikodym derivative of a measure [definition]: A nonnegative measurable function f representing ν relative to μ by ν(A)=∫_A f dμ for every measurable A; it is defined up to μ-almost-everywhere equality when such a density exists.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Real-valued random variable [definition]: A real-valued random variable is a measurable map from a probability space to ℝ with its Borel σ-algebra.
- Regular conditional distribution for a Polish-valued random variable [theorem]: If X takes values in a Polish spaceE and G⊆F is a sub-σ-algebra, there is a G-measurable probability kernel K(ω,·) with∫f,dK(ω)=E[f(X)|G](ω) almost surely for every bounded Borel f. It is unique up to a common null set. Equivalently a probability measure on a product with Polish second factor disintegrates into its first marginal and such conditional probabilities.
- Separable metric space [definition]: A metric space having a countable dense subset.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Stationary distribution of a finite Markov chain [definition]: A probability row vector π satisfying πP=π for the chain’s transition matrix P.
- Stochastic process [definition]: A family (X_t) indexed by a specified time set, with each X_t a measurable map from a probability space into a specified measurable state space. Adaptation, joint measurability and path regularity are additional conditions when required.
- Stochastic transition matrix [definition]: A square real matrix P with nonnegative entries and row sums one; P(x,y) is the one-step transition probability from x to y.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Symmetric matrix [definition]: A square matrix A equal to its transpose, so aᵢⱼ=aⱼᵢ.
- Time-homogeneous Markov chain on a finite state space [definition]: A sequence of random states in a finite set whose conditional next-state distribution given its whole past depends only on the present state, through one fixed stochastic transition matrix.
- Time-homogeneous Markov chain on a measurable state space [definition]: A discrete-time process (X_n) on a measurable space with a probability kernel P is Markov when P(X_(n+1)∈A|X₀,…,X_n)=P(X_n,A) almost surely for every measurable A; the same kernel is used at every step.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Wasserstein Dobrushin coefficient of a Markov kernel [definition]: For a Markov kernel P on a metric state space, its Wasserstein coefficient is τ_d(P)=sup_(x≠y)W_d(P(x,·),P(y,·))/d(x,y), allowing infinity.
- Wasserstein distance of order one for a lower semicontinuous metric [definition]: On a Polish space E with a finite-valued metric d lower semicontinuous for its given product topology, W_d(μ,ν)=inf_(γ coupling μ,ν)∫d(x,y)dγ(x,y), allowing the value∞. The metric d need not induce the original Polish topology.
- Weakly unimodal density on the real line [definition]: A real probability density h is weakly unimodal if some m∈ℝ makes h nondecreasing on (−∞,m) and nonincreasing on (m,∞); jumps and flat portions are allowed.
- Weighted supremum space of measurable functions [definition]: For a finite measurable weight V≥1 on a measurable space E, B_V consists of real or complex measurable f with ||f||_V=sup_x|f(x)|/V(x)<∞; with pointwise operations this is a Banach space.
- Weighted total variation norm of a signed measure [definition]: For a measurable weight V≥1 and a signed measure μ, its weighted variation norm is ‖μ‖_V=sup{|∫f dμ|:f measurable, |f|≤V}, allowing infinity. The convention V=1 is the full total variation norm, twice the usual supremum-over-events distance for differences of probability measures.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

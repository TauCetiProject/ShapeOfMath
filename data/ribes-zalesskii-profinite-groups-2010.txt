LOCATED CONCEPTS (3)

1. Frattini subgroup of a profinite group
   For a profinite group G, the Frattini subgroup Φ(G) is the intersection of all maximal open subgroups of G, the proper open subgroups contained in no larger proper open subgroup (Φ(G)=G if G is trivial). It is a closed normal subgroup, and G/Φ(G) is the Frattini quotient of G.
   Source: explains, §2.8, p. 52-53
   Note: Selected learning or proof reference; not a priority claim.

2. Burnside basis theorem for pro-p groups
   Let Π be a topologically finitely generated pro-p group and Φ(Π) its Frattini subgroup, the intersection of its maximal open subgroups. Then Π/Φ(Π) is a finite-dimensional 𝔽_p-vector space, and a subset of Π generates a dense subgroup exactly when its image spans Π/Φ(Π). Hence the minimal number of topological generators is dim_𝔽p Π/Φ(Π).
   Source: explains, §2.8, Lemma 2.8.6-Prop. 2.8.10
   Note: Selected learning or proof reference; not a priority claim.

3. Schur–Zassenhaus theorem for profinite groups
   Let G be a profinite group and P a closed normal pro-p subgroup such that G/P is finite of order prime to p. Then P has a complement in G, a closed subgroup H with H∩P=1 and HP=G, and any two complements are conjugate in G.
   Source: explains, Thm 2.3.15
   Note: Selected learning or proof reference; not a priority claim.

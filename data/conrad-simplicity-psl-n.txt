LOCATED CONCEPTS (4)

1. Simplicity of the projective special linear group
   For n≥2 and a field F, the group PSL_n(F)=SL_n(F)/(SL_n(F)∩F^×I) is simple, except for n=2 and F with two or three elements.
   Source: explains, Thms 3.5, §4
   Note: Selected learning or proof reference; not a priority claim.

2. Perfectness of SL₂ over a field with more than three elements
   For a field F with more than three elements, SL₂(F) equals its commutator subgroup. In particular SL₂(𝔽_p) is perfect for every prime p≥5, whereas SL₂(𝔽₂) and SL₂(𝔽₃) are not.
   Source: explains, Thm 3.4
   Note: Selected learning or proof reference; not a priority claim.

3. Projective special linear group over a field
   For a field F and n≥1, PSL_n(F)=SL_n(F)/Z, where Z=SL_n(F)∩F^×I is the group of scalar matrices of determinant 1, which is the centre of SL_n(F).
   Source: explains, §1
   Note: Selected learning or proof reference; not a priority claim.

4. Iwasawa’s simplicity criterion
   Let a perfect group G act doubly transitively on a set X, and suppose the stabilizer of some point has an abelian normal subgroup whose conjugates in G generate G. Then G/K is simple, where K is the kernel of the action.
   Source: explains, Thm 2.10
   Note: Selected learning or proof reference; not a priority claim.

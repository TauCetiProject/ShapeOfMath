LOCATED CONCEPTS (6)

1. Spherical harmonic decomposition of L² of a sphere
   The real Hilbert space L²(S^{n−1}) is the orthogonal sum of the finite-dimensional spaces of spherical harmonics of degrees k≥0; a polynomial of degree m restricts to a sum of harmonics of degrees at most m.
   Source: explains, Theorem 5.12
   Note: Selected proof exposition (complex-valued functions); not an origin claim.

2. Orthogonal direct-sum decomposition of a Hilbert space
   A real or complex Hilbert space H is the orthogonal direct sum ⊕_m H_m of a sequence of closed subspaces when the H_m are pairwise orthogonal and every x∈H is a norm-convergent sum ∑_m x_m with x_m∈H_m; the expansion is then unique. For pairwise orthogonal closed subspaces this holds exactly when their linear span is dense.
   Source: explains, Conditions (a)–(c) before Theorem 5.12
   Note: Definition recalled; not an origin claim.

3. Poisson representation of positive harmonic functions on the disk
   Every nonnegative harmonic function h on the unit disk is uniquely h(z)=∫_T(1−|z|²)/|ζ−z|²dμ(ζ) for a finite positive Borel measure μ on the unit circle.
   Source: explains, Corollary 6.15 with Theorems 6.9, 6.12, 6.13
   Note: Selected proof inspected for cone-022 (n=2 case); no priority claim.

4. Fischer decomposition of homogeneous polynomials
   Every real homogeneous polynomial of degreeN inℝᵈ has a unique decomposition∑_{j=0}^{⌊N/2⌋}|x|^{2j}H_{N−2j}(x), where eachH_k is homogeneous and harmonic. ConsequentlydimH_k=binom(k+d−1,d−1)−binom(k+d−3,d−1), with a negative-degree term interpreted as zero.
   Source: explains, Proposition 5.5, Theorem 5.7, Proposition 5.8, pp. 75–78
   Note: Selected proof exposition; not an origin claim.

5. Poisson-integral solution of the Dirichlet problem for the ball
   For continuous f on the unit sphere S⊂ℝⁿ, the function equal on the open unit ball to P[f](x)=∫_S f(ζ)(1−|x|²)/|x−ζ|ⁿ dσ(ζ), with σ normalized surface measure, and equal to f on S is continuous on the closed ball and harmonic in the open ball.
   Source: explains, Theorem 1.17, p. 12
   Note: Statement located; proof not inspected for cone-012.

6. Maximum principle for harmonic functions
   A real harmonic function on an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ that attains its maximum or minimum at a point is constant on the connected component containing that point. Its values lie between the infimum of its lower boundary limits and the supremum of its upper boundary limits.
   Source: explains, Maximum Principle 1.8 and Corollary 1.9, p. 7
   Note: Exposition with proof (1.8 via the mean-value property; 1.9 as its bounded-domain corollary); not an origin claim.

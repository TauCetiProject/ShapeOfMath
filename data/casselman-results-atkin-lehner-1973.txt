LOCATED CONCEPTS (3)

1. Casselman’s newform theorem for local components of GL₂
   For an irreducible infinite-dimensional admissible representation π of GL₂(F), F a nonarchimedean local field, the vectors fixed by K₁(𝔭^c)={(a b;c d)∈GL₂(O):c≡0, d≡1 mod 𝔭^c} vanish for c<a(π) and form a line for c=a(π), the conductor exponent. For the automorphic representation of a normalized newform of level N, the local component at p therefore has a(π_p)=v_p(N).
   Source: explains, §1 Theorem 1; §3 Theorem 4
   Note: Casselman's own theorem (Novodvorskii 1971 independently); recorded only as the inspected proof.

2. Automorphic representation of a classical newform
   For a normalized newform f of weight k, level N and character χ, write GL₂(A_ℚ)=GL₂(ℚ)·GL₂⁺(ℝ)·∏_pK_p with K_p the closure of Γ₀(N) in GL₂(ℤ_p), and put φ_f(γg_∞k)=(f|_k g_∞)(i)·χ(k). The right translates of φ_f span an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation π_f of GL₂(A_ℚ), a restricted tensor product ⊗′_vπ_v whose finite components π_p are irreducible admissible infinite-dimensional representations of GL₂(ℚ_p).
   Source: explains, §3 Theorem 3
   Note: Selected proof reference; not a priority claim.

3. Admissible representation of GL_n over a nonarchimedean local field
   Let F be a nonarchimedean local field with valuation ring O. A representation π of GL_n(F) on a complex vector space V is smooth if every vector is fixed by some open subgroup of GL_n(O), and admissible if moreover, for every open subgroup K of GL_n(O), the space V^K of K-fixed vectors is finite-dimensional. It is irreducible if V≠0 and V has no proper nonzero GL_n(F)-stable subspace.
   Source: explains, §1 (p. 302)
   Note: Selected proof reference; not a priority claim.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (4)

1. Singer’s attracting-cycle theorem for negative Schwarzian maps
   For a C³ interval map with negative Schwarzian derivative wherever its derivative is nonzero, the immediate basin of each attracting periodic orbit contains a critical point or an endpoint of the interval.
   Source: explains, Theorem 0.5 and proof, pp. 10-4 to 10-6
   Note: Lecture-note proof of Singer’s theorem; credited to D. Singer.

2. Schwarzian derivative of a C³ real function
   For a C³ real function f on an interval and a point x with f′(x)≠0, Sf(x)=f‴(x)/f′(x)−(3/2)(f″(x)/f′(x))². A C³ interval map has negative Schwarzian derivative if Sf(x)<0 at every x where f′(x)≠0.
   Source: explains, Definition, p. 10-1
   Note: Definition of Sf and of negative Schwarzian derivative.

3. Minimum principle for maps with negative Schwarzian derivative
   If f is C³ on an interval I, f′ has no zero in I and Sf<0 on I, then |f′| has no local minimum in the interior of I. Thus on a compact subinterval the minimum of |f′| is attained only at an endpoint.
   Source: explains, Lemma 0.4, pp. 10-3 to 10-4
   Note: Statement and proof of the minimum principle.

4. Composition rule for the Schwarzian derivative
   For C³ maps f and g with g′(x)≠0 and f′(g(x))≠0, S(f∘g)(x)=(Sf)(g(x))·g′(x)²+Sg(x). Consequently every iterate fⁿ of a C³ interval map with negative Schwarzian derivative again has negative Schwarzian derivative.
   Source: explains, Proposition 0.2 and Corollary 0.3, p. 10-3
   Note: Statement only; proof left as an exercise.

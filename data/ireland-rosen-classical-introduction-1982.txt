LOCATED CONCEPTS (3)

1. Power-sum congruence for Bernoulli numbers
   For integers m≥2 and k≥1, write B_(2k)=N_(2k)/D_(2k) in lowest terms with D_(2k)>0. Then m·N_(2k)≡D_(2k)·∑_(j=1)^(m−1) j^(2k) modulo m².
   Source: explains, Ch.15 §2 Proposition 15.2.2 with proof, pp.236–237
   Note: V_mS_m(n)≡U_mn (mod n²) for even m≥2 and all n≥1.

2. Bernoulli’s formula for sums of powers
   For integers m≥1 and n≥1, the power sum S_m(n)=0^m+1^m+⋯+(n−1)^m satisfies (m+1)S_m(n)=∑_(k=0)^m C(m+1,k)B_k n^(m+1−k), where B_k are the Bernoulli numbers (B₁=−1/2).
   Source: explains, Ch.15 §1 Theorem 1 with proof, p.230

3. Von Staudt–Clausen theorem
   For every k≥1, B_(2k)+∑1/p, summed over the primes p with (p−1) dividing 2k, is an integer. Hence the reduced denominator of B_(2k) is the squarefree product of those primes.
   Source: explains, Ch.15 §1 Theorem 3, p.233; proof §2 pp.235–236

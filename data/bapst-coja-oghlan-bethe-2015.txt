REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (20)

- Azuma–Hoeffding inequality: If a real martingale has increments bounded in absolute value by c_i, then P(|M_n−M_0|≥t)≤2exp(−t²/(2∑_{i=1}^nc_i²)) for t>0.
- Bethe free energy of factor-graph marginals: For consistent variable and factor marginals p_x,p_a, the Bethe log-partition functional is ∑_a[H(p_a)+E_{p_a}lnψ_a]−∑_x(d_x−1)H(p_x). On a limiting rooted factor-tree law one uses the corresponding expected local terms, normalized per variable node.
- Bounded martingale convergence theorem: A martingale uniformly bounded by a deterministic constant converges almost surely and in L¹. In particular, conditional expectations of a bounded X along increasing σ-algebras converge to E[X|σ(∪_nF_n)].
- Chernoff bound for sums of independent Bernoulli variables: If S is a sum of independent Bernoulli variables with mean μ, then for δ>0, P(S≥(1+δ)μ)≤[e^δ/(1+δ)^(1+δ)]^μ; for 0<δ<1, P(S≤(1−δ)μ)≤exp(−μδ²/2).
- Compactness of bounded-degree rooted graphs with finite marks: Fix a degree bound Δ and a finite set of vertex/edge marks. Isomorphism classes of connected rooted graphs of degree at mostΔ, with those marks, form a compact metrizable space for local convergence: two rooted graphs are close when their marked balls around the root agree to a large radius. Finite and countably infinite graphs are allowed.
- Configuration model for a prescribed degree sequence: Attach the prescribed number of half-edges to each vertex and match half-edges uniformly at random; collapsing each half-edge bundle gives a random multigraph with those degrees. Bipartite typed versions match compatible half-edge classes.
- Decay of boundary influence for Gibbs measures on trees: On rooted factor trees, the root marginal changes by arbitrarily little in total variation under every possible boundary condition at sufficiently large distance. This uniform condition yields uniqueness of the limiting root marginal.
- Girth of a graph: The length of a shortest cycle, with girth infinity for an acyclic graph. In a multigraph a loop has length one and two parallel edges form a cycle of length two.
- Jensen’s inequality for expectations: For an integrable real random variable X and convex φ with E|φ(X)|<∞, φ(EX)≤Eφ(X), when φ is defined on an interval containing the range of X.
- Law of total variance: For square-integrable X and sub-σ-algebra G, Var(X)=E[Var(X|G)]+Var(E[X|G]).
- Local weak convergence of bounded-degree graphs: A bounded-degree graph sequence converges locally to a random rooted graph if, for each finite radius and rooted neighborhood type, the proportion of vertices with that neighborhood converges to the corresponding root-neighborhood probability. For random graphs this convergence may be in probability.
- Markov’s inequality for nonnegative random variables: If Y is a nonnegative integrable real random variable and t>0, then P(Y≥t)≤E[Y]/t.
- Non-reconstruction for Gibbs measures on sparse graphs: The spin at a typical vertex becomes asymptotically independent of a randomly sampled distant boundary condition, measured by the expected total variation change of its conditional marginal as the boundary radius tends to infinity.
- Paley–Zygmund inequality: For nonnegative X with 0<E[X²]<∞ and 0<θ<1, P(X>θEX)≥(1−θ)²(EX)²/E[X²].
- Partition function of a finite Gibbs model: The normalizing sum Z=∑_σ exp(−βH(σ)); equivalently Z=∑_σ∏_aψ_a(σ) for positive factor weights. Its logarithm records the model’s free energy up to normalization.
- Polish space: A topological space admitting a complete separable metric that induces its topology.
- Relative entropy of finite probability distributions: D(p∥q)=∑_ωp(ω)ln(p(ω)/q(ω)), with a positive p(ω) and zero q(ω) giving +∞ and zero p terms contributing zero.
- Size-biased change of measure: Given a probability measure P and a nonnegative random variable W with 0<E_PW<∞, the W-biased measure is Q(A)=E_P[W·1_A]/E_PW; equivalently dQ/dP=W/E_PW.
- Stirling’s formula: As n→∞, n!∼√(2πn)(n/e)^n.
- Weak compactness of probabilities on a compact metric space: The Borel probability measures on a compact metric space form a compact metrizable space for weak convergence; in particular every sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence.

1. Probability mass function on a finite set

3. Product distribution on a finite product space
   For probability mass functions p_i on finite sets Ω_i, the product distribution on ∏_iΩ_i assigns (ω_i)_i the probability ∏_ip_i(ω_i).

   Source: relies_on, Notation,p.3;§2 proofs

5. Shannon entropy of a finite distribution
   For a finite probability mass function p, H(p)=−∑_ωp(ω)ln p(ω), with 0ln0=0.

6. Relative entropy of finite probability distributions
   D(p∥q)=∑_ωp(ω)ln(p(ω)/q(ω)), with a positive p(ω) and zero q(ω) giving +∞ and zero p terms contributing zero.

7. Conditional expectation given a σ-algebra
   For integrable X and a sub-σ-algebra G, E[X|G] is a G-measurable integrable random variable satisfying ∫_AE[X|G]dP=∫_AXdP for every A∈G, defined up to almost-sure equality.

- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linearity of the Lebesgue integral [theorem]: If f and g are integrable real functions and a,b are real scalars, then af+bg is integrable and ∫(af+bg)dμ=a∫f dμ+b∫g dμ.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Partition of a set [definition]: A family of nonempty pairwise disjoint subsets whose union is the set.
- Path in an undirected graph [definition]: A finite sequence of adjacent edges joining successive vertices; a simple path has no repeated vertex. Its length is the number of edges.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Probability space [definition]: A probability space is (Ω,𝔽,P), where 𝔽 is a σ-algebra on Ω and P is a measure on 𝔽 with P(Ω)=1.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Real-valued random variable [definition]: A real-valued random variable is a measurable map from a probability space to ℝ with its Borel σ-algebra.
- Rooted locally finite graph [definition]: A connected graph with a distinguished root and finitely many incident edges at every vertex; finite-radius rooted neighborhoods retain their induced graph structure.
- Separable metric space [definition]: A metric space having a countable dense subset.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Undirected multigraph [definition]: A vertex set and an edge set, with two unordered endpoints assigned to each edge; distinct edges can have the same endpoints and loops are allowed.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Variance of a real random variable [definition]: If EX²<∞, then Var(X)=E[(X−EX)²].
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

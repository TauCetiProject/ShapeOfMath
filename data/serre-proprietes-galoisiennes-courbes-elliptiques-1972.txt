LOCATED CONCEPTS (5)

1. Serre’s open image theorem for elliptic curves
   Let E be an elliptic curve over a number field F without complex multiplication over an algebraic closure. For every prime p the image of Gal(F̄/F) in Aut_ℤp(T_pE)≅GL₂(ℤ_p) is open, hence of finite index, and it equals Aut_ℤp(T_pE) for all but finitely many p.
   Source: explains, Introduction (4)-(7); §4.2 Théorème 2; §4.4 Théorème 3
   Note: Inspected proof. The Introduction credits openness for each ℓ (4) to Serre 1968 (MG IV-11) and calls the almost-all-ℓ assertion (5) the new result; originality of the combined statement is not assessed here.

2. Serre’s description of inertia on the p-torsion of an elliptic curve over an unramified p-adic field
   Let K be a finite unramified extension of ℚ_p and E/K an elliptic curve with good or multiplicative reduction. For good ordinary (height one) or multiplicative reduction, inertia acts on E[p] by matrices (θ *; 0 1) with θ the level-one fundamental character, so its image has order p−1 or p(p−1). For good supersingular (height two) reduction, E[p] is a line over 𝔽_(p²) on which inertia acts by the level-two fundamental character; its image is a nonsplit Cartan subgroup of order p²−1.
   Source: explains, §1.11-1.12, Props. 11-13 and corollaries

3. Subgroups of GL₂(𝔽_p) containing a Cartan subgroup
   Let p be prime and G a subgroup of GL₂(𝔽_p) containing a Cartan subgroup, or containing a half split Cartan subgroup (conjugate to the diagonal matrices diag(a,1)) with p≠5 in that case. Then G=GL₂(𝔽_p), or G lies in a Borel subgroup, or G lies in the normalizer of a Cartan subgroup.
   Source: explains, §2.7 Prop. 17

4. Serre’s criterion for compatible systems with abelian reductions
   Let (ρ_ℓ) be a system of semisimple ℓ-adic representations of a number field K that is rational and strictly compatible, with Frobenius polynomials in ℚ[t] independent of ℓ. Suppose that for infinitely many ℓ the semisimplified reduction of ρ_ℓ modulo ℓ is abelian and its characters, restricted to the local units at ℓ via class field theory, are products of conjugates of the inverse reduction map with exponents bounded by a fixed N. Then the system comes from a representation of one of Serre’s groups S_m over ℚ; in particular every ρ_ℓ is abelian.
   Source: explains, §3.6 Théorème 1 and corollary

5. Local reciprocity and the fundamental characters of tame inertia
   Let K be a nonarchimedean local field with residue field k of q elements and L/K an abelian tamely ramified extension. The local reciprocity map sends the units O_K^× onto the inertia group I(L/K), factoring through k^×, and the composite of the tame inertia group I_t→I(L/K) with this identification is the inverse of the fundamental character θ_(q−1):I_t→k^×: an element s acts as the reciprocity image of θ_(q−1)(s)^(−1).
   Source: explains, §1.5 Prop. 3

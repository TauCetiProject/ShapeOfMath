LOCATED CONCEPTS (3)

1. Two-sided Brownian motion
   A real process (W_t)_{t∈ℝ} with W_0=0 such that (W_t)_{t≥0} and (W_{−t})_{t≥0} are independent standard Brownian motions.
   Source: explains, §4, opening paragraph, p. 4
   Note: Defines the standard Wiener process on ℝ as two independent one-sided halves.

2. Wiener integral of a deterministic square-integrable function
   For a two-sided Brownian motion W, the Wiener integral ∫_ℝf dW is the linear isometry I:L²(ℝ)→L²(P) with I(1_{(s,t]})=W_t−W_s for s<t, extended from step functions by L² limits. Its values are jointly centred Gaussian with E[I(f)I(g)]=∫fg.
   Source: explains, §4, pp. 4–5
   Note: Constructs the Wiener integral on step functions and extends it isometrically to L²(ℝ); lists linearity, mean zero, isometry and joint Gaussianity.

3. Mandelbrot–Van Ness representation of fractional Brownian motion
   For H∈(0,1),H≠½, a two-sided standard fractional Brownian motion can be represented as B_t^H=c_H∫_ℝ[(t−s)_+^{H−½}−(−s)_+^{H−½}]dW_s, with c_H chosen for variance1 at time1. The difference kernel is square integrable; H=½ is ordinary Brownian motion.
   Source: explains, §4 Theorem 4.1 and proof, p. 6
   Note: Proves the moving-average representation by computing the variogram; later exposition, not the origin (Mandelbrot–Van Ness 1968).

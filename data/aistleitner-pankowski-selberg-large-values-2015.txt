REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (21)

- Abel's summation formula: If Aₖ=∑ⱼ₌₁ᵏaⱼ, then ∑ₖ₌₁ᴺaₖbₖ=A_N b_N+∑ₖ₌₁ᴺ⁻¹Aₖ(bₖ−bₖ₊₁).
- Borel–Carathéodory theorem: Let f be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk |z−a|≤R and 0<r<R. Then max_{|z−a|≤r}|f(z)−f(a)|≤2r/(R−r)·(max_{|z−a|≤R}Re f(z)−Re f(a)).
- Chen’s weighted quantitative Kronecker approximation theorem: Let M≥1, λ,β∈ℝⁿ satisfy u·β∈ℤ whenever u∈ℤⁿ, |u_j|≤M and u·λ=0. For δ_j>0, Δ=∑δ_j, T₁<T₂, and Λ=min{|u·λ|:u∈ℤⁿ, |u_j|≤M, u·λ≠0}, one has inf_(T₁≤t<T₂)∑δ_j‖tλ_j−β_j‖²≤(Δ/4)sin²(π/(2(M+1)))+ΔMⁿ/(4πΛ(T₂−T₁)), provided the defining minimum for Λ is nonempty.
- Convexity bound for the Selberg class: For L in the Selberg class with gamma degree d_L=2∑λ_j, every ε>0 gives |L(σ+it)|≪_{L,ε}|t|^{d_L(1−σ)/2+ε}, uniformly 0≤σ≤1 and |t|≥2.
- Expansion of the logarithmic integral below one: For 0<x<1, li(x)=C+log(−log x)+∑_{j≥1}(log x)^j/(j·j!), where C is an absolute real constant. In particular li(x)=log(−log x)+O(1) as x↑1.
- Fourier transform on Euclidean space: For an integrable f on ℝⁿ, its Fourier transform is f̂(ξ)=∫f(x)e⁻ⁱˣ·ξ dx; extend it to Schwartz functions and tempered distributions by duality.
- Fundamental theorem of arithmetic: Every integer n≥2 factors as a product of primes uniquely up to ordering.
- Hadamard three-circles theorem: For a holomorphic f on an annulus r₁≤|z−a|≤r₃, continuous on its boundary, set M(r)=max_{|z−a|=r}|f(z)|. For r₁<r₂<r₃, log M(r₂)≤[log(r₃/r₂)log M(r₁)+log(r₂/r₁)log M(r₃)]/log(r₃/r₁), with zero values interpreted by a limit.
- Local logarithmic-derivative expansion in the Selberg class: For a fixed Selberg-class L, uniformly −5/2≤σ≤7/2 and sufficiently large |t| (depending on L) at points s=σ+it which are not zeros or poles, L′(s)/L(s)=∑_{ρ:|Imρ−t|≤1}(s−ρ)⁻¹+O_L(log|t|), with nontrivial zeros counted with multiplicity.
- Local zero count in the Selberg class: For a fixed L in the Selberg class and T≥2, the number of nontrivial zeros ρ with |Imρ−T|≤1 is O_L(log(T+3)), counted with multiplicity.
- Mellin inversion formula for the exponential function: For x>0 and c>0, e^(−x)=(2πi)^(−1)∫_(c−i∞)^(c+i∞)Γ(w)x^(−w)dw, interpreted as the limit of integrals over vertical segments.
- Phragmén–Lindelöf principle for a finite-order strip: Let f be holomorphic in a<Re z<b and continuous on its closure, with |f(σ+it)|≤exp(C(1+|t|)^N) uniformly in the strip. If |f|≤M on both vertical boundary lines, then |f|≤M throughout; if the two boundary values tend uniformly to zero as t→+∞, then the interior strip supremum also tends to zero.
- Prime number theorem with de la Vallée Poussin’s error term: There is c>0 for which ψ(x)=x+O(x exp(−c√log x)) as x→∞, where ψ(x)=∑_{n≤x}Λ(n).
- Ramanujan criterion for a polynomial Euler product: For a multiplicative Dirichlet series with fixed-length Euler factors ∏_{j=1}^m(1−α_j(p)p^{−s})⁻¹, the coefficient bounds |a(n)|≪εn^ε for every ε>0 are equivalent to |α_j(p)|≤1 for every p,j. In this case |a(p)|≤m.
- Rankin’s trick for nonnegative arithmetic sums: For a_n≥0, x>0 and δ>0, one has ∑_(n>x)a_n≤x^(−δ)∑_(n≥1)a_n n^δ whenever the sum on the right is finite.
- Residue theorem: For a meromorphic function near the closure of a bounded region with piecewise smooth oriented boundary, with finitely many poles inside and none on the boundary, the boundary integral is 2πi times the sum of the residues, with winding-number weights for a general closed contour.
- Second mean-value theorem for integrals: If f is a monotone real function on [a,b] and g is continuous there, some ξ∈[a,b] satisfies ∫ₐᵇfg=f(a)∫ₐ^ξg+f(b)∫_ξᵇg.
- Selberg-class function with a polynomial Euler product: A Selberg-class function has a polynomial Euler product of length m if L_p(s)=∏_(j=1)^m(1−α_j(p)p^(−s))^(−1) for every prime p, with fixed m; its Ramanujan bound implies |α_j(p)|≤1. The length m need not equal the functional-equation degree.
- Smoothed prime lower approximation for log L: Let L be in the Selberg class with Euler logarithm b(p^k)≪p^{kδ}, δ<1/2, and a(p)=|a(p)|e^{iωp}. If1/2≤σ<1,t₀≥15 and L(σ′+it)≠0 for σ′>σ, |t−t₀|≤2(log t₀)², then for every realθ and x>2 some |t₁|≤(log t₀)² gives Re(e^{−iθ}log L(σ+i(t₀+t₁)))≥(1/2)∑_{|log(p/x)|≤1}|a(p)|p^{−σ}cos(t₀logp−ωp)(1−|log(p/x)|)−C_L[x(log t₀)⁻²+x^{2δ−2σ+1/2}logx], using the Euler-normalized logarithm continued horizontally from Re s>1 and its finite boundary values.
- Smoothed second moment of a short Dirichlet polynomial: Let Φ∈C_c^∞((1,2)), ε>0 and R(t)=∑_{n≤N}r_n n^{−it}, with N≤T^{1−ε}. Then ∫ℝ|R(t)|²Φ(t/T)dt=T(∫Φ)∑|r_n|²+O_{Φ,ε}(∑|r_n|²), uniformly in all complex r_n and T≥2.
- Stirling estimate for gamma on vertical lines: Uniformly for σ in a fixed compact real interval and |t|→∞, |Γ(σ+it)|=sqrt(2π)|t|^(σ−1/2)e^(−π|t|/2)(1+O(1/|t|)).

1. Selberg class of Dirichlet series
   The Selberg class consists of Dirichlet series L(s)=∑a(n)n^(−s) with a(1)=1 and a(n)=O_ε(n^ε) for every ε>0, for which (s−1)^mL(s) is entire of finite order for some m≥0, and Λ(s)=Qˢ∏Γ(λ_js+μ_j)L(s) satisfies Λ(s)=ω overline(Λ(1−bar(s))) with Q,λ_j>0, Re μ_j≥0 and |ω|=1. Its Euler product has log L_p(s)=∑_(j≥1)b(pʲ)p^(−js), with b(pʲ)=O(p^(jδ)) for some δ<1/2. Its degree is d_L=2∑λ_j.
   Source: relies_on, §1
   Note: The four axioms govern analytic continuation and prime-power errors.

2. Selberg-class function with a polynomial Euler product
   A Selberg-class function has a polynomial Euler product of length m if L_p(s)=∏_(j=1)^m(1−α_j(p)p^(−s))^(−1) for every prime p, with fixed m; its Ramanujan bound implies |α_j(p)|≤1. The length m need not equal the functional-equation degree.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem 1.2; §2
   Note: Only the resonance route needs polynomial Euler factors.

3. Meromorphic gamma function
   The meromorphic gamma function is the continuation of Γ(z)=∫₀^∞t^(z−1)e^(−t)dt from Re z>0 to the complex plane using Γ(z+1)=zΓ(z); it has simple poles at 0,−1,−2,… .
   Source: relies_on, §1; §§2,4
   Note: Functional equation and Mellin contour kernel.

4. Mellin inversion formula for the exponential function
   For x>0 and c>0, e^(−x)=(2πi)^(−1)∫_(c−i∞)^(c+i∞)Γ(w)x^(−w)dw, interpreted as the limit of integrals over vertical segments.
   Source: relies_on, §2 equation (12); §4
   Note: Exponential smoothing.

5. Stirling estimate for gamma on vertical lines
   Uniformly for σ in a fixed compact real interval and |t|→∞, |Γ(σ+it)|=sqrt(2π)|t|^(σ−1/2)e^(−π|t|/2)(1+O(1/|t|)).
   Source: relies_on, §§2,4
   Note: Vertical contour decay.

6. Fourier transform on Euclidean space
   For an integrable f on ℝⁿ, its Fourier transform is f̂(ξ)=∫f(x)e⁻ⁱˣ·ξ dx; extend it to Schwartz functions and tempered distributions by duality.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: Rapid decay of a smooth compactly supported weight.

7. Abel's summation formula
   If Aₖ=∑ⱼ₌₁ᵏaⱼ, then ∑ₖ₌₁ᴺaₖbₖ=A_N b_N+∑ₖ₌₁ᴺ⁻¹Aₖ(bₖ−bₖ₊₁).
   Source: relies_on, §§2–5
   Note: Prime coefficient means become weighted prime sums.

8. Chen’s weighted quantitative Kronecker approximation theorem
   Let M≥1, λ,β∈ℝⁿ satisfy u·β∈ℤ whenever u∈ℤⁿ, |u_j|≤M and u·λ=0. For δ_j>0, Δ=∑δ_j, T₁<T₂, and Λ=min{|u·λ|:u∈ℤⁿ, |u_j|≤M, u·λ≠0}, one has inf_(T₁≤t<T₂)∑δ_j‖tλ_j−β_j‖²≤(Δ/4)sin²(π/(2(M+1)))+ΔMⁿ/(4πΛ(T₂−T₁)), provided the defining minimum for Λ is nonempty.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 3.1; §§3,5
   Note: The average-square inhomogeneous approximation replaces Weber’s maximum-error result.

9. Prime number theorem with de la Vallée Poussin’s error term
   There is c>0 for which ψ(x)=x+O(x exp(−c√log x)) as x→∞, where ψ(x)=∑_{n≤x}Λ(n).
   Source: relies_on, §3 equation (22)

10. Rankin’s trick for nonnegative arithmetic sums
   For a_n≥0, x>0 and δ>0, one has ∑_(n>x)a_n≤x^(−δ)∑_(n≥1)a_n n^δ whenever the sum on the right is finite.
   Source: relies_on, §2 after equation (17)
   Note: Bounds tails of multiplicative resonators.

11. Resonance method for large values of a Dirichlet series
   Choose a Dirichlet polynomial R(t)=∑r(n)n^(−it) and a nonnegative smooth weight Φ, then compare M₂=∫L(σ+it)|R(t)|²Φ(t/T)dt with M₁=∫|R(t)|²Φ(t/T)dt. The inequality max|L|≥|M₂|/M₁ turns a coefficient choice making the weighted correlation large into a large-value bound.
   Source: explains, §2
   Note: The method is explicitly credited to Soundararajan [20].

   Note: The source proves this extension and points to earlier Titchmarsh and Conrey–Ghosh arguments; original priority remains unverified.

16. Holomorphic logarithm
   A holomorphic logarithm of a nowhere-zero holomorphic function f on an open complex domain U is a holomorphic g:U→ℂ with exp(g)=f; on a connected U any two differ by a constant integer multiple of 2πi.
   Source: relies_on, §§3–5
   Note: Explicitly used analytic input, independently inventoried after reading every source section.

17. Residue theorem
   For a meromorphic function near the closure of a bounded region with piecewise smooth oriented boundary, with finitely many poles inside and none on the boundary, the boundary integral is 2πi times the sum of the residues, with winding-number weights for a general closed contour.
   Source: relies_on, §2 after(12); §4(31)
   Note: Explicitly used analytic input, independently inventoried after reading every source section.

18. Borel–Carathéodory theorem
   Let f be holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk |z−a|≤R and 0<r<R. Then max_{|z−a|≤r}|f(z)−f(a)|≤2r/(R−r)·(max_{|z−a|≤R}Re f(z)−Re f(a)).
   Source: relies_on, §4 p18
   Note: Explicitly used analytic input, independently inventoried after reading every source section.

19. Hadamard three-circles theorem
   For a holomorphic f on an annulus r₁≤|z−a|≤r₃, continuous on its boundary, set M(r)=max_{|z−a|=r}|f(z)|. For r₁<r₂<r₃, log M(r₂)≤[log(r₃/r₂)log M(r₁)+log(r₂/r₁)log M(r₃)]/log(r₃/r₁), with zero values interpreted by a limit.
   Source: relies_on, §4 p18
   Note: Explicitly used analytic input, independently inventoried after reading every source section.

20. Phragmén–Lindelöf principle for a finite-order strip
   Let f be holomorphic in a<Re z<b and continuous on its closure, with |f(σ+it)|≤exp(C(1+|t|)^N) uniformly in the strip. If |f|≤M on both vertical boundary lines, then |f|≤M throughout; if the two boundary values tend uniformly to zero as t→+∞, then the interior strip supremum also tends to zero.
   Source: relies_on, §5 final paragraph
   Note: Explicitly used analytic input, independently inventoried after reading every source section.

21. Convexity bound for the Selberg class
   For L in the Selberg class with gamma degree d_L=2∑λ_j, every ε>0 gives |L(σ+it)|≪_{L,ε}|t|^{d_L(1−σ)/2+ε}, uniformly 0≤σ≤1 and |t|≥2.
   Source: relies_on, §1(6); §2

22. Smoothed second moment of a short Dirichlet polynomial
   Let Φ∈C_c^∞((1,2)), ε>0 and R(t)=∑_{n≤N}r_n n^{−it}, with N≤T^{1−ε}. Then ∫ℝ|R(t)|²Φ(t/T)dt=T(∫Φ)∑|r_n|²+O_{Φ,ε}(∑|r_n|²), uniformly in all complex r_n and T≥2.
   Source: relies_on, §2(15)

23. Expansion of the logarithmic integral below one
   For 0<x<1, li(x)=C+log(−log x)+∑_{j≥1}(log x)^j/(j·j!), where C is an absolute real constant. In particular li(x)=log(−log x)+O(1) as x↑1.
   Source: relies_on, §5 p20

24. Second mean-value theorem for integrals
   If f is a monotone real function on [a,b] and g is continuous there, some ξ∈[a,b] satisfies ∫ₐᵇfg=f(a)∫ₐ^ξg+f(b)∫_ξᵇg.
   Source: relies_on, §5 p20

25. Local zero count in the Selberg class
   For a fixed L in the Selberg class and T≥2, the number of nontrivial zeros ρ with |Imρ−T|≤1 is O_L(log(T+3)), counted with multiplicity.

28. Ramanujan criterion for a polynomial Euler product

   Every integer n≥2 factors as a product of primes uniquely up to ordering.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (99)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Asymptotic equivalence [definition]: The assertion f∼g along a specified limit means f(x)/g(x)→1, where g is nonzero near that limit. Sequences are the instance x=n→∞.
- Big O notation [definition]: The assertion f=O(g) along a specified limit means |f(x)|≤C|g(x)| near that limit for a fixed finite C. Subscripts specify allowed dependence on fixed parameters. For sequences use x=n→∞.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Compactly supported smooth test function on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A smooth function on an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ whose support is a compact subset of Ω; these form C_c^∞(Ω).
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complex derivative [definition]: For a complex-valued function on an open subset of C, f′(z) is the limit of (f(z+h)−f(z))/h as nonzero complex h→0, when it exists.
- Complex exponential function [definition]: The entire function exp(z)=∑ₙ₌₀∞zⁿ/n!; on imaginary arguments exp(it)=cos(t)+i sin(t).
- Complex line integral along a piecewise smooth path [definition]: For a piecewise C¹ path γ:[a,b]→ℂ and a complex function f continuous on its image, the line (contour) integral is ∫_γf(z)dz=∫_a^bf(γ(t))γ′(t)dt, summed over the finitely many C¹ pieces, with real and imaginary parts integrated separately.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Complex power series [definition]: A series ∑ₙ₌₀∞aₙ(z−z₀)ⁿ with complex coefficients, considered at points where its partial sums converge.
- Connected topological space [definition]: A topological space that is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Dirichlet polynomial [definition]: A Dirichlet polynomial is a finite sum ∑_(n≤N)a_n n^(−s) with complex coefficients.
- Dirichlet series [definition]: A Dirichlet series is ∑_(n≥1)a_n n^(−s), with complex coefficients and n^(−s)=exp(−s log n), considered on a right half-plane where it converges.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Distance from a real number to the nearest integer [definition]: For x∈ℝ, ‖x‖=min_(k∈ℤ)|x−k| is its distance to the nearest integer, taking values in [0,1/2].
- Divisibility of integers [definition]: An integer a divides b if b=ac for some integer c.
- Entire function of finite order [definition]: An entire function f has finite order if for some finite positive C,A one has |f(z)|≤exp(C(1+|z|)^A) throughout ℂ.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Euler product of a Dirichlet series [definition]: For a multiplicative coefficient function a, an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series factors as ∑a(n)n^(−s)=∏_(p prime)(∑_(j≥0)a(pʲ)p^(−js)). An Euler-product representation specifies these local factors and its domain of convergence.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Greatest common divisor of integers [definition]: For integers a,b not both zero, gcd(a,b) is their greatest positive common divisor.
- Greatest lower bound [definition]: For a subset A of a partially ordered set, its greatest lower bound is a lower bound of A lying above every lower bound of A, when one exists.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Holomorphic function of one complex variable [definition]: A function on an open subset of ℂ is holomorphic if its complex derivative exists at every point of that subset.
- Improper Riemann integral [definition]: An improper Riemann integral over an open endpoint or an unbounded interval is a limit of integrals over compact subintervals; with two improper endpoints both limits must exist separately.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Logarithmic integral below one [definition]: For 0<x<1 define li(x)=∫₀ˣdt/log t. This improper integral is finite; its derivative is 1/log x.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Meromorphic continuation [definition]: A meromorphic function on a larger connected complex domain that agrees with a given function on its original open domain.
- Meromorphic function of one complex variable [definition]: A function on a complex domain locally expressible as a quotient of holomorphic functions with denominator not identically zero; zeros of the denominator give poles after common factors cancel.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Monotone real function [definition]: A real function on a linearly ordered set is nondecreasing if x≤y impliesf(x)≤f(y), and nonincreasing if the reverse inequality holds; monotone means either.
- Multiplicative arithmetic function [definition]: An arithmetic function a:ℕ₊→ℂ is multiplicative if a(1)=1 and a(mn)=a(m)a(n) whenever gcd(m,n)=1.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Prime number [definition]: An integer p>1 is prime if its only positive divisors are 1 and p.
- Real cosine function [definition]: For real t, cos(t) is the real part of exp(it).
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Residue of a meromorphic function [definition]: For a meromorphic function f near an isolated singularity a, its residue is (2πi)⁻¹∮f(z)dz around a sufficiently small positively oriented circle containing no other singularity; equivalently it is the coefficient of (z−a)⁻¹ in the Laurent expansion.
- Riemann integral [definition]: A bounded f:[a,b]→ℝ has Riemann integral I if all tagged Riemann sums approach I as the maximum length of the partition intervals tends to zero.
- Right-half-plane zero-counting function [definition]: For a meromorphic L-function with isolated zeros, N_L(σ,T) counts its nontrivial zeros ρ=β+iγ with β>σ and 0<γ≤T, with multiplicity. A power-saving zero-density estimate bounds this by O(T^(1−η)) for some η>0.
- Schwartz space [definition]: The space S(ℝⁿ) of smooth functions φ for which supₓ|xᵅ∂ᵝφ(x)| is finite for every pair of multi-indices α,β, with the topology of these seminorms.
- Seminorm on a real or complex vector space [definition]: A nonnegative function p with p(ax)=|a|p(x) and p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y); p may vanish on nonzero vectors.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Tagged Riemann sum [definition]: For a partition a=x₀<⋯<x_n=b and tags t_i∈[x_{i−1},x_i], the Riemann sum of f is ∑ᵢf(t_i)(x_i−x_{i−1}); the partition mesh is maxᵢ(x_i−x_{i−1}).
- Tempered distribution [definition]: A continuous linear functional on the Schwartz space S(ℝⁿ).
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Topology generated by a family of seminorms [definition]: For seminorms (p_i) on a vector space, a neighborhood base at zero consists of finite intersections of sets {x:p_i(x)<ε}; neighborhoods at other points are translates.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- von Mangoldt function [definition]: The arithmetic function Λ(n)=log p if n=p^k for a prime p and integer k≥1, and Λ(n)=0 otherwise.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

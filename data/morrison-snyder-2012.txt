REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (14)

- Cyclotomic integer: An algebraic integer lying in a cyclotomic field.
- Drinfeld center as the dual category of C⊠C^op: Let C be a finite multitensor category, regarded as a module category over C⊠C^op by (X⊠Y)⊗M=X⊗M⊗Y. The dual category Fun_{C⊠C^op}(C,C) of module endofunctors is canonically tensor equivalent to the Drinfeld center Z(C): a bimodule endofunctor −⊗Z corresponds to Z with the half-braiding given by its right module structure.
- Drinfeld center of the Haagerup fusion category: The common braided center of the principal and dual Haagerup fusion categories, with the ribbon structure coming from their subfactor construction.
- Dual fusion category: For an indecomposable semisimple module category M over a fusion category C, the category Fun_C(M,M) of module endofunctors, with composition as tensor product.
- Extended Haagerup planar algebra: The shaded subfactor planar algebra of the extended Haagerup subfactor. Morrison–Snyder use a rescaled generator and lopsided normalization of it.
- Haagerup planar algebra: The shaded subfactor planar algebra of the Haagerup subfactor. Morrison–Snyder use a rescaled generator and lopsided normalization of it.
- Induction to the Drinfeld center of a fusion category: For a fusion category C over ℂ, the induction functor I:C→Z(C) is the left adjoint to the functor forgetting the half-braiding. Its underlying object is ⊕_V V⊗X⊗V*, summed over simple representatives V.
- Internal Hom in a module category: For objects M,N of a module category over C, an internal Hom is an object [M,N] of C with natural bijections Hom_C(X,[M,N])≅Hom_M(X⊗M,N). It exists in the finite semisimple setting used here.
- Jones–Wenzl idempotent: The idempotent fₙ in TLₙ(δ) with identity-diagram coefficient 1 and eᵢfₙ=fₙeᵢ=0 for every adjacent cap–cup generator. It exists for all n at generic δ; special parameters require care.
- Modular tensor category: A ribbon fusion category over ℂ whose S-matrix is invertible.
- Primitive idempotent in an algebra: A nonzero idempotent that is not a sum of two nonzero orthogonal idempotents. In a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra A, e is primitive exactly when the left ideal Ae is a simple A-module.
- Schur's lemma: A nonzero morphism between simple objects of an abelian category is an isomorphism. Consequently, the endomorphism ring of a simple object is a division ring.
- Semisimplification by negligible morphisms: Quotient a spherical linear category by its negligible morphisms and take the Karoubi envelope. Under the usual finiteness and nondegeneracy hypotheses this gives a semisimple category; the construction does not guarantee semisimplicity for an arbitrary pivotal category.
- T-matrix of a ribbon fusion category: For a ribbon fusion category over ℂ, the T-matrix is diagonal, with entry at a simple object X equal to the scalar of its ribbon twist θ_X. No additional anomaly scalar is included.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (66)

1. k-linear category
   A category whose morphism spaces are vector spaces over a field k and whose composition is k-bilinear. This convention does not require finite sums or a zero object.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1

2. Monoidal category
   A category C with a tensor bifunctor C×C→C, a unit object, and natural associativity and unit isomorphisms satisfying the pentagon and triangle identities.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1

3. Rigid monoidal category
   A monoidal category in which every object has left and right duals, with evaluation and coevaluation maps satisfying the zigzag identities.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1

4. Simple object in an abelian category
   A nonzero object whose only subobjects are zero and itself.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1

5. Semisimple abelian category
   An abelian category in which every object is a finite direct sum of simple objects.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1

6. Schur's lemma
   A nonzero morphism between simple objects of an abelian category is an isomorphism. Consequently, the endomorphism ring of a simple object is a division ring.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1

7. Split simple object in a k-linear abelian category
   A simple object X in a k-linear abelian category is split if its scalar map k→End(X) is an isomorphism.
   Source: explains, §2.1

8. Split semisimple k-linear category
   A semisimple k-linear abelian category in which every simple object is split.
   Source: explains, §2.1

9. Fusion category
   A k-linear semisimple abelian rigid monoidal category with finitely many isomorphism classes of simple objects, finite-dimensional morphism spaces, a k-bilinear tensor product and End(1)=k. Over a non-algebraically closed field, simple objects need not be split.
   Source: explains, §2.1

10. Split fusion category
   A fusion category in which every simple object has endomorphism algebra k.
   Source: explains, §2.1

11. Rational form of a tensor category
   For fields k⊂K and a semisimple K-linear abelian tensor category C_K, a rational form over k is a semisimple k-linear abelian tensor category C_k with a tensor equivalence Kar(C_k⊗_k K)≃C_K. The additional tensor structure must be preserved.
   Source: explains, §2.1

12. Complete rational form of a tensor category
   A rational form C_k of C_K for which C_k⊗_k K is already idempotent complete, so C_k⊗_k K≃C_K without adding new summands.
   Source: explains, §2.1

13. Scalar extension of a linear category
   For k⊂K, replace each morphism space Hom(X,Y) by Hom(X,Y)⊗_k K, keeping the same objects and extending composition.
   Source: explains, §2.1

14. Cyclotomic field
   A number field ℚ(ζₙ) generated by a primitive nth root of unity.
   Source: relies_on, §1

15. Cyclotomic integer
   An algebraic integer lying in a cyclotomic field.
   Source: relies_on, §1

16. Frobenius–Perron dimension in a fusion category
   For a simple object X of a fusion category over ℂ, FPdim(X) is the spectral radius of its fusion matrix, whose entries are multiplicities in X⊗Y. Extend this additively to other objects.
   Source: discusses, §1
   Note: Motivation for the question, rather than inputs to the non-cyclotomicity proof.

17. Global dimension of a fusion category
   For a fusion category over ℂ, the global dimension is the sum of the intrinsic squared norms of its simple objects. For a spherical structure it is the sum of their squared categorical dimensions.
   Source: discusses, §1
   Note: Motivation for the question, rather than inputs to the non-cyclotomicity proof.

18. Brauer's cyclotomic splitting-field theorem
   If a finite group G has exponent n, then ℚ(ζₙ) is a splitting field for G: every irreducible complex representation of G has a model over ℚ(ζₙ).
   Source: discusses, §1
   Note: Motivation for the question, rather than inputs to the non-cyclotomicity proof.

19. Cyclotomicity of Frobenius–Perron dimensions
   The Frobenius–Perron dimension of every object in a fusion category over ℂ is a cyclotomic integer.
   Source: discusses, §1
   Note: Motivation for the question, rather than inputs to the non-cyclotomicity proof.

20. Cyclotomicity of global dimensions of fusion categories
   The global dimension of a fusion category over ℂ is a cyclotomic integer.
   Source: discusses, §1
   Note: Motivation for the question, rather than inputs to the non-cyclotomicity proof.

21. II₁ subfactor
   An inclusion N⊂M of von Neumann factors of type II₁.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2

22. Jones index
   For a II₁ subfactor N⊂M, its index [M:N] is the von Neumann dimension of L²(M) as an N-module, possibly infinite.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2

23. Principal even part of a subfactor
   For a finite-index II₁ subfactor N⊂M, the tensor category of N–N bimodules generated by L²(M), closing under finite sums, summands and duals.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2

24. Dual even part of a subfactor
   For a finite-index II₁ subfactor N⊂M, the tensor category of M–M bimodules generated by L²(M)⊗_N L²(M), closing under finite sums, summands and duals.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2

25. Finite-depth subfactor
   A finite-index subfactor whose principal graphs are finite, equivalently whose even parts have only finitely many simple objects.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2

26. Haagerup subfactor
   The finite-depth hyperfinite II₁ subfactor with index (5+√13)/2 and the Haagerup principal graph pair.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2

27. Extended Haagerup subfactor
   The finite-depth hyperfinite II₁ subfactor with the extended Haagerup principal graph pair, of index approximately 4.3772.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2

28. Principal Haagerup fusion category
   The principal even part of the Haagerup subfactor, considered as a fusion category over ℂ.
   Source: explains, §1, notation for Hℓᵖ and Hℓᵈ

29. Dual Haagerup fusion category
   The dual even part of the Haagerup subfactor, considered as a fusion category over ℂ.
   Source: explains, §1, notation for Hℓᵖ and Hℓᵈ

30. Principal extended Haagerup fusion category
   The principal even part of the extended Haagerup subfactor, considered as a fusion category over ℂ.
   Source: explains, §1, notation for Hℓᵖ and Hℓᵈ

31. Dual extended Haagerup fusion category
   The dual even part of the extended Haagerup subfactor, considered as a fusion category over ℂ.
   Source: explains, §1, notation for Hℓᵖ and Hℓᵈ

32. Drinfeld center of the Haagerup fusion category
   The common braided center of the principal and dual Haagerup fusion categories, with the ribbon structure coming from their subfactor construction.
   Source: relies_on, §§1 and 5

33. Haagerup planar algebra
   The shaded subfactor planar algebra of the Haagerup subfactor. Morrison–Snyder use a rescaled generator and lopsided normalization of it.
   Source: relies_on, §3

34. Extended Haagerup planar algebra
   The shaded subfactor planar algebra of the extended Haagerup subfactor. Morrison–Snyder use a rescaled generator and lopsided normalization of it.
   Source: relies_on, §3

35. Shaded planar tangle
   A disc with interior discs and nonintersecting strings, with an even number of endpoints on each boundary, checkerboard-shaded complementary regions and marked boundary intervals; considered up to the prescribed isotopy.
   Source: explains, §2.3, Definitions 2.2–2.7

36. Shaded planar algebra
   Vector spaces P_{n,+},P_{n,−} acted on multilinearly by shaded planar tangles, compatibly with gluing and identity tangles.
   Source: explains, §2.3, Definitions 2.2–2.7

37. Uncappable element of a planar algebra
   A box killed by every operation that caps two adjacent boundary points.
   Source: explains, §2.3, Definitions 2.2–2.7

38. Rotational eigenvector in a planar algebra
   A nonzero n-box S, n>0, satisfying ρ(S)=ωS for the shading-preserving two-click rotation ρ. Since ρⁿ=id, its eigenvalue ω is an nth root of unity.
   Source: explains, §2.3, Definitions 2.2–2.7

39. Karoubi envelope of a category
   The category obtained by formally splitting idempotent endomorphisms. Its objects are pairs (X,e) with e²=e; a map (X,e)→(Y,f) is a map a:X→Y with a=fae.
   Source: explains, §2.3, Definitions 2.2–2.7

40. Principal graph pair of a finite-index subfactor
   The pair of rooted bipartite graphs recording tensoring with the generating N–M Hilbert bimodule and its dual. Even vertices are simple N–N or M–M bimodules, odd vertices are simple mixed bimodules, and edge multiplicities count their summands. The graphs may be infinite.
   Source: explains, §2.4

41. Lopsided planar algebra
   A connected shaded planar algebra, with one-dimensional 0-box spaces, normalized so the shaded contractible circle evaluates to 1. The unshaded circle then evaluates to the index, the product of the two circle moduli.
   Source: explains, §2.5

42. Quantum integer
   For n≥1, [n]_q=qⁿ⁻¹+qⁿ⁻³+⋯+q¹⁻ⁿ, and [0]_q=0. Equivalently [n]_q=(qⁿ−q⁻ⁿ)/(q−q⁻¹) when the quotient is defined; the Laurent polynomial also specializes at q=±1.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.5–3

43. Jones–Wenzl idempotent
   The idempotent fₙ in TLₙ(δ) with identity-diagram coefficient 1 and eᵢfₙ=fₙeᵢ=0 for every adjacent cap–cup generator. It exists for all n at generic δ; special parameters require care.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.5–3

44. Primitive idempotent in an algebra
   A nonzero idempotent that is not a sum of two nonzero orthogonal idempotents. In a finite-dimensional semisimple algebra A, e is primitive exactly when the left ideal Ae is a simple A-module.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.5–3

45. Negligible morphism in a spherical linear category
   In a spherical k-linear category with End(1)=k, f:X→Y is negligible if tr(gf)=0 for every g:Y→X. Such morphisms form a tensor ideal.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.5–3

46. Semisimplification by negligible morphisms
   Quotient a spherical linear category by its negligible morphisms and take the Karoubi envelope. Under the usual finiteness and nondegeneracy hypotheses this gives a semisimple category; the construction does not guarantee semisimplicity for an arbitrary pivotal category.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.5–3

47. Half-braiding
   For an object X of a monoidal category C, a natural family of isomorphisms c_Y:X⊗Y→Y⊗X, compatible with tensor products and the unit: c_{Y⊗Z}=(id_Y⊗c_Z)∘(c_Y⊗id_Z), with associators inserted.
   Source: relies_on, §5

48. Drinfeld center of a monoidal category
   For a monoidal category C, its center Z(C) has objects (X,c), where c is a half-braiding on X, and morphisms f:X→X′ compatible with the half-braidings. Tensoring these pairs makes Z(C) braided; C itself need not be braided.
   Source: relies_on, §5

49. Induction to the Drinfeld center of a fusion category
   For a fusion category C over ℂ, the induction functor I:C→Z(C) is the left adjoint to the functor forgetting the half-braiding. Its underlying object is ⊕_V V⊗X⊗V*, summed over simple representatives V.
   Source: relies_on, §5

50. Module category over a tensor category
   A category M with an action C×M→M and coherent associativity and unit isomorphisms.
   Source: relies_on, §5

51. Dual fusion category
   For an indecomposable semisimple module category M over a fusion category C, the category Fun_C(M,M) of module endofunctors, with composition as tensor product.
   Source: relies_on, §5

52. Internal Hom in a module category
   For objects M,N of a module category over C, an internal Hom is an object [M,N] of C with natural bijections Hom_C(X,[M,N])≅Hom_M(X⊗M,N). It exists in the finite semisimple setting used here.
   Source: relies_on, §5

53. Ribbon category
   A braided rigid monoidal category with a twist compatible with both the braiding and duality.
   Source: relies_on, §5

54. S-matrix of a ribbon fusion category
   For a ribbon fusion category over ℂ, the S-matrix is indexed by simple objects; its (X,Y) entry is the categorical trace of c(Y,X)c(X,Y) on X⊗Y. This is the unnormalized Hopf-link convention.
   Source: relies_on, §5

55. T-matrix of a ribbon fusion category
   For a ribbon fusion category over ℂ, the T-matrix is diagonal, with entry at a simple object X equal to the scalar of its ribbon twist θ_X. No additional anomaly scalar is included.
   Source: relies_on, §5

56. Modular tensor category
   A ribbon fusion category over ℂ whose S-matrix is invertible.
   Source: relies_on, §5

57. Rational forms pass to Drinfeld centers
   In the characteristic-zero fusion-category setting, if C_k is a rational form of C_K, the natural functor Z(C_k)⊗_k K→Z(C_K) is fully faithful, dominant, and commutes with induction. Thus Z(C_k) is a braided rational form, with idempotent completion understood.
   Source: explains, Lemma 5.1

58. Rational forms pass to dual fusion categories
   In the characteristic-zero fusion-category setting, let M_K be an indecomposable finite semisimple module category over D_K and let D_k,M_k be compatible rational forms. Scalar extension induces a fully faithful dominant functor Fun_{D_k}(M_k,M_k)⊗_k K→Fun_{D_K}(M_K,M_K), so the dual category over k is a rational form of the dual over K.
   Source: explains, Lemma 5.2

59. Non-cyclotomicity of the principal Haagerup fusion categories
   The principal even parts of the Haagerup and extended Haagerup subfactors have no complete rational form over any cyclotomic field.
   Source: introduces, Theorem 1.1
   Note: A main result of this paper.
   Origin evidence: The introduction answers the ENO cyclotomicity question negatively using the principal Haagerup and extended Haagerup fusion categories; this is a new conclusion, rather than a restated background theorem.

61. Galois conjugate of a fusion category
   For a fusion category over ℂ with a complete form over a number field k, apply an embedding k→ℂ to its associativity and other structure coefficients and extend scalars to ℂ. This gives a conjugate tensor category, up to tensor equivalence.
   Source: discusses, §6

62. Galois conjugates of the Haagerup and extended Haagerup fusion categories
   Up to tensor equivalence, the principal Haagerup even part has two Galois conjugates and the principal extended Haagerup even part has three. All the nonoriginal conjugates are nonunitary. The normal closures of their complete fields of definition have Galois groups D₈ (order eight) and C₂×A₄, respectively.
   Source: introduces, §6
   Origin evidence: The paper computes the Galois closures and classifies the resulting Haagerup and extended Haagerup Galois conjugates; these computations are among its announced new results.

63. Quotient of a linear category by an ideal
   For a k-linear category C and a linear ideal I closed under composition on either side, C/I has the same objects and morphism spaces Hom_C(X,Y)/I(X,Y), with induced composition.

   A collection of operations with specified input and output types, with associative substitution into inputs and identity operations.

65. Planar isotopy of tangle diagrams
   A continuous deformation of embedded diagrams preserving their incidences, boundary components and marked data.
   Source: explains, Definition 2.2

66. Drinfeld center as the dual category of C⊠C^op
   Let C be a finite multitensor category, regarded as a module category over C⊠C^op by (X⊠Y)⊗M=X⊗M⊗Y. The dual category Fun_{C⊠C^op}(C,C) of module endofunctors is canonically tensor equivalent to the Drinfeld center Z(C): a bimodule endofunctor −⊗Z corresponds to Z with the half-braiding given by its right module structure.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (154)

- Abelian category [definition]: An additive category with kernels and cokernels in which every monomorphism is a kernel and every epimorphism is a cokernel.
- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Additive category [definition]: A category with abelian-group morphism sets, composition additive in each variable, a zero object and finite biproducts.
- Adjoint functors [definition]: Functors F:C→D and G:D→C with natural bijections Hom_D(FX,Y)≅Hom_C(X,GY); F is left adjoint to G.
- Adjoint of a bounded Hilbert-space operator [definition]: The adjoint of a bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space is the unique bounded operator T* satisfying ⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,T*y⟩ for every x,y.
- Algebraic integer [definition]: A complex number satisfying a monic polynomial with integer coefficients.
- Associative algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity.
- Banach space [definition]: A real or complex normed vector space complete for the metric induced by its norm.
- Basis of a vector space [definition]: A linearly independent family of vectors whose linear span is the whole vector space.
- Bifunctor [definition]: A functor C×D→E from a product of two categories to another category.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Braided monoidal category [definition]: A monoidal category with natural isomorphisms c(X,Y):X⊗Y→Y⊗X satisfying the two hexagon identities.
- C*-algebra [definition]: A complex *-algebra with a complete submultiplicative norm satisfying ∥a*a∥=∥a∥²; an identity is not required.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Category [definition]: Objects and morphisms with associative composition and identity morphisms.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for complex inner-product spaces [theorem]: In a complex inner-product space, |⟨u,v⟩|²≤⟨u,u⟩⟨v,v⟩ for all u,v.
- Center of an associative algebra [definition]: The subalgebra of elements commuting with every element of the associative algebra.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Cokernel of a morphism in a category with a zero object [definition]: For a morphism f:X→Y in a category with a zero object, a cokernel of f is a morphism c:Y→C with c∘f=0 such that every g:Y→W with g∘f=0 factors as g=h∘c for a unique h:C→W.
- Commutant of a set of Hilbert-space operators [definition]: For a set S of bounded operators on a complex Hilbert space H, the commutant S′ is the set of bounded operators on H commuting with every element of S. The double commutant (bicommutant) is S″=(S′)′.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Completion of a metric space [definition]: A complete metric space containing an isometric dense copy of the given metric space. It is unique up to an isometry fixing that copy.
- Complex *-algebra, possibly nonunital [definition]: A complex vector space with associative bilinear multiplication and a conjugate-linear involution a↦a* satisfying (ab)*=b*a*. A multiplicative identity is not required.
- Complex Hilbert space [definition]: A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex inner-product space [definition]: A complex vector space with a positive definite Hermitian form ⟨x,y⟩, linear in x and conjugate-linear in y. Its induced norm is ∥x∥=√⟨x,x⟩.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Composition of functions [definition]: For f:A→B and g:B→C, the composite g∘f:A→C sends x to g(f(x)).
- Connes fusion of Hilbert bimodules [construction]: The relative tensor product of an M–N and an N–P Hilbert bimodule, formed using bounded vectors and completion in the canonical relative inner product, producing an M–P Hilbert bimodule.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Coproduct of a family of objects in a category [definition]: For a family (X_i)_{i∈I} of objects of a category, a coproduct is an object Q with morphisms q_i:X_i→Q such that for every object W and morphisms f_i:X_i→W there is a unique f:Q→W with f∘q_i=f_i for all i.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Division ring [definition]: A ring with identity in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element has a two-sided multiplicative inverse. Multiplication need not be commutative.
- Double-dual functor [definition]: The covariant monoidal endofunctor X↦X** obtained by taking a chosen dual twice in a rigid monoidal category.
- Dual object in a monoidal category [definition]: An object paired with another by evaluation and coevaluation maps satisfying the two zigzag identities; left and right duals reverse the order of the factors.
- Epimorphism in a category [definition]: A morphism f:X→Y of a category is an epimorphism if it is right cancellable: a∘f=b∘f implies a=b for every object W and all morphisms a,b:Y→W.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence of categories [definition]: A functor with an inverse up to natural isomorphism.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factor (von Neumann algebra) [definition]: A von Neumann algebra whose center consists only of the scalar multiples of the identity.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Field extension [definition]: An inclusion of one field into another.
- Finite biproduct in a category [definition]: An object with injections and projections making it simultaneously the product and coproduct of a finite family of objects, with the usual compatible identity and zero composites.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Finite-dimensional vector space [definition]: A vector space having a finite basis.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Functor [definition]: A map of categories preserving objects, morphisms, identities and composition.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Hilbert bimodule over von Neumann algebras [definition]: An M–N Hilbert bimodule is a Hilbert space with commuting normal unital *-representations of von Neumann algebras M and Nᵒᵖ, giving left M and right N actions.
- Hilbert direct sum [definition]: For a family of complex Hilbert spaces H_i, the Hilbert direct sum consists of families (v_i) with ∑_i‖v_i‖²<∞, with coordinatewise linear operations and inner product ∑_i⟨v_i,w_i⟩. The nonnegative sum is the supremum of its finite partial sums.
- Homeomorphism [definition]: A bijection between topological spaces that is continuous and has a continuous inverse.
- Homotopy of continuous maps [definition]: A homotopy from continuous f₀:X→Y to f₁:X→Y is a continuous map F:X×[0,1]→Y with F(x,0)=f₀(x) and F(x,1)=f₁(x). A homotopy relative to A⊆X additionally keeps F(a,t) constant in t for each a∈A.
- Hyperfinite von Neumann algebra [definition]: A von Neumann algebra that is the weak closure of an increasing union of finite-dimensional *-subalgebras.
- Idempotent element of an algebra [definition]: An element e satisfying e²=e.
- Idempotent endomorphism [definition]: An endomorphism e:X→X satisfying e∘e=e.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Integer polynomial [definition]: An integer polynomial is a real polynomial all of whose coefficients lie in ℤ.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Intertwiner of algebra representations [definition]: A linear map T between modules over an algebra A such that T(av)=aT(v) for every a∈A and v in the source module.
- Isomorphism in a category [definition]: A morphism f:X→Y having an inverse g:Y→X, so gf=id_X and fg=id_Y.
- Kernel of a morphism in a category with a zero object [definition]: For a morphism f:X→Y in a category with a zero object, a kernel of f is a morphism k:K→X with f∘k=0 such that every g:W→X with f∘g=0 factors as g=k∘h for a unique h:W→K.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Linearly independent family of vectors [definition]: A family of vectors is linearly independent if every finite linear combination equalling zero has all coefficients zero.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Module functor between module categories [definition]: For module categories M,N over a monoidal category C, a module functor is a functor F:M→N with natural isomorphisms F(X⊗M)≅X⊗F(M) compatible with the associativity and unit constraints. Morphisms of module functors are natural transformations commuting with these isomorphisms; a module equivalence is a module functor that is an equivalence of categories.
- Module over an associative algebra [definition]: A vector space on which the algebra acts linearly, compatibly with multiplication and the identity.
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Monoidal natural transformation [definition]: A natural transformation between strong monoidal functors compatible with their tensor-product and unit isomorphisms.
- Monomorphism in a category [definition]: A morphism f:X→Y of a category is a monomorphism if it is left cancellable: f∘a=f∘b implies a=b for every object W and all morphisms a,b:W→X.
- Natural isomorphism [definition]: A natural transformation whose component at every object is an isomorphism.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Natural transformation [definition]: A family of maps between the values of two functors, commuting with every morphism in their source category.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normal representation of a von Neumann algebra [definition]: A unital star representation π:M→B(K) of a von Neumann algebra M on a complex Hilbert space K such that every vector state x↦⟨π(x)ξ,ξ⟩, with ξ∈K a unit vector, is a normal state of M. K with this action is a (left) Hilbert M-module.
- Normal state on a von Neumann algebra [definition]: A state φ of a von Neumann algebra M on a Hilbert space H is normal if there are vectors ξ₁,ξ₂,… in H with ∑ᵢ‖ξᵢ‖²=1 and φ(x)=∑ᵢ⟨xξᵢ,ξᵢ⟩ for every positive x∈M.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Number field [definition]: A finite extension of ℚ.
- Object of finite length in an abelian category [definition]: An object X of an abelian category has finite length if it has a finite chain of subobjects 0=X_0⊂X_1⊂⋯⊂X_n=X whose successive quotients X_i/X_{i−1}, the cokernels of the inclusions, are simple objects. Such a chain is a Jordan–Hölder (composition) series of X.
- Operator norm of a bounded linear operator [definition]: For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
- Opposite algebra [construction]: The opposite algebra Aᵒᵖ of an associative algebra A has the same underlying vector space and identity, with reversed multiplication a·b:=ba.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Orthogonal idempotents in an algebra [definition]: Idempotents e and f satisfying ef=fe=0.
- Orthogonal projection onto a closed Hilbert subspace [definition]: For a closed subspace V of a Hilbert space H, Pᵥx is the unique v∈V for which x−v is orthogonal to V; Pᵥ is a bounded self-adjoint idempotent operator.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Pivotal category [definition]: A rigid monoidal category with a monoidal natural isomorphism from the identity functor to the double-dual functor.
- Positive element and order in a C*-algebra [definition]: An element is positive if it has the form b*b. For self-adjoint a,b, write a≤b when b−a is positive.
- Positive linear functional on a C*-algebra [definition]: A complex linear map φ:A→ℂ satisfying φ(a*a)≥0 for every a∈A.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Product of a family of objects in a category [definition]: For a family (X_i)_{i∈I} of objects of a category, a product is an object P with morphisms p_i:P→X_i such that for every object W and morphisms f_i:W→X_i there is a unique f:W→P with p_i∘f=f_i for all i.
- Product of categories [definition]: C×D has pairs of objects and pairs of morphisms, with identities and composition defined componentwise.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Quotient vector space [definition]: For a subspace U⊆V, the cosets V/U form a vector space with addition and scalar multiplication induced from V.
- Rational number [definition]: A real x is rational if x=a/b for integers a,b with b≠0; otherwise it is irrational.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real polynomial [definition]: A real polynomial is a finite sum ∑ⱼ₌₀ᵐaⱼxʲ with real coefficients and nonnegative integer m.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Representable functor [definition]: A functor naturally isomorphic to Hom(A,−), or to Hom(−,A) in the contravariant case.
- Ring with identity (not necessarily commutative) [definition]: A ring is an abelian group R under addition with an associative multiplication R×R→R that distributes over addition on both sides and has an identity element 1. Commutativity of multiplication is not assumed. A ring homomorphism preserves addition, multiplication and 1.
- Root of unity [definition]: In a field, a root of unity is an element ζ with ζⁿ=1 for some positive integer n; its order is the least such n.
- Self-dual cone in a Hilbert space [definition]: For a subset P of a complex Hilbert space H, the dual cone is P°={ξ∈H : ⟨ξ,η⟩≥0 for every η∈P}. P is a self-dual cone if P=P°; such a P is a closed convex cone.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Spherical category [definition]: A pivotal category in which the left and right traces of every endomorphism coincide, so each endomorphism has a single trace in End(1). For a tensor category over a field this is equivalent to requiring dim(X)=dim(X*) for every object X.
- Splitting of an idempotent endomorphism [definition]: Maps r:X→Y and i:Y→X with ir=e and ri=id_Y exhibit Y as the image of the idempotent e.
- Standard form of a von Neumann algebra [definition]: A standard form (M,H,J,P) of a von Neumann algebra M on H consists of a conjugate-linear isometric involution J of H and a self-dual cone P⊆H with JMJ=M′, JcJ=c* for every c in the center of M, Jξ=ξ for ξ∈P, and aJaJ(P)⊆P for a∈M. Every von Neumann algebra has a standard form, unique up to unitary equivalence (Haagerup); H is then written L²(M), with commuting right action ξ·a=Ja*Jξ.
- Star homomorphism between C*-algebras [definition]: A star homomorphism φ:A→B between C*-algebras is a complex-linear multiplicative map satisfying φ(a*)=φ(a)*. The algebras and map need not be unital. A unital star homomorphism additionally preserves the multiplicative identities when they exist.
- Star representation of a C*-algebra [definition]: A star representation of a C*-algebra A on a complex Hilbert space H is a star-preserving algebra homomorphism π:A→B(H). It is cyclic if π(A)ξ is dense in H for some vector ξ.
- State of a C*-algebra [definition]: A state on a C*-algebra is a positive complex linear functional of norm one; on a unital C*-algebra this is equivalent to positivity and ρ(1)=1.
- Strong monoidal functor [definition]: A functor equipped with coherent isomorphisms preserving tensor products and the tensor unit.
- Strong operator topology on bounded operators [definition]: On the bounded linear operators X→Y between normed spaces, the strong operator topology is the coarsest topology making T↦Tx continuous for every x∈X; a net T_i converges to T exactly when ‖(T_i−T)x‖→0 for each x. For Hilbert spaces H→K this is the usual strong operator topology.
- Subalgebra over a field [definition]: A vector subspace of an algebra over a field that contains the identity and is closed under multiplication; the inherited operations make it an algebra over the same field.
- Subobject in a category [definition]: An equivalence class of monomorphisms into a fixed object, where two are equivalent if an isomorphism of their domains commutes with the maps.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Temperley–Lieb algebra [definition]: TL_n(δ) is spanned by planar noncrossing pairings of n top and n bottom points. Multiply by stacking and replace every closed loop by δ; equivalently its generators satisfy eᵢ²=δeᵢ, eᵢeᵢ₊₁eᵢ=eᵢ and eᵢeⱼ=eⱼeᵢ for abs(i−j)>1.
- Tensor category over a field [definition]: A k-linear abelian rigid monoidal category with a k-bilinear tensor product and End(1)=k, finite-dimensional morphism spaces, and finite length for every object. Semisimplicity and finitely many simple isomorphism classes are not required.
- Tensor ideal of morphisms in a linear monoidal category [definition]: A family of linear subspaces of morphism spaces closed under composition on either side and tensoring with arbitrary morphisms.
- Tensor product of vector spaces [definition]: The vector space V⊗W with its universal bilinear map from V×W.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Trace in a pivotal monoidal category [definition]: Closing an endomorphism using evaluation, coevaluation and the pivotal structure gives its left and right traces, which lie in End(1). They are scalars when End(1) is the ground field; the two traces need not agree.
- Trace of a square matrix [definition]: The sum of the diagonal entries of a square matrix.
- Tracial L²-space of a finite von Neumann algebra [definition]: The Hilbert completion of M for the inner product ⟨x,y⟩=τ(y*x), with the natural left and right multiplication actions.
- Tracial state on a C*-algebra [definition]: A tracial state on a C*-algebra A is a state τ satisfying τ(ab)=τ(ba) for all a,b∈A. It is faithful if τ(a*a)=0 implies a=0.
- Twist in a braided monoidal category [definition]: A natural automorphism θ of the identity with θ₁=id and θ_{X⊗Y}=c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y}(θ_X⊗θ_Y).
- Type II₁ factor [definition]: An infinite-dimensional von Neumann algebra with center ℂ1 admitting a faithful normal tracial state.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Unitary operator on a complex Hilbert space [definition]: A surjective complex linear map preserving the inner product.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- Von Neumann algebra [definition]: A von Neumann algebra on a complex Hilbert space H is a *-subalgebra M of the bounded operators on H that contains the identity and is closed in the weak operator topology; by the bicommutant theorem, equivalently M=M″. It is in particular a unital C*-algebra.
- Von Neumann bicommutant theorem [theorem]: If A is a *-subalgebra of the bounded operators on a complex Hilbert space that contains the identity, then its double commutant A″ equals the closure of A in the strong operator topology and also its closure in the weak operator topology.
- Von Neumann dimension of a Hilbert module [definition]: A Hilbert module over a finite factor is represented by a projection on a sum of copies of L²(M); its dimension is the extended matrix trace of that projection.
- Weak operator topology [definition]: The weak operator topology on bounded operators H→K is the coarsest topology for which T↦⟨Tx,y⟩ is continuous for every x∈H,y∈K; a net converges exactly when all these matrix coefficients converge.
- Zero morphism in a category with a zero object [definition]: In a category with a zero object 0, the zero morphism X→Y is the composite X→0→Y of the unique morphisms through 0. Any composite of a zero morphism with another morphism is again a zero morphism.
- Zero object in a category [definition]: An object 0 of a category such that for every object X there is exactly one morphism 0→X and exactly one morphism X→0.

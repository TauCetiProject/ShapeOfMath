REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (27)

- Absolute rapid decay of the cuspidal spectral kernel sum: For compactly supported smooth f on G(A_F)¹, the sum ∑_π|K_(π,f)(x,y)| over cuspidal isotypic kernels converges and decays faster than every inverse power of every simple-root coordinate on each pair of Siegel sets. It is consequently integrable over pairs of normalized connected-reductive period quotients.
- Admissibility of irreducible unitary adelic representations: Every irreducible continuous unitary representation of the adelic group of a connected reductive number-field group is admissible. In particular each cuspidal automorphic isotypic block has finite-dimensional spaces of a fixed archimedean compact type and fixed finite-level invariants, after accounting for its finite automorphic multiplicity.
- Arthur truncation operator: For fixed minimal-parabolic data and sufficiently regular T, Arthur’s operator on an automorphic function is Λ^Tφ(x)=∑_P(−1)^dim(a_P/a_G)∑_(δ∈P(F)∖G(F))φ_P(δx)·τ̂_P(H_P(δx)−T_P), over standard F-parabolics. Here τ̂_P is the dual-chamber indicator and T_P is the projection of T to a_P. Proper constant terms vanish for cusp forms, so Λ^Tφ=φ there.
- Borel–Harish-Chandra finite-volume theorem: For a connected reductive group G over a number field, G(F)∖G(A_F)¹ has finite volume for Haar measure on G(A_F)¹. Equivalently A_G G(F)∖G(A_F) has finite invariant volume.
- F-supercuspidal local test function: At a finite place v,a compactly supported locally constant function on G(F_v) is F-supercuspidal if every left or right translate has zero integral along the unipotent radical of each proper F-defined parabolic subgroup.
- Finiteness of first cohomology of a local-field torus: If k is R,C, or a finite extension of Q_p and T is an algebraic torus over k, then H¹(k,T) is a finite abelian group.
- Finiteness of the Tate–Shafarevich group of a torus: For every algebraic torus T over a number field F, Sha¹(F,T) is finite. This asserts finite kernel, not finite H¹(F,T).
- Galois-cohomological classification of rational orbits: For an algebraic action H on an affine F-variety X, a point x∈X(F), and its stabilizer I, the H(F)-orbits of F-points in the geometric orbit H(Fsep)x are naturally bijective to ker[H¹(F,I)→H¹(F,H)]. The kernel means classes mapping to the distinguished point, and the orbit of x corresponds to the trivial class.
- Godement compactness criterion for a reductive adelic quotient: For a connected reductive number-field group G, the normalized quotient G(F)∖G(A_F)¹ is compact exactly when every F-split torus in G is central, equivalently G has F-rank zero after removing its split central directions. In particular an anisotropic reductive group has compact normalized adelic quotient.
- Hilbert finite generation and closed-orbit separation for reductive actions: For a reductive group G acting algebraically on an affine variety X over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, O(X)^G is finitely generated. The map π:X→Spec O(X)^G is surjective; two geometric points have the same image exactly when their orbit closures intersect. Every fiber contains exactly one closed orbit.
- Integration along a closed subgroup of a local algebraic group: Let k be R,C or a finite extension of Q_p, G a smooth affine k-group and H a closed smooth subgroup. With Haar measure dh, q_*f(gH)=∫_(H(k))f(gh)dh maps C_c∞(G(k)) onto C_c∞(G(k)/H(k)). Here smooth means C∞ at archimedean places and locally constant at nonarchimedean places; the target is the analytic homogeneous quotient, not all rational points of an affine quotient variety.
- Lang–Steinberg theorem: For a smooth connected affine algebraic group over a finite field with Frobenius F, the map x↦x⁻¹F(x) is surjective over an algebraic closure; equivalently its first Galois cohomology is trivial.
- Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem: If measurable fₙ converge almost everywhere to f and |fₙ|≤g for an integrable g, then f is integrable and ∫|fₙ−f|→0; consequently their integrals converge.
- Local topology of an algebraic orbit in characteristic zero: For a smooth algebraic group acting on an affine variety over R,C or a finite extension of Q_p, the map G(k)/G_x(k)→G(k)x is a homeomorphism onto its orbit with the local-field topology. The rational orbit is open and closed inside the k-points of its geometric orbit; if that geometric orbit is Zariski closed, the rational orbit is closed in the ambient k-points.
- Matsushima’s affineness criterion: For a connected reductive group G over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field and a closed subgroup H, H is reductive exactly when it occurs as the stabilizer of a point in a finite-dimensional algebraic G-representation with closed orbit. Equivalently, the homogeneous quotient G/H is affine exactly when H is reductive. Consequently stabilizers of closed orbits in affine G-varieties are reductive.
- Peter–Weyl approximation with loss of differentiability: Let K be a compact subgroup of a real Lie group G. There is an integer ℓ depending only on K such that, for every integer r>ℓ and f∈C_c^r(G), its left-right compact-type projections f_(τ,σ) sum absolutely to f in C_c^(r−ℓ)(G). Supports lie in K·supp(f)·K; for every continuous seminorm p there, ∑_(τ,σ)p(f_(τ,σ)) is bounded by a continuous C_c^r seminorm of f. Each projection is the character-average projector for irreducible K-types τ,σ.
- Rapid decrease of smooth square-integrable cusp forms: For a connected reductive number-field group, every smooth vector in its cuspidal L²-space is a rapidly decreasing smooth function on G(F)∖G(A_F)¹. More precisely, for fixed compactly supported smooth f, a Siegel set S and B>0, |R(f)φ(x)|≤C_(f,S,B)‖φ‖₂|α(a_x)|_∞^(−B) for every φ in the cuspidal L²-space, x∈S and simple root α.
- Reductive integral models at almost every finite place: Let G be connected reductive over a number field F and let 𝒢 be an affine integral group model over O_(F,S). After enlarging S by finitely many primes, 𝒢 is smooth and all its geometric fibers are connected reductive. Hence 𝒢(O_v) is hyperspecial in G(F_v) for every v outside S.
- Reductivity of involution-fixed subgroups in characteristic zero: For a connected reductive group over a characteristic-zero field and an algebraic involution σ, the fixed subgroup G^σ is smooth and its identity component is reductive. The fixed subgroup itself need not be connected.
- Relatively τ-elliptic element: In the commuting-involution setup G=Res_(M/F)G₀, θ=στ, a relatively τ-semisimple γ is relatively τ-elliptic if C^τ_(G,δ)/Z(G₀), with δ=γγ^(−θ), is anisotropic over the ground field. The centralizer here is in all G; anisotropy of a possibly disconnected reductive quotient means its identity component has no nontrivial split torus.
- Richardson’s semisimple closed-orbit criterion for an involution: Let G be connected reductive over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, σ an involution, and Q the affine image of the Cartan map g↦gσ(g)⁻¹. For a semisimple q∈Q, its conjugation orbit under G^σ is Zariski closed in Q. This is the implication of Richardson’s criterion used in Hahn’s proof.
- Smooth Hensel lifting over a complete discrete valuation ring: Let R be a complete discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal 𝔭 and residue field k, and A a smooth finitely presented R-algebra. Every R-algebra homomorphism A→k lifts to A→R. Equivalently each k-point of a smooth affine finite-presentation R-space lifts to an R-point.
- Spreading out smooth affine morphisms over a number field: Finite-presentation affine algebraic data over a number field can be defined over O_(F,S) after inverting finitely many primes; if a given generic morphism is smooth, its integral model becomes smooth after enlarging S.
- Strongly relatively τ-regular element: A relatively τ-semisimple γ for which the τ-centralizer of γγ^{-θ} in G^σ is a connected torus.
- Tamagawa measure of a reductive group over a number field: For a connected reductive number-field group G of dimension d, choose an invariant F-rational gauge form and its standard local measures m_v. Let ρ_G be the Galois representation on X*(G)⊗C and r_G the leading nonzero Laurent coefficient of its finite-place Artin L-function at s=1. The Tamagawa measure is r_G⁻¹|d_F|^(−d/2)(∏_(v infinite)m_v)(∏_(v finite)L_v(1,ρ_G)m_v). It is independent of rescaling that gauge form by F×.
- Twisted relative orbital integral: Over a local field k, for relatively semisimple γ∈G(k), put H=G^σ(k)×G^θ(k) and L=(G_γ^(σθ))°(k). Given a unitary character χ of H trivial on L and compatible Haar measures, the twisted relative orbital integral is ∫_(L∖H)χ(x,y)f(x⁻¹γy)d[x,y], for compactly supported smooth f. In the convention χ(x,y)=μ₁(x)μ₂(y)⁻¹, relevance requires this actual character to be trivial on L. The adelic version replaces local points by adelic points and is defined when the integral converges.
- Twisted relative spectral trace: For a cuspidal representation π,the integral of its convolution-kernel K_{π(f)}(x,y) against μ(x,y^{-1}) over the normalized G^σ and G^θ automorphic quotients. For a K-finite test function it is the sum of products of the corresponding period integrals over an orthonormal basis.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (56)

1. Number field
   A finite extension of ℚ.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, algebraic-group setup

2. Affine algebraic group over a field
   An affine algebraic group over a field F is specified by a finitely generated commutative Hopf F-algebra A: for each commutative F-algebra R its group of R-points is Hom_(F-alg)(A,R), with multiplication induced by the coproduct. Equivalently it is a finite-type affine group scheme over F.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, algebraic-group setup

3. Connected reductive algebraic group
   A smooth connected affine algebraic group whose largest connected normal unipotent subgroup over an algebraic closure is trivial.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, algebraic-group setup

4. Restriction of scalars of an algebraic group
   For a finite field extension M/F and an M-group H, Res_{M/F}H is the F-group with R-points H(R⊗_F M) for each F-algebra R.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, algebraic-group setup

5. Algebraic torus
   An algebraic group that becomes isomorphic over an algebraic closure to a finite product of copies of the multiplicative group.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, algebraic-group setup

6. Anisotropic reductive group over a field
   A reductive group over F containing no nontrivial F-split torus; an anisotropic torus has no positive-dimensional split subtorus.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, algebraic-group setup

7. Parabolic subgroup of a reductive algebraic group
   A closed algebraic subgroup P of a connected reductive F-group G such that, after base change to an algebraic closure, P contains a Borel subgroup of G. Equivalently G/P is projective.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, algebraic-group setup

8. Unipotent radical of an algebraic group
   The largest smooth connected normal unipotent subgroup of an algebraic group.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, algebraic-group setup

9. Place of a number field
   An equivalence class of nontrivial absolute values on a number field; its completion is denoted F_v. Archimedean and nonarchimedean places give its infinite and finite places.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.2–4, local/global measures and unramified orbital factors

10. Adele ring of a number field
   The restricted product A_F=∏′_v F_v of all completions of a number field,where almost every finite component lies in the valuation ring O_v.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.2–4, local/global measures and unramified orbital factors

11. Adelic points of an algebraic group
   For an algebraic group G over a number field F,the restricted product G(A_F)=∏′_v G(F_v),using integral compact subgroups at almost all finite places.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.2–4, local/global measures and unramified orbital factors

12. Haar measure on a locally compact group
   A nonzero Radon measure on a locally compact group, i.e. a Borel measure finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets and inner regular on open sets, invariant under left translation: μ(gA)=μ(A). A right Haar measure is defined analogously with right translations.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.2–4, local/global measures and unramified orbital factors

13. Unimodular locally compact group
   A locally compact group whose left Haar measures are also right invariant.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.2–4, local/global measures and unramified orbital factors

14. Tamagawa measure of a reductive group over a number field
   For a connected reductive number-field group G of dimension d, choose an invariant F-rational gauge form and its standard local measures m_v. Let ρ_G be the Galois representation on X*(G)⊗C and r_G the leading nonzero Laurent coefficient of its finite-place Artin L-function at s=1. The Tamagawa measure is r_G⁻¹|d_F|^(−d/2)(∏_(v infinite)m_v)(∏_(v finite)L_v(1,ρ_G)m_v). It is independent of rescaling that gauge form by F×.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.2–4, local/global measures and unramified orbital factors

15. Hyperspecial maximal compact subgroup
   For a reductive group over a nonarchimedean local field,a subgroup given by the valuation-ring points of a smooth affine integral group scheme whose special fiber is connected reductive.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.2–4, local/global measures and unramified orbital factors

   For a smooth connected affine algebraic group over a finite field with Frobenius F, the map x↦x⁻¹F(x) is surjective over an algebraic closure; equivalently its first Galois cohomology is trivial.

   Note: Actual reading requirement; its later inspected proof route is kept separate from the source’s use and from origin attribution.

56. Reductive integral models at almost every finite place
   Let G be connected reductive over a number field F and let 𝒢 be an affine integral group model over O_(F,S). After enlarging S by finitely many primes, 𝒢 is smooth and all its geometric fibers are connected reductive. Hence 𝒢(O_v) is hyperspecial in G(F_v) for every v outside S.
   Source: relies_on, §3 Proposition3.4 proof
   Note: Actual reading requirement; its later inspected proof route is kept separate from the source’s use and from origin attribution.

57. Richardson’s semisimple closed-orbit criterion for an involution
   Let G be connected reductive over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field, σ an involution, and Q the affine image of the Cartan map g↦gσ(g)⁻¹. For a semisimple q∈Q, its conjugation orbit under G^σ is Zariski closed in Q. This is the implication of Richardson’s criterion used in Hahn’s proof.
   Source: relies_on, §2 Lemma2.4
   Note: Explicit Richardson7.5 input. Proof unknown, not inferred.

58. Cartan map of an algebraic involution
   For an involution σ of an affine algebraic group, the Cartan map is B_σ(g)=gσ(g)⁻¹. It is constant on right cosets of the fixed subgroup and equivariant for the corresponding twisted conjugation action; its affine scheme-theoretic image is often used as a model of the symmetric space. No rational-point surjectivity is included in this definition.
   Source: relies_on, §2.4 equations(2.4)–(2.5)
   Note: The two affine images and their equivariance are used throughout the orbit argument.

59. Local topology of an algebraic orbit in characteristic zero
   For a smooth algebraic group acting on an affine variety over R,C or a finite extension of Q_p, the map G(k)/G_x(k)→G(k)x is a homeomorphism onto its orbit with the local-field topology. The rational orbit is open and closed inside the k-points of its geometric orbit; if that geometric orbit is Zariski closed, the rational orbit is closed in the ambient k-points.
   Source: relies_on, §3 Proposition3.2
   Note: A closed geometric orbit gives the local closed quotient support needed for convergence.

60. Integration along a closed subgroup of a local algebraic group
   Let k be R,C or a finite extension of Q_p, G a smooth affine k-group and H a closed smooth subgroup. With Haar measure dh, q_*f(gH)=∫_(H(k))f(gh)dh maps C_c∞(G(k)) onto C_c∞(G(k)/H(k)). Here smooth means C∞ at archimedean places and locally constant at nonarchimedean places; the target is the analytic homogeneous quotient, not all rational points of an affine quotient variety.
   Source: relies_on, §3 Proposition3.2
   Note: Pushforward is onto functions on the actual local homogeneous image, not all S(k).

61. Borel–Harish-Chandra finite-volume theorem
   For a connected reductive group G over a number field, G(F)∖G(A_F)¹ has finite volume for Haar measure on G(A_F)¹. Equivalently A_G G(F)∖G(A_F) has finite invariant volume.
   Source: relies_on, §4 Theorem4.1 volume factors
   Note: Finite covolume is needed for the normalized stabilizer quotient.

62. Godement compactness criterion for a reductive adelic quotient
   For a connected reductive number-field group G, the normalized quotient G(F)∖G(A_F)¹ is compact exactly when every F-split torus in G is central, equivalently G has F-rank zero after removing its split central directions. In particular an anisotropic reductive group has compact normalized adelic quotient.
   Source: relies_on, §4 Theorem4.1 elliptic volume factor
   Note: The anisotropy condition supplies compactness modulo the specified center.

63. Peter–Weyl approximation with loss of differentiability
   Let K be a compact subgroup of a real Lie group G. There is an integer ℓ depending only on K such that, for every integer r>ℓ and f∈C_c^r(G), its left-right compact-type projections f_(τ,σ) sum absolutely to f in C_c^(r−ℓ)(G). Supports lie in K·supp(f)·K; for every continuous seminorm p there, ∑_(τ,σ)p(f_(τ,σ)) is bounded by a continuous C_c^r seminorm of f. Each projection is the character-average projector for irreducible K-types τ,σ.
   Source: relies_on, §5 Proposition5.2 proof, printedpp15–16
   Note: Actual quantified compact-type approximation used to pass from K-finite to arbitrary smooth tests. This occurrence is independent of the later global kernel proof route.

64. Admissibility of irreducible unitary adelic representations
   Every irreducible continuous unitary representation of the adelic group of a connected reductive number-field group is admissible. In particular each cuspidal automorphic isotypic block has finite-dimensional spaces of a fixed archimedean compact type and fixed finite-level invariants, after accounting for its finite automorphic multiplicity.
   Source: relies_on, §5 equations(5.4),(5.5) and Proposition5.2 K-finite sums
   Note: Finite kernel sums for a K-finite test require finite compact-type/finite-level multiplicities, not K-finiteness of the test alone. This actual theorem input was missing from the initial source inventory.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (314)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Absolute Galois group [definition]: The group Gal(F^sep/F), with its Krull topology, where F^sep is a separable closure.
- Absolute value on a field [definition]: A map |·|:K→R_{≥0} that vanishes exactly at zero, is multiplicative and satisfies |x+y|≤|x|+|y|. It is nonarchimedean if the stronger bound |x+y|≤max(|x|,|y|) holds.
- Adelic Iwasawa decomposition [theorem]: For a connected reductive group G over a number field F and an F-parabolic subgroup P, there is a maximal compact subgroup K⊂G(A_F) such that G(A_F)=P(A_F)K. The local factors can be chosen hyperspecial outside finitely many places.
- Adjoint representation of an algebraic group [definition]: The algebraic representation Ad:G→GL(Lie G) obtained by differentiating the conjugation maps h↦ghg⁻¹ at the identity.
- Admissible unitary representation of an adelic reductive group [definition]: For a connected reductive number-field group, a continuous unitary representation is admissible if, for every compact open K_f⊂G(A_f) and every irreducible type τ of a maximal compact K_∞⊂G(F_∞), the subspace of K_f-fixed vectors in its τ-isotypic subspace is finite-dimensional.
- Affine algebraic variety over a field [definition]: An affine algebraic variety is specified here by a finitely generated reduced commutative algebra A over a field F. Its k-points, for extensions k/F, are the F-algebra homomorphisms A→k. Morphisms are induced by algebra homomorphisms in the opposite direction. Reducible affine varieties are allowed.
- Affine group scheme of finite presentation over a ring [definition]: A commutative Hopf R-algebra A that is finitely presented as an R-algebra, regarded through its group-valued functor B↦Hom_(R-alg)(A,B). This coordinate account defines the affine group scheme without requiring general scheme theory.
- Affine integral model over a ring of S-integers [definition]: For a finitely generated F-algebra A and the ring O_(F,S) of a number field with finitely many finite primes inverted, an affine integral model is a finitely generated O_(F,S)-subalgebra A₀⊂A with F⊗A₀≅A. In the group case require compatible Hopf-algebra operations. This injective coordinate-algebra convention gives the usual flat model over the Dedekind base.
- Algebraic action on an affine variety [definition]: An action of an affine algebraic group G on an affine variety X by a morphism G×X→X; in coordinate algebras it is a coaction O(X)→O(G)⊗O(X) satisfying the counit and coassociativity identities.
- Algebraic character of an affine algebraic group [definition]: A morphism of algebraic groups G→G_m over the specified field. Characters form an abelian group by pointwise multiplication.
- Algebraic closure of a field [definition]: An algebraic extension of a field that is itself algebraically closed. This specifies an object, without including the existence or uniqueness-up-to-isomorphism theorem.
- Algebraic element of a field extension [definition]: An element α of L/F is algebraic over F if a nonzero polynomial in F[x] vanishes at α.
- Algebraic representation of an affine algebraic group [definition]: A morphism of algebraic groups G→GL(V) for a finite-dimensional vector space V; equivalently an action with regular matrix coefficient functions. A faithful representation identifies G with a closed algebraic subgroup of GL(V).
- Algebraically closed field [definition]: A field in which every nonconstant polynomial has a root.
- Almost-everywhere convergence of measurable functions [definition]: Measurable functions fₙ converge almost everywhere to f if the pointwise limit fₙ(x)→f(x) holds outside a measurable null set.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Analytic manifold over a local field [definition]: A Hausdorff topological space with charts to open subsets of K^n, for a local field K, whose transition functions are locally given by convergent K-power series. Over R this means real analytic, over C complex analytic, and over a nonarchimedean field its usual convergent power-series notion.
- Artin L-function [definition]: For an Artin representation ρ factoring through Gal(L/F), the Euler product over finite places L(s,ρ)=∏_v det(1−ρ(Frob_w)(#k_v)^(−s)|V^(I_w))^(−1), initially for Re(s)>1, together with its meromorphic continuation. The local factor uses inertia-fixed vectors, so ramified places are included.
- Artin representation of a number field [definition]: A finite-dimensional complex representation of the absolute Galois group of a number field that is continuous and has finite image, equivalently one factoring through the Galois group of a finite Galois extension.
- Associative algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity.
- Automorphic form on a reductive group over a number field [definition]: A complex function on G(A_F) for a connected reductive F-group, smooth at infinity and right invariant under an open compact subgroup at the finite places, left G(F)-invariant, of moderate growth, K∞-finite for a chosen maximal compact subgroup, and Z(g_∞)-finite.
- Automorphism of a group [definition]: A bijective group homomorphism from a group to itself; automorphisms form a group under composition.
- Automorphism of an affine algebraic group [definition]: An isomorphism G→G of algebraic groups over the specified field; equivalently an invertible endomorphism of its coordinate Hopf algebra.
- Base change of an affine algebraic group [definition]: For a field extension L/F and an affine F-group with coordinate Hopf algebra A, its base change to L has coordinate Hopf algebra L⊗_F A.
- Base change of an affine group scheme [definition]: For R→S and an affine group scheme represented by A, its base change is represented by S⊗_R A with the induced Hopf operations. A residue-field fiber is this construction for S=R/𝔪.
- Basis of a free module [definition]: A family (e_i) in a module over a commutative ring such that every element is a unique finite linear combination of the e_i with coefficients in that ring. A module with such a family is free.
- Basis of a vector space [definition]: A linearly independent family of vectors whose linear span is the whole vector space.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel subgroup of a connected affine algebraic group [definition]: Over an algebraically closed field, a maximal smooth connected solvable closed subgroup. Over F, a subgroup is an F-Borel subgroup if its base change to an algebraic closure is such a Borel subgroup.
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- C^r topology on functions with compact support [definition]: For an integer r≥0 on a smooth real manifold, the space C_K^r of r-times continuously differentiable functions supported in a fixed compact K has the seminorms given by uniform bounds on coordinate derivatives of order≤r, using finitely many charts near K. C_c^r has the locally convex inductive-limit topology over compact supports. On a Lie group, invariant derivatives give the same topology.
- Cardinality of a set [definition]: The size of a set, with two sets having the same cardinality when there is a bijection between them. A set has finite cardinality n when it is in bijection with {1,…,n}; infinite cardinalities are distinguished by the same bijection criterion.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Center of an affine algebraic group [definition]: The closed subgroup Z(G) whose elements commute with every element after every extension of the ground algebra. Over a characteristic-zero field it is equivalently the closed subgroup whose geometric points form the center of G over an algebraic closure.
- Center of an associative algebra [definition]: The subalgebra of elements commuting with every element of the associative algebra.
- Character of a finite-dimensional representation [definition]: For a finite-dimensional representation ρ of a group G, its character is the function χ_ρ(g)=trρ(g). For a unitary representation of a compact group it is a continuous class function; the character of a general representation need not be one-dimensional.
- Characteristic of a field [definition]: The least positive integer n with n·1=0 in the field, if one exists, and zero otherwise.
- Closed subgroup of an affine algebraic group [definition]: A subgroup represented by the quotient of its ambient group’s coordinate Hopf algebra by a Hopf ideal.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative Hopf algebra over a commutative ring [definition]: A commutative R-algebra A with R-algebra maps Δ:A→A⊗_R A, ε:A→R and S:A→A satisfying the coassociativity, counit and inverse identities: (Δ⊗id)Δ=(id⊗Δ)Δ and m(S⊗id)Δ=m(id⊗S)Δ=ηε.
- Commutative Hopf algebra over a field [definition]: A Hopf algebra over a field whose underlying algebra multiplication is commutative; its coproduct need not be cocommutative.
- Commutative algebra over a commutative ring [definition]: A commutative ring A with a specified unital ring homomorphism R→A; algebra homomorphisms commute with these structure maps.
- Commutative algebra over a field [definition]: A unital associative algebra over a field whose multiplication is commutative, equivalently a commutative ring equipped with a unital homomorphism from that field.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Commutator subgroup [definition]: The subgroup [G,G] generated by all commutators xyx⁻¹y⁻¹ with x,y∈G.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Compactly supported smooth test function on an adelic group [definition]: A compactly supported complex function on G(A_F) that is smooth in the archimedean variables and locally constant in the nonarchimedean variables. Factorizable test functions are restricted tensors of local test functions.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Completion of a field with an absolute value [definition]: The metric completion of K for d(x,y)=|x−y|, with the field operations extended continuously. A completion is regarded as containing K through its canonical isometric embedding.
- Completion of a metric space [definition]: A complete metric space containing an isometric dense copy of the given metric space. It is unique up to an isometry fixing that copy.
- Complex Hilbert space [definition]: A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex derivative [definition]: For a complex-valued function on an open subset of C, f′(z) is the limit of (f(z+h)−f(z))/h as nonzero complex h→0, when it exists.
- Complex exponential function [definition]: The entire function exp(z)=∑ₙ₌₀∞zⁿ/n!; on imaginary arguments exp(it)=cos(t)+i sin(t).
- Complex inner-product space [definition]: A complex vector space with a positive definite Hermitian form ⟨x,y⟩, linear in x and conjugate-linear in y. Its induced norm is ∥x∥=√⟨x,x⟩.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Complex power series [definition]: A series ∑ₙ₌₀∞aₙ(z−z₀)ⁿ with complex coefficients, considered at points where its partial sums converge.
- Composition of functions [definition]: For f:A→B and g:B→C, the composite g∘f:A→C sends x to g(f(x)).
- Connected component of a topological space [definition]: A maximal connected subset, with connectedness understood in the subspace topology.
- Connected topological space [definition]: A topological space that is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets.
- Constant term along a parabolic subgroup [definition]: For P=MN with unipotent radical N and a suitable automorphic function φ, its constant term is φ_P(g)=∫_(N(F)∖N(A_F))φ(ng)dn. Cuspidality means these terms vanish for every proper F-parabolic.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Continuous nonabelian 1-cocycle [definition]: For a topological group Γ acting continuously on a group H, a continuous map c:Γ→H satisfying c(στ)=c(σ)σ(c(τ)). Cocycles are equivalent when c′(σ)=h⁻¹c(σ)σ(h) for one h∈H.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex subset of a real vector space [definition]: A subset C of a real vector space is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and0≤t≤1. The vector space may be infinite-dimensional.
- Convolution of functions on a locally compact group [definition]: With a chosen left Haar measure, (f*g)(x)=∫_G f(y)g(y⁻¹x)dy when the integral exists, in particular for compactly supported continuous functions. The order matters for noncommutative groups.
- Coroot of a root [definition]: In a finite root system with specified invariant inner product, the coroot associated to α is α∨=2α/(α,α), identified with the corresponding vector in the dual space via the inner product.
- Cuspidal L²-space of a reductive group [definition]: The closed subspace of L²(G(F)∖G(A_F)¹) generated by the square-integrable smooth cuspidal automorphic forms, with the right regular unitary action. It is the Hilbert realization of the cuspidal spectrum.
- Decomposition group at a prime of a Galois extension [definition]: For a finite Galois extension L/F and a prime w above v, the subgroup D_w⊂Gal(L/F) of automorphisms preserving w.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Derivation of an algebra [definition]: A linear map D:A→A on an associative algebra over a field satisfying D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b).
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Derived subgroup of a smooth affine algebraic group [definition]: The smallest closed algebraic subgroup containing all geometric commutators xyx⁻¹y⁻¹. For a connected group it is the closed connected subgroup generated by these commutators, with the corresponding ground-field descent.
- Determinant of a square matrix [definition]: For an n×n matrix A over a commutative ring, det A=∑_{σ∈Sₙ}sgn(σ)∏_{i=1}ⁿA_{i,σ(i)}, with the 0×0 determinant equal to one.
- Diagonalizable linear endomorphism [definition]: A linear endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space admitting a basis of eigenvectors over its ground field. Diagonalizability over an algebraic closure is specified separately.
- Dimension of a vector space [definition]: The cardinality of a basis of the vector space; a finite basis with n vectors gives dimension n.
- Discrete subgroup of a locally compact group [definition]: A subgroup whose induced topology is discrete; equivalently the identity is isolated in the subgroup.
- Discrete valuation on a field [definition]: A surjective map v:K×→Z satisfying v(xy)=v(x)+v(y) and v(x+y)≥min(v(x),v(y)), extended by v(0)=+∞.
- Discriminant of a number field [definition]: For an integral basis ω₁,…,ω_n of F, the integer d_F=det(Tr_(F/Q)(ω_iω_j))_(i,j). A change of integral basis leaves this determinant unchanged.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Double coset of two subgroups [definition]: For subgroups H,K⊂G, the double coset HgK={hgk:h∈H,k∈K}; H∖G/K denotes the partition into these subsets.
- Dual Weyl chamber for a simple root system [definition]: The open cone of H in the real root span for which (ω_i,H)>0 for every fundamental weight ω_i of the chosen simple system. Its indicator is the dual-chamber cutoff used in Arthur truncation; partial parabolic chambers use the corresponding quotient root space.
- Eigenvalue of a linear endomorphism [definition]: A scalar λ for which a linear endomorphism T has a nonzero vector v with Tv=λv.
- Element integral over a commutative ring [definition]: For a ring inclusion R⊂A, an element a∈A is integral over R if it satisfies a monic polynomial with coefficients in R.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Exterior power of a module over a commutative ring [definition]: The module Λ^n_R M equipped with the universal alternating R-multilinear map M^n→Λ^n_R M; equivalently the corresponding quotient of the tensor power by tensors with repeated entries. This is the alternating quotient convention, not the anti-invariant tensor submodule.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Field embedding [definition]: An injective homomorphism of fields preserving the multiplicative identity.
- Field extension [definition]: An inclusion of one field into another.
- Finite crystallographic root system [definition]: A finite spanning set Φ of nonzero vectors in a real inner-product space, preserved by the orthogonal reflection s_α in each root α, and satisfying2(β,α)/(α,α)∈Z for all α,β∈Φ. Nonreduced systems are allowed; reduced means the only multiples of α in Φ are ±α. A simple system is a basis whose root expansions are all nonnegative or all nonpositive integer combinations.
- Finite field [definition]: A field whose underlying set is finite.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Finite-dimensional vector space [definition]: A vector space having a finite basis.
- Finitely generated commutative algebra [definition]: An R-algebra A generated as an algebra by finitely many elements; equivalently a quotient of R[X₁,…,X_n] for some finite n.
- Finitely presented commutative algebra [definition]: An R-algebra isomorphic to R[X₁,…,X_n]/(f₁,…,f_m) for finite n,m.
- Fixed subgroup of an algebraic-group involution [definition]: The closed subgroup G^σ whose R-points are {g∈G(R):σ(g)=g} for each algebra over the ground field.
- Fixed vector subspace of a group representation [definition]: For a representation π on V and a subgroup H, the subspace V^H={v:π(h)v=v for every h∈H}. This means pointwise fixed vectors, not merely an invariant subspace.
- Formal power series over a commutative ring [definition]: An expression ∑a_α X^α with coefficients in a commutative ring and multi-indices α in N^n, added coefficientwise and multiplied by the finite Cauchy sums defining each coefficient, without any assertion of analytic convergence.
- Frobenius element at a prime of a Galois extension [definition]: The element of D_w/I_w acting on the residue extension by x↦x^(#k_v). At an unramified prime it gives a conjugacy class in Gal(L/F), independently of the chosen w above v.
- Frobenius endomorphism in characteristic p [definition]: The field endomorphism x↦x^p of a field of characteristic p; over F_q the q-power iterate x↦x^q is the corresponding q-Frobenius.
- Fréchet derivative between normed spaces [definition]: The Fréchet derivative of f at x is a bounded linear map A with ∥f(x+h)−f(x)−Ah∥/∥h∥→0 as h→0.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Fundamental weight of a root system [definition]: For a simple system α₁,…,α_r, the fundamental weights ω_i are the dual basis to the simple coroots: (ω_i,α_j∨)=δ_ij. They belong to the real root span; integrality for a specified algebraic group is a separate lattice condition.
- Galois field extension [definition]: An algebraic field extension that is both normal and separable.
- Galois group of a finite Galois extension [definition]: The group of field automorphisms of the extension that fix the base field pointwise.
- General linear group over a commutative ring [definition]: The group GL_n(R) of square matrices over a commutative ring R having a two-sided matrix inverse, with matrix multiplication.
- Geometric character lattice of a reductive group [definition]: For a connected reductive F-group, X*(G)=Hom_(Fsep-groups)(G_Fsep,G_m) with pointwise multiplication and its natural absolute-Galois action. It is a finitely generated free abelian group; its fixed elements are the F-rational characters.
- Geometrically connected affine algebraic group [definition]: An affine F-group whose coordinate algebra after extension to an algebraic closure has no idempotents other than 0 and 1. This is the coordinate-algebra account of connectedness after base change.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Group action on a set [definition]: A map G×X→X, written (g,x)↦gx, satisfying ex=x and (gh)x=g(hx).
- Group homomorphism [definition]: A map f:G→H satisfying f(xy)=f(x)f(y) for all x,y.
- Hausdorff topological space [definition]: A topological space in which any two distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods.
- Hilbert direct sum [definition]: For a family of complex Hilbert spaces H_i, the Hilbert direct sum consists of families (v_i) with ∑_i‖v_i‖²<∞, with coordinatewise linear operations and inner product ∑_i⟨v_i,w_i⟩. The nonnegative sum is the supremum of its finite partial sums.
- Holomorphic function of one complex variable [definition]: A function on an open subset of ℂ is holomorphic if its complex derivative exists at every point of that subset.
- Homeomorphism [definition]: A bijection between topological spaces that is continuous and has a continuous inverse.
- Homomorphism of algebras over a field [definition]: A linear map of unital associative algebras over the same field preserving multiplication and the multiplicative identity.
- Homomorphism of commutative rings [definition]: A map between commutative rings preserving addition, multiplication and the multiplicative identity.
- Homomorphism of modules over a commutative ring [definition]: An additive map f:M→N of modules over the same commutative ring satisfying f(rm)=rf(m).
- Hopf algebra [definition]: A unital associative algebra H with coassociative coproduct Δ:H→H⊗H and counit ε:H→k that are algebra homomorphisms, and an antipode S satisfying m(S⊗id)Δ=m(id⊗S)Δ=ηε, where m and η are multiplication and unit.
- Hopf ideal of a commutative Hopf algebra [definition]: An ideal I of a commutative Hopf algebra H satisfying Δ(I)⊂I⊗H+H⊗I, ε(I)=0 and S(I)⊂I, so the quotient inherits the Hopf operations.
- Ideal of a commutative ring [definition]: An additive subgroup I of a commutative ring R such that ri belongs to I for every r in R and i in I.
- Idele group of a number field [definition]: The restricted product ∏′_v F_v× with respect to O_v× at finite places. As a group it is the unit group of the adele ring, but its restricted-product topology is specified independently rather than taken as the subspace topology from A_F.
- Idelic norm [definition]: For an idele a=(a_v), |a|_A=∏_v|a_v|_v using the normalized local multiplicative norms; almost all factors are1. This is a continuous homomorphism A_F×→R_{>0}.
- Idempotent element of an algebra [definition]: An element e satisfying e²=e.
- Identity component of an affine algebraic group [definition]: The connected component containing the identity, with its induced closed subgroup structure. In coordinate terms over an algebraic closure it is the factor of the coordinate algebra selected by the primitive idempotent whose value at the identity is1; this component descends to the ground field.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Inertia group at a prime of a Galois extension [definition]: The subgroup I_w of the decomposition group consisting of automorphisms acting trivially on the residue field at w.
- Infinite product of complex numbers or functions [definition]: The limit of the finite partial products ∏_{j=1}^N a_j as N→∞, when it exists; for functions the mode of convergence is specified, such as locally uniform convergence on the stated domain. Formal products mean stabilization of each coefficient instead of analytic convergence.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Integral basis of a number field [definition]: A finite Z-module basis of the ring of integers O_F: every algebraic integer in F is a unique integer linear combination of the basis elements.
- Integrated operator of a unitary representation [definition]: For f∈L¹(G), the bounded operator π(f) determined weakly by ⟨π(f)v,w⟩=∫_G f(g)⟨π(g)v,w⟩dg. This uses a fixed Haar measure and a unitary representation of a locally compact group.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Invariant coordinate ring [definition]: For an algebraic action G on an affine variety X, O(X)^G is the subalgebra of regular functions f satisfying f(gx)=f(x) after every extension of the ground algebra. Equivalently the coaction sends f to1⊗f.
- Invariant gauge form on a smooth affine algebraic group [definition]: A nowhere-zero left-invariant algebraic differential form ω of degree d=dim_F Lie(G): in coordinates it is an element of Λ^d Ω_(O(G)/F) whose value at every geometric point is nonzero. It is defined over F, and its invariant choices differ by F×.
- Involution of an algebraic group [definition]: An algebraic-group automorphism σ satisfying σ²=id. In involution setups the identity automorphism is allowed unless order exactly two is required.
- Irreducible polynomial [definition]: A nonzero nonunit polynomial over a specified coefficient ring that cannot be written as a product of two nonunits. Over a field, both factors would have smaller positive degree.
- Irreducible unitary representation [definition]: A nonzero unitary representation with no closed invariant complex linear subspace other than0 and the whole Hilbert space.
- Isotypic subspace for a compact-group representation [definition]: For a continuous unitary representation of a compact group K and an irreducible finite-dimensional K-representation τ, its τ-isotypic subspace is the closed span of all subrepresentations isomorphic to τ. Equivalently it is the image of d_τ∫_K conjugate(χ_τ(k))π(k)dk.
- K-finite function under right translation [definition]: For a subgroup K of G, a complex function f on G is K-finite if the functions x↦f(xk), k∈K, span a finite-dimensional complex vector space. In automorphic applications K is compact.
- Kernel of an integral operator [definition]: For a measure space, a measurable function K(x,y) representing an operator by (Tf)(x)=∫K(x,y)f(y)dμ(y), on the specified class of functions where the expression is defined. A square-integrable kernel is an element of L² of the product measure space.
- Krull topology on an infinite Galois group [definition]: On Gal(L/F), the subgroup fixing each finite intermediate extension E/F is an open neighbourhood of the identity; their cosets form a basis. For an absolute Galois group take L=F^sep.
- Kähler differentials of an algebra [definition]: For a commutative F-algebra A, the A-module Ω_(A/F) generated by symbols da, a∈A, subject to additivity, F-linearity and d(ab)=a·db+b·da.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Levi subgroup of a parabolic subgroup [definition]: A reductive subgroup M of a parabolic P such that multiplication identifies P with the semidirect product M⋉R_u(P), where R_u(P) is the unipotent radical. This defines a Levi subgroup without incorporating its existence theorem.
- Lie algebra of a real Lie group [definition]: The real vector space of left-invariant smooth vector fields on a real Lie group, with bracket [X,Y] acting on smooth functions as X∘Y−Y∘X. It is identified with the tangent space at the identity.
- Lie algebra of an affine algebraic group [definition]: For a coordinate Hopf algebra A with counit ε, the F-vector space of linear maps D:A→F satisfying D(ab)=ε(a)D(b)+ε(b)D(a). Extend them to invariant derivations using the coproduct; their commutator induces the Lie bracket.
- Lie algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with a bilinear bracket [x,y] satisfying [x,x]=0 and [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear representation of a group [definition]: A group homomorphism from a group G to the invertible linear maps of a vector space, equivalently a linear action of G.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Linearly independent family of vectors [definition]: A family of vectors is linearly independent if every finite linear combination equalling zero has all coefficients zero.
- Local field [definition]: A nondiscrete locally compact Hausdorff topological field. Both archimedean and nonarchimedean local fields are allowed.
- Local measure defined by an algebraic gauge form [definition]: On the local-field points of a smooth algebraic group, write an invariant gauge form in analytic coordinates as ω=f(x)dx₁∧…∧dx_d and use |f(x)|_v times product additive Haar measure. For the number-field standard convention take vol(O_v)=1 at finite places, dx over R, and2 dxdy over C.
- Local-field topology on points of an affine algebraic group [definition]: For a local field F and an affine embedding G⊂A^n, give G(F) the subspace topology from F^n. Polynomial coordinate maps show this topology is independent of the chosen affine embedding.
- Localization of a commutative ring [definition]: For a multiplicatively closed subset S of a commutative ring R containing 1, S⁻¹R consists of fractions r/s, identifying r/s with r′/s′ when t(rs′−r′s)=0 for some t in S, with the usual fraction operations.
- Locally compact Hausdorff space [definition]: A Hausdorff topological space in which every point has a neighbourhood with compact closure.
- Locally compact group [definition]: A topological group whose underlying space is locally compact and Hausdorff.
- Locally constant function [definition]: A function that is constant on some open neighbourhood of each point of its domain.
- Locally convex topological vector space [definition]: A real or complex topological vector space whose zero has a neighborhood base of convex sets; equivalently its topology can be generated by seminorms.
- L²-space on an automorphic quotient [definition]: Complex square-integrable functions modulo almost-everywhere equality on Γ∖G, for a discrete subgroup Γ of a unimodular locally compact group G and the quotient Haar measure; the inner product is ∫_(Γ∖G)f·conj(h).
- Matrix height on an adelic linear algebraic group [definition]: Fix a faithful F-algebraic representation ρ of G. For g=(g_v), take H(g)=∏_v max(1,max_(i,j)|ρ(g_v)_(ij)|_v,max_(i,j)|ρ(g_v⁻¹)_(ij)|_v), using normalized local multiplicative norms. Almost all factors are1; equivalent faithful choices give comparable polynomial growth conditions.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Maximal compact subgroup of a Lie group [definition]: A compact subgroup of a real Lie group maximal among its compact subgroups under inclusion.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Meromorphic continuation [definition]: A meromorphic function on a larger connected complex domain that agrees with a given function on its original open domain.
- Meromorphic function of one complex variable [definition]: A function on a complex domain locally expressible as a quotient of holomorphic functions with denominator not identically zero; zeros of the denominator give poles after common factors cancel.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Minimal polynomial of an algebraic element [definition]: The unique monic polynomial of least positive degree over F vanishing at an algebraic element α; it generates the ideal of all polynomials over F vanishing at α.
- Moderate growth of an adelic function [definition]: A function f on G(A_F) has moderate growth if |f(g)|≤C H(g)^N for some constants C,N>0 and a fixed faithful adelic matrix height H. Uniform moderate growth requires a common N for all derivatives from U(g_∞⊗C), with constants allowed to depend on the derivative.
- Module over a commutative ring [definition]: An abelian group M with an action of a commutative ring R satisfying r(m+n)=rm+rn, (r+s)m=rm+sm, (rs)m=r(sm), and 1m=m. Unlike a vector space, its scalar ring need not be a field.
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Morphism of affine algebraic groups [definition]: A homomorphism defined by regular coordinate functions; in coordinate algebras it is a homomorphism of commutative Hopf algebras in the opposite direction, preserving coproduct, counit and antipode.
- Multiplicative algebraic group [definition]: The affine F-group G_m with R-points R×, represented by F[t,t⁻¹], whose coproduct sends t to t⊗t.
- Multiplicity of a polynomial root [definition]: The multiplicity of a root a of a polynomial P is the largest m≥1 for which (x−a)^m divides P. A root of multiplicity1 is simple.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nilpotent element of a ring [definition]: An element a such that a^n=0 for some positive integer n.
- Nilpotent ideal of a commutative ring [definition]: An ideal I for which I^n=0 for some positive integer n, meaning every product of n elements of I is zero.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normal algebraic field extension [definition]: An algebraic field extension L/k is normal if every irreducible polynomial in k[X] with a root in L splits into linear factors over L. Equivalently, the minimal polynomial over k of every element of L splits over L.
- Normal subgroup [definition]: A subgroup H of G satisfying gHg⁻¹=H for every g∈G.
- Normalized local multiplicative norm on a number field [definition]: At a real place use the usual absolute value, at a complex place use the square of the usual complex modulus, and at a finite place v use |x|_v=(#k_v)^(−ord_v(x)), with |0|_v=0. The complex normalization is multiplicative but need not satisfy the ordinary triangle inequality.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Orbit and stabilizer of an algebraic group action [definition]: For an algebraic action G on X and x∈X(k), the geometric orbit consists of gx for g∈G(k̄), and the stabilizer G_x is the closed k-subgroup fixing x after every algebra extension. An orbit is closed if it is closed in the geometric Zariski topology.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Orthogonal projection onto a closed Hilbert subspace [definition]: For a closed subspace V of a Hilbert space H, Pᵥx is the unique v∈V for which x−v is orthogonal to V; Pᵥ is a bounded self-adjoint idempotent operator.
- Orthonormal basis of a Hilbert space [definition]: An orthonormal family whose linear span is dense in the Hilbert space.
- Orthonormal family in a Hilbert space [definition]: Vectors (e_i) with ⟨e_i,e_j⟩=0 for i≠j and ‖e_i‖=1.
- Parabolic Harish–Chandra map [definition]: Fix compatible Iwasawa data G(A_F)=M(A_F)N(A_F)K for P=MN. The map H_P:G(A_F)→a_P=Hom(X*_F(M),R) is specified by ⟨χ,H_P(mnk)⟩=log|χ(m)|_A. It extends the Levi’s logarithmic character map using its Iwasawa component.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Partition of a set [definition]: A family of nonempty pairwise disjoint subsets whose union is the set.
- Polynomial over a field [definition]: A formal finite sum a₀+a₁x+⋯+aₙxⁿ with coefficients in a field, with the usual coefficientwise addition and multiplication.
- Polynomial ring over a commutative ring [definition]: The ring R[X₁,…,X_n] of formal finite sums of monomials with coefficients in R, with coefficientwise addition and the usual multiplication of monomials.
- Positive split central subgroup of an adelic reductive group [definition]: For the largest F-split torus T in the center of a connected reductive number-field group G, A_G=Hom(X*(T),R_(>0)) is its positive real split part, embedded diagonally in T(F_∞) and trivially at finite places. This is the standard central factor complementary to G(A_F)¹, not all of T(F_∞)°.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Prime ideal of a commutative ring [definition]: A proper ideal P of a commutative ring R such that ab in P implies a in P or b in P.
- Prime number [definition]: An integer p>1 is prime if its only positive divisors are 1 and p.
- Product formula for a number field [theorem]: For every nonzero a in a number field F, ∏_v|a|_v=1 using normalized local multiplicative norms at every finite and infinite place. Only finitely many factors differ from1.
- Product measure [construction]: For σ-finite measure spaces, the product measure on the product σ-algebra assigns a measurable rectangle A×B the value μ(A)ν(B); its completion also includes subsets of product-null sets.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Quotient Haar measure for unimodular groups [definition]: For a closed unimodular subgroup H of a unimodular locally compact group G with fixed Haar measures, the right G-invariant measure on H∖G characterized by ∫_G f(g)dg=∫_(H∖G)∫_H f(hg)dh d[g] for compactly supported continuous f.
- Quotient module [definition]: For a submodule N of an R-module M, the cosets M/N carry addition and scalar multiplication induced from M.
- Quotient of a commutative ring by an ideal [definition]: For an ideal I of R, the cosets R/I form a ring under (r+I)+(s+I)=r+s+I and (r+I)(s+I)=rs+I.
- Quotient of a group by a normal subgroup [definition]: For a normal subgroup H of G, the cosets G/H form a group under (gH)(g′H)=gg′H.
- Quotient of an abelian group by a subgroup [definition]: For an abelian group G and subgroup H,the cosets G/H form an abelian group under (g+H)+(g′+H)=g+g′+H.
- Quotient of an affine algebraic group by a normal subgroup [definition]: For a smooth affine group G over a characteristic-zero field and a closed normal subgroup N, G/N is the affine algebraic group with surjective quotient homomorphism G→G/N, geometric kernel N, and universal factorization property for algebraic homomorphisms killing N. Its geometric points are the cosets G(k̄)/N(k̄); over the ground field surjectivity on rational points is not asserted.
- Quotient topology [definition]: For a surjection q:X→Y, a subset U⊆Y is open exactly when q⁻¹(U) is open in X.
- Quotient vector space [definition]: For a subspace U⊆V, the cosets V/U form a vector space with addition and scalar multiplication induced from V.
- Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space [definition]: A Borel measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space that is finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets (each has measure equal to the infimum over open supersets) and inner regular on open sets (each has measure equal to the supremum over compact subsets). This is the Folland–Cohn convention; on σ-compact spaces it coincides with compact inner regularity on all Borel sets.
- Rapid decrease on an automorphic quotient [definition]: A smooth function on G(F)∖G(A_F)¹ is rapidly decreasing if on every Siegel set, for every B>0 and every simple relative root α, its absolute value is bounded by C·|α(a)|_∞^(−B), where a is the split-torus coordinate of the point and C may depend on B and the Siegel set.
- Rational number [definition]: A real x is rational if x=a/b for integers a,b with b≠0; otherwise it is irrational.
- Rational points of an affine algebraic group [definition]: For an affine F-group G with coordinate Hopf algebra A and commutative F-algebra R, the group G(R)=Hom_(F-alg)(A,R), with group multiplication induced by the coproduct of A.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real Lie group [definition]: A group that is a smooth real manifold, with smooth multiplication and inversion.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Reduced commutative ring [definition]: A commutative ring whose only nilpotent element is0.
- Reductive affine algebraic group, allowing components [definition]: A smooth affine algebraic group whose identity component is a connected reductive algebraic group. In this catalogue a disconnected group may therefore be reductive; connectedness is separately specified when required.
- Relative stabilizer for two algebraic involutions [definition]: For involutions σ,θ of an affine group G and γ∈G(k), the k-group G_γ^(σθ) has R-points {(x,y)∈G^σ(R)×G^θ(R):x⁻¹γy=γ}. It is the stabilizer of γ under the left-right action.
- Residue field of a discretely valued field [definition]: The quotient O_v/m_v of the valuation ring by its maximal ideal.
- Restricted product of topological groups [definition]: For groups G_i with chosen open subgroups K_i, the group of tuples g_i with g_i∈K_i for all but finitely many i, with the topology whose basic opens are products of open sets equal to K_i outside finitely many coordinates.
- Restricted tensor product of vector spaces [definition]: For vector spaces V_i with specified nonzero reference vectors v_i, the vector space spanned by tensors ⊗_i w_i for which w_i=v_i outside a finite set, subject to the usual multilinear relations. This is the algebraic restricted tensor product, without a Hilbert completion.
- Restriction in first Galois cohomology [definition]: For an extension L/F and an F-group H, the map H¹(F,H)→H¹(L,H_L) restricts a cocycle to the corresponding absolute Galois subgroup after compatible separable-closure choices. For a completion F_v it is the localization map; its pointed-set class is independent of the choices.
- Ring of integers of a number field [definition]: The subring O_K of a number field K consisting of its elements integral over Z, namely the roots in K of monic integer-coefficient polynomials.
- Roots of a reductive group relative to a split torus [definition]: For a maximal split torus T of a connected reductive group G, the nontrivial characters occurring as weights in Ad on Lie G are the relative roots. A choice of minimal parabolic containing T specifies the positive roots; the simple roots are the positive roots not expressible as a sum of two positive roots.
- Scheme-theoretic image of an affine morphism [definition]: For an affine morphism represented contravariantly by a ring map A→B, its scheme-theoretic image has coordinate algebra A/ker(A→B), equivalently the image subalgebra of B. This is a closed affine subspace of the target; it need not have all its rational points in the set-theoretic image of the rational-point map.
- Semidirect product of groups [definition]: Given a homomorphism α:G→Aut(H), the group H⋊_αG has underlying set H×G and multiplication (h,g)(h′,g′)=(hα(g)(h′),gg′).
- Seminorm on a real or complex vector space [definition]: A nonnegative function p with p(ax)=|a|p(x) and p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y); p may vanish on nonzero vectors.
- Semisimple element of an affine algebraic group [definition]: An element whose image in a faithful finite-dimensional algebraic representation is diagonalizable over an algebraic closure. This property is independent of the faithful representation; that independence is a theorem, rather than an additional part of the definition.
- Separable algebraic field extension [definition]: An algebraic extension L/F is separable if the minimal polynomial over F of every element of L has distinct roots in an algebraic closure.
- Separable closure of a field [definition]: A separable algebraic extension F^sep/F that has no further nontrivial finite separable algebraic extension, equivalently the separable elements in a chosen algebraic closure.
- Separable polynomial over a field [definition]: A polynomial having no repeated root in an algebraic closure of its coefficient field.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Siegel set for an adelic reductive group [definition]: Fix a minimal F-parabolic P=MN of a connected reductive number-field group, a compatible maximal compact K, and the maximal split torus T₀ of the derived group in M. Let A_(T₀)=Hom(X*(T₀),R_(>0)), embedded diagonally in T₀(F_∞), be its positive real split part. For compact Ω⊂P(A_F)∩G(A_F)¹ and t>0, a Siegel set is Ω{a∈A_(T₀):|α(a)|_∞≥t for every simple relative root α}K.
- Sign of a permutation [definition]: The sign of a permutation of {1,…,n} is (−1) to the number of inversions, where an inversion is a pair i<j with σ(i)>σ(j).
- Smooth affine algebraic group [definition]: An affine algebraic group over F whose coordinate algebra is smooth over F. In the finite-type characteristic-zero case all affine group schemes are smooth, but that assertion is a separate theorem.
- Smooth finitely presented commutative algebra [definition]: A finitely presented R-algebra A such that, for every R-algebra B with nilpotent ideal I, every R-algebra map A→B/I lifts to A→B. This is the infinitesimal lifting formulation of smoothness for affine finite-presentation algebras.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Smooth morphism of affine finite-presentation spaces [definition]: A morphism represented contravariantly by A→B is smooth when B, as an A-algebra, is finitely presented and satisfies the nilpotent-ideal lifting property.
- Smooth real manifold [definition]: A Hausdorff second-countable topological space with charts to open subsets of R^n whose transition maps are smooth. Smooth functions and maps are those whose expressions in these charts are smooth.
- Smooth vector field on a manifold [definition]: An R-linear derivation of the algebra C∞(M) of smooth real functions on a smooth manifold M. In local coordinates it acts as ∑_i X^i(x)∂_i with smooth coefficient functions.
- Smooth vector of an adelic unitary representation [definition]: A vector in a strongly continuous unitary representation of G(A_F) that is fixed by an open compact subgroup at finite places and whose orbit map under the real Lie group G(F_∞) is infinitely Fréchet differentiable in Hilbert norm in local coordinates.
- Solvable group [definition]: A group whose iterated commutator subgroups reach the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps.
- Solvable smooth affine algebraic group [definition]: A smooth affine algebraic group whose group of points over an algebraic closure is solvable. For the characteristic-zero groups used here this agrees with termination of the algebraic derived series.
- Split algebraic torus [definition]: An algebraic F-group isomorphic over F to a finite product of copies of G_m. An F-torus need only become such a product after extending F to an algebraic closure.
- Subalgebra over a field [definition]: A vector subspace of an algebra over a field that contains the identity and is closed under multiplication; the inherited operations make it an algebra over the same field.
- Subgroup [definition]: A subset of a group containing the identity and closed under multiplication and inverses.
- Submodule of a module over a commutative ring [definition]: An additive subgroup of a module stable under multiplication by every scalar of its coefficient ring.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Subspace topology [definition]: For A⊆X, the topology whose open sets are A∩U for U open in X.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric group of a finite set [definition]: The group of all bijections of a finite set to itself under composition.
- Tate–Shafarevich group of a number-field torus [definition]: For a torus T over a number field F, Sha¹(F,T) is the kernel of H¹(F,T)→∏_v H¹(F_v,T), over all places. Since T is commutative these H¹ sets and their kernel are abelian groups; its classes are torsors locally trivial at every place.
- Tensor product of commutative algebras [definition]: For commutative R-algebras A,B, the module A⊗_R B with multiplication (a⊗b)(a′⊗b′)=aa′⊗bb′ and unit 1⊗1; it is the coproduct of A and B among commutative R-algebras.
- Tensor product of modules over a commutative ring [definition]: For R-modules M,N, the R-module M⊗_R N with a bilinear map (m,n)↦m⊗n through which every R-bilinear map M×N→P factors uniquely by an R-linear map.
- Tensor product of vector spaces [definition]: The vector space V⊗W with its universal bilinear map from V×W.
- Topological field [definition]: A field with a topology making addition, multiplication, additive inversion and inversion on the nonzero elements continuous.
- Topological group [definition]: A group with a topology for which multiplication G×G→G and inversion G→G are continuous.
- Topological ring [definition]: A ring with a topology for which addition, multiplication and additive inversion are continuous; the rings here are commutative with identity.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Topological vector space over ℝ or ℂ [definition]: A real or complex vector space with a topology for which vector addition and scalar multiplication are continuous.
- Topology generated by a family of seminorms [definition]: For seminorms (p_i) on a vector space, a neighborhood base at zero consists of finite intersections of sets {x:p_i(x)<ε}; neighborhoods at other points are translates.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Trace in a finite field extension [definition]: For a finite field extension L/K and a∈L, Tr_(L/K)(a) is the trace of the K-linear multiplication map x↦ax on L.
- Trace of a square matrix [definition]: The sum of the diagonal entries of a square matrix.
- Twisted centralizer in an algebraic group [definition]: For an automorphism τ of G, a τ-stable closed subgroup H⊂G and δ∈G(k), C^τ_(H,δ) is the k-subgroup with R-points {x∈H(R):xδτ(x)⁻¹=δ}. The ambient H is part of the specification.
- Uniform convergence of functions into a metric space [definition]: A sequence fₙ:X→(Y,d) converges uniformly to f if for every ε>0, d(fₙ(x),f(x))<ε for every x∈X once n is sufficiently large independently of x.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Unipotent affine algebraic group [definition]: An affine algebraic group that, after extension to an algebraic closure, has a faithful algebraic representation with all matrices upper triangular and every diagonal entry 1.
- Unitary operator on a complex Hilbert space [definition]: A surjective complex linear map preserving the inner product.
- Unitary representation of a topological group [definition]: A group homomorphism π from a topological group to the unitary operators of a complex Hilbert space, strongly continuous in the sense that g↦π(g)v is norm-continuous for each vector v.
- Universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra [definition]: The unital associative algebra U(g) with a linear map i:g→U(g) satisfying i([x,y])=i(x)i(y)−i(y)i(x), universal for such maps from g into associative algebras. Its universal property does not include an unproved assertion of injectivity.
- Valuation ring of a discretely valued field [definition]: For a discrete valuation v on K, the ring O_v={x:v(x)≥0}; its maximal ideal is m_v={x:v(x)>0}.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- Weight of a split torus representation [definition]: For an algebraic representation ρ of a split torus T, a character α:T→G_m is a weight if V_α={v:ρ(t)v=α(t)v after every algebra extension} is nonzero. V_α is its weight space.
- Z(g)-finite smooth function on a Lie group [definition]: A smooth complex function f on a real Lie group is Z(g)-finite when the span of zf, for z in the center of U(g⊗_R C), is finite-dimensional. The action differentiates right translations and extends to the enveloping algebra.
- Zariski topology on an affine variety [definition]: On the k-points of an affine variety with coordinate algebra A, closed sets are the common zero sets {x:f(x)=0 for all f∈I} of subsets I⊂A. Geometric closedness is tested after extension to an algebraic closure.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

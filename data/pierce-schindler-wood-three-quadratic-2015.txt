REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (45)

- Adjugate identity and matrix inverse: For a square matrix A over a commutative ring, its adjugate is the transpose of its cofactor matrix and satisfies A adj(A)=adj(A)A=(det A)I. Over a field, det A≠0 therefore gives A⁻¹=adj(A)/det A.
- Affine hypersurface Bézout inequality: Let W⊂kⁿ over an algebraically closed field be a nonempty affine variety of pure dimensionr, and let f be a nonzero polynomial of degreeD≥1 that vanishes identically on none of its irreducible components. If W∩V(f) is nonempty, it is pure of dimensionr−1 and its total geometric degree is at most D times that ofW.
- Bertini smoothness for general linear sections: Let Y⊂P^N_k be a smooth locally closed subvariety over an algebraically closed field, and0≤c≤N. A nonempty Zariski open set of codimension-c projective linear subspaces L has Y∩L empty or smooth of pure dimension dimY−c, with the intersection transverse. This holds in every characteristic.
- Bounded representatives of real quadratic fibers: Let Q:R^k→R^r be a tuple of homogeneous quadratic polynomials such that DQ has rank r at every nonzero common zero. For any 0<A₂≤A₁ there is Λ depending only on Q,A₁,A₂ such that every soluble fiber Q(x)=ν with A₂≤|ν|≤A₁ has a real solution with |x|≤Λ.
- Bounded-degree affine finite-field point upper bound: For an affine variety V⊆𝔸ʳ over 𝔽_q of dimension n cut out by boundedly many equations of bounded degrees, #V(𝔽_q)=O(qⁿ), with the implied constant depending only on the ambient dimension, number of equations and degree bound.
- Change of variables theorem for Euclidean integrals: For a C¹ diffeomorphism Φ:U→V between open subsets of ℝⁿ and a nonnegative measurable or integrable f on V, ∫_Vf(y)dy=∫_Uf(Φ(x))|det DΦ(x)|dx.
- Chinese remainder theorem for integers: For pairwise coprime positive integers m₁,…,m_r and residues a_i modulo m_i, there is a unique residue modulo ∏m_i reducing to a_i modulo every m_i.
- Deligne–Hooley point bound for projective complete intersections: For a projective complete intersection V of dimension n over 𝔽_q with geometric singular locus of dimension s, #V(𝔽_q)=1+q+⋯+qⁿ+O(q^((n+s+1)/2)); the constant can be bounded in terms of the ambient dimension and defining degrees. Use s=−1 if V is smooth.
- Diagonalization of a smooth pencil of quadrics: Let A,B be symmetric k×k matrices over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two, with k≥3. If their two projective quadratic equations define a smooth codimension-two intersection, then an invertible change of variables makes both matrices diagonal, diag(a_i),diag(b_i), with every pair(a_i,b_i) nonzero and all ratios[a_i:b_i] distinct. Equivalently det(sA+tB) has k distinct projective roots; in particular every nonzero pencil member has rank at least k−1.
- Dirichlet simultaneous rational approximation: For α∈ℝᵈ and an integer N≥1 there are integers 1≤q≤Nᵈ and p₁,…,p_d with max_j|qα_j−p_j|<1/N. If a common divisor is cancelled from (p₁,…,p_d,q), the resulting approximation errors |α_j−p_j/q| do not increase.
- Discriminant of equal-degree complete intersections: For1≤c≤N and d≥2, there is a primitive integer polynomial Δ in the coefficients of c degree-d forms in N+1 variables, unique up to sign, whose specialization over every field of characteristic different from two is absolutely irreducible and vanishes exactly when the forms fail to cut out a smooth codimension-c projective intersection geometrically. Its degree in each form’s coefficients is binom(N+1,c)d^(c−1)(d−1)^(N−c+1), hence its total degree is c times this number.
- Discriminant polynomial detecting singular fibers of a quadratic net: For a k-variable integral quadratic net with nonsingular determinant equation, there is a nonzero homogeneous H_Q∈ℤ[n₁,n₂,n₃], of positive degree at most 2(k+1)k(k−1), such that, over any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero or odd characteristic where the specialized determinant equation remains nonsingular, H_Q(n)=0 exactly when some x with Q(x)=n has Jacobian rank below three.
- Euler product of a Dirichlet series: For a multiplicative coefficient function a, an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series factors as ∑a(n)n^(−s)=∏_(p prime)(∑_(j≥0)a(pʲ)p^(−js)). An Euler-product representation specifies these local factors and its domain of convergence.
- Fiber dimension theorem for algebraic varieties: For a dominant morphism of irreducible algebraic varieties, the generic fiber has dimension dim X−dim Y; if all fibers have dimension r, then dim X=dim Y+r.
- Fourier transform of the triangular kernel: For ε>0 and sinc(t)=sin(πt)/(πt), with sinc(0)=1, one has ∫_R sinc²(εt) exp(2πitu)dt=ε^(−1)(1−|u|/ε)_+ for every real u. The d-dimensional product identity follows by integrating one coordinate at a time.
- Fubini’s theorem for integrable functions: An integrable function on the product of σ-finite measure spaces has integrable almost-everywhere sections, and its product integral equals either iterated integral.
- Geometry of the symmetric determinant hypersurface: Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two and for n≥2, the locus of singular symmetric n×n matrices is an irreducible reduced determinant hypersurface in the affine space Sym_n. Its singular locus is exactly the matrices of rank at most n−2, which has codimension three in Sym_n.
- Heine–Borel theorem in finite-dimensional normed spaces: A subset of a finite-dimensional real normed vector space is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded.
- Hilbert Nullstellensatz: Over an algebraically closed field, maximal ideals of a finitely generated algebra have residue field equal to the ground field, and the ideal of the zero set of an ideal is its radical.
- Integer sublevel bound for a homogeneous polynomial: If P∈ℚ[x₁,…,x_s] is nonzero homogeneous of positive degree d, then for M,N≥1 the number of x∈ℤˢ with max|x_i|≤N and |P(x)|≤M is O_P(N^(s−1)M^(1/d)).
- Integer sublevel bound for a univariate polynomial: For a polynomialP∈Q[x] of degree d≥1 with leading coefficienta≠0, andM≥1, #{x∈Z:|P(x)|≤M}≤d(2(M/|a|)^(1/d)+1). In particular it isO_(a,d)(M^(1/d)), uniformly in all the other coefficients.
- Inverse function theorem in Euclidean space: A continuously differentiable map between open subsets of ℝⁿ whose derivative is invertible at a point restricts near that point to a C¹ diffeomorphism. A C^k or smooth map has an inverse of the same regularity.
- Irreducibility of a smooth projective plane equation: A nonzero homogeneous polynomial F∈k[x,y,z] of positive degree whose projective plane equation is Jacobian-nonsingular over an algebraic closure is irreducible over that algebraic closure. Thus its projective curve is geometrically integral.
- Kollár effective Nullstellensatz degree bound: Let n≥2,D≥3 and K be a field. If homogeneous forms f_i of common degree D in K[x₀,…,x_n] generate I, then (√I)^(D^n)⊆I. Consequently, if affine polynomials g_i in n variables have degrees at most D and h vanishes on their common zeros over an algebraic closure, then h^s=Σ_i a_i g_i for some1≤s≤D^n and a_i∈K[x₁,…,x_n].
- Mean value theorem for real functions: If f is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), there exists c∈(a,b) such that f(b)−f(a)=f′(c)(b−a).
- Minimum attainment for lower semicontinuous functions on compact sets: A lower semicontinuous function from a nonempty compact space into (−∞,∞] attains its infimum.
- Multivariate Hensel lifting with a nonunit Jacobian determinant: Let F∈ℤ_p[x₁,…,x_r]ʳ and a∈ℤ_pʳ. If v_p(det DF(a))=ν<∞ and F(a)≡0 mod p^(2ν+1), then F has a zero b∈ℤ_pʳ with b≡a mod p^(ν+1).
- Nonemptiness of inverse limits of finite sets: For a sequence of nonempty finite sets X_n and maps f_n:X_(n+1)→X_n, there exists a coherent tuple (x_n) with f_n(x_(n+1))=x_n for every n. Equivalently the countable inverse limit is nonempty; the maps need not be surjective.
- Orthogonality of finite cyclic exponential sums: For integers q≥1 and m, Σ_(a modq) exp(2πiam/q) equalsq ifq dividesm and0 otherwise. On (Z/qZ)^k, the product character sum similarly vanishes unless every frequency is divisible byq, in which case it equalsq^k.
- Plancherel theorem for Fourier series on the circle: Fourier coefficients give an isometric isomorphism from L² of the probability-normalized unit circle to ℓ²(ℤ). Equivalently, the functions ζ↦ζⁿ, n∈ℤ, are a complete orthonormal system.
- Poisson summation for Euclidean Schwartz functions: For f∈𝒮(ℝᵈ), γ>0 and f̂(ξ)=∫f(x)e^(−2πix·ξ)dx, γᵈ∑_{n∈ℤᵈ}f(x+γn)=∑_{k∈ℤᵈ}f̂(k/γ)e^(2πik·x/γ), with absolute convergence.
- Prime number theorem in a fixed arithmetic progression: For fixed integers q≥1 and a with gcd(a,q)=1, π(x;q,a)=#{primesp≤x:p≡a modq} satisfies π(x;q,a)∼x/(φ(q)logx) asx→∞. The modulus is fixed; this statement asserts no uniform error bound asq varies.
- Prime-power root count with fixed leading coefficient: ForP∈Z[x] of degree d≥1 and leading coefficienta≠0, every primep and integerf≥0 satisfy #{x modp^f:P(x)≡0modp^f}≤d·p^(f−f/d+v_p(a)/d)≤d|a|^(1/d)p^(f−f/d). Thus the constant depends only on a,d, uniformly in p,f and the other coefficients.
- Prime-power solution bound for integer linear congruences: Let A be an m×n integer matrix of rank r≥1 over Q, b∈Z^m, p a prime and e≥1. If δ is the least p-adic valuation among the nonzero r×r minors of A, then #{x mod p^e:Ax≡b mod p^e}≤p^min(ne,(n−r)e+δ).
- Prime-power sublevel bound for a homogeneous polynomial: For a nonzero homogeneous P∈ℤ[x₁,…,x_s] of positive degree d, uniformly for primes p and integers f≥0, #{x∈(ℤ/pᶠℤ)ˢ:P(x)≡0 mod pᶠ}=O_P(p^(sf−f/d)).
- Primitive prime-power root count for a smooth homogeneous equation: Fix a homogeneous f∈Z[x₁,…,x_r] of positive degree with r≥2 whose projective hypersurface overQ is smooth. For every primep there is C_(f,p)>0 such that #{x modp^e:p∤(x₁,…,x_r), f(x)≡0 modp^e}≤C_(f,p)p^(e(r−1)) for all e≥1. The constants C_(f,p) can be chosen bounded outside a finite set of primes depending onf.
- Projective elimination and closed projection: Let k be algebraically closed. For finitely many polynomials f_i(t₁,…,t_m;x₀,…,x_N) homogeneous in x, the set of parameters t for which the f_i(t;x) have a common nonzero x is Zariski closed in A^m_k. Equivalently the projection A^m_k×P^N_k→A^m_k takes closed algebraic subsets to closed subsets.
- Quadratic oscillatory integral bounds: Let A be a real symmetric n×n matrix with eigenvalues ρ_j, w∈C_c^∞(Rⁿ) supported in [−1,1]ⁿ, and I(A,λ)=∫exp(2πi(xᵀAx−λ·x))w(x)dx. Uniformly in A and λ, |I(A,λ)|≤C_w∏_j min(1,|ρ_j|^(−1/2)), with a zero-eigenvalue factor interpreted as one. If |λ|≥max(1,4√n‖A‖op), then |I(A,λ)|≤C_(M,w)|λ|^(−M) for every M≥1.
- Rank characterization of a nonsingular quadratic determinant net: For k≥4 over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two, a quadratic net has a Jacobian-nonsingular determinant equation exactly when every nonzero combination of its matrices has rank at least k−1 and the common projective zero set has its three gradients linearly independent at every point.
- Rankin’s trick for nonnegative arithmetic sums: For a_n≥0, x>0 and δ>0, one has ∑_(n>x)a_n≤x^(−δ)∑_(n≥1)a_n n^δ whenever the sum on the right is finite.
- Singular integral of a system of quadratic forms: For real quadratic forms Q₁,…,Q_r and compactly supported smooth w, put I_w(θ)=∫_(ℝᵏ)w(x)exp(2πi∑θ_iQ_i(x))dx. When I_w is integrable, the singular integral at µ∈ℝʳ is J_w(µ)=∫_(ℝʳ)I_w(θ)exp(−2πiθ·µ)dθ.
- Singular series of a system of integral quadratic forms: For integral-matrix Q₁,…,Q_r in k variables and n∈ℤʳ, put T(n;q)=∑_(a mod q,gcd(a₁,…,a_r,q)=1)∑_(x mod q)exp(2πia·(Q(x)−n)/q). When absolutely convergent, the singular series is S(n)=∑_(q≥1)q^(−k)T(n;q).
- Smith diagonalization of an integer matrix: For every m×n integer matrix A there are unimodular integer matrices U,V such that UAV is rectangular diagonal, with positive nonzero entries d₁,…,d_r satisfying d₁|d₂|⋯|d_r, followed by zeros. The number r is the rank of A over Q.
- Spectral theorem for a real symmetric matrix: Every real symmetric matrix admits an orthonormal basis of real eigenvectors; equivalently A=UΛUᵀ with U orthogonal and Λ real diagonal.
- Subpower bounds for the divisor function: For every ε>0, the numberτ(n) of positive divisors satisfies τ(n)≤C_ε n^ε for all n≥1. More generally, for every fixed C≥1 and ε>0, C^ω(n)≤C_(C,ε)n^ε, whereω(n) is the number of distinct prime divisors.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (65)

1. Quadratic form with an integral symmetric matrix
   A quadratic form with an integral matrix is Q(x)=xᵀAx for a symmetric matrix A over ℤ. In the monomial expansion every mixed coefficient is even. This convention is stronger than merely requiring integer values on ℤⁿ.
   Source: relies_on, §1 p.1
   Note: Fixes the even mixed-coefficient convention.

2. Quadratic net with a nonsingular determinant equation
   A triple of k-variable quadratic forms over a field of characteristic different from two has a nonsingular determinant equation if det(a₁A₁+a₂A₂+a₃A₃)=0 is a Jacobian-nonsingular projective plane equation. The condition is geometric, after extension to an algebraic closure.
   Source: relies_on, §§1,3
   Note: Geometric condition essential to every main theorem.

3. Singular integral of a system of quadratic forms
   For real quadratic forms Q₁,…,Q_r and compactly supported smooth w, put I_w(θ)=∫_(ℝᵏ)w(x)exp(2πi∑θ_iQ_i(x))dx. When I_w is integrable, the singular integral at µ∈ℝʳ is J_w(µ)=∫_(ℝʳ)I_w(θ)exp(−2πiθ·µ)dθ.
   Source: relies_on, §5.1
   Note: Defines the real factor in every representation main term.

4. Singular series of a system of integral quadratic forms
   For integral-matrix Q₁,…,Q_r in k variables and n∈ℤʳ, put T(n;q)=∑_(a mod q,gcd(a₁,…,a_r,q)=1)∑_(x mod q)exp(2πia·(Q(x)−n)/q). When absolutely convergent, the singular series is S(n)=∑_(q≥1)q^(−k)T(n;q).
   Source: relies_on, §8
   Note: Defines the arithmetic factor.

5. Projective complete intersection
   A projective complete intersection of codimension r in projective N-space over a field is the closed subscheme cut out by r homogeneous polynomials forming a regular sequence in the homogeneous coordinate ring. Its dimension is N−r.
   Source: relies_on, §§3–4,6,13
   Note: Used for discriminants, finite-field counts and rank geometry.

6. Rank characterization of a nonsingular quadratic determinant net
   For k≥4 over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from two, a quadratic net has a Jacobian-nonsingular determinant equation exactly when every nonzero combination of its matrices has rank at least k−1 and the common projective zero set has its three gradients linearly independent at every point.
   Source: relies_on, Proposition 3.1
   Note: Converts determinant nonsingularity to Jacobian and rank bounds.

7. Discriminant polynomial detecting singular fibers of a quadratic net
   For a k-variable integral quadratic net with nonsingular determinant equation, there is a nonzero homogeneous H_Q∈ℤ[n₁,n₂,n₃], of positive degree at most 2(k+1)k(k−1), such that, over any algebraically closed field of characteristic zero or odd characteristic where the specialized determinant equation remains nonsingular, H_Q(n)=0 exactly when some x with Q(x)=n has Jacobian rank below three.
   Source: relies_on, Proposition 3.3
   Note: Controls exceptional fibers and local lower bounds.

8. Bounded-degree affine finite-field point upper bound
   For an affine variety V⊆𝔸ʳ over 𝔽_q of dimension n cut out by boundedly many equations of bounded degrees, #V(𝔽_q)=O(qⁿ), with the implied constant depending only on the ambient dimension, number of equations and degree bound.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 4.1; §7
   Note: Counts points on determinantal loci.

9. Deligne–Hooley point bound for projective complete intersections
   For a projective complete intersection V of dimension n over 𝔽_q with geometric singular locus of dimension s, #V(𝔽_q)=1+q+⋯+qⁿ+O(q^((n+s+1)/2)); the constant can be bounded in terms of the ambient dimension and defining degrees. Use s=−1 if V is smooth.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 4.2; §6
   Note: Gives the refined good-prime complete-sum cancellation.

10. Integer sublevel bound for a homogeneous polynomial
   If P∈ℚ[x₁,…,x_s] is nonzero homogeneous of positive degree d, then for M,N≥1 the number of x∈ℤˢ with max|x_i|≤N and |P(x)|≤M is O_P(N^(s−1)M^(1/d)).
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 4.8; §12
   Note: Counts small real values and zero fibers.

11. Prime-power sublevel bound for a homogeneous polynomial
   For a nonzero homogeneous P∈ℤ[x₁,…,x_s] of positive degree d, uniformly for primes p and integers f≥0, #{x∈(ℤ/pᶠℤ)ˢ:P(x)≡0 mod pᶠ}=O_P(p^(sf−f/d)).
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 4.10; §12
   Note: Controls high divisibility of H_Q.

12. Multivariate Hensel lifting with a nonunit Jacobian determinant
   Let F∈ℤ_p[x₁,…,x_r]ʳ and a∈ℤ_pʳ. If v_p(det DF(a))=ν<∞ and F(a)≡0 mod p^(2ν+1), then F has a zero b∈ℤ_pʳ with b≡a mod p^(ν+1).
   Source: relies_on, Proposition 6.7 proof
   Note: Allows nonunit Jacobian determinant at bad primes.

13. Hilbert Nullstellensatz
   Over an algebraically closed field, maximal ideals of a finitely generated algebra have residue field equal to the ground field, and the ideal of the zero set of an ideal is its radical.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 6.8
   Note: Ideal identity controls the maximal Jacobian minor.

14. Spectral theorem for a real symmetric matrix
   Every real symmetric matrix admits an orthonormal basis of real eigenvectors; equivalently A=UΛUᵀ with U orthogonal and Λ real diagonal.
   Source: relies_on, §5

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (229)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Absolute value on a field [definition]: A map |·|:K→R_{≥0} that vanishes exactly at zero, is multiplicative and satisfies |x+y|≤|x|+|y|. It is nonarchimedean if the stronger bound |x+y|≤max(|x|,|y|) holds.
- Affine algebraic variety over a field [definition]: An affine algebraic variety is specified here by a finitely generated reduced commutative algebra A over a field F. Its k-points, for extensions k/F, are the F-algebra homomorphisms A→k. Morphisms are induced by algebra homomorphisms in the opposite direction. Reducible affine varieties are allowed.
- Affine linear subspace over a field [definition]: An affine linear subspace of kⁿ is a translate a+W of a vector subspace W≤kⁿ. Its dimension is dimW, independently of the chosen a.
- Affine scheme [construction]: The affine scheme Spec R is the prime spectrum of R with the structure sheaf characterized on standard opens by O(D(f))=R_f and the localization restriction maps. Its stalk at 𝔭 is R_𝔭. An affine scheme is any locally ringed space isomorphic to one of these.
- Algebraic closure of a field [definition]: An algebraic extension of a field that is itself algebraically closed. This specifies an object, without including the existence or uniqueness-up-to-isomorphism theorem.
- Algebraic dual of a vector space [construction]: For a vector space V over a field k, its algebraic dual V* is Hom_k(V,k), with pointwise addition and scalar multiplication. No topology or continuity condition is imposed.
- Algebraic element of a field extension [definition]: An element α of L/F is algebraic over F if a nonzero polynomial in F[x] vanishes at α.
- Algebraically closed field [definition]: A field in which every nonconstant polynomial has a root.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Associative algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity.
- Asymptotic equivalence [definition]: The assertion f∼g along a specified limit means f(x)/g(x)→1, where g is nonzero near that limit. Sequences are the instance x=n→∞.
- Base change and fibers of a scheme [construction]: For X→S and S′→S, the base change is X_(S′)=X×_S S′ with its projection to S′. The fiber at s∈S is X_s=X×_S Spec κ(s), where κ(s)=O_(S,s)/𝔪_s is the residue field of the local ring. A geometric fiber extends this residue field to an algebraic closure.
- Basis of a vector space [definition]: A linearly independent family of vectors whose linear span is the whole vector space.
- Big O notation [definition]: The assertion f=O(g) along a specified limit means |f(x)|≤C|g(x)| near that limit for a fixed finite C. Subscripts specify allowed dependence on fixed parameters. For sequences use x=n→∞.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- C^k map between Euclidean open sets [definition]: For a finite integer k≥0, a map between open Euclidean sets is C^k if all component partial derivatives of total order at most k exist and are continuous; order zero is the component itself, so C^0 means continuous. A map is C^∞ when it is C^k for every finite k.
- Cardinality of a set [definition]: The size of a set, with two sets having the same cardinality when there is a bijection between them. A set has finite cardinality n when it is in bijection with {1,…,n}; infinite cardinalities are distinguished by the same bijection criterion.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Characteristic of a field [definition]: The least positive integer n with n·1=0 in the field, if one exists, and zero otherwise.
- Closed immersion of schemes [definition]: A scheme morphism i:Z→X is a closed immersion if it is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset and the structure-sheaf map O_X→i_*O_Z is surjective on stalks. Affinely it is induced by a quotient map of rings R→R/I.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative algebra over a commutative ring [definition]: A commutative ring A with a specified unital ring homomorphism R→A; algebra homomorphisms commute with these structure maps.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Compactly supported smooth test function on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A smooth function on an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ whose support is a compact subset of Ω; these form C_c^∞(Ω).
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complete quadratic exponential sum [definition]: For an integral symmetric matrix A, positive integer q, and integer vector b, the complete quadratic exponential sum is ∑_(x∈(ℤ/qℤ)^k)exp(2πi(xᵀAx+bᵀx)/q). The sum is independent of the residue representatives.
- Completion of a field with an absolute value [definition]: The metric completion of K for d(x,y)=|x−y|, with the field operations extended continuously. A completion is regarded as containing K through its canonical isometric embedding.
- Completion of a metric space [definition]: A complete metric space containing an isometric dense copy of the given metric space. It is unique up to an isometry fixing that copy.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex exponential function [definition]: The entire function exp(z)=∑ₙ₌₀∞zⁿ/n!; on imaginary arguments exp(it)=cos(t)+i sin(t).
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Complex power series [definition]: A series ∑ₙ₌₀∞aₙ(z−z₀)ⁿ with complex coefficients, considered at points where its partial sums converge.
- Composition of functions [definition]: For f:A→B and g:B→C, the composite g∘f:A→C sends x to g(f(x)).
- Congruence modulo an integer [definition]: The relation a≡b (mod m) means that m divides a−b.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Continuously differentiable Euclidean map [definition]: A map on an open Euclidean set whose first coordinate partial derivatives exist and are continuous.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Coprime integers [definition]: Integers whose greatest common divisor is1.
- C¹ diffeomorphism between Euclidean open sets [definition]: A C¹ diffeomorphism Φ:U→V between open subsets of R^n is a bijection such that both Φ and Φ⁻¹ are continuously differentiable.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Dense subset of a topological space [definition]: A subset is dense if its closure is the whole space.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Determinant of a square matrix [definition]: For an n×n matrix A over a commutative ring, det A=∑_{σ∈Sₙ}sgn(σ)∏_{i=1}ⁿA_{i,σ(i)}, with the 0×0 determinant equal to one.
- Determinant polynomial of a net of quadratic forms [definition]: For three symmetric k×k matrices A₁,A₂,A₃ over a field, the determinant polynomial of their net is F(a₁,a₂,a₃)=det(a₁A₁+a₂A₂+a₃A₃), homogeneous of degree k. Its zero locus parametrizes singular linear combinations of the three forms.
- Dimension of a finite-type scheme [definition]: The dimension of a scheme is the supremum of the lengths of chains of irreducible closed subsets of its underlying space. On an affine chart SpecA it equals the Krull dimension of A; for a finite-type scheme over a field it is the maximum of these dimensions over any finite affine open cover.
- Dimension of a vector space [definition]: The cardinality of a basis of the vector space; a finite basis with n vectors gives dimension n.
- Dirichlet series [definition]: A Dirichlet series is ∑_(n≥1)a_n n^(−s), with complex coefficients and n^(−s)=exp(−s log n), considered on a right half-plane where it converges.
- Discrete valuation on a field [definition]: A surjective map v:K×→Z satisfying v(xy)=v(x)+v(y) and v(x+y)≥min(v(x),v(y)), extended by v(0)=+∞.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Divisibility of integers [definition]: An integer a divides b if b=ac for some integer c.
- Divisor function d(n) [definition]: d(n) is the number of positive divisors of n, equivalently the number of ordered positive integer pairs (a,b) satisfying ab=n.
- Dominant scheme morphism [definition]: A morphism of schemes f:X→Y is dominant if its image is dense in the underlying topological space of Y. For a morphism between integral affine schemes SpecB→SpecA, dominance is equivalent to injectivity of the ring map A→B.
- Eigenvalue of a linear endomorphism [definition]: A scalar λ for which a linear endomorphism T has a nonzero vector v with Tv=λv.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².

- Extended real line [definition]: The ordered set ℝ∪{−∞,+∞}, with−∞<r<+∞ for every real r.

- Fiber of a scheme morphism [definition]: For a scheme morphism f:X→Y and a point y∈Y with residue field κ(y), its fiber over y is X_y=X×_Y Specκ(y), regarded as a κ(y)-scheme. - Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Field extension [definition]: An inclusion of one field into another.

- Finite field [definition]: A field whose underlying set is finite.

- Finite-dimensional vector space [definition]: A vector space having a finite basis.
- Finitely generated commutative algebra [definition]: An R-algebra A generated as an algebra by finitely many elements; equivalently a quotient of R[X₁,…,X_n] for some finite n.

- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.

- Greatest common divisor of integers [definition]: For integers a,b not both zero, gcd(a,b) is their greatest positive common divisor.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Hermitian complex matrix [definition]: A square complex matrix A is Hermitian if its conjugate transpose A* equals A; equivalently A_(ij)=overline(A_(ji)) for every i,j.
- Homeomorphism [definition]: A bijection between topological spaces that is continuous and has a continuous inverse.
- Homogeneous polynomial over a field [definition]: A polynomial over a field in finitely many variables is homogeneous of degree d if all its nonzero monomials have total degree d. The zero polynomial is homogeneous of every degree by convention.
- Homogeneous real polynomial [definition]: A real polynomial in finitely many variables whose nonzero monomials all have the same total degree.
- Homomorphism of algebras over a field [definition]: A linear map of unital associative algebras over the same field preserving multiplication and the multiplicative identity.

- Integral domain [definition]: An integral domain is a nonzero commutative ring R in which ab=0 implies a=0 or b=0.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.

- Jacobian nonsingularity of a projective plane equation [definition]: For a nonzero homogeneous polynomial F in three variables over a field K, its projective equation is Jacobian-nonsingular if no nonzero vector over an algebraic closure satisfies F=∂₁F=∂₂F=∂₃F=0. This tests the equation as a scheme, retaining multiplicities rather than replacing F by its square-free part.
- Krull dimension of a topological space [definition]: The Krull dimension of a topological space is the supremum of the lengths r of chains Z₀⊊Z₁⊊⋯⊊Z_r of nonempty irreducible closed subsets. The dimension of a scheme is the dimension of its underlying space.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Linearly independent family of vectors [definition]: A family of vectors is linearly independent if every finite linear combination equalling zero has all coefficients zero.
- Local commutative ring [definition]: A local ring is a commutative ring with exactly one maximal ideal. A homomorphism of local rings is local if it sends the maximal ideal of its source into the maximal ideal of its target.
- Localization of a commutative ring [definition]: For a multiplicatively closed subset S of a commutative ring R containing 1, S⁻¹R consists of fractions r/s, identifying r/s with r′/s′ when t(rs′−r′s)=0 for some t in S, with the usual fraction operations.
- Locally ringed space [definition]: A locally ringed space is a topological space X with a sheaf of commutative rings O_X whose stalk at each point is a local ring. A morphism consists of a continuous map f:X→Y and compatible ring maps O_Y(U)→O_X(f⁻¹U) whose induced maps on stalks are local.
- Lower semicontinuous extended-real function [definition]: A function u:X→(−∞,∞] on a topological space is lower semicontinuous if {x:u(x)>a} is open for every real a.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Maximal ideal [definition]: A maximal ideal of a commutative ring R is a proper ideal contained in no larger proper ideal.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Minimal polynomial of an algebraic element [definition]: The unique monic polynomial of least positive degree over F vanishing at an algebraic element α; it generates the ideal of all polynomials over F vanishing at α.
- Module over a commutative ring [definition]: An abelian group M with an action of a commutative ring R satisfying r(m+n)=rm+rn, (r+s)m=rm+sm, (rs)m=r(sm), and 1m=m. Unlike a vector space, its scalar ring need not be a field.
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Morphism of schemes [definition]: A morphism of schemes is a morphism of their locally ringed spaces. On affine opens it is induced contravariantly by a unital homomorphism of commutative rings. An S-scheme is a scheme with a specified morphism to S; morphisms over S commute with these structure maps.
- Morphism of schemes of finite type [definition]: A morphism X→S is locally of finite type if locally on affine source and target charts it comes from a ring map R→A with A generated by finitely many elements as an R-algebra. It is of finite type if it is also quasi-compact: inverse images of quasi-compact open subsets are quasi-compact.
- Multiplicative arithmetic function [definition]: An arithmetic function a:ℕ₊→ℂ is multiplicative if a(1)=1 and a(mn)=a(m)a(n) whenever gcd(m,n)=1.
- Multiplicity of a polynomial root [definition]: The multiplicity of a root a of a polynomial P is the largest m≥1 for which (x−a)^m divides P. A root of multiplicity1 is simple.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nilpotent element of a ring [definition]: An element a such that a^n=0 for some positive integer n.
- Nilpotent ideal of a commutative ring [definition]: An ideal I for which I^n=0 for some positive integer n, meaning every product of n elements of I is zero.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Orthonormal basis of a Hilbert space [definition]: An orthonormal family whose linear span is dense in the Hilbert space.
- Orthonormal family in a Hilbert space [definition]: Vectors (e_i) with ⟨e_i,e_j⟩=0 for i≠j and ‖e_i‖=1.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Perfect field [definition]: A field k is perfect if every algebraic extension of k is separable. In characteristic zero this always holds; in characteristic p>0 it is equivalent to surjectivity of x↦xᵖ on k.
- Polynomial over a field [definition]: A formal finite sum a₀+a₁x+⋯+aₙxⁿ with coefficients in a field, with the usual coefficientwise addition and multiplication.
- Polynomial ring over a commutative ring [definition]: The ring R[X₁,…,X_n] of formal finite sums of monomials with coefficients in R, with coefficientwise addition and the usual multiplication of monomials.
- Positive semidefinite complex matrix [definition]: A Hermitian n×n complex matrix A is positive semidefinite if z*Az≥0 for every z∈ℂⁿ.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Prime ideal of a commutative ring [definition]: A proper ideal P of a commutative ring R such that ab in P implies a in P or b in P.
- Prime number [definition]: An integer p>1 is prime if its only positive divisors are 1 and p.
- Prime spectrum and Zariski topology [definition]: Spec R is the set of prime ideals of a commutative ring R with closed sets V(I)={𝔭:I⊆𝔭}. Its standard open subsets are D(f)={𝔭:f∉𝔭}; these form a topology basis.
- Product measure [construction]: For σ-finite measure spaces, the product measure on the product σ-algebra assigns a measurable rectangle A×B the value μ(A)ν(B); its completion also includes subsets of product-null sets.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Projective scheme over a field [definition]: A k-scheme is projective if it admits a closed immersion over k into some projective space Pⁿ_k. Projectivity includes the specified algebraic scheme structure, rather than only its set of geometric points.
- Projective space as a scheme [construction]: Projective n-space over a field k is obtained by gluing the n+1 standard affine charts Spec k[x₀/x_i,…,x_n/x_i], omitting x_i/x_i, with the standard coordinate-ratio changes on overlaps. Its points over an extension field are nonzero vectors modulo common nonzero scaling.
- Projective space over a field [definition]: For a field F, projective n-space consists of nonzero vectors in F^(n+1) modulo multiplication by nonzero scalars; points are written [x₀:⋯:x_n]. Homogeneous polynomial equations define its projective algebraic subsets.
- Quadratic form over a field of characteristic different from two [definition]: On a finite-dimensional vector space over a field F with char(F)≠2, a quadratic form is a map Q with Q(ax)=a²Q(x) whose polarization B(x,y)=(Q(x+y)−Q(x)−Q(y))/2 is bilinear. Thus Q(x)=B(x,x).
- Quotient module [definition]: For a submodule N of an R-module M, the cosets M/N carry addition and scalar multiplication induced from M.
- Quotient of a commutative ring by an ideal [definition]: For an ideal I of R, the cosets R/I form a ring under (r+I)+(s+I)=r+s+I and (r+I)(s+I)=rs+I.
- Quotient of an abelian group by a subgroup [definition]: For an abelian group G and subgroup H,the cosets G/H form an abelian group under (g+H)+(g′+H)=g+g′+H.
- Rank of a linear map [definition]: The dimension of the image of a linear map.
- Rational number [definition]: A real x is rational if x=a/b for integers a,b with b≠0; otherwise it is irrational.
- Rational point of a scheme [definition]: For a k-scheme X and a field extension L/k, an L-rational point of X is a morphism Spec L→X over Spec k. The set of these morphisms is denoted X(L); this includes k-rational points when L=k.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real orthogonal matrix [definition]: A real square matrix U with UᵀU=I, equivalently a linear map preserving the Euclidean inner product.
- Real polynomial in finitely many variables [definition]: A finite real linear combination of monomials x₁^{a₁}⋯x_d^{a_d} with nonnegative integer exponents.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Reduced commutative ring [definition]: A commutative ring whose only nilpotent element is0.
- Residue field at a prime ideal [definition]: For a commutative ring R and prime ideal 𝔭, its residue field is κ(𝔭)=R_𝔭/𝔭R_𝔭, equivalently the fraction field of R/𝔭. For a maximal ideal 𝔪 it is R/𝔪.
- Ring of p-adic integers [definition]: For a prime p, ℤ_p is the subring {x∈ℚ_p:v_p(x)≥0}, with its p-adic topology.
- Schatten norm of a finite matrix [definition]: For a real or complex matrix M with singular values σ_j and1≤p<∞, its Schatten p-norm is ‖M‖_p=(∑σ_j^p)^(1/p); ‖M‖_∞=maxσ_j is the operator norm. The p=1 case is the nuclear norm and p=2 is the Frobenius norm.
- Scheme [definition]: A scheme is a locally ringed space admitting an open cover by affine schemes, with the given local-ringed-space structures on those opens.
- Schwartz space [definition]: The space S(ℝⁿ) of smooth functions φ for which supₓ|xᵅ∂ᵝφ(x)| is finite for every pair of multi-indices α,β, with the topology of these seminorms.
- Seminorm on a real or complex vector space [definition]: A nonnegative function p with p(ax)=|a|p(x) and p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y); p may vanish on nonzero vectors.
- Separable algebraic field extension [definition]: An algebraic extension L/F is separable if the minimal polynomial over F of every element of L has distinct roots in an algebraic closure.
- Separable polynomial over a field [definition]: A polynomial having no repeated root in an algebraic closure of its coefficient field.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Sheaf on a topological space [definition]: A sheaf of sets on X assigns a set F(U) to each open U and restriction maps compatible with identities and composition, such that sections agreeing on every overlap of any open cover glue to one unique section. Sheaves of groups, rings or modules carry the respective operations compatibly with restriction.
- Sign of a permutation [definition]: The sign of a permutation of {1,…,n} is (−1) to the number of inversions, where an inversion is a pair i<j with σ(i)>σ(j).
- Singular locus of a projective complete intersection [definition]: For a projective complete intersection cut out by f₁,…,f_r over a perfect field, the geometric singular locus consists of its points where the r×(N+1) matrix (∂f_i/∂x_j) has rank less than r. The empty locus has dimension −1.
- Singular values of a complex matrix [construction]: For a complex m×n matrix M, its singular values σ₁≥⋯≥σ_q≥0, q=min(m,n), are the nonnegative square roots of the eigenvalues of M*M, with the additional forced zero eigenvalues omitted when n>m.
- Smooth finitely presented commutative algebra [definition]: A finitely presented R-algebra A such that, for every R-algebra B with nilpotent ideal I, every R-algebra map A→B/I lifts to A→B. This is the infinitesimal lifting formulation of smoothness for affine finite-presentation algebras.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Smooth morphism of affine finite-presentation spaces [definition]: A morphism represented contravariantly by A→B is smooth when B, as an A-algebra, is finitely presented and satisfies the nilpotent-ideal lifting property.
- Smooth morphism of schemes [definition]: A morphism of schemes is smooth if locally on affine source and target charts its corresponding algebra map is smooth, meaning finitely presented and formally smooth for nilpotent-ideal lifting. Smoothness over a field is used for nonsingular geometric fibers, not merely regularity over an imperfect field.
- Stalk of a sheaf [definition]: The stalk F_x is the set of germs of sections near x: pairs(U,s) with x∈U and s∈F(U), identified when their restrictions agree on some smaller neighborhood of x. Algebraic operations on a sheaf induce the same operations on its stalks.
- Subgroup [definition]: A subset of a group containing the identity and closed under multiplication and inverses.
- Submodule of a module over a commutative ring [definition]: An additive subgroup of a module stable under multiplication by every scalar of its coefficient ring.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric group of a finite set [definition]: The group of all bijections of a finite set to itself under composition.
- Symmetric matrix [definition]: A square matrix A equal to its transpose, so aᵢⱼ=aⱼᵢ.
- Tempered distribution [definition]: A continuous linear functional on the Schwartz space S(ℝⁿ).
- Tensor product of commutative algebras [definition]: For commutative R-algebras A,B, the module A⊗_R B with multiplication (a⊗b)(a′⊗b′)=aa′⊗bb′ and unit 1⊗1; it is the coproduct of A and B among commutative R-algebras.
- Tensor product of modules over a commutative ring [definition]: For R-modules M,N, the R-module M⊗_R N with a bilinear map (m,n)↦m⊗n through which every R-bilinear map M×N→P factors uniquely by an R-linear map.
- Topological ring [definition]: A ring with a topology for which addition, multiplication and additive inversion are continuous; the rings here are commutative with identity.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Topology generated by a family of seminorms [definition]: For seminorms (p_i) on a vector space, a neighborhood base at zero consists of finite intersections of sets {x:p_i(x)<ε}; neighborhoods at other points are translates.
- Total geometric degree of an affine variety [definition]: Over an algebraically closed field, the degree of an irreducible affine variety W⊂kⁿ of dimensionr is the maximum number of points in a finite intersection W∩L with an affine linear subspace L of dimensionn−r. The total geometric degree of a reducible variety is the sum over all its irreducible components, including components of smaller dimension; the empty set has degree0.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Unimodular integer matrix [definition]: A square matrix with integer entries is unimodular if its determinant is 1 or −1; equivalently it has an inverse with integer entries.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Valuation ring of a discretely valued field [definition]: For a discrete valuation v on K, the ring O_v={x:v(x)≥0}; its maximal ideal is m_v={x:v(x)>0}.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- Zariski tangent and cotangent space at a rational point [construction]: For a k-rational point x of a k-scheme X, its cotangent space is 𝔪_x/𝔪_x² over k, where 𝔪_x is the maximal ideal of O_(X,x). Its dual Hom_k(𝔪_x/𝔪_x²,k) is the Zariski tangent space T_xX; equivalently tangent vectors are k-morphisms Spec k[ε]/(ε²)→X reducing to x.
- Zariski topology on an affine variety [definition]: On the k-points of an affine variety with coordinate algebra A, closed sets are the common zero sets {x:f(x)=0 for all f∈I} of subsets I⊂A. Geometric closedness is tested after extension to an algebraic closure.
- p-adic valuation of a rational number [definition]: For a prime p and nonzero rational x, v_p(x) is the unique integer r such that x=p^r a/b with integers a,b not divisible by p; put v_p(0)=+∞.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

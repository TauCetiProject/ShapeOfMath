LOCATED CONCEPTS (4)

1. Order bounds for the Riemann integral
   If integrable f satisfies m≤f≤M on [a,b], then m(b−a)≤∫_a^b f≤M(b−a).
   Source: explains, p. 121 display with Theorem 3.2.3
   Note: Selected proof exposition; not an origin claim.

2. Equivalence of the Riemann and Darboux integrals
   A bounded f:[a,b]→ℝ is Riemann integrable, in the sense that its tagged Riemann sums converge as the mesh tends to zero, exactly when its lower and upper Darboux integrals are equal; the common value is then the Riemann integral.
   Source: explains, Theorems 3.2.3 and 3.2.5
   Note: Exposition; not an origin claim.

3. Lower and upper Darboux integrals
   For bounded f:[a,b]→ℝ and a partition a=x₀<⋯<x_n=b, the lower and upper Darboux sums are ∑m_i(x_i−x_{i−1}) and ∑M_i(x_i−x_{i−1}), where m_i and M_i are the infimum and supremum of f on [x_{i−1},x_i]. The lower (upper) Darboux integral is the supremum of all lower sums (infimum of all upper sums).
   Source: explains, §3.1, pp. 120–121
   Note: Exposition of upper and lower sums and integrals; not an origin claim.

4. Darboux criterion for Riemann integrability
   A bounded function on a compact rectangle is Riemann integrable if and only if for every ε>0 there is a rectangular partition whose upper and lower Darboux sums differ by less than ε.
   Source: explains, Theorem 3.2.7 (interval) and Theorem 7.1.12 (rectangles), pp. 133, 447
   Note: Statement; the rectangle proof is left as Exercise 7.1.12. Not a priority claim.

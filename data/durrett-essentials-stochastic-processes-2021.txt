LOCATED CONCEPTS (4)

1. Population law of the binary Yule process
   For one initial particle splitting into two at rateβ per particle without death, P(N_t=n)=e^{−βt}(1−e^{−βt})^{n−1} for n≥1. All integer moments are finite at every finite time.
   Source: explains, Theorem 4.2 and proof, pp. 133–134
   Note: Textbook computation from the forward equation; not a priority claim.

2. Binary Yule process
   A continuous-time population process without deaths in which each particle, independently of all other particles and of the past, lives for an exponential time of rate β>0 and then splits into two particles. N_t denotes the number of particles alive at time t.
   Source: explains, Examples 4.4 and 4.10, pp. 127, 133
   Note: Definition as a pure-birth branching chain.

3. Minimum of independent exponential random variables
   If T₁,…,Tₙ are independent and Tᵢ is exponential with rate λᵢ, then V=min Tᵢ is exponential with rate λ₁+⋯+λₙ, the index I of the minimum satisfies P(I=i)=λᵢ/(λ₁+⋯+λₙ), and I and V are independent.
   Source: explains, Theorem 2.1 and proof, pp. 81–82
   Note: Exponential races.

4. Kolmogorov forward equations for a continuous-time Markov chain
   For a time-homogeneous continuous-time Markov chain on a countable state space with jump rates q(k,j) and total rates λ_j=∑_{k≠j}q(j,k), under conditions that hold for finite chains and birth–death chains, the transition probabilities satisfy p′_t(i,j)=∑_{k≠j}p_t(i,k)q(k,j)−λ_jp_t(i,j), that is, p′_t=p_tQ.
   Source: explains, §4.2, (4.10)–(4.11), p. 131
   Note: Heuristic derivation; validity asserted for the chains considered.

REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (15)

- Aleksandrov–Fenchel inequality for mixed volumes: For convex bodies K₁,…,K_d⊂ℝᵈ, V(K₁,K₂,K₃,…,K_d)²≥V(K₁,K₁,K₃,…,K_d)V(K₂,K₂,K₃,…,K_d).
- Banach–Mazur distance between origin-symmetric convex bodies: For origin-symmetric convex bodies K,L⊂ℝᵈ, d_BM(K,L)=inf{t≥1:L⊆TK⊆tL for an invertible linear map T}.
- Best L² approximation by spherical polynomials: Forf∈L²(S^{d−1}),E_N(f) is the infimum of∥f−P∥₂ over spherical polynomialsP of degree at mostN. Rates of decay ofE_N quantify polynomial approximation.
- Cauchy’s projection formula for convex bodies: For a convex body K and unit u, the volume of K projected onto u⊥ equals ½∫|u·v| dS_K(v).
- Curvature density under an invertible linear map: ForA∈GL(d) and a convex bodyK with surface-area densityf_K, f_AK(v)=|detA|²|Aᵀv|^(−d−1) f_K(Aᵀv/|Aᵀv|) almost everywhere onS^{d−1}.
- Determinant-integral formula for zonoid volume: For a full-dimensional symmetric zonoidZ with generating measureµ defined byh_Z(u)=∫|u·v|dµ(v), |Z|=(2ᵈ/d!)∫⋯∫|det(v₁,…,v_d)|dµ(v₁)⋯dµ(v_d).
- Difference body of a convex body: The origin-symmetric body K−K=K+(−K), where−K={−x:x∈K}. Some sources use the normalized difference body½(K−K).
- Generating-measure representation theorem for symmetric zonoids: An origin-symmetric convex body is a zonoid exactly when it has a finite even positive generating measure on the unit sphere. That generating measure is unique.
- Invertibility of the cosine transform on smooth even functions: For d≥3, the spherical cosine transform is a bijection ofC∞ even functions onS^{d−1} onto itself, where even meansf(−u)=f(u).
- Nikolskii inequality for spherical polynomials: For a spherical polynomialP of degree at mostN≥1 onS^{d−1}, ∥P∥∞≤C_dN^{(d−1)/2}∥P∥₂, with spherical area normalized to probability.
- Projection body associated with a quermassintegral: For 0≤i≤d−2, Π_iK is the convex body with support function h_{Π_iK}(u)=W_i(K|u⊥), where the quermassintegral is taken in the (d−1)-dimensional space u⊥.
- Schneider’s projection-functional monotonicity theorem: For a full-dimensional convex body K⊂ℝᵈ, set P(K)=|ΠK|/|K|^{d−1}. Then P(K)≥P(ΠK), with equality exactly when K is homothetic to Π²K.
- Smooth symmetric support functions are generalized zonoids: An origin-symmetric convex body withC∞ support function is a generalized zonoid: h_K=Cν for a finite even signed measureν, equivalentlyK+M=L for symmetric zonoidsL,M.
- Spherical Radon transform on spherical harmonics: For d≥3, the normalized spherical Radon transform acts on degree-k harmonics by zero for oddk and by(−1)^{k/2}[1·3⋯(k−1)]/[(d−1)(d+1)⋯(d+k−3)] for evenk; the degree-zero multiplier is1.
- Weil’s formula for bounded curvature densities of projection bodies: For d≥3 and a convex bodyK with bounded surface-area densityf_K relative to unnormalized spherical area, ΠK has density f_ΠK(u)=(1/(d−1)!)∫_{(S^{d−1}∩u⊥)^{d−1}}det_{u⊥}(v₁,…,v_{d−1})²∏_jf_K(v_j)dℋ^{d−2}(v_j), for almost everyu.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (35)

1. Convex body in Euclidean space
   A compact convex subset of ℝᵈ with nonempty interior.
   Source: relies_on, §1
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

2. Support function of a convex body
   For a convex body K⊂ℝᵈ, h_K(u)=max{x·u:x∈K}; it may be restricted to the unit sphere or extended positively homogeneously to ℝᵈ.
   Source: relies_on, §1
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

3. Banach–Mazur distance between origin-symmetric convex bodies
   For origin-symmetric convex bodies K,L⊂ℝᵈ, d_BM(K,L)=inf{t≥1:L⊆TK⊆tL for an invertible linear map T}.
   Source: relies_on, §1
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

4. Surface area measure of a convex body
   The measure S_K on the unit sphere assigning a Borel set of directions the boundary area of points of K whose outer unit normal lies in that set; points with nonunique normals form a boundary-area null set.
   Source: relies_on, §1
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

5. Curvature function of a convex body
   When the surface area measure S_K is absolutely continuous with respect to spherical area measure, its Radon–Nikodym density f_K is called the curvature function.
   Source: relies_on, §1
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

6. Projection body of a convex body
   The origin-symmetric convex body ΠK whose support function at a unit vector u is the (d−1)-dimensional volume of the orthogonal projection of K onto u⊥.
   Source: relies_on, §1 throughout
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

7. Cauchy’s projection formula for convex bodies
   For a convex body K and unit u, the volume of K projected onto u⊥ equals ½∫|u·v| dS_K(v).
   Source: relies_on, §1 p.2
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

8. Spherical harmonic
   The restriction to the unit sphere in ℝᵈ of a homogeneous polynomial annihilated by the Euclidean Laplacian. Its degree is the degree of that polynomial.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

9. Normalized spherical Radon transform
   For a function f on S^{d−1}, Rf(u) is its average over the equatorial sphere S^{d−1}∩u⊥; the normalization gives R1=1.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: Used in the source-specific selected proof or background definitions.

40. Curvature density under an invertible linear map
   ForA∈GL(d) and a convex bodyK with surface-area densityf_K, f_AK(v)=|detA|²|Aᵀv|^(−d−1) f_K(Aᵀv/|Aᵀv|) almost everywhere onS^{d−1}.
   Source: explains, §2,p.5,linear-action identities citingLutwak[Lu2]; complete normal-map computation supplied here
   Note: The specified proof is examined here; this is not a claim that the theorem originated in this source.

41. Best L² approximation by spherical polynomials
   Forf∈L²(S^{d−1}),E_N(f) is the infimum of∥f−P∥₂ over spherical polynomialsP of degree at mostN. Rates of decay ofE_N quantify polynomial approximation.
   Source: relies_on, §2,definition and uses ofU_a
   Note: The auxiliary proof norms are built from these standard finite-degree approximation errors.

42. Nikolskii inequality for spherical polynomials
   For a spherical polynomialP of degree at mostN≥1 onS^{d−1}, ∥P∥∞≤C_dN^{(d−1)/2}∥P∥₂, with spherical area normalized to probability.
   Source: relies_on, §2,L²/sup interpolation for approximation classes
   Note: A finite-degreeL²-to-sup estimate is the background behind the approximation/interpolation tools.

43. Spherical Radon transform on spherical harmonics
   For d≥3, the normalized spherical Radon transform acts on degree-k harmonics by zero for oddk and by(−1)^{k/2}[1·3⋯(k−1)]/[(d−1)(d+1)⋯(d+k−3)] for evenk; the degree-zero multiplier is1.
   Source: relies_on, §2,p.5
   Note: The harmonic contraction uses the exact classical eigenvalues.

44. Invertibility of the cosine transform on smooth even functions
   For d≥3, the spherical cosine transform is a bijection ofC∞ even functions onS^{d−1} onto itself, where even meansf(−u)=f(u).
   Source: relies_on, §3,signed cosine representation
   Note: Smooth support functions are inverted through the even cosine transform.

45. Smooth symmetric support functions are generalized zonoids
   An origin-symmetric convex body withC∞ support function is a generalized zonoid: h_K=Cν for a finite even signed measureν, equivalentlyK+M=L for symmetric zonoidsL,M.
   Source: relies_on, §3,paragraph before(10)
   Note: The signed generating representation is invoked for the smooth-body approximation argument.

46. Generating-measure representation theorem for symmetric zonoids
   An origin-symmetric convex body is a zonoid exactly when it has a finite even positive generating measure on the unit sphere. That generating measure is unique.
   Source: relies_on, §3,construction(10)
   Note: A positive generating density defines an actual zonoid.

47. Determinant-integral formula for zonoid volume
   For a full-dimensional symmetric zonoidZ with generating measureµ defined byh_Z(u)=∫|u·v|dµ(v), |Z|=(2ᵈ/d!)∫⋯∫|det(v₁,…,v_d)|dµ(v₁)⋯dµ(v_d).
   Source: relies_on, §3,polynomial-volume claim after(10)
   Note: The determinant integral makes volume polynomial in the generating density parameter.

48. Weil’s formula for bounded curvature densities of projection bodies
   For d≥3 and a convex bodyK with bounded surface-area densityf_K relative to unnormalized spherical area, ΠK has density f_ΠK(u)=(1/(d−1)!)∫_{(S^{d−1}∩u⊥)^{d−1}}det_{u⊥}(v₁,…,v_{d−1})²∏_jf_K(v_j)dℋ^{d−2}(v_j), for almost everyu.
   Source: relies_on, §2 and§3
   Note: The multilinear determinant operator is the existing named curvature formula, not a new concept for the local symbolQ.

49. Curvature density under an invertible linear map
   ForA∈GL(d) and a convex bodyK with surface-area densityf_K, f_AK(v)=|detA|²|Aᵀv|^(−d−1) f_K(Aᵀv/|Aᵀv|) almost everywhere onS^{d−1}.
   Source: relies_on, §2,p.5
   Note: The change of coordinates for the curvature density is an explicitly imported theorem.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (108)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Absolute continuity of measures [definition]: A measure ν is absolutely continuous with respect to μ on the same σ-algebra if μ(A)=0 implies ν(A)=0.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Area measure on the Euclidean unit sphere [definition]: On S^{d−1}⊂ℝᵈ, spherical area is the restriction of (d−1)-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Its probability normalization divides by its total mass.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Boundary of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The boundary of A is its closure with its interior removed.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for complex inner-product spaces [theorem]: In a complex inner-product space, |⟨u,v⟩|²≤⟨u,u⟩⟨v,v⟩ for all u,v.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Compactly supported smooth test function on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A smooth function on an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ whose support is a compact subset of Ω; these form C_c^∞(Ω).
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex Hilbert space [definition]: A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex inner-product space [definition]: A complex vector space with a positive definite Hermitian form ⟨x,y⟩, linear in x and conjugate-linear in y. Its induced norm is ∥x∥=√⟨x,x⟩.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Composition of functions [definition]: For f:A→B and g:B→C, the composite g∘f:A→C sends x to g(f(x)).
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex subset of ℝᵈ [definition]: C⊆ℝᵈ is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and 0≤t≤1.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Determinant of a square matrix [definition]: For an n×n matrix A over a commutative ring, det A=∑_{σ∈Sₙ}sgn(σ)∏_{i=1}ⁿA_{i,σ(i)}, with the 0×0 determinant equal to one.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Finite signed measure [definition]: A differenceµ=µ₁−µ₂ of two finite positive measures on the sameσ-algebra. It is real-valued and countably additive.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Generating measure of an origin-symmetric zonoid [definition]: A finite even positive measureµ onS^{d−1} generating an origin-symmetric zonoidZ satisfiesh_Z(u)=∫|⟨u,v⟩|dµ(v). Even means invariance underv↦−v.
- Greatest lower bound [definition]: For a subset A of a partially ordered set, its greatest lower bound is a lower bound of A lying above every lower bound of A, when one exists.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Hausdorff measure of a metric space [definition]: For s≥0, the s-dimensional Hausdorff measure is the limit as δ↓0 of the infimum of normalized sums of diameters^s over countable covers by sets of diameter at most δ.
- Homogeneous real polynomial [definition]: A real polynomial in finitely many variables whose nonzero monomials all have the same total degree.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Interior of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The interior of A is the union of all open subsets contained in A.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Lebesgue measure on Euclidean space [definition]: The completion of the Borel measure on ℝⁿ assigning each rectangular box the product of its side lengths.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Orthogonal projection onto a closed Hilbert subspace [definition]: For a closed subspace V of a Hilbert space H, Pᵥx is the unique v∈V for which x−v is orthogonal to V; Pᵥ is a bounded self-adjoint idempotent operator.
- Outer unit normal of a convex body [definition]: A unit vector u is an outer normal at x∈∂K if (y−x)·u≤0 for every y∈K; at boundary points with a unique such u, this gives the Gauss map.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Product measure [construction]: For σ-finite measure spaces, the product measure on the product σ-algebra assigns a measurable rectangle A×B the value μ(A)ν(B); its completion also includes subsets of product-null sets.
- Pushforward measure [definition]: For a measurable map f:X→Y and measure μ on X, the pushforward f_*μ on Y satisfies f_*μ(B)=μ(f⁻¹(B)).
- Radon–Nikodym derivative of a measure [definition]: A nonnegative measurable function f representing ν relative to μ by ν(A)=∫_A f dμ for every measurable A; it is defined up to μ-almost-everywhere equality when such a density exists.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real polynomial in finitely many variables [definition]: A finite real linear combination of monomials x₁^{a₁}⋯x_d^{a_d} with nonnegative integer exponents.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Sign of a permutation [definition]: The sign of a permutation of {1,…,n} is (−1) to the number of inversions, where an inversion is a pair i<j with σ(i)>σ(j).
- Smooth function on a Euclidean sphere [definition]: A scalar function onS^{d−1} is smooth if it is the restriction of aC∞ function on a neighborhood of the sphere inℝᵈ.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Spherical cosine transform [definition]: For an integrable functionf onS^{d−1}, Cf(u)=∫_{S^{d−1}}|⟨u,v⟩|f(v)dσ(v), using probability-normalized spherical area. The same formula definesCµ for finite signed measuresµ.
- Spherical polynomial [definition]: The restriction toS^{d−1} of a real polynomial in d variables; its degree is the least possible total degree of an extending polynomial.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric group of a finite set [definition]: The group of all bijections of a finite set to itself under composition.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- Weak partial derivative of a locally integrable function [definition]: A locally integrable v is the ith weak derivative of u on an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ if ∫Ω u ∂ᵢφ=−∫Ω vφ for every smooth compactly supported test function φ.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

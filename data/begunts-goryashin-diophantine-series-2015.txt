REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (18)

- Almost-everywhere value of the irrationality exponent: Lebesgue-almost every real θ is irrational and has irrationality exponent two.
- Binary expansion of a nonnegative number: For a nonnegative real number, a representation x=∑_kb_k2ᵏ with digits b_k∈{0,1}; integers use finitely many nonnegative powers, while fractional parts may use a convergent series of negative powers. Dyadic fractional values use the terminating convention when uniqueness is needed.
- Comparison test for nonnegative real series: If 0≤aₙ≤bₙ, convergence of ∑bₙ implies convergence of ∑aₙ; divergence of ∑aₙ implies divergence of ∑bₙ.
- Congruence modulo an integer: The relation a≡b (mod m) means that m divides a−b.
- Convergence criterion for a real p-series: For p∈ℝ, the nonnegative series ∑ₙ≥1n^(−p) converges exactly when p>1.
- Irrationality exponent of e: The base e=exp(1) of the natural logarithm has irrationality exponent two.
- Leading singularity of a real polylogarithm at one: For each real s<1, Li_s(z)∼Γ(1−s)(log(1/z))^(s−1) as z→1 from below.
- Logarithmic real power-series identity: For 0≤z<1, ∑_(n≥1)zⁿ/n=−log(1−z).
- Lower bound two for the irrationality exponent: Every irrational real number θ has irrationality exponent μ(θ)≥2.
- Mean value theorem for real functions: If f is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), there exists c∈(a,b) such that f(b)−f(a)=f′(c)(b−a).
- Monotone convergence theorem for Lebesgue integrals: If 0≤fₙ≤fₙ₊₁ and fₙ→f pointwise, then ∫fₙdμ increases to ∫f dμ, allowing infinity.
- Principal real arccosine: The principal real arccosine arccos:[−1,1]→[0,π] is the inverse of the restriction of cosine to [0,π].
- Reciprocal invariance of the irrationality exponent: For every nonzero irrational real θ, μ(1/θ)=μ(θ), including infinite values.
- Roth’s theorem on irrationality exponents: Every irrational real number algebraic over ℚ has irrationality exponent two.
- Salikhov’s upper bound for the irrationality exponent of π: Put ψ(x)=Γ′(x)/Γ(x) for x>0 and χ=5−ψ(2/5)+ψ(1/3)+log5−(5/2)log2. Let t₃ be the positive real root and t₁ the upper-half-plane root of5t³−381t²−1525t−1875=0, and put h(t)=|t²+6t+25|³|t|^(3/2)/|25−t|⁵. Then the irrationality exponent ofπ satisfies μ(π)≤1−(χ+logh(t₃))/(χ+logh(t₁)), whose right-hand side is approximately7.606308520711.
- Sum and convergence of a real geometric series: For a real r with |r|<1, the geometric series ∑ₙ≥0rⁿ converges absolutely and has sum 1/(1−r).
- Taylor’s theorem with Lagrange remainder: If f has n continuous derivatives on an interval and an (n+1)st derivative between a and x, then f(x)=∑_{j=0}ⁿf⁽ʲ⁾(a)(x−a)ʲ/j!+f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾(ξ)(x−a)ⁿ⁺¹/(n+1)! for some ξ between a and x.
- Wallis power-integral bounds: There are constants c,C>0 such that for every integer n≥1, c/√n≤∫₀^(π/2)sinⁿx dx≤C/√n; the identical bounds hold with cosine in place of sine.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (27)

1. Irrationality exponent of an irrational real number
   For an irrational real θ, μ(θ) is the supremum of real μ for which 0<|θ−p/q|<q^(−μ) has infinitely many solutions in integers p and positive integers q; the value may be infinite.
   Source: relies_on, p. 2; §3
   Note: Controls spacings of large trigonometric terms.

2. Sum and convergence of a real geometric series
   For a real r with |r|<1, the geometric series ∑ₙ≥0rⁿ converges absolutely and has sum 1/(1−r).
   Source: relies_on, §§1–3
   Note: Geometric tails in residue classes and dyadic bounds.

3. Convergence criterion for a real p-series
   For p∈ℝ, the nonnegative series ∑ₙ≥1n^(−p) converges exactly when p>1.
   Source: relies_on, pp. 1–3; §§1–4
   Note: Absolute convergence for α>1, rational cases and summability threshold.

4. Comparison test for nonnegative real series
   If 0≤aₙ≤bₙ, convergence of ∑bₙ implies convergence of ∑aₙ; divergence of ∑aₙ implies divergence of ∑bₙ.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–4
   Note: Comparison of positive tails and grouped contributions.

5. Absolute convergence of a real series
   A real series ∑aₙ converges absolutely if ∑|aₙ| is finite; it converges conditionally if ∑aₙ converges and ∑|aₙ| diverges.
   Source: relies_on, throughout
   Note: Absolute versus conditional convergence is the target distinction.

6. Real sine function
   The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
   Source: relies_on, throughout
   Note: The source studies powers of sin(πnθ).

7. Real cosine function
   For real t, cos(t) is the real part of exp(it).
   Source: relies_on, throughout
   Note: The source studies powers of cos(πnθ).

8. π
   π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
   Source: relies_on, throughout
   Note: Specifies the periodic arguments.

9. Congruence modulo an integer
   The relation a≡b (mod m) means that m divides a−b.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2
   Note: Residue-class decompositions and parity cases.

10. Leading singularity of a real polylogarithm at one
   For each real s<1, Li_s(z)∼Γ(1−s)(log(1/z))^(s−1) as z→1 from below.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 3.2; §3
   Note: Explicitly imported asymptotic in the proof of Theorem 3.

11. Monotone convergence theorem for Lebesgue integrals
   If 0≤fₙ≤fₙ₊₁ and fₙ→f pointwise, then ∫fₙdμ increases to ∫f dμ, allowing infinity.
   Source: relies_on, §4
   Note: Interchange of sum and integral of absolute terms.

12. Mean value theorem for real functions
   If f is continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b), there exists c∈(a,b) such that f(b)−f(a)=f′(c)(b−a).
   Source: relies_on, §5, p. 10
   Note: Control perturbations of partial sums.

13. Taylor’s theorem with Lagrange remainder
   If f has n continuous derivatives on an interval and an (n+1)st derivative between a and x, then f(x)=∑_{j=0}ⁿf⁽ʲ⁾(a)(x−a)ʲ/j!+f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾(ξ)(x−a)ⁿ⁺¹/(n+1)! for some ξ between a and x.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–3
   Note: First-order power estimates and arccos behavior.

14. Roth’s theorem on irrationality exponents
   Every irrational real number algebraic over ℚ has irrationality exponent two.
   Source: relies_on, p. 2; footnote p. 1
   Note: Used for algebraic-irrational corollaries, not used in the main spacing proof.

15. Irrationality exponent of e
   The base e=exp(1) of the natural logarithm has irrationality exponent two.
   Source: relies_on, p. 2
   Note: Used in the numerical e example.

   Put ψ(x)=Γ′(x)/Γ(x) for x>0 and χ=5−ψ(2/5)+ψ(1/3)+log5−(5/2)log2. Let t₃ be the positive real root and t₁ the upper-half-plane root of5t³−381t²−1525t−1875=0, and put h(t)=|t²+6t+25|³|t|^(3/2)/|25−t|⁵. Then the irrationality exponent ofπ satisfies μ(π)≤1−(χ+logh(t₃))/(χ+logh(t₁)), whose right-hand side is approximately7.606308520711.
   Source: relies_on, pp. 2–3
   Note: Used in the numerical sine(n) and cosine(n) examples.

17. Almost-everywhere value of the irrationality exponent
   Lebesgue-almost every real θ is irrational and has irrationality exponent two.
   Source: relies_on, p. 3
   Note: Used in the metric deduction of Theorem 4, independent of its analytic proof.

18. Irrationality exponent of an irrational real number
   For an irrational real θ, μ(θ) is the supremum of real μ for which 0<|θ−p/q|<q^(−μ) has infinitely many solutions in integers p and positive integers q; the value may be infinite.
   Source: explains, p. 2
   Note: Definition or statement and argument inspected; exposition does not settle priority.

19. Rational-parameter classification of trigonometric power series
   Let θ=p/q be in lowest terms with q>0 and 0<α≤1. The cosine-power series ∑cosⁿ(πnθ)/nᵅ converges conditionally if p,q are odd and otherwise diverges to +∞. The sine-power series converges absolutely if q is odd, conditionally if q≡2 mod 4, and diverges to +∞ if 4 divides q.
   Source: explains, Theorems 1–2; §§1–2
   Note: Definition or statement and argument inspected; exposition does not settle priority.

20. Irrationality-exponent criterion for trigonometric power series
   If θ is irrational with finite irrationality exponent μ and α>1−1/(2μ−2), then both ∑sinⁿ(πnθ)/nᵅ and ∑cosⁿ(πnθ)/nᵅ converge absolutely.
   Source: explains, Theorem 3; §3
   Note: Definition or statement and argument inspected; exposition does not settle priority.

21. Almost-everywhere convergence of trigonometric power series
   For α>1/2, both ∑sinⁿ(πnθ)/nᵅ and ∑cosⁿ(πnθ)/nᵅ converge absolutely for Lebesgue-almost every real θ.
   Source: explains, Theorem 4; §4
   Note: Definition or statement and argument inspected; exposition does not settle priority.

22. Dense irrational parameters of trigonometric power-series divergence
   There is a dense set E of irrational real numbers such that for every θ∈E and every α≤1 both ∑sinⁿ(πnθ)/nᵅ and ∑cosⁿ(πnθ)/nᵅ diverge.
   Source: explains, Theorem 5; §5
   Note: Definition or statement and argument inspected; exposition does not settle priority.

23. Lower bound two for the irrationality exponent
   Every irrational real number θ has irrationality exponent μ(θ)≥2.
   Source: relies_on, p.2; §3
   Note: Justifies the μ≥2 range used in the spacing exponent.

24. Reciprocal invariance of the irrationality exponent
   For every nonzero irrational real θ, μ(1/θ)=μ(θ), including infinite values.
   Source: relies_on, p.2 corollary; Lemma 3.1 p.6
   Note: Transfers approximation bounds to 1/θ.

25. Wallis power-integral bounds
   There are constants c,C>0 such that for every integer n≥1, c/√n≤∫₀^(π/2)sinⁿx dx≤C/√n; the identical bounds hold with cosine in place of sine.
   Source: relies_on, §4 p.8
   Note: Explicit recalled bound in the independent metric proof.

26. Principal real arccosine
   The principal real arccosine arccos:[−1,1]→[0,π] is the inverse of the restriction of cosine to [0,π].
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 3.1 p.6
   Note: Inverts cosine near its maxima.

27. Logarithmic real power-series identity
   For 0≤z<1, ∑_(n≥1)zⁿ/n=−log(1−z).
   Source: relies_on, §3 p.7
   Note: Treats the α=1 dyadic contribution.

28. Binary expansion of a nonnegative number
   For a nonnegative real number, a representation x=∑_kb_k2ᵏ with digits b_k∈{0,1}; integers use finitely many nonnegative powers, while fractional parts may use a convergent series of negative powers. Dyadic fractional values use the terminating convention when uniqueness is needed.
   Source: relies_on, §5 pp.9–10
   Note: Specifies the sparse dyadic construction.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (74)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Algebraic element of a field extension [definition]: An element α of L/F is algebraic over F if a nonzero polynomial in F[x] vanishes at α.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Asymptotic equivalence [definition]: The assertion f∼g along a specified limit means f(x)/g(x)→1, where g is nonzero near that limit. Sequences are the instance x=n→∞.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complex exponential function [definition]: The entire function exp(z)=∑ₙ₌₀∞zⁿ/n!; on imaginary arguments exp(it)=cos(t)+i sin(t).
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Complex power series [definition]: A series ∑ₙ₌₀∞aₙ(z−z₀)ⁿ with complex coefficients, considered at points where its partial sums converge.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Digamma function on the positive real axis [definition]: For x>0, the real digamma function is ψ(x)=Γ′(x)/Γ(x), the logarithmic derivative of the positive-real gamma function.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Field extension [definition]: An inclusion of one field into another.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Gamma function on the positive real axis [definition]: For s>0, Γ(s)=∫₀∞e^(−t)t^(s−1)dt, with the integral understood as an improper real integral.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Improper Riemann integral [definition]: An improper Riemann integral over an open endpoint or an unbounded interval is a limit of integrals over compact subintervals; with two improper endpoints both limits must exist separately.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Lebesgue measure on Euclidean space [definition]: The completion of the Borel measure on ℝⁿ assigning each rectangular box the product of its side lengths.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Polynomial over a field [definition]: A formal finite sum a₀+a₁x+⋯+aₙxⁿ with coefficients in a field, with the usual coefficientwise addition and multiplication.
- Polynomial ring over a commutative ring [definition]: The ring R[X₁,…,X_n] of formal finite sums of monomials with coefficients in R, with coefficientwise addition and the usual multiplication of monomials.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Rational number [definition]: A real x is rational if x=a/b for integers a,b with b≠0; otherwise it is irrational.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real polylogarithm inside the unit interval [definition]: For s∈ℝ and 0<z<1, Li_s(z)=∑ₙ≥1zⁿ/nˢ; this defines the real restriction of the polylogarithm by its convergent power series.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Riemann integral [definition]: A bounded f:[a,b]→ℝ has Riemann integral I if all tagged Riemann sums approach I as the maximum length of the partition intervals tends to zero.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Tagged Riemann sum [definition]: For a partition a=x₀<⋯<x_n=b and tags t_i∈[x_{i−1},x_i], the Riemann sum of f is ∑ᵢf(t_i)(x_i−x_{i−1}); the partition mesh is maxᵢ(x_i−x_{i−1}).
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (6)

1. Tychonoff’s theorem
   An arbitrary product of compact topological spaces, with the product topology, is compact.
   Source: explains, Appendix C, Theorem C.20
   Note: Selected proof exposition; not an origin claim.

2. Finite-intersection characterization of compactness
   A topological space is compact if and only if every family of closed subsets with the finite intersection property, meaning every finite subfamily has nonempty intersection, has nonempty total intersection.
   Source: explains, Appendix C, Proposition C.7
   Note: Statement located.

3. Spectral theorem for bounded self-adjoint operators
   A bounded self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space has a unique projection-valued spectral measure, supported on its real spectrum, representing the operator and its bounded Borel functional calculus.
   Source: explains, Theorem 9.16 and proof, pp. 316–319 (normal case), with Corollary 9.20(1)
   Note: The self-adjoint theorem is the special case of the normal one.

4. Sobolev inequality on Euclidean space
   For1≤p<d and p*=dp/(d−p), every u∈W¹,ᵖ(ℝᵈ) satisfies ‖u‖_{p*}≤C_{d,p}‖∇u‖_p.
   Source: explains, Lemma 11.38 and Theorem 11.39(1) with proofs, pp.405–406
   Note: Inspected textbook proof; not a priority claim.

5. Separability of continuous functions on a compact metric space
   For a compact metric space K, the Banach space C(K) with supremum norm is separable.
   Source: explains, Proposition 2.8, p.42
   Note: C(K) separable iff K metrisable; the metrisable ⇒ separable direction inspected for cone-085.

6. Riesz representation theorem for Hilbert-space functionals
   Every bounded linear functional ℓ on a real or complex Hilbert space H is uniquely of the form x↦⟨x,y⟩ for some y∈H, with ‖ℓ‖=‖y‖; the inner product is linear in its first argument, so in the complex case y depends conjugate-linearly on ℓ.
   Source: explains, §3.3, Theorem 3.15 and proof, pp. 103–104
   Note: Exposition and proof over K=ℝ or ℂ: isometry from Cauchy–Schwarz; surjectivity from a unit vector in N(φ)^⊥ (Theorem 3.13). Not an origin claim.

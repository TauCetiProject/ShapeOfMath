LOCATED CONCEPTS (2)

1. Interlacing and positive partial fractions
   If p,q are monic real polynomials of degrees d,d−1 with real roots weakly interlacing, then after cancellation q/p has only simple real poles and a partial-fraction expansion∑_jc_j/(z−r_j), with c_j≥0. A denominator root of multiplicity m cancels at least m−1 times. Conversely such a ratio gives weak interlacing after reinserting common real factors.
   Source: explains, Lemma 1.20(1) with proof and Remark 1.21, pp.13–14
   Note: Exposition of the strict case and a continuity remark for the weak case; not a priority claim.

2. Interlacing of real-rooted polynomials
   The real zeros of degree-n and degree-(n−1) real-rooted polynomials interlace if, when listed with multiplicity, α₁≤β₁≤α₂≤⋯≤β_{n−1}≤α_n.
   Source: explains, §1.1, (1.1.1) and relaxed inequalities, p.2
   Note: Definition of strict and weak interlacing; not a priority claim.

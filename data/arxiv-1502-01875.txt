REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (10)

- Atomicity of Radon measures on scattered compacta: Every finite Radon measure on a compact Hausdorff scattered space is purely atomic; the same holds for finite signed regular measures via total variation.
- Bounded linear extension operator for continuous functions: For a closed subspace L of a compact Hausdorff space K, a bounded linear operator T:C(L)→C(K) is an extension operator if (Tf)|L=f for every f∈C(L).
- Free-set theorem for finite-valued set mappings: For n∈ℕ, |X|≥ℵ_n and S:[X]^n→[X]^{<ω}, there is A∈[X]^{n+1} such that a∉S(A minus {a}) for every a∈A.
- Fréchet–Urysohn property of finite-subset compacta: For every set X and n<∞, σ_n(2^X) is a compact Hausdorff Fréchet–Urysohn space.
- Orthonormal coordinates for a real Hilbert space: Every real Hilbert space H is linearly isometric to ℓ²(X;ℝ) for an orthonormal basis indexed by some set X; the coordinates of h are its inner products with the basis vectors.
- Real Stone–Weierstrass theorem: A unital real subalgebra of the continuous real functions on a compact Hausdorff space is uniformly dense if it separates points.
- Riesz–Markov representation on a compact space: Every positive linear functional on the continuous real functions on a compact Hausdorff space is integration against a unique finite regular Borel measure.
- Signed Riesz–Markov representation on a compact Hausdorff space: Every bounded real linear functional on C(K), K compact Hausdorff, is integration against a unique finite signed regular Borel measure μ; its operator norm is |μ|(K).
- Weak topology on a normed vector space: The weak topology on a normed space X is the coarsest topology making every bounded linear functional X→ℝ or ℂ continuous.
- Weak-star topology on a continuous dual: The weak-star topology σ(X*,X) on the continuous dual of a normed space X is the topology of pointwise convergence on X.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (20)

1. Bounded linear extension operator for continuous functions
   For a closed subspace L of a compact Hausdorff space K, a bounded linear operator T:C(L)→C(K) is an extension operator if (Tf)|L=f for every f∈C(L).
   Source: relies_on, §1
   Note: Central operator notion.

2. Compact space of subsets with bounded finite cardinality
   For a set X and n∈ℕ, σ_n(2^X) is the subspace of {0,1}^X consisting of characteristic functions of subsets of X of cardinality at most n, with the product topology.
   Source: relies_on, §1–4
   Note: Underlying compact spaces.

3. Aleph sequence of infinite cardinals
   The alephs enumerate infinite well-ordered cardinalities: ℵ₀ is countable infinity, ℵ_{α+1} is the least larger infinite cardinal, and at a limit ordinal λ, ℵ_λ=sup_{α<λ}ℵ_α.
   Source: relies_on, §1–4
   Note: Cardinal thresholds and ω₁ ordering.

4. Weak-star topology on a continuous dual
   The weak-star topology σ(X*,X) on the continuous dual of a normed space X is the topology of pointwise convergence on X.
   Source: relies_on, §2–4
   Note: Adjoint measure-valued extensions.

5. Riesz–Markov representation on a compact space
   Every positive linear functional on the continuous real functions on a compact Hausdorff space is integration against a unique finite regular Borel measure.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: Measure representation; signed extension required.

6. Scattered topological space
   A topological space is scattered if every nonempty subspace has an isolated point.
   Source: relies_on, §1–3
   Note: Atomicity of Radon measures on finite-subset spaces.

7. Fréchet–Urysohn space
   A topological space is Fréchet–Urysohn if, whenever x lies in the closure of A, there is a sequence in A converging to x.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma3.5,Theorem4.1
   Note: Sequential continuity criterion.

8. Free-set theorem for finite-valued set mappings
   For n∈ℕ, |X|≥ℵ_n and S:[X]^n→[X]^{<ω}, there is A∈[X]^{n+1} such that a∉S(A minus {a}) for every a∈A.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma2.1,Theorem3.7
   Note: Imported high-cardinality combinatorics.

9. Real Stone–Weierstrass theorem
   A unital real subalgebra of the continuous real functions on a compact Hausdorff space is uniformly dense if it separates points.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem3.10,Lemma5.2
   Note: Finite-coordinate approximation.

10. Weak topology on a normed vector space
   The weak topology on a normed space X is the coarsest topology making every bounded linear functional X→ℝ or ℂ continuous.
   Source: relies_on, §5
   Note: Hilbert weak balls.

11. Real Hilbert space
   A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
   Source: relies_on, §5
   Note: Nonseparable Hilbert coordinates.

12. Extension norms between finite-subset compacta at ℵ₁
   For 1≤m<n and |X|=ℵ₁, η(σ_m(2^X),σ_n(2^X))=2n−2m+1.
   Source: introduces, Theorem4.1
   Note: Exact small-cardinality norm computation.
   Origin evidence: Checked introduction contrast between high-cardinality canonical norm and improved ω₁ construction; proof supplies both bounds; prior [Ma] is credited only for existence, not this optimum.

13. Nonexistence of extension operators between distinct weak Hilbert balls
   If H is a nonseparable real Hilbert space and 0<λ<μ, there is no bounded linear extension operator C(λB_H)→C(μB_H), where both balls carry the weak topology.
   Source: introduces, Theorem1.2;§5
   Note: Stronger than known nonexistence of a continuous retraction.
   Origin evidence: Checked §1 explicitly identifies Corson–Lindenstrauss [CL,Proposition1] as non-retraction, then states the stronger linear-extension obstruction and proves it in §5.

14. Signed Riesz–Markov representation on a compact Hausdorff space
   Every bounded real linear functional on C(K), K compact Hausdorff, is integration against a unique finite signed regular Borel measure μ; its operator norm is |μ|(K).
   Source: relies_on, §2;Theorems3.7,4.1
   Note: The adjoint identifies C(K)* with signed Radon measures, not just positive functionals.

15. Atomicity of Radon measures on scattered compacta
   Every finite Radon measure on a compact Hausdorff scattered space is purely atomic; the same holds for finite signed regular measures via total variation.
   Source: relies_on, §1,p2;Theorem3.7
   Note: The measure-valued extension estimates rely on atomic signed Radon measures.

16. Fréchet–Urysohn property of finite-subset compacta
   For every set X and n<∞, σ_n(2^X) is a compact Hausdorff Fréchet–Urysohn space.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma3.5;Theorem4.1
   Note: Sequential continuity suffices on the finite-subset compacta.

17. Orthonormal coordinates for a real Hilbert space
   Every real Hilbert space H is linearly isometric to ℓ²(X;ℝ) for an orthonormal basis indexed by some set X; the coordinates of h are its inner products with the basis vectors.
   Source: relies_on, §5
   Note: Pass from a nonseparable Hilbert ball to coordinate ℓ²(X).

18. Extension norms between finite-subset compacta at large cardinals
   For 1≤m<n and |X|≥ℵ_{n−1}, η(σ_m(2^X),σ_n(2^X))=∑_{k=0}^m binom(n,k)binom(n−k−1,m−k).
   Source: introduces, Corollary3.8
   Note: Exact high-cardinality extension norm.
   Origin evidence: Checked introduction contrast between cardinal regimes, canonical construction proof, finite-restriction approximation and sharpness conclusion. The earlier Marciszewski reference is credited for existence only; priority of the canonical construction itself remains separately unresolved.

19. Uniqueness of natural finite-subset extension families
   Fix1≤m<n. There is exactly one familyT_X:C(σ_m(2^X))→C(σ_n(2^X)) indexed by finite setsX, consisting of linear extension operators uniformly bounded inX, and natural under every injectionu:Y→X via pullbackf(χ_A)↦f(χ_{u⁻¹(A)}).
   Source: introduces, Theorem3.10
   Note: A universal characterization of the natural uniformly bounded extension family.
   Origin evidence: The paragraph precedingTheorem3.10 announces that it will justify canonical/natural status, followed by a new proof using the source finite-restriction approximation. No earlier citation claims this characterization. Priority of an explicit extension construction is not asserted: the retained contribution is its proved uniqueness property.

20. One-sided free sequence for a finite-valued set mapping
   If X is uncountable, n≥1 and S maps finite subsets of X to finite subsets of X, there are distinct z₁,…,z_n such that z_j∉S({z_i:i∈I}) whenever I⊂{1,…,n} is nonempty and j<min I or j>max I.
   Source: explains, Lemma2.3
   Note: The exact finite sequence lemma used in the sharpℵ₁ lower bound; no unverified original-priority claim for this auxiliary lemma.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (74)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Associative algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Cardinality of a set [definition]: The size of a set, with two sets having the same cardinality when there is a bijection between them. A set has finite cardinality n when it is in bijection with {1,…,n}; infinite cardinalities are distinguished by the same bijection criterion.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Continuous dual of a normed vector space [definition]: The vector space X* of bounded linear maps X→its scalar field, with norm ‖ℓ‖=sup_{‖x‖≤1}|ℓ(x)|.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Finite signed measure [definition]: A differenceµ=µ₁−µ₂ of two finite positive measures on the sameσ-algebra. It is real-valued and countably additive.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Hausdorff topological space [definition]: A topological space in which any two distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Initial topology for a family of maps [definition]: For maps f_i:X→Y_i into topological spaces, the initial topology on X is generated by the sets f_i⁻¹(U), where U is open in Y_i; it is the coarsest topology making every f_i continuous.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Locally compact Hausdorff space [definition]: A Hausdorff topological space in which every point has a neighbourhood with compact closure.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Operator norm of a bounded linear operator [definition]: For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Ordinal number [definition]: An ordinal is a transitive set whose membership relation is a well ordering; ordinals represent order types of well-ordered sets.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Purely atomic finite measure [definition]: A finite measure is purely atomic if it is a countable sum ∑_j a_jδ_{x_j} of positive point masses, with ∑_j a_j<∞; finite signed measures are purely atomic when their total variation is.
- Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space [definition]: A Borel measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space that is finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets (each has measure equal to the infimum over open supersets) and inner regular on open sets (each has measure equal to the supremum over compact subsets). This is the Folland–Cohn convention; on σ-compact spaces it coincides with compact inner regularity on all Borel sets.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real ℓ² space indexed by an arbitrary set [definition]: For a set X, ℓ²(X;ℝ) consists of families a_x with sup_{F⊂X finite}∑_{x∈F}|a_x|²<∞, with norm given by the square root of that supremum and the corresponding sum inner product.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Space of continuous real functions on a compact Hausdorff space [definition]: For a compact Hausdorff space K, C(K) is the real Banach space of continuous functions K→ℝ with norm ||f||∞=max_K|f|.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Subspace topology [definition]: For A⊆X, the topology whose open sets are A∩U for U open in X.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Uniform convergence of functions into a metric space [definition]: A sequence fₙ:X→(Y,d) converges uniformly to f if for every ε>0, d(fₙ(x),f(x))<ε for every x∈X once n is sufficiently large independently of x.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Well-ordered set [definition]: A totally ordered set is well ordered if every nonempty subset has a least element.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

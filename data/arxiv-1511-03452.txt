REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (30)

- Apartment containment from a Weyl-isometric chamber set: In a thick affine building with its maximal apartment system, any set of chambers Weyl-isometric to a subset of a model apartment is contained in an apartment.
- Automorphic norm decay on compact-open invariant vectors: Let G be a connected simply connected absolutely almost simple Q-group, S finite containing infinity, Γ a congruence lattice in G_S, and p∈S with G(Q_p) noncompact. Fix p-adic Cartan data and a maximal strongly orthogonal positive-root system R, with height η_R≥1, and a compact open K⊂G(Q_p). For0<ε<1/2 there is C_(Γ,K,ε)>0 such that ‖P_K U_gP_K‖_(L²₀(Γ∖G_S))≤C_(Γ,K,ε)η_R(g)^(−(1/2−ε)/r) for every g∈G(Q_p), where r=2 in Q_p-rank1 and r=1 otherwise, U_g is right translation and P_K is normalized Haar averaging.
- Binary Pinsker inequality: For a,b∈[0,1], the binary relative entropy D(a‖b)=a log(a/b)+(1−a)log((1−a)/(1−b)) satisfies D(a‖b)≥2(a−b)², using the usual zero/infinite endpoint conventions.
- Birkhoff pointwise ergodic theorem: For a probability-preserving measurable transformation T and f∈L¹, the averages n⁻¹∑_(j=0)^(n−1)f∘T^j converge almost surely and in L¹ to E[f|I], where I is the invariant σ-algebra; if T is ergodic this limit is the constant E[f].
- Compact-open integer submodules are local-field lattices: For a finite-dimensional vector space V over a nonarchimedean local field F, an O_F-submodule L⊂V is an O_F-lattice if and only if it is compact and open in V.
- Compactness of the integer ring of a nonarchimedean local field: If F is complete for a nontrivial discrete valuation with finite residue field, its integer ring O_F is compact and open. Every quotient O_F/π^nO_F is finite for a uniformizerπ and n≥1.
- Compressed Koopman averaging operator: For a measurable dynamical state space with conditional expectation P_Xf=E[f|F_X], its averaging operator is T=P_XU_SP_X on L²(Y), restricted when desired to L²(F_X). This is a contraction and fixes constants; the family P_XU_S^nP_X need not equal T^n.
- Determinant scaling of additive p-adic Haar volume: For A∈GL_d(Q_p), additive Haar measure v on Q_p^d and Borel E⊂Q_p^d, v(AE)=|detA|_p v(E). This is independent of the scalar normalization of v.
- Entropy is affine under ergodic decomposition: For a probability-preserving measurable T on a standard Borel probability space, let μ=∫μ_xdμ(x) be its ergodic decomposition. Then h_μ(T)=∫h_(μ_x)(T)dμ(x), allowing +∞. For every finite measurable partition ξ one likewise has h_μ(T,ξ)=∫h_(μ_x)(T,ξ)dμ(x).
- Entropy scaling under iterates and inversion: For a probability-preserving transformation T, h_μ(Tᵏ)=k h_μ(T) for every integer k≥1. If T is invertible with measurable inverse, h_μ(T⁻¹)=h_μ(T), and therefore h_μ(Tᵏ)=|k|h_μ(T) for every nonzero integer k, allowing infinite entropy.
- Finite Galois splitting of an algebraic torus: Every algebraic torus over a field becomes isomorphic to G_m^r over some finite Galois extension of that field.
- Finite semilinear Galois descent for vector spaces: If L/F is finite Galois and V is a finite-dimensional L-vector space with semilinear Gal(L/F)-action, the natural L-linear map L⊗_F V^Gal(L/F)→V, a⊗v↦av, is an isomorphism. In particular every Galois-stable L-subspace descends to the F-space of its fixed vectors.
- First Borel–Cantelli lemma: If ∑ₙP(Aₙ)<∞, then P(⋂ₘ⋃ₙ≥ₘAₙ)=0: almost surely only finitely many Aₙ occur.
- Gallery convexity of a building apartment: If chambers C,D of a thick affine building lie in an apartment A, every minimal gallery in the building from C to D is contained in A.
- Haar entropy of a semisimple homogeneous translation: Let G be a finite product of groups of real or p-adic points of connected reductive characteristic-zero algebraic groups, Γ a lattice with Haar probability m, and a a semisimple element supported in a Q_p-factor. For x↦xa⁻¹ on Γ∖G, h_m(a)=∑_(|λ|_p>1)log|λ|_p, where λ runs through the eigenvalues of Ad(a) on that factor Lie algebra, with multiplicity and the unique extended p-adic absolute value. Every invariant probability μ satisfies h_μ(a)≤h_m(a).
- Iwahori subgroup of a simply connected p-adic group: For a connected simply connected absolutely almost simple Q_p-group G of positive split rank, an Iwahori subgroup is the pointwise stabilizer of a chamber in its Bruhat–Tits building. It is compact open and all such subgroups are conjugate. In this simply connected convention the connected parahoric chamber fixer and the full pointwise chamber fixer agree.
- Local exponential coordinates for a p-adic algebraic group: Let G⊂GL_n be a connected reductive Q_p-group with Lie algebra g. On some open neighborhood U of0 in g(Q_p), the matrix exponential is an analytic bijection onto an open identity neighborhood of G(Q_p), with analytic inverse logarithm and derivative identity at0. It is Ad-equivariant where both sides are defined. After shrinking U and compatibly normalizing additive Haar measure on g and Haar measure on G, vol(expA)=vol(A) for every Borel A⊂U.
- Markov’s inequality for nonnegative random variables: If Y is a nonnegative integrable real random variable and t>0, then P(Y≥t)≤E[Y]/t.
- One-point compactification of a locally compact space: For a noncompact locally compact Hausdorff space Y, its one-point compactification is Y∪{∞}, whose opens are the open subsets of Y together with complements of compact closed subsets of Y. It is compact Hausdorff; if Y is second countable it is metrizable.
- Proper metrization of a discrete–compact double quotient: Let G be a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff group, Γ a closed discrete subgroup, and K a compact subgroup. The double quotient Γ∖G/K is second countable and has a compatible proper metric; this applies in particular to Γ∖G for a lattice Γ.
- Properness of a compact-group quotient: If a compact topological group K acts continuously on a Hausdorff space Y, the orbit quotient map q:Y→Y/K is closed and has compact fibers; in particular q⁻¹(C) is compact for every compact C⊆Y/K.
- Reduced-word criterion for building galleries: A non-stuttering gallery in a thick affine building is minimal if and only if its panel-type word is reduced in the model Coxeter system. Consequently its endpoint Weyl distance is the product of that reduced word.
- Sector of an affine building: A sector in an apartment is a translate v+C of a closed Weyl chamber C of its finite root system; v is its vertex. Sectors have opposite direction when their chamber cones are C and−C.
- Semisimple elements lie in rational maximal tori: If G is a connected reductive algebraic group over a characteristic-zero field F and a∈G(F) is semisimple, then some F-defined maximal torus T⊂G contains a.
- Shannon–McMillan–Breiman theorem for a finite partition: For an invertible ergodic probability-preserving transformation S and finite measurable partition ξ, −(2n+1)⁻¹logm((∨_(i=−n)^nS^(-i)ξ)(y)) converges almost surely to h_m(S,ξ), where the expression uses the atom containing y.
- Special vertex of an affine building: A vertex v in an apartment is special if its stabilizer in the affine Weyl group maps onto the full finite Weyl group under the linear-part homomorphism. Equivalently every apartment wall direction has a parallel wall through v; the condition is independent of the chosen apartment.
- Struble’s proper left-invariant metrization theorem: Every second-countable locally compact Hausdorff group has a compatible proper left-invariant metric.
- Uniform local injectivity of a discrete quotient on compact sets: Let Γ be a discrete subgroup of a locally compact Hausdorff group G and q:G→Γ∖G. For every compact Ω⊆Γ∖G there is an open identity neighborhood V⊆G such that q is injective on gV whenever q(g)∈Ω.
- Wall criterion for minimal galleries in an affine Coxeter complex: In an affine Coxeter complex, gallery distance between chambers equals the number of walls separating them. A gallery is minimal exactly when it crosses each separating wall once and no other wall. A chamber E lies on some minimal gallery from C to D exactly when no wall separates E from both C and D.
- Weak convergence of probability measures on a metric space: Measures μ_n converge weakly to μ if ∫f dμ_n→∫f dμ for every bounded continuous real function f on the space.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (63)

1. Compressed Koopman averaging operator
   For a measurable dynamical state space with conditional expectation P_Xf=E[f|F_X], its averaging operator is T=P_XU_SP_X on L²(Y), restricted when desired to L²(F_X). This is a contraction and fixes constants; the family P_XU_S^nP_X need not equal T^n.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: The L² compression/conditional expectation setup is used throughout.

2. Relative entropy of finite probability distributions
   D(p∥q)=∑_ωp(ω)ln(p(ω)/q(ω)), with a positive p(ω) and zero q(ω) giving +∞ and zero p terms contributing zero.
   Source: relies_on, §3
   Note: The rate is binary relative entropy with natural logarithms.

3. Markov’s inequality for nonnegative random variables
   If Y is a nonnegative integrable real random variable and t>0, then P(Y≥t)≤E[Y]/t.
   Source: relies_on, §3.2; §5.2
   Note: Exponential tail and packing estimates use Markov.

4. Binary Pinsker inequality
   For a,b∈[0,1], the binary relative entropy D(a‖b)=a log(a/b)+(1−a)log((1−a)/(1−b)) satisfies D(a‖b)≥2(a−b)², using the usual zero/infinite endpoint conventions.
   Source: relies_on, §6, Theorem6.7
   Note: Converts binary entropy inequalities to expectation deviations.

5. Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy of a probability-preserving transformation
   For a probability-preserving transformation S and finite measurable partition ξ, set h_m(S,ξ)=lim_(n→∞)n⁻¹H_m(∨_(i=0)^(n−1)S^(-i)ξ), equivalently the infimum over n by entropy subadditivity. Its measure entropy is h_m(S)=sup_ξh_m(S,ξ), possibly infinite.
   Source: relies_on, §5–§7
   Note: All entropy comparisons use measure entropy.

6. Shannon–McMillan–Breiman theorem for a finite partition
   For an invertible ergodic probability-preserving transformation S and finite measurable partition ξ, −(2n+1)⁻¹logm((∨_(i=−n)^nS^(-i)ξ)(y)) converges almost surely to h_m(S,ξ), where the expression uses the atom containing y.
   Source: relies_on, AppendixA
   Note: Imported typical finite-partition entropy theorem.

7. Birkhoff pointwise ergodic theorem
   For a probability-preserving measurable transformation T and f∈L¹, the averages n⁻¹∑_(j=0)^(n−1)f∘T^j converge almost surely and in L¹ to E[f|I], where I is the invariant σ-algebra; if T is ergodic this limit is the constant E[f].
   Source: relies_on, AppendixA
   Note: Imported bad-set visit-frequency theorem.

8. First Borel–Cantelli lemma
   If ∑ₙP(Aₙ)<∞, then P(⋂ₘ⋃ₙ≥ₘAₙ)=0: almost surely only finitely many Aₙ occur.
   Source: relies_on, AppendixA
   Note: Summable exceptional-set argument.

9. Lattice in a locally compact group
   A lattice in a locally compact group G is a discrete subgroup Γ for which Γ\G carries a finite nonzero right G-invariant measure; normalize it to probability. It is cocompact if Γ\G is compact.
   Source: relies_on, §4.1.1
   Note: The homogeneous quotient carries invariant probability.

10. Haar measure on a locally compact group
   A nonzero Radon measure on a locally compact group, i.e. a Borel measure finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets and inner regular on open sets, invariant under left translation: μ(gA)=μ(A). A right Haar measure is defined analogously with right translations.
   Source: relies_on, §4–§7; AppendixB
   Note: Compact-subgroup averaging and homogeneous quotient volume.

11. Field of p-adic numbers
   For a prime p, ℚ_p is the completion of ℚ for the p-adic absolute value |x|_p=p^{-v_p(x)}.
   Source: relies_on, §4.1.2; AppendixB
   Note: The non-archimedean local field isQ_p.

12. Connected reductive algebraic group
   A smooth connected affine algebraic group whose largest connected normal unipotent subgroup over an algebraic closure is trivial.
   Source: relies_on, §4.1.2; AppendixB
   Note: Arithmetic applications use reductiveQ-groups and their local forms.

13. Weak convergence of probability measures on a metric space
   Measures μ_n converge weakly to μ if ∫f dμ_n→∫f dμ for every bounded continuous real function f on the space.
   Source: relies_on, §6
   Note: Probability-preserving limits use weak measure convergence; §7 instead requires vague/subprobability convergence.

14. Metric space
   A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
   Source: relies_on, §6 proof approximations
   Note: Continuous approximation of open/closed indicators is used on the metrizable quotient.

   A probability-preserving system with state σ-algebra F_X has the Markov module property if there is a closed U_S-invariant subspace M⊆L²(Y), containing L²(F_X) and stable under multiplication by every bounded F_X-measurable function, such that P_XU_SP_Xf=P_XU_Sf for every f∈M.
   Source: explains, Definition2.6

- Absolute value on a field [definition]: A map |·|:K→R_{≥0} that vanishes exactly at zero, is multiplicative and satisfies |x+y|≤|x|+|y|. It is nonarchimedean if the stronger bound |x+y|≤max(|x|,|y|) holds.
- Abstract simplicial complex [definition]: An abstract simplicial complex on a vertex set V is a family of finite subsets of V closed under taking subsets and containing every singleton. Members are simplices, their subsets are faces, and a simplex with n+1 vertices has dimension n. An isomorphism is a vertex bijection preserving simplices in both directions.
- Adele ring of a number field [definition]: The restricted product A_F=∏′_v F_v of all completions of a number field,where almost every finite component lies in the valuation ring O_v.
- Adelic points of an algebraic group [definition]: For an algebraic group G over a number field F,the restricted product G(A_F)=∏′_v G(F_v),using integral compact subgroups at almost all finite places.
- Adjoint representation of an algebraic group [definition]: The algebraic representation Ad:G→GL(Lie G) obtained by differentiating the conjugation maps h↦ghg⁻¹ at the identity.
- Affine Coxeter complex of a crystallographic root system [construction]: For an irreducible reduced finite crystallographic root system Φ, the affine Coxeter complex is the Euclidean simplicial tessellation cut out by hyperplanes ⟨x,α⟩=k for α∈Φ,k∈ℤ. Its maximal cells are alcoves, and its walls are the reflecting hyperplanes. The affine Weyl group acts simply transitively on alcoves; labeling one alcove’s vertices by opposite walls and propagating by this action gives its vertex types.
- Affine Weyl group of a reduced crystallographic root system [construction]: For a reduced finite crystallographic root system Φ in E, its affine Weyl group is generated by s_(α,k)(x)=x−(⟨x,α⟩−k)α∨, α∈Φ,k∈ℤ. Here α∨=2α/⟨α,α⟩; equivalently W_aff=Q∨⋊W with coroot lattice Q∨ and finite Weyl group W.
- Affine algebraic group over a field [definition]: An affine algebraic group over a field F is specified by a finitely generated commutative Hopf F-algebra A: for each commutative F-algebra R its group of R-points is Hom_(F-alg)(A,R), with multiplication induced by the coproduct. Equivalently it is a finite-type affine group scheme over F.
- Affine algebraic variety over a field [definition]: An affine algebraic variety is specified here by a finitely generated reduced commutative algebra A over a field F. Its k-points, for extensions k/F, are the F-algebra homomorphisms A→k. Morphisms are induced by algebra homomorphisms in the opposite direction. Reducible affine varieties are allowed.
- Affine map between real vector spaces [definition]: A map of the form x↦Ax+b, where A is linear and b is fixed.
- Algebraic character of an affine algebraic group [definition]: A morphism of algebraic groups G→G_m over the specified field. Characters form an abelian group by pointwise multiplication.
- Algebraic closure of a field [definition]: An algebraic extension of a field that is itself algebraically closed. This specifies an object, without including the existence or uniqueness-up-to-isomorphism theorem.
- Algebraic element of a field extension [definition]: An element α of L/F is algebraic over F if a nonzero polynomial in F[x] vanishes at α.
- Algebraic representation of an affine algebraic group [definition]: A morphism of algebraic groups G→GL(V) for a finite-dimensional vector space V; equivalently an action with regular matrix coefficient functions. A faithful representation identifies G with a closed algebraic subgroup of GL(V).
- Algebraic torus [definition]: An algebraic group that becomes isomorphic over an algebraic closure to a finite product of copies of the multiplicative group.
- Algebraically closed field [definition]: A field in which every nonconstant polynomial has a root.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Almost-sure convergence of real random variables [definition]: Xₙ→X almost surely means P({ω:limₙXₙ(ω)=X(ω)})=1.
- Analytic manifold over a local field [definition]: A Hausdorff topological space with charts to open subsets of K^n, for a local field K, whose transition functions are locally given by convergent K-power series. Over R this means real analytic, over C complex analytic, and over a nonarchimedean field its usual convergent power-series notion.
- Apartment of an affine building [definition]: An apartment is one of the distinguished subcomplexes in an affine building’s apartment system, with its Euclidean affine-Coxeter realization. The maximal apartment system consists of every subcomplex isomorphic to the model apartment in the building-theoretic sense.
- Associative algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity.
- Automorphism of a group [definition]: A bijective group homomorphism from a group to itself; automorphisms form a group under composition.
- Base change of an affine algebraic group [definition]: For a field extension L/F and an affine F-group with coordinate Hopf algebra A, its base change to L has coordinate Hopf algebra L⊗_F A.
- Basis of a free module [definition]: A family (e_i) in a module over a commutative ring such that every element is a unique finite linear combination of the e_i with coefficients in that ring. A module with such a family is free.
- Basis of a vector space [definition]: A linearly independent family of vectors whose linear span is the whole vector space.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel subgroup of a connected affine algebraic group [definition]: Over an algebraically closed field, a maximal smooth connected solvable closed subgroup. Over F, a subgroup is an F-Borel subgroup if its base change to an algebraic closure is such a Borel subgroup.
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Cardinality of a set [definition]: The size of a set, with two sets having the same cardinality when there is a bijection between them. A set has finite cardinality n when it is in bijection with {1,…,n}; infinite cardinalities are distinguished by the same bijection criterion.
- Cartan decomposition for a simply connected p-adic group [theorem]: Let G be a connected simply connected absolutely almost simple Q_p-group of positive split rank. There exist a compact open vertex stabilizer U⊂G(Q_p), a maximal Q_p-split torus A, a positive system of nonmultipliable relative roots, and finite Ω⊂C_(G(Q_p))(A(Q_p)) such that G(Q_p)=U A⁺ΩU, where A⁺={a∈A(Q_p):|α(a)|_p≥1 for every positive relative root α}. A fixed Cartan parametrization chooses the A⁺ and Ω components of each U-double coset.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for complex inner-product spaces [theorem]: In a complex inner-product space, |⟨u,v⟩|²≤⟨u,u⟩⟨v,v⟩ for all u,v.
- Center of an affine algebraic group [definition]: The closed subgroup Z(G) whose elements commute with every element after every extension of the ground algebra. Over a characteristic-zero field it is equivalently the closed subgroup whose geometric points form the center of G over an algebraic closure.
- Centralizer of a subgroup in a group [definition]: For H≤G, its centralizer is C_G(H)={g∈G:gh=hg for every h∈H}. It is a subgroup of G; this is distinct from the normalizer, which need only preserve H under conjugation.
- Chamber of an affine building [definition]: A chamber of an affine building is a maximal simplex; an alcove of an apartment is a chamber in that apartment.
- Closed subgroup of an affine algebraic group [definition]: A subgroup represented by the quotient of its ambient group’s coordinate Hopf algebra by a Hopf ideal.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative Hopf algebra over a field [definition]: A Hopf algebra over a field whose underlying algebra multiplication is commutative; its coproduct need not be cocommutative.
- Commutative algebra over a commutative ring [definition]: A commutative ring A with a specified unital ring homomorphism R→A; algebra homomorphisms commute with these structure maps.
- Commutative algebra over a field [definition]: A unital associative algebra over a field whose multiplication is commutative, equivalently a commutative ring equipped with a unital homomorphism from that field.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Commutator subgroup [definition]: The subgroup [G,G] generated by all commutators xyx⁻¹y⁻¹ with x,y∈G.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete discretely valued field [definition]: A field with a discrete valuation v which is complete for the metric defined by any absolute value c^(−v(x)) with c>1, extended by |0|=0.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Completion of a field with an absolute value [definition]: The metric completion of K for d(x,y)=|x−y|, with the field operations extended continuously. A completion is regarded as containing K through its canonical isometric embedding.
- Completion of a metric space [definition]: A complete metric space containing an isometric dense copy of the given metric space. It is unique up to an isometry fixing that copy.
- Complex Hilbert space [definition]: A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex inner-product space [definition]: A complex vector space with a positive definite Hermitian form ⟨x,y⟩, linear in x and conjugate-linear in y. Its induced norm is ∥x∥=√⟨x,x⟩.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Composition of functions [definition]: For f:A→B and g:B→C, the composite g∘f:A→C sends x to g(f(x)).
- Conditional expectation given a σ-algebra [definition]: For integrable X and a sub-σ-algebra G, E[X|G] is a G-measurable integrable random variable satisfying ∫_AE[X|G]dP=∫_AXdP for every A∈G, defined up to almost-sure equality.
- Connected component of a topological space [definition]: A maximal connected subset, with connectedness understood in the subspace topology.
- Connected semisimple algebraic group [definition]: A connected semisimple algebraic group over a field is a connected reductive algebraic group whose center is finite after base change to an algebraic closure.
- Connected topological space [definition]: A topological space that is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex subset of ℝᵈ [definition]: C⊆ℝᵈ is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and 0≤t≤1.
- Coroot of a root [definition]: In a finite root system with specified invariant inner product, the coroot associated to α is α∨=2α/(α,α), identified with the corresponding vector in the dual space via the inner product.
- Coxeter system [definition]: A Coxeter system (W,S) is a group generated by involutions S with only relations (st)^(m_st)=1, where m_ss=1 and m_st=m_ts∈{2,3,…,∞} for s≠t, with∞ imposing no relation. Equivalently any assignment of S to involutions in another group satisfying these relations extends uniquely to a homomorphism from W.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Derivation of an algebra [definition]: A linear map D:A→A on an associative algebra over a field satisfying D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b).
- Determinant of a square matrix [definition]: For an n×n matrix A over a commutative ring, det A=∑_{σ∈Sₙ}sgn(σ)∏_{i=1}ⁿA_{i,σ(i)}, with the 0×0 determinant equal to one.
- Diagonalizable linear endomorphism [definition]: A linear endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space admitting a basis of eigenvectors over its ground field. Diagonalizability over an algebraic closure is specified separately.
- Dimension of an affine variety [definition]: For an affine variety with reduced finitely generated coordinate algebra A, its dimension is the Krull dimension of A. Its points over an extension K/k are the k-algebra maps A→K; reducible varieties are allowed.
- Discrete subgroup of a locally compact group [definition]: A subgroup whose induced topology is discrete; equivalently the identity is isolated in the subgroup.
- Discrete valuation on a field [definition]: A surjective map v:K×→Z satisfying v(xy)=v(x)+v(y) and v(x+y)≥min(v(x),v(y)), extended by v(0)=+∞.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Divisibility of integers [definition]: An integer a divides b if b=ac for some integer c.
- Double coset of two subgroups [definition]: For subgroups H,K⊂G, the double coset HgK={hgk:h∈H,k∈K}; H∖G/K denotes the partition into these subsets.
- Eigenvalue of a linear endomorphism [definition]: A scalar λ for which a linear endomorphism T has a nonzero vector v with Tv=λv.
- Entropy of a finite measurable partition [definition]: For a finite measurable partition ξ of a probability space, H_m(ξ)=−∑_(A∈ξ)m(A)logm(A), with0log0=0. The join ξ∨η consists of the nonempty intersections of their atoms.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Ergodic measure-preserving transformation [definition]: A probability-preserving transformation T is ergodic if every measurable set A with μ(T⁻¹A△A)=0 has μ(A) equal0 or1.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Expectation of an integrable real random variable [definition]: If E abs(X)=∫abs(X)dP<∞, the expectation of X is EX=∫X dP.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Field embedding [definition]: An injective homomorphism of fields preserving the multiplicative identity.
- Field extension [definition]: An inclusion of one field into another.
- Finite Galois field extension [definition]: A finite Galois extension L/k is a field extension of finite vector-space dimension which is both normal and separable. Its automorphisms fixing k form the finite Galois group Gal(L/k).
- Finite crystallographic root system [definition]: A finite spanning set Φ of nonzero vectors in a real inner-product space, preserved by the orthogonal reflection s_α in each root α, and satisfying2(β,α)/(α,α)∈Z for all α,β∈Φ. Nonreduced systems are allowed; reduced means the only multiples of α in Φ are ±α. A simple system is a basis whose root expansions are all nonnegative or all nonpositive integer combinations.
- Finite field [definition]: A field whose underlying set is finite.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Finite-dimensional vector space [definition]: A vector space having a finite basis.
- Finitely generated commutative algebra [definition]: An R-algebra A generated as an algebra by finitely many elements; equivalently a quotient of R[X₁,…,X_n] for some finite n.
- Finitely presented commutative algebra [definition]: An R-algebra isomorphic to R[X₁,…,X_n]/(f₁,…,f_m) for finite n,m.
- Formal power series over a commutative ring [definition]: An expression ∑a_α X^α with coefficients in a commutative ring and multi-indices α in N^n, added coefficientwise and multiplied by the finite Cauchy sums defining each coefficient, without any assertion of analytic convergence.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Galois group of a finite Galois extension [definition]: The group of field automorphisms of the extension that fix the base field pointwise.
- General linear group over a commutative ring [definition]: The group GL_n(R) of square matrices over a commutative ring R having a two-sided matrix inverse, with matrix multiplication.
- Geometrically connected affine algebraic group [definition]: An affine F-group whose coordinate algebra after extension to an algebraic closure has no idempotents other than 0 and 1. This is the coordinate-algebra account of connectedness after base change.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Group action on a set [definition]: A map G×X→X, written (g,x)↦gx, satisfying ex=x and (gh)x=g(hx).
- Group homomorphism [definition]: A map f:G→H satisfying f(xy)=f(x)f(y) for all x,y.
- Hausdorff topological space [definition]: A topological space in which any two distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods.
- Homeomorphism [definition]: A bijection between topological spaces that is continuous and has a continuous inverse.
- Homomorphism of algebras over a field [definition]: A linear map of unital associative algebras over the same field preserving multiplication and the multiplicative identity.
- Homomorphism of commutative rings [definition]: A map between commutative rings preserving addition, multiplication and the multiplicative identity.
- Homomorphism of modules over a commutative ring [definition]: An additive map f:M→N of modules over the same commutative ring satisfying f(rm)=rf(m).
- Hopf algebra [definition]: A unital associative algebra H with coassociative coproduct Δ:H→H⊗H and counit ε:H→k that are algebra homomorphisms, and an antipode S satisfying m(S⊗id)Δ=m(id⊗S)Δ=ηε, where m and η are multiplication and unit.
- Hopf ideal of a commutative Hopf algebra [definition]: An ideal I of a commutative Hopf algebra H satisfying Δ(I)⊂I⊗H+H⊗I, ε(I)=0 and S(I)⊂I, so the quotient inherits the Hopf operations.
- Ideal of a commutative ring [definition]: An additive subgroup I of a commutative ring R such that ri belongs to I for every r in R and i in I.
- Idempotent element of an algebra [definition]: An element e satisfying e²=e.
- Identity component of an affine algebraic group [definition]: The connected component containing the identity, with its induced closed subgroup structure. In coordinate terms over an algebraic closure it is the factor of the coordinate algebra selected by the primitive idempotent whose value at the identity is1; this component descends to the ground field.
- Index of a subgroup [definition]: For a subgroup H≤G, the index [G:H] is the cardinality of the set of left cosets gH. Left and right cosets have the same cardinality by inversion.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Irreducible polynomial [definition]: A nonzero nonunit polynomial over a specified coefficient ring that cannot be written as a product of two nonunits. Over a field, both factors would have smaller positive degree.
- Koopman operator of a probability-preserving transformation [construction]: For a probability-preserving transformation S, its Koopman operator on L^p,1≤p≤∞, is U_Sf=f∘S. It is an isometry; for invertible S with measurable inverse it is unitary on complex L².
- Krull dimension of a commutative ring [definition]: The Krull dimension of a commutative ring R is the supremum of lengths r of strictly increasing chains 𝔭₀⊊⋯⊊𝔭_r of prime ideals. For the zero ring, which has no prime ideals, use dimension−1.
- Lattice in a vector space over a nonarchimedean local field [definition]: For a finite-dimensional vector space V over a nonarchimedean local field F, an O_F-lattice is an O_F-submodule generated by an F-basis of V. Equivalently it is a finite-rank free O_F-submodule whose F-span is V; this is distinct from a discrete lattice in a real vector space.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Left-invariant metric on a group [definition]: A metric d on a group G is left invariant if d(gx,gy)=d(x,y) for all g,x,y∈G. On a topological group it is compatible if its metric topology equals the given topology.
- Lie algebra of an affine algebraic group [definition]: For a coordinate Hopf algebra A with counit ε, the F-vector space of linear maps D:A→F satisfying D(ab)=ε(a)D(b)+ε(b)D(a). Extend them to invariant derivations using the coproduct; their commutator induces the Lie bracket.
- Lie algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with a bilinear bracket [x,y] satisfying [x,x]=0 and [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear representation of a group [definition]: A group homomorphism from a group G to the invertible linear maps of a vector space, equivalently a linear action of G.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Linearly independent family of vectors [definition]: A family of vectors is linearly independent if every finite linear combination equalling zero has all coefficients zero.
- Local field [definition]: A nondiscrete locally compact Hausdorff topological field. Both archimedean and nonarchimedean local fields are allowed.
- Local-field topology on points of an affine algebraic group [definition]: For a local field F and an affine embedding G⊂A^n, give G(F) the subspace topology from F^n. Polynomial coordinate maps show this topology is independent of the chosen affine embedding.
- Localization of a commutative ring [definition]: For a multiplicatively closed subset S of a commutative ring R containing 1, S⁻¹R consists of fractions r/s, identifying r/s with r′/s′ when t(rs′−r′s)=0 for some t in S, with the usual fraction operations.
- Locally compact Hausdorff space [definition]: A Hausdorff topological space in which every point has a neighbourhood with compact closure.
- Locally compact group [definition]: A topological group whose underlying space is locally compact and Hausdorff.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Maximal algebraic torus [definition]: A maximal torus of an affine algebraic group G over a field F is an F-subgroup T that is an algebraic torus and is maximal among such subgroups; equivalently, T becomes a maximal torus of G after extension to an algebraic closure. Maximality concerns algebraic tori, not only F-split tori.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measurable state space of a dynamical system [construction]: For a probability-preserving transformation S of (Y,F,m), a state space is a measurable map π:Y→X to a measurable space. It carries π_*m and is identified inside Y with the sub-σ-algebra π⁻¹F_X; S need not induce a map on X.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Measure-preserving transformation [definition]: A measurable map T from a measure space (Ω,F,μ) to itself preserves μ if μ(T⁻¹A)=μ(A) for every measurable A.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Minimal polynomial of an algebraic element [definition]: The unique monic polynomial of least positive degree over F vanishing at an algebraic element α; it generates the ideal of all polynomials over F vanishing at α.
- Module over a commutative ring [definition]: An abelian group M with an action of a commutative ring R satisfying r(m+n)=rm+rn, (r+s)m=rm+sm, (rs)m=r(sm), and 1m=m. Unlike a vector space, its scalar ring need not be a field.
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Morphism of affine algebraic groups [definition]: A homomorphism defined by regular coordinate functions; in coordinate algebras it is a homomorphism of commutative Hopf algebras in the opposite direction, preserving coproduct, counit and antipode.
- Multiplicative algebraic group [definition]: The affine F-group G_m with R-points R×, represented by F[t,t⁻¹], whose coproduct sends t to t⊗t.
- Multiplicity of a polynomial root [definition]: The multiplicity of a root a of a polynomial P is the largest m≥1 for which (x−a)^m divides P. A root of multiplicity1 is simple.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nilpotent element of a ring [definition]: An element a such that a^n=0 for some positive integer n.
- Nilpotent ideal of a commutative ring [definition]: An ideal I for which I^n=0 for some positive integer n, meaning every product of n elements of I is zero.
- Nonarchimedean local field [definition]: A field complete for a nontrivial discrete valuation and having a finite residue field.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normal algebraic field extension [definition]: An algebraic field extension L/k is normal if every irreducible polynomial in k[X] with a root in L splits into linear factors over L. Equivalently, the minimal polynomial over k of every element of L splits over L.
- Normal subgroup [definition]: A subgroup H of G satisfying gHg⁻¹=H for every g∈G.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Number field [definition]: A finite extension of ℚ.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Operator norm of a bounded linear operator [definition]: For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Orthogonal projection onto a closed Hilbert subspace [definition]: For a closed subspace V of a Hilbert space H, Pᵥx is the unique v∈V for which x−v is orthogonal to V; Pᵥ is a bounded self-adjoint idempotent operator.
- Panel of an affine building [definition]: A panel is a codimension-one face of a chamber. Two distinct chambers are adjacent if they share a panel; its type is the vertex label omitted by that panel.
- Parabolic subgroup of a reductive algebraic group [definition]: A closed algebraic subgroup P of a connected reductive F-group G such that, after base change to an algebraic closure, P contains a Borel subgroup of G. Equivalently G/P is projective.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Partition of a set [definition]: A family of nonempty pairwise disjoint subsets whose union is the set.
- Place of a number field [definition]: An equivalence class of nontrivial absolute values on a number field; its completion is denoted F_v. Archimedean and nonarchimedean places give its infinite and finite places.
- Pointwise stabilizer of a set under a group action [definition]: For a group action G on X and A⊆X, its pointwise stabilizer is {g∈G:ga=a for every a∈A}, a subgroup of G. This is distinct from merely preserving A as a set.
- Polish space [definition]: A topological space admitting a complete separable metric that induces its topology.
- Polynomial over a field [definition]: A formal finite sum a₀+a₁x+⋯+aₙxⁿ with coefficients in a field, with the usual coefficientwise addition and multiplication.
- Polynomial ring over a commutative ring [definition]: The ring R[X₁,…,X_n] of formal finite sums of monomials with coefficients in R, with coefficientwise addition and the usual multiplication of monomials.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Prime ideal of a commutative ring [definition]: A proper ideal P of a commutative ring R such that ab in P implies a in P or b in P.
- Prime number [definition]: An integer p>1 is prime if its only positive divisors are 1 and p.
- Probability kernel between measurable spaces [definition]: A probability kernel from(S,Σ) to(E,ℰ) assigns a probability measure K(s,·) onE to each s∈S, with s↦K(s,A) Σ-measurable for every A∈ℰ. A random probability measure is such a kernel with a probability space as its domain.
- Probability mass function on a finite set [definition]: A function p from a finite set Ω to [0,1] satisfying ∑_{ω∈Ω}p(ω)=1; the probability of A⊆Ω is ∑_{ω∈A}p(ω).
- Probability space [definition]: A probability space is (Ω,𝔽,P), where 𝔽 is a σ-algebra on Ω and P is a measure on 𝔽 with P(Ω)=1.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Proper metric space [definition]: A metric space is proper if every closed bounded ball is compact.
- Pushforward measure [definition]: For a measurable map f:X→Y and measure μ on X, the pushforward f_*μ on Y satisfies f_*μ(B)=μ(f⁻¹(B)).
- Quotient Haar measure for unimodular groups [definition]: For a closed unimodular subgroup H of a unimodular locally compact group G with fixed Haar measures, the right G-invariant measure on H∖G characterized by ∫_G f(g)dg=∫_(H∖G)∫_H f(hg)dh d[g] for compactly supported continuous f.
- Quotient of a commutative ring by an ideal [definition]: For an ideal I of R, the cosets R/I form a ring under (r+I)+(s+I)=r+s+I and (r+I)(s+I)=rs+I.
- Quotient topology [definition]: For a surjection q:X→Y, a subset U⊆Y is open exactly when q⁻¹(U) is open in X.
- Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space [definition]: A Borel measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space that is finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets (each has measure equal to the infimum over open supersets) and inner regular on open sets (each has measure equal to the supremum over compact subsets). This is the Folland–Cohn convention; on σ-compact spaces it coincides with compact inner regularity on all Borel sets.
- Rational number [definition]: A real x is rational if x=a/b for integers a,b with b≠0; otherwise it is irrational.
- Rational points of an affine algebraic group [definition]: For an affine F-group G with coordinate Hopf algebra A and commutative F-algebra R, the group G(R)=Hom_(F-alg)(A,R), with group multiplication induced by the coproduct of A.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real-valued random variable [definition]: A real-valued random variable is a measurable map from a probability space to ℝ with its Borel σ-algebra.
- Reduced commutative ring [definition]: A commutative ring whose only nilpotent element is0.
- Reflection in a linear hyperplane in Euclidean space [definition]: For a unit vector u, reflection in u⊥ is R_u(x)=x−2(x·u)u.
- Reflection in an affine Euclidean hyperplane [definition]: For a unit vector u and c∈ℝ, reflection in {x:⟨x,u⟩=c} is x↦x−2(⟨x,u⟩−c)u. It is the affine isometry fixing that hyperplane pointwise and reversing its normal direction.
- Residue field of a discretely valued field [definition]: The quotient O_v/m_v of the valuation ring by its maximal ideal.
- Restricted product of topological groups [definition]: For groups G_i with chosen open subgroups K_i, the group of tuples g_i with g_i∈K_i for all but finitely many i, with the topology whose basic opens are products of open sets equal to K_i outside finitely many coordinates.
- Roots of a reductive group relative to a split torus [definition]: For a maximal split torus T of a connected reductive group G, the nontrivial characters occurring as weights in Ad on Lie G are the relative roots. A choice of minimal parabolic containing T specifies the positive roots; the simple roots are the positive roots not expressible as a sum of two positive roots.
- Second-countable topological space [definition]: A topological space is second countable if it has a countable basis: a sequence of open sets such that every open set is their union over some index set.
- Semidirect product of groups [definition]: Given a homomorphism α:G→Aut(H), the group H⋊_αG has underlying set H×G and multiplication (h,g)(h′,g′)=(hα(g)(h′),gg′).
- Semilinear Galois action on a vector space [definition]: For a finite Galois extension L/F with group Γ, a semilinear Γ-action on an L-vector space V is an additive group action satisfying σ(av)=σ(a)σ(v) for a∈L,v∈V. The fixed vectors form an F-vector space V^Γ.
- Semisimple element of an affine algebraic group [definition]: An element whose image in a faithful finite-dimensional algebraic representation is diagonalizable over an algebraic closure. This property is independent of the faithful representation; that independence is a theorem, rather than an additional part of the definition.
- Separable algebraic field extension [definition]: An algebraic extension L/F is separable if the minimal polynomial over F of every element of L has distinct roots in an algebraic closure.
- Separable metric space [definition]: A metric space having a countable dense subset.
- Separable polynomial over a field [definition]: A polynomial having no repeated root in an algebraic closure of its coefficient field.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Shannon entropy of a finite distribution [definition]: For a finite probability mass function p, H(p)=−∑_ωp(ω)ln p(ω), with 0ln0=0.
- Sign of a permutation [definition]: The sign of a permutation of {1,…,n} is (−1) to the number of inversions, where an inversion is a pair i<j with σ(i)>σ(j).
- Small p-adic matrix exponential and logarithm [theorem]: For matrices over Q_p with the maximum-entry norm, the series expX=∑_(n≥0)Xⁿ/n! and log(I+X)=∑_(n≥1)(−1)^(n+1)Xⁿ/n converge when ‖X‖<p^(−1/(p−1)), and define inverse analytic isometries between that open ball about0 and the corresponding ball aboutI. Conjugation commutes with these series whenever the displayed arguments lie in the domains; exp is not asserted to be a homomorphism on noncommuting matrices.
- Smooth affine algebraic group [definition]: An affine algebraic group over F whose coordinate algebra is smooth over F. In the finite-type characteristic-zero case all affine group schemes are smooth, but that assertion is a separate theorem.
- Smooth finitely presented commutative algebra [definition]: A finitely presented R-algebra A such that, for every R-algebra B with nilpotent ideal I, every R-algebra map A→B/I lifts to A→B. This is the infinitesimal lifting formulation of smoothness for affine finite-presentation algebras.
- Solvable group [definition]: A group whose iterated commutator subgroups reach the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps.
- Solvable smooth affine algebraic group [definition]: A smooth affine algebraic group whose group of points over an algebraic closure is solvable. For the characteristic-zero groups used here this agrees with termination of the algebraic derived series.
- Split algebraic torus [definition]: An algebraic F-group isomorphic over F to a finite product of copies of G_m. An F-torus need only become such a product after extending F to an algebraic closure.
- Standard Borel measurable space [definition]: A measurable space is standard Borel if it is measurably isomorphic to a Borel subset of a Polish space, with the induced Borel σ-algebra. Only the measurable structure, rather than a chosen compatible topology, is part of this definition.
- Subgroup [definition]: A subset of a group containing the identity and closed under multiplication and inverses.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Subspace topology [definition]: For A⊆X, the topology whose open sets are A∩U for U open in X.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Symmetric group of a finite set [definition]: The group of all bijections of a finite set to itself under composition.
- Tensor product of commutative algebras [definition]: For commutative R-algebras A,B, the module A⊗_R B with multiplication (a⊗b)(a′⊗b′)=aa′⊗bb′ and unit 1⊗1; it is the coproduct of A and B among commutative R-algebras.
- Tensor product of modules over a commutative ring [definition]: For R-modules M,N, the R-module M⊗_R N with a bilinear map (m,n)↦m⊗n through which every R-bilinear map M×N→P factors uniquely by an R-linear map.
- Tensor product of vector spaces [definition]: The vector space V⊗W with its universal bilinear map from V×W.
- Topological field [definition]: A field with a topology making addition, multiplication, additive inversion and inversion on the nonzero elements continuous.
- Topological group [definition]: A group with a topology for which multiplication G×G→G and inversion G→G are continuous.
- Topological ring [definition]: A ring with a topology for which addition, multiplication and additive inversion are continuous; the rings here are commutative with identity.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Uniformizer of a discretely valued field [definition]: For a normalized discrete valuation v on K, a uniformizer is an element π∈K with v(π)=1; it generates the maximal ideal of the valuation ring.
- Unimodular locally compact group [definition]: A locally compact group whose left Haar measures are also right invariant.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Unipotent affine algebraic group [definition]: An affine algebraic group that, after extension to an algebraic closure, has a faithful algebraic representation with all matrices upper triangular and every diagonal entry 1.
- Unitary operator on a complex Hilbert space [definition]: A surjective complex linear map preserving the inner product.
- Valuation ring of a discretely valued field [definition]: For a discrete valuation v on K, the ring O_v={x:v(x)≥0}; its maximal ideal is m_v={x:v(x)>0}.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- Wall of an affine building apartment [definition]: A wall in an apartment is a reflecting affine hyperplane of its affine Coxeter complex, regarded with its induced subcomplex. Its two closed half-spaces are half-apartments.
- Weight of a split torus representation [definition]: For an algebraic representation ρ of a split torus T, a character α:T→G_m is a weight if V_α={v:ρ(t)v=α(t)v after every algebra extension} is nonzero. V_α is its weight space.
- Weyl chamber of a finite root system [definition]: For a finite root system Φ in E, an open Weyl chamber is a connected component of E∖⋃_(α∈Φ)α⊥; its closure is a closed Weyl chamber, a simplicial cone after choosing simple roots. Opposite chambers are negatives of one another.
- Weyl group of a finite root system [construction]: For a finite crystallographic root system Φ in a real inner-product space, its Weyl group is the group generated by the linear reflections s_α in α⊥ for α∈Φ. It acts on Φ; since Φ spans, this action is faithful and the group is finite.
- Weyl isometry between chamber sets [definition]: A Weyl isometry (strong isometry) between sets of chambers in buildings with the same typed Coxeter model is a bijection f satisfying δ(fC,fD)=δ(C,D) for every pair of chambers in its domain.
- Word length in a Coxeter system [definition]: For a Coxeter system (W,S), ℓ(w) is the minimum length of a word in S representing w; a word of length ℓ(w) is reduced.
- p-adic valuation of a rational number [definition]: For a prime p and nonzero rational x, v_p(x) is the unique integer r such that x=p^r a/b with integers a,b not divisible by p; put v_p(0)=+∞.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

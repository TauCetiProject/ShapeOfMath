- Birkhoff pointwise ergodic theorem: For a probability-preserving measurable transformation T and f∈L¹, the averages n⁻¹∑_(j=0)^(n−1)f∘T^j converge almost surely and in L¹ to E[f|I], where I is the invariant σ-algebra; if T is ergodic this limit is the constant E[f].
- Boundary attraction without a positive Lotka–Volterra equilibrium: Let Fᵢ(x)=xᵢexp(bᵢ+∑ⱼAᵢⱼxⱼ) on the nonnegative orthant, with every positive forward orbit bounded. If Ax=−b has no strictly positive solution, then dist(Fⁿ(x),∂ℝ_+ᵈ)→0 for every strictly positive x.
- Chebyshev's inequality: If EX²<∞ and t>0, then P(abs(X−EX)≥t)≤Var(X)/t².
- Convergence to equilibrium for finite irreducible aperiodic Markov chains: Every finite irreducible aperiodic Markov chain has a unique strictly positive stationary law π, and Pⁿ(x,y)→π(y) for every pair of states, hence in total variation from every initial law.
- Existence of a QSD for a bounded nonlinear Poisson population: Let X_(t+1)=ε(N₁,…,N_d) conditionally on X_t=x, with independent Nᵢ∼Poisson(Fᵢ(x)/ε). Suppose F is continuous and bounded, Fᵢ(x)>0 for strictly positive x, and the boundary of the nonnegative orthant is absorbing. For each ε>0 this chain has a quasi-stationary distribution supported on ε{1,2,…}ᵈ, with survival eigenvalue0<λ_ε<1.
- Growth criterion for one structured population: Consider one structured stochastic population on a compact forward-invariant K containing0, over an ergodic compact metric environmental homeomorphism, with continuous nonnegative matrices of a fixed primitive zero pattern. Let r be the common top exponent of A(ω,0). If r>0, the population is stochastically persistent. If r<0 and A(ω,x)≤A(ω,0) entrywise for all ω,x, every positive population trajectory tends to0 for almost every environmental path.
- Gärtner–Ellis theorem with finite differentiable limiting cumulant: Let μ_ε be probability laws on ℝᵈ such that Λ(λ)=lim_(ε↓0)εlog∫exp(⟨λ,z⟩/ε)dμ_ε(z) exists and is finite for every λ∈ℝᵈ. If Λ is differentiable on all ℝᵈ, then μ_ε satisfies a large-deviation principle at speed1/ε with good convex rate Λ*(z)=sup_λ(⟨λ,z⟩−Λ(λ)); good means each finite sublevel set is compact.
- Invariance of a small-noise QSD limit with survival tending to one: LetS⊂ℝᵈ be closed,F:S→S continuous withboundedimage,andP_ε a smallrandomperturbation ofF withcommonabsorbingS₀ andQSDsμ_ε,survivalsλ_ε. Ifε_n↓0,μ_(ε_n) convergesweaklytoμ andλ_(ε_n)→1, thenμ isF-invariant.
- Invasion growth rate of a structured population in a stationary environment: For a stationary ergodic community/environment trajectory and an absent species i with linearized update Z_(t+1)=A_i(X_t,E_(t+1))Z_t, its invasion growth rate is the almost-sure asymptotic per-capita rate lim_(t→∞)t⁻¹log‖Z_t‖ when the positive-matrix hypotheses make it independent of the positive initial direction.
- Jensen’s inequality for expectations: For an integrable real random variable X and convex φ with E|φ(X)|<∞, φ(EX)≤Eφ(X), when φ is defined on an interval containing the range of X.
- Lyapunov continuity at uniformly supported nonnegative cocycles: Let an ergodic homeomorphism of compact metric Ω preserve Q. Let A:Ω→M_d(ℝ) be continuous and nonnegative, with a fixed zero pattern and no zero rows. Then the top exponent γ(B) of continuous nonnegative cocycles is continuous at A in uniform matrix norm, even when A is reducible. In particular, this applies at a positive diagonal cocycle and to primitive cocycles with fixed positive entry pattern.
- Morse-attractor concentration of small-noise quasi-stationary laws: Let F:S→S be continuous with bounded image on closed Euclidean S, with closed absorbing S₀ and invariant S₊=S∖S₀. Let QSDs μ_ε have kernels P_ε and survivals λ_ε. Suppose a finite continuous action ρ on S₊×S vanishes exactly at y=F(x), is uniformly positive off each fixed tracking tube, and satisfies compact-uniform lower LDP bounds. Require β_δ(ε)=sup_xP_ε(x,{y:d(y,F(x))≥δ})≤e^(−c_δ/ε) eventually, and, for every c>0, some neighborhood V of S₀ satisfies inf_(x∈V∩S₊)P_ε(x,S₀)≥e^(−c/ε) eventually. If a finite Morse decomposition separates S₀ from S₊, its non-attractor Morse sets in S₊ are transitive, and μ_ε charges every neighborhood of some positive attractor, every weak QSD limit is F-invariant and supported on the positive Morse attractors; λ_ε≥1−e^(−c/ε) eventually for some c>0.
- Open dense Ricker parameters with a hyperbolic Morse decomposition: For an open dense set of r>0, F_r(x)=x exp(r(1−x)) has a unique hyperbolic attracting periodic orbit in(0,∞), and on its compact global invariant set it admits a finite Morse decomposition consisting of that orbit, {0}, and transitive uniformly expanding invariant sets in(0,∞).
- Perron splitting of a uniformly positive matrix cocycle: Let θ be a homeomorphism of compact metric Ω and A:Ω→M_d(ℝ) continuous and nonnegative. Suppose some s≥1 makes A^(s)(ω)=A(θ^(s−1)ω)⋯A(ω) strictly positive for every ω. There are continuous positive unit vectors u(ω),v(ω) such that A(ω)u(ω) is a positive multiple of u(θω), A(ω)ker(v(ω)ᵀ)⊆ker(v(θω)ᵀ), and ℝᵈ=ℝu(ω)⊕ker(v(ω)ᵀ). Some C>0,0<α<1 give ‖A^(n)(ω)b‖≤Cαⁿ‖A^(n)(ω)u(ω)‖ for every unit b⊥v(ω), uniformly in ω and n≥0.
- Perron–Frobenius theorem for nonnegative matrices: A nonnegative real matrix has a nonnegative eigenvalue equal to its spectral radius. If it is irreducible, that eigenvalue is simple and has a strictly positive eigenvector, unique up to scalar.
- Persistence from a symmetric iid storage effect: Let Xᵢ,t+1=Xᵢ,t[Eᵢ,t+1 f(E₁,t+1X₁,t+E₂,t+1X₂,t)+s], i=1,2, with0<s<1, f positive C¹, f′<0 and f(x)→0. Let the environmental pairs be iid, exchangeable, in[e_min,M]² with e_min>0, and P(E₁≠E₂)>0. If Elog(Eᵢf(0)+s)>0, the two-species system is stochastically persistent.
- Poisson Lotka–Volterra process: With habitat scale ε>0 and an exponential discrete Lotka–Volterra map F, let population counts update conditionally independently as N_(i,t+1)|X_t∼Poisson(F_i(X_t)/ε), and set X_(i,t+1)=εN_(i,t+1). The density process lives on εℤ_(≥0)^k and coordinate extinction is absorbing.
- Positive stationary statistics for spatial Beverton–Holt dynamics: Let D be a fixed primitive nonnegative d×d matrix with Dᵀ stochastic (so D is column-stochastic), c_j>0, and E_t a stationary ergodic sequence in[a,b]ᵈ with0<a≤b<∞. For X_i,t+1=∑_jD_ijE_j,t+1X_j,t/(1+c_jX_j,t), let r be the top exponent of D diag(E_t). If r>0, on the full two-sided environmental path space there is a unique strictly positive random equilibrium X*, and every nonzero nonnegative deterministic initial state has laws converging weakly to m=law(X*) and empirical state measures converging weakly to m almost surely.
- Positive-attractor QSD survival and boundary concentration estimate: LetF:S→S becontinuous withboundedimage onclosedS⊂ℝᵈ, andP_ε smallrandomperturbations withabsorbingS₀ andQSDsμ_ε. - QSD concentration under global attraction to an absorbing boundary: Let F:S→S be continuous with bounded image on closed Euclidean S, preserving both closed S₀ and S₊=S∖S₀. Let P_ε be small random perturbations absorbed in S₀, with QSDs μ_ε. If dist(Fⁿ(x),S₀)→0 for every x∈S, every weak limit of μ_ε as ε↓0 is supported on S₀. No assumption λ_ε→1 or strict trapping neighborhood of the whole boundary is required.
- QSD survival and boundary mass from a trapping set: Letμ be aQSD with survivalλ for a kernelP absorbed inS₀. IfU⊆S₀ᶜ hasμ(U)>0 andP(x,U)≥1−β forx∈U, thenλ≥1−β. IfalsoP(x,S₀)≥a>0 forx∈V⊆S₀ᶜ, thenμ(V)≤(1−λ)/a≤β/a.
- Quasi-stationary law of an irreducible finite killed chain: LetQ be an irreducible finite substochastic matrix with0<ρ(Q)<1. Its cemetery-augmentedchain is absorbed almostsurely, and its unique quasi-stationary distribution is the positive left Perroneigenvector normalized tosum1; its one-stepsurvivalprobability isρ(Q), and its meansurvivaltime is1/(1−ρ(Q)).
- Recurrence transfer from uniform future hitting probabilities: For a time-homogeneous Markov chainX and measurable setsB,C, suppose someβ>0 satisfiesP_x(X_s∈C for somes≥1)≥β for everyx∈B. Then almost surely, ifX visitsB infinitelyoften, it also visitsC infinitelyoften.
- Uniform deviation bounds for bounded nonlinear Poisson kernels: Let F:ℝ_+ᵈ→ℝ_+ᵈ be continuous and bounded, preserving the boundary and its complement, and let P_ε be its independent-coordinate scaled Poisson kernel. The action ρ(x,y)=∑ᵢ[yᵢlog(yᵢ/Fᵢ(x))−yᵢ+Fᵢ(x)] has compact-uniform one-step lower LDP bounds for positive starting states. For every δ>0 some c_δ>0 gives sup_xP_ε(x,{y:‖y−F(x)‖∞≥δ})≤2d e^(−c_δ/ε). For every c>0 an open boundary neighborhood V has inf_(x∈V)P_ε(x,∂ℝ_+ᵈ)≥e^(−c/ε).
- Weighted boundary-equilibrium criterion for Lotka–Volterra permanence: For Fᵢ(x)=xᵢexp(bᵢ+∑ⱼAᵢⱼxⱼ), suppose there is a compact forward-invariant set absorbing all nonnegative states. If some pᵢ>0 satisfy ∑ᵢpᵢ(bᵢ+∑ⱼAᵢⱼxⱼ*)>0 at every boundary fixed point x*, then F is permanent.
- Weighted invasion criterion for structured-population persistence: For a structured stochastic population map over an ergodic compact metric environmental system (Ω,θ,Q) with θ a homeomorphism, assume a compact forward-invariant population set K and continuous Aᵢ(ω,x) with fixed primitive zero patterns. 1. Quasi-stationary distribution of an absorbing Markov chain
   For a Markov chain absorbed almost surely in A, a quasi-stationary distribution is a probability μ supported on Aᶜ and a number0<λ<1 with ∫P(x,B)dμ(x)=λμ(B) for every measurable B⊆Aᶜ. Thus the law started from μ remains μ after conditioning on survival for any positive number of steps.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1–2.2
   Note: QSD vocabulary and finite-matrix interpretation are explicitly credited to Darroch–Seneta, Barbour and later literature.

2. Small random perturbation of a discrete map
   For a continuous map F:S→S on a metric space, a family of Markov kernels P_ε is a small random perturbation if for every δ>0, sup_(x∈S)P_ε(x,{y:d(y,F(x))≥δ}) tends to0 as ε tends to0.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1
   Note: Small-noise kernels approximate a deterministic skeleton.

3. Large deviation principle at small-noise speed1/ε
   A family of probability measures μ_ε on a topological space satisfies an LDP with speed1/ε and lower semicontinuous rate I:S→[0,∞] if liminf_(ε↓0)εlogμ_ε(U)≥−inf_UI for every open U and limsup_(ε↓0)εlogμ_ε(C)≤−inf_CI for every closed C; log0=−∞.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2
   Note: The rate hypotheses require uniform kernel versions beyond this basic LDP definition.

4. Attractor of a continuous discrete map
   For a continuous map F on a metric space, an attractor is a compact invariant set A with a neighborhood U such that for every neighborhood V of A, F^n(U)⊆V for all sufficiently large n.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2
   Note: Positive attractors and the extinction attractor control QSD limits.

5. Morse decomposition of discrete dynamics
   On a compact invariant set, a Morse decomposition is a finite ordered family of disjoint isolated compact invariant sets K₁,…,K_m such that every complete orbit outside their union has its alpha limit in one K_j and omega limit in a K_i with j<i. Isolated means maximal invariant in some neighborhood.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem6; Example4
   Note: The QSD concentration theorem uses a finite Morse decomposition.

6. Ergodic stationary random sequence
   A stationary random sequence is ergodic when its induced probability measure on sequence space is ergodic under the left shift.

7. Stochastic persistence by empirical occupation near extinction
   For a stochastic population process on a compact metric state space S with closed extinction set S₀, empirical stochastic persistence means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that, from each x∉S₀, almost surely limsup_(n→∞)(n+1)⁻¹#{0≤j≤n:d(X_j,S₀)≤δ}≤ε.

8. Top Lyapunov exponent of a matrix product
   For a sequence of random matrices B_n, the top Lyapunov exponent is the limit r=lim_(n→∞)n⁻¹log‖B_n⋯B₁‖ when it exists almost surely and is constant. Existence and equality with a particular vector’s growth require additional hypotheses.

9. Birkhoff pointwise ergodic theorem

- Variance of a real random variable [definition]: If EX²<∞, then Var(X)=E[(X−EX)²].
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (12)

- ACL characterization of first-order Sobolev functions: For an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ and1≤p≤∞, f∈W¹,ᵖ(Ω) exactly when f∈Lᵖ has a representative absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of almost every coordinate-parallel line in Ω, with its almost-everywhere classical partial derivatives in Lᵖ. Those derivatives are its weak partial derivatives.
- Affine change of variables for Lebesgue integration: For an invertible real n×n matrix A, b∈ℝⁿ and nonnegative measurable or integrable g, ∫_{ℝⁿ}g(Ax+b)|detA|dx=∫_{ℝⁿ}g(y)dy.
- Bi-Lipschitz map: A bijection between metric spaces for which both the map and its inverse are Lipschitz.
- Fubini’s theorem for integrable functions: An integrable function on the product of σ-finite measure spaces has integrable almost-everywhere sections, and its product integral equals either iterated integral.
- Fundamental theorem of Lebesgue integration: If g∈L¹([a,b]), F(x)=∫_a^xg is absolutely continuous and F′=g almost everywhere. Conversely every absolutely continuous F has an integrable a.e. derivative and F(x)−F(a)=∫_a^xF′.
- Hausdorff measure of a metric space: For s≥0, the s-dimensional Hausdorff measure is the limit as δ↓0 of the infimum of normalized sums of diameters^s over countable covers by sets of diameter at most δ.
- Jacobian matrix of a differentiable Euclidean map: For f:ℝⁿ→ℝᵐ, its Jacobian matrix at a point has entries ∂f_i/∂x_j. For m=n its determinant is the Jacobian determinant.
- Jordan curve theorem: A simple closed curve in the plane separates the plane into exactly two connected components, one bounded and one unbounded, and is the boundary of both.
- Lebesgue differentiation theorem: Almost every point of a locally integrable function on ℝⁿ is a Lebesgue point.
- Mora-Corral–Pratelli smoothing of planar piecewise affine homeomorphisms: A planar piecewise affine homeomorphism in W¹,¹ can be approximated arbitrarily closely, both uniformly and in W¹,¹, by smooth diffeomorphisms, preserving boundary values when the continuous boundary extension is prescribed.
- Rank of a linear map: The dimension of the image of a linear map.
- Vitali covering lemma for balls: From a family of Euclidean balls with bounded radii one can choose pairwise disjoint balls whose fivefold concentric dilations cover the union of the original family.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (22)

1. First-order Sobolev space W¹,ᵖ
   For an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ and 1≤p≤∞, W¹,ᵖ(Ω) consists of Lᵖ functions whose first weak partial derivatives are also in Lᵖ, with norm combining the function and derivative Lᵖ norms; vector-valued versions are componentwise.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.1 and§1.2
   Note: The endpoint p=1 is the central setting; weak derivatives and their L¹ approximation are required.

2. Homeomorphism
   A bijection between topological spaces that is continuous and has a continuous inverse.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.1
   Note: Injectivity and continuity of the inverse must survive approximation.

3. Diffeomorphism between open Euclidean sets
   A bijection between open subsets of Euclidean spaces that is smooth and has a smooth inverse.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.1; end of§4.4
   Note: Smooth homeomorphic approximants, not arbitrary smooth approximants.

4. Uniform convergence of functions into a metric space
   A sequence fₙ:X→(Y,d) converges uniformly to f if for every ε>0, d(fₙ(x),f(x))<ε for every x∈X once n is sufficiently large independently of x.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.1
   Note: Uniform approximation is simultaneous with Sobolev approximation.

5. Piecewise affine homeomorphism on a planar domain
   A homeomorphism on an open subset of ℝ² that is affine on every triangle of a locally finite triangulation; the triangulation need not be finite.
   Source: relies_on, §§1.1–4.4
   Note: The main construction produces locally finitely piecewise affine maps.

6. Lebesgue differentiation theorem
   Almost every point of a locally integrable function on ℝⁿ is a Lebesgue point.
   Source: relies_on, §1.1; Lemmas4.3–4.6
   Note: Good squares are selected around Lebesgue points of Df.

7. Vitali covering lemma for balls
   From a family of Euclidean balls with bounded radii one can choose pairwise disjoint balls whose fivefold concentric dilations cover the union of the original family.
   Source: relies_on, §2 Step1
   Note: A covering selection bounds the exceptional boundary set.

8. Jordan curve theorem
   A simple closed curve in the plane separates the plane into exactly two connected components, one bounded and one unbounded, and is the boundary of both.
   Source: relies_on, §2 Steps2–4
   Note: Polygonal boundary separation and ordering of shortest paths are used.

9. Mora-Corral–Pratelli smoothing of planar piecewise affine homeomorphisms
   A planar piecewise affine homeomorphism in W¹,¹ can be approximated arbitrarily closely, both uniformly and in W¹,¹, by smooth diffeomorphisms, preserving boundary values when the continuous boundary extension is prescribed.
   Source: relies_on, End of proof of Theorem1.1, p.57; reference[27] TheoremA
   Note: This predecessor converts the constructed piecewise affine approximation into a smooth one; not a new theorem here.

10. Bi-Lipschitz map
   A bijection between metric spaces for which both the map and its inverse are Lipschitz.
   Source: relies_on, §§1,2 and Theorem4.20
   Note: Bi-Lipschitz coordinate changes reduce polygonal domains to grid domains.

11. Piecewise affine homeomorphism on a planar domain
   A homeomorphism on an open subset of ℝ² that is affine on every triangle of a locally finite triangulation; the triangulation need not be finite.
   Source: explains, §1 pp.2–3
   Note: The paper explicitly fixes locally finite triangulations, rather than only finite ones.

12. Diffeomorphic approximation of planar W¹,¹ homeomorphisms
   For an open Ω⊆ℝ², every homeomorphism f∈W¹,¹(Ω,ℝ²) admits smooth diffeomorphisms and locally finitely piecewise affine homeomorphisms arbitrarily close both uniformly and in W¹,¹. If f extends continuously to ∂Ω, approximants can have the same boundary values. On a polygonal domain, when f is piecewise affine on the boundary, the affine approximant can use a finite triangulation.
   Source: introduces, Theorem1.1;§4.4
   Note: Main reusable endpoint approximation theorem.
   Origin evidence: The introduction identifies p=1 as the case left open by Iwaniec–Kovalev–Onninen[18], whose quoted result assumes1<p<∞. Theorem1.1 and§4.4 construct precisely this missing endpoint approximation; smooth conversion is separately credited to[27].

13. Jacobian matrix of a differentiable Euclidean map
   For f:ℝⁿ→ℝᵐ, its Jacobian matrix at a point has entries ∂f_i/∂x_j. For m=n its determinant is the Jacobian determinant.
   Source: relies_on, §§3–4
   Note: Affine replacements and degenerate derivative matrices use their coordinate Jacobians.

14. Determinant of a square matrix
   For an n×n matrix A over a commutative ring, det A=∑_{σ∈Sₙ}sgn(σ)∏_{i=1}ⁿA_{i,σ(i)}, with the 0×0 determinant equal to one.
   Source: relies_on, §4,definitionM⁺/M⁰
   Note: The derivative classes are separated by determinant zero or positive.

15. Rank of a linear map
   The dimension of the image of a linear map.
   Source: relies_on, §3 and§4 degenerate matrices
   Note: The nonzero determinant-zero case is precisely rank one.

16. Hausdorff measure of a metric space
   For s≥0, the s-dimensional Hausdorff measure is the limit as δ↓0 of the infimum of normalized sums of diameters^s over countable covers by sets of diameter at most δ.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem2.1 and its proof
   Note: Boundary derivative integrals are with respect to one-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

17. ACL characterization of first-order Sobolev functions
   For an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ and1≤p≤∞, f∈W¹,ᵖ(Ω) exactly when f∈Lᵖ has a representative absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of almost every coordinate-parallel line in Ω, with its almost-everywhere classical partial derivatives in Lᵖ. Those derivatives are its weak partial derivatives.
   Source: explains, §4 grid-side selection and(4.46); complete weak-slicing proof supplied in008-proof-notes.txt
   Note: The specified proof is examined here; this is not a claim that the theorem originated in this source.

   For an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ and1≤p≤∞, f∈W¹,ᵖ(Ω) exactly when f∈Lᵖ has a representative absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of almost every coordinate-parallel line in Ω, with its almost-everywhere classical partial derivatives in Lᵖ. Those derivatives are its weak partial derivatives.
   Source: relies_on, §4.2 and(4.46)
   Note: The weak Sobolev derivative is used as the derivative of restrictions to almost all selected line segments.

19. Fundamental theorem of Lebesgue integration
   If g∈L¹([a,b]), F(x)=∫_a^xg is absolutely continuous and F′=g almost everywhere. Conversely every absolutely continuous F has an integrable a.e. derivative and F(x)−F(a)=∫_a^xF′.
   Source: relies_on, §§2–4 curve/edge estimates
   Note: Absolute-continuous side restrictions are estimated by integrals of their tangential derivatives.

20. Affine change of variables for Lebesgue integration
   For an invertible real n×n matrix A, b∈ℝⁿ and nonnegative measurable or integrable g, ∫_{ℝⁿ}g(Ax+b)|detA|dx=∫_{ℝⁿ}g(y)dy.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma4.9,proof and normalization steps
   Note: The source explicitly changes variables between endpoint/segment coordinates and planar area.

21. Fubini’s theorem for integrable functions
   An integrable function on the product of σ-finite measure spaces has integrable almost-everywhere sections, and its product integral equals either iterated integral.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma4.9 and the grid-side selection
   Note: Averages over endpoints and segments are compared with area integrals using iterated integration.

22. Planar piecewise affine extension with boundary-length control
   For the open unit square S and an injective piecewise affine g:∂S→ℝ², there is a finitely piecewise affine homeomorphism h:S→ℝ² extending continuously as g on∂S with ∫_S|Dh|≤C∫_{∂S}|Dτg|dℋ¹, for an absolute constant C. The image is the bounded polygonal region determined by the boundary curve.
   Source: introduces, Theorem2.1 andRemark2.2
   Note: Standalone reusable extension theorem; the local auxiliary grids are kept in source notes.
   Origin evidence: The introduction distinguishes the new square-extension construction and endpointW¹,¹ control from prior planar bi-Lipschitz approximation;§2 proves this standalone perimeter-to-energy bound in full. It is not attributed to the predecessor smoothing result.

23. Rank-one W¹,¹ extension estimate for a square
   For the open unit square S and M=diag(1,0), an injective piecewise affine g:∂S→ℝ² with ∫_{∂S}|Dτg−Mτ|dℋ¹<δ admits a finitely piecewise affine homeomorphic extension h with ∫_S|Dh−M|≤Cδ, provided0<δ≤δ₀ for absolute constants C,δ₀.
   Source: introduces, Theorem3.1
   Note: Standalone reusable extension theorem; the local auxiliary grids are kept in source notes.
   Origin evidence: The introduction explicitly identifies the degenerate rank-one square case as the new obstruction atp=1;§3 proves the controlled extension near diag(1,0). The estimate is independently reusable beyond its local grid notation.

24. Diffeomorphic approximation of planar W¹,¹ homeomorphisms
   For an open Ω⊆ℝ², every homeomorphism f∈W¹,¹(Ω,ℝ²) admits smooth diffeomorphisms and locally finitely piecewise affine homeomorphisms arbitrarily close both uniformly and in W¹,¹. If f extends continuously to ∂Ω, approximants can have the same boundary values. On a polygonal domain, when f is piecewise affine on the boundary, the affine approximant can use a finite triangulation.
   Source: introduces, Theorem4.20
   Note: The strengthened polygonal finite-triangulation conclusion belongs to the same endpoint approximation theorem identity.
   Origin evidence: The source’s introduction explicitly distinguishes the new endpointW¹,¹ theorem from priorp>1 or bi-Lipschitz approximation, and singles outTheorem4.20 as the stronger finite-polygon boundary version of this result. No separate theorem identity is created for this strengthened case.

25. Unique shortest paths in a closed simple polygon
   In the closed region bounded by a simple polygon in the plane, any two points are joined by a shortest path lying in the region. It is unique, and it is a polygonal path whose bends occur only at vertices of the boundary.
   Source: explains, Theorem 2.1 proof, Step 3
   Note: Recalled as a well-known fact with a short angle-sum proof of uniqueness; not a priority claim.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (102)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Absolutely continuous function on a real interval [definition]: A real function f on [a,b] is absolutely continuous if for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that any finite disjoint family of intervals (a_i,b_i) with ∑(b_i−a_i)<δ has ∑|f(b_i)−f(a_i)|<ε.
- Affine map between real vector spaces [definition]: A map of the form x↦Ax+b, where A is linear and b is fixed.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Basis of a vector space [definition]: A linearly independent family of vectors whose linear span is the whole vector space.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Boundary of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The boundary of A is its closure with its interior removed.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Cardinality of a set [definition]: The size of a set, with two sets having the same cardinality when there is a bijection between them. A set has finite cardinality n when it is in bijection with {1,…,n}; infinite cardinalities are distinguished by the same bijection criterion.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Compactly supported smooth test function on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A smooth function on an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ whose support is a compact subset of Ω; these form C_c^∞(Ω).
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Composition of functions [definition]: For f:A→B and g:B→C, the composite g∘f:A→C sends x to g(f(x)).
- Connected component of a topological space [definition]: A maximal connected subset, with connectedness understood in the subspace topology.
- Connected topological space [definition]: A topological space that is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex hull [definition]: The smallest convex set containing a given set of points.
- Convex subset of ℝᵈ [definition]: C⊆ℝᵈ is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and 0≤t≤1.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Dimension of a vector space [definition]: The cardinality of a basis of the vector space; a finite basis with n vectors gives dimension n.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Fréchet derivative between normed spaces [definition]: The Fréchet derivative of f at x is a bounded linear map A with ∥f(x+h)−f(x)−Ah∥/∥h∥→0 as h→0.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Geometric n-simplex [definition]: An n-simplex is conv(v₀,…,vₙ), where v₁−v₀,…,vₙ−v₀ are linearly independent. Its faces are the hulls of subsets of its vertices.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Interior of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The interior of A is the union of all open subsets contained in A.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Lebesgue measure on Euclidean space [definition]: The completion of the Borel measure on ℝⁿ assigning each rectangular box the product of its side lengths.
- Lebesgue point of a locally integrable function [definition]: A point x is a Lebesgue point of f if the average of |f(y)−f(x)| over balls centered at x tends to zero as their radii tend to zero.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Linearly independent family of vectors [definition]: A family of vectors is linearly independent if every finite linear combination equalling zero has all coefficients zero.
- Lipschitz map between metric spaces [definition]: A map f:(X,d)→(Y,e) is Lipschitz if e(f(x),f(y))≤L d(x,y) for one finite constant L and all x,y∈X.
- Locally finite triangulation of a Euclidean set [definition]: A family of Euclidean simplices covering the set, closed under taking faces and meeting in common faces, such that every point has a neighborhood meeting only finitely many simplices.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Product measure [construction]: For σ-finite measure spaces, the product measure on the product σ-algebra assigns a measurable rectangle A×B the value μ(A)ν(B); its completion also includes subsets of product-null sets.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Sign of a permutation [definition]: The sign of a permutation of {1,…,n} is (−1) to the number of inversions, where an inversion is a pair i<j with σ(i)>σ(j).
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Subspace topology [definition]: For A⊆X, the topology whose open sets are A∩U for U open in X.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric group of a finite set [definition]: The group of all bijections of a finite set to itself under composition.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- Weak partial derivative of a locally integrable function [definition]: A locally integrable v is the ith weak derivative of u on an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ if ∫Ω u ∂ᵢφ=−∫Ω vφ for every smooth compactly supported test function φ.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (10)

- Borell–Brascamp–Lieb inequality at positive power: For p>0, λ∈(0,1), nonnegative integrable u₀,u₁ with positive integrals I₀,I₁ and compact supports Ω₀,Ω₁, if h((1−λ)x+λy)≥M_p(u₀(x),u₁(y);λ), then ∫_{(1−λ)Ω₀+λΩ₁}h≥M_{p/(np+1)}(I₀,I₁;λ), using the BBL zero convention.
- Figalli–Maggi–Pratelli stability of Brunn–Minkowski: For convex bodies K,L⊂ℝⁿ and0<λ<1, the relative Brunn–Minkowski deficit is at least[nm/(ΛMθ_n²)] A(K,L)², where m=min(|K|^(1/n),|L|^(1/n)), M=max(|K|^(1/n),|L|^(1/n)), Λ=max(λ/(1−λ),(1−λ)/λ), and θ_n depends only on n.
- Groemer’s Hausdorff stability of Brunn–Minkowski: For n≥2,λ∈(0,1) and convex bodies K,L with volume ratio and normalized diameters bounded, the relative Brunn–Minkowski deficit is at least c H₀(K,L)^(n+1), where c>0 depends only on n,λ,that ratio and diameter bound; H₀ normalizes volume and centroid.
- Hadwiger’s rotational Minkowski averaging theorem: For every convex body K there are rotationsρ_j for which k⁻¹(ρ₁K+⋯+ρ_kK) converges in Hausdorff distance to a ball with the same mean width asK. After translating K so s(K)=0, the limiting ball is centered at0.
- Lebesgue’s differentiability theorem for monotone functions: A bounded nondecreasing real function on [a,b] is differentiable with finite nonnegative derivative almost everywhere, its derivative is integrable, and ∫_a^bF′≤F(b)−F(a).
- Mean-width rearrangement comparison for elliptic solutions: Letu solve a rotationally invariant proper elliptic zero-boundary problem on a bounded convexΩ and satisfy the positive inward boundary derivative condition. If0<p<1 and each transformedG_p^θ has convex nonnegative superlevel set in(x,t,A), then the corresponding solutionv on the ballΩ♯ of equal mean width satisfies||v||_r≥||u||_r for every0<r≤∞.
- Minkowski comparison of torsion functions: If Ω_λ=(1−λ)Ω₀+λΩ₁ for bounded open convex sets and u_i solve−Δu_i=c>0 with zero boundary data inΩ_i, then sqrt(u_λ((1−λ)x+λy))≥(1−λ)sqrt(u₀(x))+λsqrt(u₁(y)) forx∈Ω₀,y∈Ω₁.
- Power-Minkowski subsolution principle for elliptic equations: Letu_i be positive classical zero-boundary solutions on bounded convexΩ_i,0≤p<1. If their equation operators satisfy the power-transformed mixed quasi-concavity condition atλ, and whenp>0 the positive inward boundary derivative condition holds, then theirp-Minkowski convolution is a viscosity subsolution of the equation on(1−λ)Ω₀+λΩ₁.
- Square-root concavity of the torsion function: If Ω is a bounded open convex Euclidean set and u is its zero-boundary torsion solution−Δu=c withc>0, then sqrt(u) is concave onΩ.
- Variational torsional rigidity of a Euclidean domain: For a bounded open domain Ω, torsional rigidity is T(Ω)=sup_{0≠v∈H₀¹(Ω)}(∫_Ω|v|)²/∫_Ω|∇v|². Equivalently T(Ω)=∫_Ωu for the weak solution of −Δu=1 with zero boundary values.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (25)

1. Borell–Brascamp–Lieb inequality at positive power
   For p>0, λ∈(0,1), nonnegative integrable u₀,u₁ with positive integrals I₀,I₁ and compact supports Ω₀,Ω₁, if h((1−λ)x+λy)≥M_p(u₀(x),u₁(y);λ), then ∫_{(1−λ)Ω₀+λΩ₁}h≥M_{p/(np+1)}(I₀,I₁;λ), using the BBL zero convention.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.1;§3
   Note: Earlier BBL, not an origin claim here.

2. Brunn–Minkowski inequality for convex-body volume
   For convex bodies K,L⊂ℝⁿ and λ∈[0,1], |(1−λ)K+λL|¹⁄ⁿ≥(1−λ)|K|¹⁄ⁿ+λ|L|¹⁄ⁿ. For 0<λ<1 equality holds exactly when K,L are homothetic.
   Source: relies_on, §2–5
   Note: Level-set volume inequality.

3. p-Minkowski supremal convolution
   For positive p, 0<λ<1 and nonnegative functions u_i on compact convex supports Ω_i, their p-Minkowski convolution at z is the supremum of M_p(u₀(x),u₁(y);λ) over z=(1−λ)x+λy, x∈Ω₀,y∈Ω₁, using the stated BBL zero convention.
   Source: relies_on, §2.5;§3–7
   Note: Supremal convolution of power-concave inputs.

4. Volume-normalized centroid-aligned Hausdorff distance of convex bodies
   For convex bodies K,L⊂ℝⁿ, H₀(K,L) is the Hausdorff distance between their volume-one dilates after translating their centroids to the origin.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.2
   Note: Support normalization.

5. Relative asymmetry of two sets after volume normalization
   For measurable K,L⊂ℝⁿ with positive finite volume, A(K,L)=inf_x |K△(x+λL)|/|K| with λ=(|K|/|L|)¹⁄ⁿ; △ denotes symmetric difference.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.3
   Note: Translation-optimized normalized support distance.

6. Variational torsional rigidity of a Euclidean domain
   For a bounded open domain Ω, torsional rigidity is T(Ω)=sup_{0≠v∈H₀¹(Ω)}(∫_Ω|v|)²/∫_Ω|∇v|². Equivalently T(Ω)=∫_Ωu for the weak solution of −Δu=1 with zero boundary values.
   Source: relies_on, §6
   Note: Variational functional in applications.

7. Mean support function on the unit sphere
   For nonempty compact K⊂ℝⁿ, M*(K)=∫_{S^{n−1}}h_K(u)dσ(u), with σ normalized to total mass one. This is half the mean width under the convention that width in direction u is h_K(u)+h_K(−u).
   Source: relies_on, §2.2;§6–7
   Note: Mean width is twice the normalized mean support function.

8. Smooth Dirichlet solvability for Poisson’s equation
   On a bounded smooth Euclidean domain, smooth right-hand side f and zero boundary values have a unique solution of−Δu=f smooth up to the boundary.
   Source: relies_on, §6
   Note: Torsion PDE solutions.

9. Lebesgue’s differentiability theorem for monotone functions
   A bounded nondecreasing real function on [a,b] is differentiable with finite nonnegative derivative almost everywhere, its derivative is integrable, and ∫_a^bF′≤F(b)−F(a).
   Source: relies_on, §3
   Note: Differentiating inverse level-set integrals.

10. Support-volume stability of the positive-power BBL inequality
   Fix p>0 and λ∈(0,1). For bounded nonnegative p-concave u_i on compact convex supports Ω_i with I_i=∫u_i>0, if h dominates their p-Minkowski convolution and ∫h≤M_{p/(np+1)}(I₀,I₁;λ)+ε, then for sufficiently small ε, |(1−λ)Ω₀+λΩ₁|≤M_{1/n}(|Ω₀|,|Ω₁|;λ)[1+2(n+M_{p/(np+1)}(I₀,I₁;λ)⁻¹)ε^{p/(p+1)}].
   Source: introduces, Theorem4.1
   Note: Main support-volume stability estimate.
   Origin evidence: Checked §1 explicitly calls Theorem4.1 the main new stability result and distinguishes existing PL/Brunn–Minkowski stability; the complete proof on pp13–15 supplies the positive-power support estimate.

   For n≥2,λ∈(0,1) and convex bodies K,L with volume ratio and normalized diameters bounded, the relative Brunn–Minkowski deficit is at least c H₀(K,L)^(n+1), where c>0 depends only on n,λ,that ratio and diameter bound; H₀ normalizes volume and centroid.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (132)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Area measure on the Euclidean unit sphere [definition]: On S^{d−1}⊂ℝᵈ, spherical area is the restriction of (d−1)-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Its probability normalization divides by its total mass.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Boundary of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The boundary of A is its closure with its interior removed.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- C^k map between Euclidean open sets [definition]: For a finite integer k≥0, a map between open Euclidean sets is C^k if all component partial derivatives of total order at most k exist and are continuous; order zero is the component itself, so C^0 means continuous. A map is C^∞ when it is C^k for every finite k.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Compactly supported smooth test function on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A smooth function on an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ whose support is a compact subset of Ω; these form C_c^∞(Ω).
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Connected topological space [definition]: A topological space that is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets.
- Continuous dual of a normed vector space [definition]: The vector space X* of bounded linear maps X→its scalar field, with norm ‖ℓ‖=sup_{‖x‖≤1}|ℓ(x)|.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex body in Euclidean space [definition]: A compact convex subset of ℝᵈ with nonempty interior.
- Convex function on a convex subset of a real vector space [definition]: A function f:C→R on a convex subset of a real vector space satisfying f((1−t)x+ty)≤(1−t)f(x)+tf(y) for x,y∈C and 0≤t≤1. Unlike the separate interval-convex-function identity, the domain may be infinite-dimensional.
- Convex subset of a real vector space [definition]: A subset C of a real vector space is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and0≤t≤1. The vector space may be infinite-dimensional.
- Convex subset of ℝᵈ [definition]: C⊆ℝᵈ is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and 0≤t≤1.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Diameter of a Euclidean set [definition]: The diameter of A⊂ℝⁿ is sup{|x−y|:x,y∈A}, possibly infinite.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Distribution on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A continuous linear functional on C_c^∞(Ω), where test functions on each fixed compact support converge with all derivatives uniformly. It need not extend to a tempered distribution.
- Distributional Laplacian [definition]: For a distribution T on an open Euclidean set, its Laplacian is defined by (ΔT)(φ)=T(Δφ), with Δ=∑ᵢ∂ᵢ².
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Euclidean unit sphere [definition]: The unit sphere S^(n−1) is {x∈ℝⁿ:|x|=1}, with the subspace topology and its rotation-invariant surface measure when needed.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- First-order Sobolev space W¹,ᵖ [definition]: For an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ and 1≤p≤∞, W¹,ᵖ(Ω) consists of Lᵖ functions whose first weak partial derivatives are also in Lᵖ, with norm combining the function and derivative Lᵖ norms; vector-valued versions are componentwise.
- Fréchet derivative between normed spaces [definition]: The Fréchet derivative of f at x is a bounded linear map A with ∥f(x+h)−f(x)−Ah∥/∥h∥→0 as h→0.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Hausdorff distance between compact sets [definition]: For nonempty compact sets A,B in a metric space, d_H(A,B) is the maximum of sup_{a∈A}inf_{b∈B}d(a,b) and sup_{b∈B}inf_{a∈A}d(a,b).
- Hausdorff measure of a metric space [definition]: For s≥0, the s-dimensional Hausdorff measure is the limit as δ↓0 of the infimum of normalized sums of diameters^s over countable covers by sets of diameter at most δ.
- Hermitian complex matrix [definition]: A square complex matrix A is Hermitian if its conjugate transpose A* equals A; equivalently A_(ij)=overline(A_(ji)) for every i,j.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Interior of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The interior of A is the union of all open subsets contained in A.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Lebesgue measure on Euclidean space [definition]: The completion of the Borel measure on ℝⁿ assigning each rectangular box the product of its side lengths.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Locally convex topological vector space [definition]: A real or complex topological vector space whose zero has a neighborhood base of convex sets; equivalently its topology can be generated by seminorms.
- Löwner order on Hermitian matrices [definition]: For Hermitian matrices A,B of the same size, A≤B means that B−A is positive semidefinite.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Minkowski sum of subsets of a vector space [definition]: For subsets A,B of a vector space, A+B={a+b:a∈A,b∈B}.
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Monotone real function [definition]: A real function on a linearly ordered set is nondecreasing if x≤y impliesf(x)≤f(y), and nonincreasing if the reverse inequality holds; monotone means either.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative power mean with the Borell–Brascamp–Lieb zero convention [definition]: For p>0 and 0<λ<1, the BBL mean M_p(a,b;λ) is ((1−λ)aᵖ+λbᵖ)¹⁄ᵖ when a,b>0, and is defined to be 0 when ab=0. This zero convention concerns extension beyond positive supports.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Operator norm of a bounded linear operator [definition]: For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Positive inward boundary derivative condition [definition]: A differentiable functionu>0 on a domainΩ with zero boundary data satisfies the positive inward derivative condition if for everyx∈∂Ω and every inward directionν atx, liminf_{Ω∋y→x}∂_νu(y)>0.
- Positive semidefinite complex matrix [definition]: A Hermitian n×n complex matrix A is positive semidefinite if z*Az≥0 for every z∈ℂⁿ.
- Power-concave function with positive exponent [definition]: For p>0, a nonnegative function u on a convex subset Ω of ℝⁿ is p-concave if uᵖ is concave on Ω, equivalently u((1−λ)x+λy)≥((1−λ)u(x)ᵖ+λu(y)ᵖ)¹⁄ᵖ for x,y∈Ω and 0≤λ≤1.
- Power-transformed elliptic quasi-concavity condition [definition]: For0<p<1 defineG_{i,p}^θ(x,t,A)=F_i(x,t^(1/p),t^(1/p−1)θ,t^(1/p−3)A). The mixed quasi-concavity condition atλ asksG_{λ,p}^θ((1−λ)ξ₀+λξ₁)≥min(G_{0,p}^θ(ξ₀),G_{1,p}^θ(ξ₁)) forξ_i=(x_i,t_i,A_i),t_i>0 and every fixedθ. Atp=0 useG_{i,0}^θ(x,t,A)=F_i(x,e^t,e^tθ,e^tA).
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Proper degenerate elliptic equation operator [definition]: A continuous scalar equation operator F(x,r,p,X) on symmetric matrices is proper degenerate elliptic in the standard sign convention if F(x,r,p,X)≤F(x,s,p,Y) wheneverr≤s andY≤X; reversing the whole equation reverses both inequalities.
- Quasi-concave function [definition]: A real functionf on a convex set is quasi-concave if f((1−t)x+ty)≥min(f(x),f(y)) for0≤t≤1, equivalently all its superlevel sets are convex.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real orthogonal group [definition]: The group O(n) of real n×n matrices U satisfying UᵀU=I; these are the linear isometries of Euclidean space.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Rotational invariance of a scalar elliptic operator [definition]: An operatorF(x,r,p,X) is rotationally invariant if F(ρx,r,ρp,ρXρᵀ)=F(x,r,p,X) for everyρ∈SO(n), with the same equation domain after rotating its spatial argument.
- Seminorm on a real or complex vector space [definition]: A nonnegative function p with p(ax)=|a|p(x) and p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y); p may vanish on nonzero vectors.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Smooth domain in Euclidean space [definition]: An open connected Euclidean set whose boundary is locally the graph of a C∞ function, with the domain locally on one side.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Sobolev space H⁻¹ of a Euclidean domain [definition]: The continuous dual of H₀¹, with the Hilbert-space dual norm. A locally integrable function defines an element by pairing against test functions whenever that pairing extends continuously.
- Sobolev space H₀¹ of a Euclidean domain [definition]: The closure of smooth compactly supported functions in the W¹,² norm. Its elements have zero boundary trace in the usual regular-domain setting.
- Steiner point of a convex body [definition]: For K⊂ℝⁿ a convex body, its Steiner point is s(K)=n∫_{S^(n−1)}u h_K(u)dσ(u), withσ the normalized rotation-invariant sphere measure. It is translation covariant and additive under Minkowski addition.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Subspace topology [definition]: For A⊆X, the topology whose open sets are A∩U for U open in X.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support function of a convex body [definition]: For a convex body K⊂ℝᵈ, h_K(u)=max{x·u:x∈K}; it may be restricted to the unit sphere or extended positively homogeneously to ℝᵈ.
- Support function of a nonempty compact Euclidean set [definition]: For nonempty compact K⊂ℝⁿ, h_K(u)=max{x·u:x∈K}; this equals the support function of its convex hull.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric matrix [definition]: A square matrix A equal to its transpose, so aᵢⱼ=aⱼᵢ.
- Test-function topology on C_c^∞ of an open Euclidean set [definition]: The finest locally convex topology making every inclusion D_K→C_c^∞(Ω) continuous, where D_K consists of smooth functions supported in a fixed compact K⊂Ω and has the seminorms max_{|α|≤m}sup|∂^αφ|.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Topological vector space over ℝ or ℂ [definition]: A real or complex vector space with a topology for which vector addition and scalar multiplication are continuous.
- Topology generated by a family of seminorms [definition]: For seminorms (p_i) on a vector space, a neighborhood base at zero consists of finite intersections of sets {x:p_i(x)<ε}; neighborhoods at other points are translates.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Transpose of a matrix [definition]: For anm×n matrixA=(a_ij), the transposedn×m matrixAᵀ has entries(Aᵀ)_ij=a_ji.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Upper semicontinuous function [definition]: An extended-real-valued function f on a topological space is upper semicontinuous if {x:f(x)<a} is open for every real a.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Weak Dirichlet Laplacian from H₀¹ to H⁻¹ [definition]: The operator −Δ:H₀¹→H⁻¹ defined by ⟨−Δu,v⟩=∫∇u·∇v. On a bounded domain it is an isomorphism when H₀¹ carries its gradient norm; its inverse solves the weak zero-boundary Poisson equation.
- Weak partial derivative of a locally integrable function [definition]: A locally integrable v is the ith weak derivative of u on an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ if ∫Ω u ∂ᵢφ=−∫Ω vφ for every smooth compactly supported test function φ.
- Weighted power mean of two positive numbers [definition]: For a,b>0 and v∈[0,1], M_t(v;a,b)=(vaᵗ+(1−v)bᵗ)¹⁄ᵗ for t≠0, with M_0(v;a,b)=aᵛb¹⁻ᵛ. The cases t=1 and −1 are the weighted arithmetic and harmonic means.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (13)

1. Wall criterion for minimal galleries in an affine Coxeter complex
   In an affine Coxeter complex, gallery distance between chambers equals the number of walls separating them. A gallery is minimal exactly when it crosses each separating wall once and no other wall. A chamber E lies on some minimal gallery from C to D exactly when no wall separates E from both C and D.
   Source: explains, §3.6/§16.1
   Note: Selected exact statement/proof inspected; source-specific priority not inferred.

2. Gallery convexity of a building apartment
   If chambers C,D of a thick affine building lie in an apartment A, every minimal gallery in the building from C to D is contained in A.
   Source: explains, §4.5
   Note: Selected exact statement/proof inspected; source-specific priority not inferred.

3. Reduced-word criterion for building galleries
   A non-stuttering gallery in a thick affine building is minimal if and only if its panel-type word is reduced in the model Coxeter system. Consequently its endpoint Weyl distance is the product of that reduced word.
   Source: explains, §15.1
   Note: Selected exact statement/proof inspected; source-specific priority not inferred.

4. Weyl isometry between chamber sets
   A Weyl isometry (strong isometry) between sets of chambers in buildings with the same typed Coxeter model is a bijection f satisfying δ(fC,fD)=δ(C,D) for every pair of chambers in its domain.
   Source: explains, §15.5definition
   Note: Selected exact statement/proof inspected; source-specific priority not inferred.

5. Apartment containment from a Weyl-isometric chamber set
   In a thick affine building with its maximal apartment system, any set of chambers Weyl-isometric to a subset of a model apartment is contained in an apartment.
   Source: explains, §15.5fullproof
   Note: Selected exact statement/proof inspected; source-specific priority not inferred.

6. Canonical retraction to a building apartment
   For a thick affine building, apartment A and chamber C⊂A, there is a canonical type-preserving retraction ρ_(A,C):Δ→A. On every apartment containing C it is the unique isomorphism to A fixing the overlap pointwise. It preserves gallery distances from faces of C and does not increase gallery distance between chambers.
   Source: explains, §4.2 Proposition and proof, pp.53–54
   Note: Selected exact statement/proof inspected; source-specific priority not inferred.

7. Comparison of adjacent-center apartment retractions
   Let adjacent C,C′ lie in an apartment A, let H,H′ be the half-apartments containing them and r their common-wall reflection. For another chamber D, the retractions centered at C,C′ agree in H if d(C′,D)>d(C,D), and agree in H′ if the inequality reverses. If the distances are equal, ρ_C(D)∈H′, ρ_C′(D)∈H, and rρ_C(D)=ρ_C′(D).
   Source: explains, §15.4 Proposition 2 and proof, pp.246–247
   Note: Selected exact statement/proof inspected; source-specific priority not inferred.

8. Coxeter subcomplex characterization of maximal apartments
   A chamber subcomplex of a thick affine building is an apartment in the maximal apartment system if it is type-preservingly isomorphic to the model affine Coxeter complex.
   Source: explains, §15.2 Theorem and proof, pp.241–242
   Note: Selected exact statement/proof inspected; Garrett states it for thick buildings in general. Source-specific priority not inferred.

9. Folding an affine apartment onto a half-apartment
   For a wall H in an affine Coxeter apartment and a chosen closed half-apartment H⁺, its folding fixes H⁺ and reflects the other half across H. It preserves chamber incidence, does not increase gallery distances, and a gallery crossing H maps to one with a repeated consecutive chamber at that crossing.
   Source: explains, §3.3, §3.5 (converse part), §3.6, pp.36–49
   Note: Foldings of thin chamber complexes and their existence in Coxeter complexes; not a priority claim.

10. Simplicial map between simplicial complexes
   A simplicial map f:X→Y between abstract simplicial complexes is a function on vertices sending the vertex set of every simplex of X onto a simplex of Y, possibly of lower dimension. A retraction of X onto a subcomplex Y is a simplicial map X→Y restricting to the identity on Y.
   Source: explains, §3.1, p.33
   Note: Learning reference; not a priority claim.

11. Chamber complex
   A chamber complex is an abstract simplicial complex in which every simplex lies in a maximal simplex, called a chamber, and any two chambers are joined by a gallery of chambers whose consecutive members share a codimension-one face. A chamber subcomplex is a subcomplex that is itself a chamber complex whose chambers are chambers of the ambient complex. It is thin, or thick, if every codimension-one face of a chamber lies in exactly two, or at least three, chambers.
   Source: explains, §3.1, pp.32–33
   Note: Learning reference; not a priority claim.

12. Standard uniqueness lemma for chamber complex maps
   Let X,Y be chamber complexes with every codimension-one face in Y contained in at most two chambers. If two chamber maps X→Y (simplicial maps sending chambers to chambers) agree pointwise on a chamber C and both send non-stuttering galleries starting at C to non-stuttering galleries, then they are equal.
   Source: explains, §3.2 Lemma and proof, pp.35–36
   Note: The ‘standard uniqueness argument’; not a priority claim.

13. Gate property for panels in a building
   For a panel P of a building and a chamber D there is a unique chamber proj_P D containing P nearest to D, and d(C,D)=d(C,proj_P D)+d(proj_P D,D) for every chamber C⊇P. Thus for distinct chambers C,C′⊇P either one lies on a minimal gallery from the other to D, or d(C,D)=d(C′,D) and a third chamber of P begins minimal galleries to D from both.
   Source: explains, §15.4 Proposition 1, pp.245–246
   Note: Proved there in the trichotomy form for two adjacent chambers; not a priority claim.

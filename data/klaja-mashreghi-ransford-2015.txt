REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (12)

- Biholomorphic automorphism of the unit disk: A bijective holomorphic map 𝔻→𝔻 with holomorphic inverse; these maps are z↦c(z−a)/(1−āz), where |c|=1 and |a|<1.
- Bounded positive operator: A bounded self-adjoint operator A is positive, written A≥0, if ⟨Ax,x⟩≥0 for every vector x.
- Carathéodory’s approximation theorem by finite Blaschke products: Every holomorphic f:𝔻→ℂ with |f|≤1 is a locally uniform limit of finite Blaschke products. If f(0)=0, the approximating products can also be chosen to vanish at zero.
- Comparison of numerical radius and operator norm: For a bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space, w(T)≤∥T∥≤2w(T). If T is self-adjoint, w(T)=∥T∥.
- Continuity of holomorphic functional calculus under compact uniform convergence: If f_n→f uniformly on compact subsets of a common neighborhood of σ(T), for a bounded Banach-space operator T, then f_n(T)→f(T) in operator norm.
- Convex hull: The smallest convex set containing a given set of points.
- Disk algebra: The algebra A(𝔻) of complex functions continuous on the closed unit disk and holomorphic on the open unit disk, with the supremum norm.
- Heine–Cantor theorem: A continuous map from a compact metric space into a metric space is uniformly continuous.
- Hermitian angle between nonzero complex vectors: For nonzero vectors x,y in a complex inner-product space, their Hermitian angle is the unique θ∈[0,π/2] with cos θ=|⟨x,y⟩|/(∥x∥∥y∥).
- Norm of a bounded self-adjoint operator from its quadratic form: For a bounded self-adjoint operator A on a complex Hilbert space, ∥A∥=sup_{∥x∥=1}|⟨Ax,x⟩|.
- Partial-fraction decomposition over ℂ: A complex rational function is a polynomial plus a finite sum of terms c/(z−a)^j, one collection for each finite pole, with j bounded by its multiplicity.
- Spectral mapping theorem for holomorphic functional calculus: If f is holomorphic near the spectrum of a bounded operator T, then σ(f(T))=f(σ(T)).

LOCATED CONCEPTS (28)

1. Bounded linear operator
   A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
   Source: relies_on, §1, opening paragraph
   Note: The paper assumes bounded Hilbert-space operators.

2. Operator norm of a bounded linear operator
   For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–4
   Note: Norm bounds and norm convergence of f(rT).

3. Adjoint of a bounded Hilbert-space operator
   The adjoint of a bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space is the unique bounded operator T* satisfying ⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,T*y⟩ for every x,y.
   Source: relies_on, §§3–4
   Note: Decompose T into its self-adjoint real and imaginary parts; positivity comparisons use B*AB.

4. Bounded self-adjoint operator
   A bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space is self-adjoint if T=T*.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 3.1
   Note: The self-adjoint parts have operator norm equal to numerical radius.

5. Bounded positive operator
   A bounded self-adjoint operator A is positive, written A≥0, if ⟨Ax,x⟩≥0 for every vector x.
   Source: relies_on, Theorems 3.2 and 4.3, proof of Theorem 4.1
   Note: Operator inequalities mean positivity of their quadratic forms.

6. Spectrum of a bounded operator
   For a bounded operator T on a complex Banach space, σ(T) is the set of complex λ for which λI−T has no bounded two-sided inverse.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 2.1
   Note: Initially assume the spectrum lies in the open disk, then use radial approximation.

7. Riesz–Dunford holomorphic functional calculus
   For a bounded operator T on a complex Banach space and f holomorphic near σ(T), define f(T)=(2πi)⁻¹∮Γ f(z)(zI−T)⁻¹ dz, where Γ encloses σ(T) inside that neighborhood.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 2.1
   Note: Evaluate finite Blaschke products and locally uniformly convergent analytic functions at T.

8. Spectral mapping theorem for holomorphic functional calculus
   If f is holomorphic near the spectrum of a bounded operator T, then σ(f(T))=f(σ(T)).
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 2.1, p. 3
   Note: Explicitly invoked to place the spectrum of B(T) in the disk.

9. Carathéodory’s approximation theorem by finite Blaschke products
   Every holomorphic f:𝔻→ℂ with |f|≤1 is a locally uniform limit of finite Blaschke products. If f(0)=0, the approximating products can also be chosen to vanish at zero.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 2.1, p. 3
   Note: Approximate by finite Blaschke products; source directs the reader to Garnett [7, §1.2].

10. Partial-fraction decomposition over ℂ
   A complex rational function is a polynomial plus a finite sum of terms c/(z−a)^j, one collection for each finite pole, with j bounded by its multiplicity.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Lemma 2.3
   Note: Expand the rational function with simple boundary poles.

11. Comparison of numerical radius and operator norm
   For a bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space, w(T)≤∥T∥≤2w(T). If T is self-adjoint, w(T)=∥T∥.
   Source: relies_on, §1, equation (1.1), and proofs of Theorems 2.1 and 3.1
   Note: An actual input, not merely a comparison result.

12. Disk algebra
   The algebra A(𝔻) of complex functions continuous on the closed unit disk and holomorphic on the open unit disk, with the supremum norm.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2, Theorems 2.1 and 4.1
   Note: The function class of both mapping theorems.

13. Finite Blaschke product
   A function B(z)=c∏ₖ₌₁ⁿ(z−aₖ)/(1−āₖz), with |c|=1 and |aₖ|<1. Constant unimodular functions are allowed when n=0.
   Source: relies_on, Lemmas 2.2–2.3 and proof of Theorem 2.1
   Note: Their boundary poles and positivity drive the alternative proof.

21. Berger–Stampfli numerical-radius mapping theorem
   If w(T)≤1 and f∈A(𝔻) has f(0)=0, then f(T)=lim_{r↑1}f(rT) exists in operator norm and w(f(T))≤∥f∥∞.
   Source: explains, Theorem 2.1 and proof
   Note: Abstract and §1 credit Berger–Stampfli [3]; this is another proof of the same theorem.

22. Drury’s teardrop theorem
   If w(T)≤1 and f∈A(𝔻) satisfies ∥f∥∞≤1, then W(f(T)) lies in the convex hull of the closed unit disk and the closed disk centered at f(0) with radius 1−|f(0)|². Consequently w(f(T))≤1+|f(0)|−|f(0)|²≤5/4.
   Source: explains, Theorem 4.1 and proof
   Note: Explicitly attributed to Drury [6]; this paper simplifies his proof.

23. Local norm estimate from numerical radius
   For a bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space with w(T)≤1 and a vector x with ∥x∥≤1, ∥Tx∥²≤2+2√(1−|⟨Tx,x⟩|²).
   Source: introduces, Theorem 3.1; abstract; §3
   Note: The equivalent angle and 2×2-matrix formulations in Propositions 5.1–5.2 belong to this same result. Theorem 3.2 is a consequence used in the alternative proof of Drury; the Q(T,t,s) notation stays source data.
   Origin evidence: The abstract explicitly claims a local improvement of the known global norm/radius inequality, and §3 establishes this estimate as Theorem 3.1. In contrast, §1 and §4 identify Berger–Stampfli and Drury as earlier results and §4 credits Drury with the parameter-region description. No predecessor is credited for the local estimate. This records the paper’s checked contribution claim, not a proof of unique historical priority.

24. Toeplitz–Hausdorff theorem
   The numerical range of every bounded operator on a complex Hilbert space is convex.
   Source: discusses, §1, after definition of W(T)
   Note: Convexity is recalled without proof; the paper does not name Toeplitz or Hausdorff here.

25. Berger’s power inequality for numerical radius
   For every bounded Hilbert-space operator T and integer n≥1, w(Tⁿ)≤w(T)ⁿ.
   Source: discusses, §1
   Note: Credits Berger [2] and Pearcy [10]; motivates the more general theorem.

26. Aleksandrov–Clark probability measures for a Schur function vanishing at zero
   For holomorphic f:𝔻→𝔻 with f(0)=0 and |γ|=1, the Aleksandrov–Clark measure μγ is the probability measure on the unit circle satisfying (1−γ̄f(z))⁻¹=∫(1−ζ̄z)⁻¹ dμγ(ζ) for z∈𝔻.
   Source: discusses, §5.1
   Note: Related alternate proof route, credited to Clark/Aleksandrov, with references [11]–[12]. It is not an input to the finite-Blaschke proof.

27. Crouzeix’s numerical-range functional-calculus theorem
   There is an absolute finite constant C such that ∥f(T)∥≤C sup_{z∈W(T)}|f(z)| for every bounded Hilbert-space operator T and f holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closure of W(T).
   Source: discusses, §5.3
   Note: A contextual comparison with Crouzeix [4]. The paper gives the historical bound C≤11.08; no claim about today’s best constant is made.

28. Continuity of holomorphic functional calculus under compact uniform convergence
   If f_n→f uniformly on compact subsets of a common neighborhood of σ(T), for a bounded Banach-space operator T, then f_n(T)→f(T) in operator norm.
   Source: explains, ProofTheorem2.1,p.3,with the contour norm estimate written out in005-proof-notes.txt
   Note: The specified proof is examined here; this is not a claim that the theorem originated in this source.

31. Norm of a bounded self-adjoint operator from its quadratic form
   For a bounded self-adjoint operator A on a complex Hilbert space, ∥A∥=sup_{∥x∥=1}|⟨Ax,x⟩|.
   Source: explains, Introduction and proofTheorem3.1; complete elementary proof recorded here

32. Hermitian angle between nonzero complex vectors
   For nonzero vectors x,y in a complex inner-product space, their Hermitian angle is the unique θ∈[0,π/2] with cos θ=|⟨x,y⟩|/(∥x∥∥y∥).
   Source: relies_on, Proposition5.1
   Note: The final reformulation explicitly uses this standard angle, with the modulus convention.

   For a bounded self-adjoint operator A on a complex Hilbert space, ∥A∥=sup_{∥x∥=1}|⟨Ax,x⟩|.
   Source: relies_on, ProofTheorem3.1

34. Heine–Cantor theorem
   A continuous map from a compact metric space into a metric space is uniformly continuous.
   Source: relies_on, ProofTheorem2.1, last paragraph
   Note: The radial disk-algebra approximants are uniformly Cauchy on the closed disk.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (88)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Associative algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity.
- Banach space [definition]: A real or complex normed vector space complete for the metric induced by its norm.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for complex inner-product spaces [theorem]: In a complex inner-product space, |⟨u,v⟩|²≤⟨u,u⟩⟨v,v⟩ for all u,v.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex Hilbert space [definition]: A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex derivative [definition]: For a complex-valued function on an open subset of C, f′(z) is the limit of (f(z+h)−f(z))/h as nonzero complex h→0, when it exists.
- Complex exponential function [definition]: The entire function exp(z)=∑ₙ₌₀∞zⁿ/n!; on imaginary arguments exp(it)=cos(t)+i sin(t).
- Complex inner-product space [definition]: A complex vector space with a positive definite Hermitian form ⟨x,y⟩, linear in x and conjugate-linear in y. Its induced norm is ∥x∥=√⟨x,x⟩.
- Complex line integral along a piecewise smooth path [definition]: For a piecewise C¹ path γ:[a,b]→ℂ and a complex function f continuous on its image, the line (contour) integral is ∫_γf(z)dz=∫_a^bf(γ(t))γ′(t)dt, summed over the finitely many C¹ pieces, with real and imaginary parts integrated separately.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Complex power series [definition]: A series ∑ₙ₌₀∞aₙ(z−z₀)ⁿ with complex coefficients, considered at points where its partial sums converge.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex subset of ℝᵈ [definition]: C⊆ℝᵈ is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and 0≤t≤1.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Holomorphic function of one complex variable [definition]: A function on an open subset of ℂ is holomorphic if its complex derivative exists at every point of that subset.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Polynomial over a field [definition]: A formal finite sum a₀+a₁x+⋯+aₙxⁿ with coefficients in a field, with the usual coefficientwise addition and multiplication.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Rational function over a field [definition]: An element of k(x), represented by a quotient p(x)/q(x) of polynomials over k with q≠0; two quotients are equal when their cross-products agree.
- Real cosine function [definition]: For real t, cos(t) is the real part of exp(it).
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Riemann integral [definition]: A bounded f:[a,b]→ℝ has Riemann integral I if all tagged Riemann sums approach I as the maximum length of the partition intervals tends to zero.
- Riemann integral of a continuous Banach-valued function [definition]: For continuous f:[a,b]→X with X Banach, its integral is the norm limit of tagged sums ∑f(t_i)(x_i−x_{i−1}) as the partition mesh tends to zero.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Tagged Riemann sum [definition]: For a partition a=x₀<⋯<x_n=b and tags t_i∈[x_{i−1},x_i], the Riemann sum of f is ∑ᵢf(t_i)(x_i−x_{i−1}); the partition mesh is maxᵢ(x_i−x_{i−1}).
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Uniform convergence of functions into a metric space [definition]: A sequence fₙ:X→(Y,d) converges uniformly to f if for every ε>0, d(fₙ(x),f(x))<ε for every x∈X once n is sufficiently large independently of x.
- Uniform convergence on compact subsets [definition]: A sequence of functions converges locally uniformly on a topological space if it converges uniformly on every compact subset.
- Uniformly continuous map between metric spaces [definition]: A map f:X→Y between metric spaces such that for every ε>0 some δ>0 satisfies d_Y(f(x),f(y))<ε whenever d_X(x,y)<δ. The same δ works at every point.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

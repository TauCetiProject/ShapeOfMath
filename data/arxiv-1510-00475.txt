REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (10)

- Boundary-vertex harmonic extension asymptotics: For a regular harmonic structure on a connected p.c.f. self-similar set whose boundary pointsp_i are cell fixed points and whose complementsK∖{p_i} are connected, letA_i act on boundary values by harmonic extension into thei-th cell and letP remove constants. Letu_i be thep_i-column ofD and letv_i be the right r_i-eigenvector ofA_i normalized by(u_i,v_i)=1. Thenr_i^(−n)P A_i^n x→(u_i,x)Pv_i for every boundary vectorx.
- Bounded martingale convergence theorem: A martingale uniformly bounded by a deterministic constant converges almost surely and in L¹. In particular, conditional expectations of a bounded X along increasing σ-algebras converge to E[X|σ(∪_nF_n)].
- Cell energy formula for a p.c.f. harmonic function: For a regular harmonic structure(D,r), harmonicf with boundary vectorx and a cell wordw, its Hino-normalized energy measure satisfiesν_f(K_w)=−2r_w⁻¹(A_wx)^TD(A_wx), whereA_w is harmonic restriction to that cell.
- Furstenberg positivity for finite i.i.d. unimodular two-dimensional matrix products: Let A₁,A₂,… be i.i.d. matrices with a finitely supported law μ on real 2×2 matrices of determinant ±1. If the closed group generated by supp μ is noncompact and strongly irreducible, then lim_(n→∞)n⁻¹log‖A_n⋯A₁‖ exists almost surely and is a strictly positive constant.
- Nonnegative martingale convergence theorem: A nonnegative right-continuous martingale converges almost surely to a finite integrable random variable as t→∞. Its limiting expectation may be strictly smaller than its initial expectation.
- One-dimensional energy-image density theorem: For a strongly local regular symmetric Dirichlet form and a bounded domain function f, the pushforward under f of its energy measure ν_f is absolutely continuous with respect to one-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Consequently ν_f has no point masses.
- Rank-one harmonic energy densities on a p.c.f. self-similar set: Let a connected p.c.f. self-similar set carry a regular harmonic structure, with every boundary point p fixed by a cell map and K∖{p} connected. For finitely many harmonic functions h₁,…,h_m and ν=∑_i a_iν_(h_i) with a_i>0, the matrix (dν_(h_i,h_j)/dν)_(i,j) has rank at most one ν-almost everywhere; it has rank one wherever ν is nonzero.
- Spectral theorem for a real symmetric matrix: Every real symmetric matrix admits an orthonormal basis of real eigenvectors; equivalently A=UΛUᵀ with U orthogonal and Λ real diagonal.
- Spectrum of a boundary-fixed harmonic extension matrix: For a regular harmonic structure on a connected p.c.f. self-similar set with every boundaryp_i fixed by a cell map andK\{p_i} connected, the harmonic extension matrixA_i has simple eigenvalues1 andr_i, every other eigenvalue has modulus<r_i, and thep_i-columnu_i ofD is a nonzero left r_i-eigenvector orthogonal to constants. There is a nonnegative right r_i-eigenvectorv_i uniquely normalized by(u_i,v_i)=1.
- Weak convergence of probability measures on a metric space: Measures μ_n converge weakly to μ if ∫f dμ_n→∫f dμ for every bounded continuous real function f on the space.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (25)

1. Regular symmetric Dirichlet form
   A symmetric Dirichlet form on L²(E,m), where E is locally compact separable metric and m is a full-support Radon measure, is regular if its domain intersected with compactly supported continuous functions is dense both in the domain’s E₁ norm and in C_c(E) for the uniform norm.
   Source: relies_on, §2, p.3
   Note: Standard Dirichlet-form background is recalled following Kigami [13].

2. Strong locality of a symmetric Dirichlet form
   A symmetric Dirichlet form E is strongly local if E(u,v)=0 whenever u is constant on a neighborhood of the support of v and v has compact support, for domain functions for which this support condition applies.
   Source: relies_on, §1; §2
   Note: Local energy measures require strong locality.

3. Regular harmonic structure on a post-critically finite self-similar set
   For a p.c.f. self-similar structure, a symmetric nonpositive matrix D on boundary functions, with kernel the constants and nonnegative off-diagonal entries, together with weights 0<r_i<1, is a regular harmonic structure if its boundary energy E₀(u)=−uᵀDu equals the minimum of the first-level energy ∑_i r_i⁻¹E₀(v∘ψ_i) over extensions v of u to the first-level vertices.
   Source: relies_on, §2, pp.2–4
   Note: Graph-energy compatibility and regularity define the model.

4. Harmonic function for a regular harmonic structure
   For the resistance energy induced by a regular harmonic structure on a p.c.f. self-similar set, a harmonic function is an energy-domain function minimizing energy among functions with its prescribed values on the finite boundary V₀.
   Source: relies_on, §2, pp.4–6
   Note: Harmonic extension operators and energies use boundary-energy minimizers.

5. Energy measure of a strongly local regular Dirichlet form
   For a strongly local regular Dirichlet form E, the energy measure ν_f of a bounded domain function f is the positive measure characterized on bounded compactly supported domain test functions g by ∫g dν_f=2E(f,fg)−E(f²,g). This uses Hino’s normalization; polarization gives mutual energy measures.
   Source: relies_on, §2, pp.4–6
   Note: Energy and mutual energy measures are inherited background, attributed to [4–6,13].

6. Strongly irreducible family of invertible real matrices
   A family of invertible endomorphisms of a finite-dimensional real vector space is strongly irreducible if no finite union of proper nonzero linear subspaces is invariant under every member of the family.
   Source: relies_on, Definition 3.4; Theorem 3.6(I)
   Note: Strong irreducibility is the hypothesis of an imported Furstenberg random-matrix result.

7. Radon–Nikodym derivative of a measure
   A nonnegative measurable function f representing ν relative to μ by ν(A)=∫_A f dμ for every measurable A; it is defined up to μ-almost-everywhere equality when such a density exists.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 2.8; Theorem 3.6(II)
   Note: Densities of mutual energy measures are central.

8. Orthogonal projection onto a closed Hilbert subspace
   For a closed subspace V of a Hilbert space H, Pᵥx is the unique v∈V for which x−v is orthogonal to V; Pᵥ is a bounded self-adjoint idempotent operator.
   Source: relies_on, §2; proof of Theorem 3.6
   Note: The mean-zero harmonic boundary space and its projected operators are used.

9. Spectral theorem for a real symmetric matrix
   Every real symmetric matrix admits an orthonormal basis of real eigenvectors; equivalently A=UΛUᵀ with U orthogonal and Λ real diagonal.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 2.4; (A3); proof of Theorem 3.6
   Note: Eigenvalues and square roots of symmetric matrices are used.

10. Determinant of a square matrix
   For an n×n matrix A over a commutative ring, det A=∑_{σ∈Sₙ}sgn(σ)∏_{i=1}ⁿA_{i,σ(i)}, with the 0×0 determinant equal to one.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 3.6, pp.13–14
   Note: A determinant/norm ratio controls angular concentration.

11. Operator norm of a bounded linear operator
   For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 3.6
   Note: Products of harmonic extension operators are estimated in operator norm.

12. Nonnegative martingale convergence theorem
   A nonnegative right-continuous martingale converges almost surely to a finite integrable random variable as t→∞. Its limiting expectation may be strictly smaller than its initial expectation.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 3.6(II), p.14
   Note: The paper invokes martingale convergence for normalized cell densities; whether bounded convergence alone supplies every density is a pending proof-route question.

13. Weak convergence of probability measures on a metric space
   Measures μ_n converge weakly to μ if ∫f dμ_n→∫f dμ for every bounded continuous real function f on the space.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem 3.6; Conjecture 2.12
   Note: The radial distributions are compared weakly.

19. Rank-one harmonic energy densities on a p.c.f. self-similar set
   Let a connected p.c.f. self-similar set carry a regular harmonic structure, with every boundary point p fixed by a cell map and K∖{p} connected. For finitely many harmonic functions h₁,…,h_m and ν=∑_i a_iν_(h_i) with a_i>0, the matrix (dν_(h_i,h_j)/dν)_(i,j) has rank at most one ν-almost everywhere; it has rank one wherever ν is nonzero.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem 3.6(II), p.14
   Note: This is the precise finite-harmonic rank-one density consequence actually needed; the broader index/AF-martingale representation theorem is unnecessary for this reading route.

20. Bounded martingale convergence theorem
   A martingale uniformly bounded by a deterministic constant converges almost surely and in L¹. In particular, conditional expectations of a bounded X along increasing σ-algebras converge to E[X|σ(∪_nF_n)].
   Source: relies_on, Theorem 3.6(II), p.14
   Note: The chosen ν-hat is a finite sum of two harmonic energy measures, so both diagonal densities and the polarized mixed density are bounded. Bounded martingale convergence identifies all cell-density limits.

21. Furstenberg positivity for finite i.i.d. unimodular two-dimensional matrix products
   Let A₁,A₂,… be i.i.d. matrices with a finitely supported law μ on real 2×2 matrices of determinant ±1. If the closed group generated by supp μ is noncompact and strongly irreducible, then lim_(n→∞)n⁻¹log‖A_n⋯A₁‖ exists almost surely and is a strictly positive constant.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem 3.6(I), pp.13–14
   Note: The inspected positive-exponent theorem suffices directly for the determinant/norm ratio; this replaces no source occurrence or theorem identity.

22. Boundary-vertex harmonic extension asymptotics
   For a regular harmonic structure on a connected p.c.f. self-similar set whose boundary pointsp_i are cell fixed points and whose complementsK∖{p_i} are connected, letA_i act on boundary values by harmonic extension into thei-th cell and letP remove constants. Letu_i be thep_i-column ofD and letv_i be the right r_i-eigenvector ofA_i normalized by(u_i,v_i)=1. Thenr_i^(−n)P A_i^n x→(u_i,x)Pv_i for every boundary vectorx.
   Source: relies_on, Lemmas 2.4–2.5; proof of Theorem 3.6(I)
   Note: Repeated boundary operators have a simple dominant nonconstant eigenvalue; exact imported theorem retained.

23. One-dimensional energy-image density theorem
   For a strongly local regular symmetric Dirichlet form and a bounded domain function f, the pushforward under f of its energy measure ν_f is absolutely continuous with respect to one-dimensional Lebesgue measure. Consequently ν_f has no point masses.
   Source: relies_on, §2, p.4
   Note: No point masses on the countable vertex set allow symbolic coding and relative cell densities.

24. Independent family of random variables
   For distinct i₁,…,iₖ and Borel A₁,…,Aₖ, independence requires P(⋂ⱼ{Xᵢⱼ∈Aⱼ})=∏ⱼP(Xᵢⱼ∈Aⱼ).
   Source: relies_on, §2 product measure; Theorem 3.6(I)
   Note: Bernoulli symbol choices are independent with full support.

25. Boundary averaging coefficients of harmonic energy measures
   For a regular harmonic structure with boundary size at least three, D²=−γD (γ>0), boundary fixed points with connected punctured complements, and invertible harmonic extension operators, choose harmonic functions h_k spanning modulo constants. For a cell word w put a_j(w)=∑_k⟨u_j,A_wh_k⟩², with u_j the j-th column of D, and b_j(w)=a_j(w)/∑_i a_i(w). These positive coefficients average boundary limits of harmonic-energy densities to the cell average.
   Source: explains, Proposition 2.9; (2.4)
   Note: The standard SG coefficients were introduced earlier by Bell–Ho–Strichartz; Hino gives a more general intrinsic construction and alternate proof.

26. Hino’s sharp disk bound for three-boundary harmonic energy coefficients
   For boundary averaging coefficients b_j(w) of a regular harmonic structure with exactly three boundary points, every finite cell word w satisfies ∑_(j=1)^3 b_j(w)²<1/2. The upper bound is sharp over all cell words.
   Source: introduces, Theorem 3.2; proof p.11
   Note: Significant extension from standard SG to the stated three-boundary p.c.f. harmonic structures.
   Origin evidence: Checked Hino’s explicit generalization claim and complete proof against Bell–Ho–Strichartz Theorem 6.5: the predecessor states the disk bound for standard SG only. Hino retains that credit and proves the broader three-boundary theorem, whose sharpness follows from his concentration result.

27. Hino’s radial concentration theorem for three-boundary harmonic energy coefficients
   For boundary averaging coefficients b_j(w) of a regular harmonic structure with three boundary points, let π:S^N→K be the coding map and ν the chosen sum of harmonic energy measures. If κ is either a full-support Bernoulli product measure with strongly irreducible projected harmonic extension matrices, or a Borel measure with π_*κ≪ν, then ∑_(j=1)^3 b_j(ω₁⋯ω_n)²→1/2 for κ-almost every ω.
   Source: introduces, Theorem 3.6; proof pp.13–15
   Note: The introduced conclusion is radial concentration only; the angular conjecture is not solved.
   Origin evidence: Checked Bell–Ho–Strichartz Conjecture 7.2 (standard SG radial law) and Conjecture 7.1 (separate angular claim). Hino explicitly proves the radial assertion in his broader framework and adds the energy-dominated Case II. This is a new result, unlike his recalled predecessor disk/averaging formulas.

28. Spectrum of a boundary-fixed harmonic extension matrix
   For a regular harmonic structure on a connected p.c.f. self-similar set with every boundaryp_i fixed by a cell map andK\{p_i} connected, the harmonic extension matrixA_i has simple eigenvalues1 andr_i, every other eigenvalue has modulus<r_i, and thep_i-columnu_i ofD is a nonzero left r_i-eigenvector orthogonal to constants. There is a nonnegative right r_i-eigenvectorv_i uniquely normalized by(u_i,v_i)=1.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma2.4; subsequent boundary/density formulas
   Note: Explicit imported spectral theorem used by the original source independently of the later selected proof of the asymptotic.

29. Cell energy formula for a p.c.f. harmonic function
   For a regular harmonic structure(D,r), harmonicf with boundary vectorx and a cell wordw, its Hino-normalized energy measure satisfiesν_f(K_w)=−2r_w⁻¹(A_wx)^TD(A_wx), whereA_w is harmonic restriction to that cell.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma2.3;(2.1);Lemmas2.8/Proposition2.9
   Note: Exact imported local-energy identity is actual source machinery.

- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Positive semidefinite real matrix [definition]: A symmetric real matrix A such that xᵀAx≥0 for every real column vector x.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Probability space [definition]: A probability space is (Ω,𝔽,P), where 𝔽 is a σ-algebra on Ω and P is a measure on 𝔽 with P(Ω)=1.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Pushforward measure [definition]: For a measurable map f:X→Y and measure μ on X, the pushforward f_*μ on Y satisfies f_*μ(B)=μ(f⁻¹(B)).
- Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space [definition]: A Borel measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space that is finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets (each has measure equal to the infimum over open supersets) and inner regular on open sets (each has measure equal to the supremum over compact subsets). This is the Folland–Cohn convention; on σ-compact spaces it coincides with compact inner regularity on all Borel sets.
- Rank of a linear map [definition]: The dimension of the image of a linear map.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real orthogonal matrix [definition]: A real square matrix U with UᵀU=I, equivalently a linear map preserving the Euclidean inner product.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real-valued random variable [definition]: A real-valued random variable is a measurable map from a probability space to ℝ with its Borel σ-algebra.
- Resistance energy from a regular harmonic structure [definition]: Given a regular harmonic structure on a p.c.f. self-similar set, define E_m(u)=∑_(|w|=m)r_w⁻¹E₀(u∘ψ_w|V₀), with r_w the product of the cell weights. The resistance-energy domain is the continuous functions with sup_m E_m(u)<∞, and their energy is lim_m E_m(u), with bilinear polarization.
- Separable metric space [definition]: A metric space having a countable dense subset.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Sign of a permutation [definition]: The sign of a permutation of {1,…,n} is (−1) to the number of inversions, where an inversion is a pair i<j with σ(i)>σ(j).
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric Dirichlet form [definition]: A closed densely defined nonnegative symmetric form E on real L²(X,m) satisfying the Markov contraction property: for u∈D(E), v=(0∨u)∧1 belongs to D(E) and E(v,v)≤E(u,u).
- Symmetric group of a finite set [definition]: The group of all bijections of a finite set to itself under composition.
- Symmetric matrix [definition]: A square matrix A equal to its transpose, so aᵢⱼ=aⱼᵢ.
- Topological group [definition]: A group with a topology for which multiplication G×G→G and inversion G→G are continuous.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Transpose of a matrix [definition]: For anm×n matrixA=(a_ij), the transposedn×m matrixAᵀ has entries(Aᵀ)_ij=a_ji.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

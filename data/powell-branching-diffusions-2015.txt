REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (20)

- Additive-martingale spine change of measure for a branching diffusion: Weight the branching law up to t by M_t/M₀, where M_t=e^{(λ−β(m−1))t}∑_uφ(X_u(t)). Under the weighted law a distinguished lineage moves by the φ-ground-state transform, branches at accelerated rate mβ with size-biased offspring law kP(A=k)/m, and chooses one child uniformly to continue the spine; other children start independent original branching families.
- C¹ domain in Euclidean space: An open connected subset of ℝᵈ whose boundary near every point can, after a rigid change of coordinates, be written as the graph of a C¹ function, with the domain locally on one side.
- Dini’s theorem: If continuous real functions on a compact space converge pointwise monotonically to a continuous limit, the convergence is uniform.
- Fisher–Kolmogorov–Petrovsky–Piskunov equation: A reaction–diffusion equation ∂_tu=DΔu+r u(1−u), with D,r>0, on a specified spatial domain and with specified initial and boundary conditions. It appears in population models and branching Brownian survival probabilities.
- Fubini’s theorem for integrable functions: An integrable function on the product of σ-finite measure spaces has integrable almost-everywhere sections, and its product integral equals either iterated integral.
- Intrinsic ultracontractivity on bounded Lipschitz domains: The killed symmetric uniformly elliptic diffusion semigroup on a bounded connected Lipschitz domain with smooth coefficients is intrinsically ultracontractive: for each t>0 its kernel satisfies p_t(x,y)≤C_tφ₁(x)φ₁(y).
- Jensen’s inequality for expectations: For an integrable real random variable X and convex φ with E|φ(X)|<∞, φ(EX)≤Eφ(X), when φ is defined on an interval containing the range of X.
- Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem: If measurable fₙ converge almost everywhere to f and |fₙ|≤g for an integrable g, then f is integrable and ∫|fₙ−f|→0; consequently their integrals converge.
- Many-to-few lemma for constant-rate branching Markov processes: Let Q_k be the multiple-spine law with unchanged motion, m_j=EAʲ, and let D_s(v) count marks on a skeleton particle v alive at s. For nonnegative measurable F of k terminal particle positions (or their ancestral paths), E_P∑_{(u₁,…,u_k)∈N_tᵏ}F(u₁,…,u_k)=E_{Q_k}[F(ξ¹,…,ξᵏ)exp{β∫₀ᵗ∑_{v marked alive at s}(m_{D_s(v)}−1)ds}]. Offspring moments through k are finite; repeated labels are included. Killed marks contribute zero. This is the unchanged-motion specialization of the full Harris–Roberts lemma. 
- Many-to-two second-moment formula for branching Markov processes: For rate-β branching with offspring mean m and finite factorial second moment q=E[A(A−1)], set T_tf=e^{β(m−1)t}P_tᴰf. Then E_x[(∑_uf(X_u(t)))(∑_ug(X_u(t)))]=T_t(fg)(x)+βq∫₀ᵗT_s[(T_{t−s}f)(T_{t−s}g)](x)ds for bounded nonnegative f,g.
- Method of moments for convergence in distribution: If probability laws have all moments, each moment converges to the corresponding finite moment of a probability law uniquely determined by its moments, then the laws converge weakly to that law.
- Moment determinacy from a finite exponential moment: A real probability law whose moment generating function is finite in a neighborhood of zero is uniquely determined by its moments. In particular exponential distributions are moment-determinate.
- Moment recursion for binary branching Markov processes: For binary rate-β branching with killed-motion semigroup P_tᴰ, set T_t=e^{βt}P_tᴰ and u_n(t,x)=E_x(∑_{v∈N_t}f(X_v(t)))ⁿ for bounded nonnegative f. Then u_n(t)=T_t(fⁿ)+β∑_{j=1}^{n−1}binomial(n,j)∫₀ᵗT_s[u_j(t−s)u_{n−j}(t−s)]ds. This includes repeated particle labels.
- Nonnegative martingale convergence theorem: A nonnegative right-continuous martingale converges almost surely to a finite integrable random variable as t→∞. Its limiting expectation may be strictly smaller than its initial expectation.
- Parseval’s identity for an orthonormal Hilbert basis: For an orthonormal basis (e_k) of a separable Hilbert space, x=∑_k⟨x,e_k⟩e_k in norm and ‖x‖²=∑_k|⟨x,e_k⟩|².
- Poincaré inequality on H₀¹ of a bounded domain: For a bounded domain D⊂ℝᵈ there is C_D<∞ such that ‖u‖_{L²(D)}≤C_D‖∇u‖_{L²(D)} for all u∈H₀¹(D).
- Probability generating function: For a nonnegative integer-valued random variable A, G(s)=E[sᴬ]=∑_{k≥0}P(A=k)sᵏ for 0≤s≤1, with extension where the series converges.
- Spectral characterization of H₀¹ and the Dirichlet Laplacian domain: Let λ_n,φ_n be Dirichlet eigenpairs of−½Δ on a bounded C¹ domain. A function f∈L² is in H₀¹ exactly when∑λ_n|⟨f,φ_n⟩|²<∞, and then∫|∇f|²=2∑λ_n|⟨f,φ_n⟩|². For f∈H₀¹, its weak Laplacian lies in L² exactly when∑λ_n²|⟨f,φ_n⟩|²<∞. No global H² boundary regularity is asserted.
- Strong Markov property of Brownian motion: At every almost surely finite stopping time T, the process B_{T+t}−B_T is a standard Brownian motion independent of the information through time T.
- Uniform exponential mixing of the ground-state-transformed Dirichlet diffusion: For a bounded connected Lipschitz domain and a smooth symmetric uniformly elliptic killed diffusion, let φ be its positive L²-normalized principal eigenfunction and γ=λ₂−λ₁. Its ground-state transition density K_t satisfies sup_{x,y}|K_t(x,y)/φ(y)²−1|≤C_εe^{−γt} for every t≥ε>0.

1. Branching diffusion killed at the boundary of a domain

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (133)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Almost-everywhere convergence of measurable functions [definition]: Measurable functions fₙ converge almost everywhere to f if the pointwise limit fₙ(x)→f(x) holds outside a measurable null set.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Almost-sure convergence of real random variables [definition]: Xₙ→X almost surely means P({ω:limₙXₙ(ω)=X(ω)})=1.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Binomial coefficient [definition]: For integers 0≤k≤n, binom(n,k) counts the k-element subsets of an n-element set and equals n!/(k!(n−k)!).
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Characteristic function of a real probability distribution [definition]: For a real random variable X, φ_X(t)=Eexp(itX), t∈ℝ. It is always defined and determines the law of X.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Compactly supported smooth test function on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A smooth function on an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ whose support is a compact subset of Ω; these form C_c^∞(Ω).
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Conditional expectation given a σ-algebra [definition]: For integrable X and a sub-σ-algebra G, E[X|G] is a G-measurable integrable random variable satisfying ∫_AE[X|G]dP=∫_AXdP for every A∈G, defined up to almost-sure equality.
- Connected topological space [definition]: A topological space that is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets.
- Continuous dual of a normed vector space [definition]: The vector space X* of bounded linear maps X→its scalar field, with norm ‖ℓ‖=sup_{‖x‖≤1}|ℓ(x)|.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Continuous-time martingale [definition]: An integrable process M_t adapted to a filtration F_t, t≥0, with E[M_t|F_s]=M_s almost surely whenever s≤t. Right-continuity of paths is an additional regularity condition when needed.
- Continuously differentiable Euclidean map [definition]: A map on an open Euclidean set whose first coordinate partial derivatives exist and are continuous.
- Convergence in probability [definition]: Random variables X_n converge in probability to X if P(|X_n−X|>ε)→0 for every ε>0; metric-valued variables use their metric in place of absolute difference.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex function on a real interval [definition]: A real function φ on an interval I is convex if φ(tx+(1−t)y)≤tφ(x)+(1−t)φ(y) for x,y∈I and 0≤t≤1.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Distribution of a real random variable [definition]: The distribution μₓ of X is defined by μₓ(B)=P(X∈B) for Borel B⊆ℝ.
- Eigenvalue of a densely defined operator [definition]: For A:D(A)⊆H→H, a scalar λ is an eigenvalue if there is a nonzero v∈D(A) with Av=λv. The operator need not be bounded or defined on all of H.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Expectation of an integrable real random variable [definition]: If E abs(X)=∫abs(X)dP<∞, the expectation of X is EX=∫X dP.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Filtration of σ-algebras [definition]: An increasing family (F_t) of sub-σ-algebras on a fixed probability space, indexed by ordered time: F_s⊆F_t for s≤t.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- First Dirichlet spectral gap [definition]: For a bounded-domain self-adjoint Dirichlet operator with ordered eigenvalues λ₁<λ₂≤⋯, the first spectral gap is λ₂−λ₁.
- First-order Sobolev space W¹,ᵖ [definition]: For an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ and 1≤p≤∞, W¹,ᵖ(Ω) consists of Lᵖ functions whose first weak partial derivatives are also in Lᵖ, with norm combining the function and derivative Lᵖ norms; vector-valued versions are componentwise.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Identically distributed random variables [definition]: A family is identically distributed when all its random variables have the same distribution.
- Independent family of random variables [definition]: For distinct i₁,…,iₖ and Borel A₁,…,Aₖ, independence requires P(⋂ⱼ{Xᵢⱼ∈Aⱼ})=∏ⱼP(Xᵢⱼ∈Aⱼ).
- Independent σ-algebras [definition]: A family of sub-σ-algebras is independent if the probability of the intersection of events from any finite subfamily equals the product of their probabilities.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Itô integral with respect to Brownian motion [definition]: For an adapted process H with E∫₀ᵀ|H_s|²ds<∞, ∫H_s dB_s is the L² limit of left-endpoint adapted step-process sums. Localization extends the definition to locally square-integrable integrands.
- Itô stochastic differential equation driven by Brownian motion [definition]: An adapted continuous process X solves dX_t=b(X_t)dt+σ(X_t)dB_t if it satisfies X_t=X₀+∫₀ᵗb(X_s)ds+∫₀ᵗσ(X_s)dB_s almost surely.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Lebesgue measure on Euclidean space [definition]: The completion of the Borel measure on ℝⁿ assigning each rectangular box the product of its side lengths.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Lipschitz map between metric spaces [definition]: A map f:(X,d)→(Y,e) is Lipschitz if e(f(x),f(y))≤L d(x,y) for one finite constant L and all x,y∈X.
- Markov transition semigroup [definition]: A family of transition kernels P_t(x,A), t≥0, with P₀(x,·)=δ_x and P_{s+t}(x,A)=∫P_t(y,A)P_s(x,dy). Sub-Markov semigroups may lose mass at a cemetery state.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Moment generating function [definition]: For a real random variable X, M_X(t)=Eexp(tX) at real t for which the expectation is finite.
- Multiple-spine change of measure for constant-rate branching [construction]: For rate-β branching with offspring A and moments m_j=EAʲ finite for1≤j≤k, start one ancestor with k marks. A particle carrying j marks moves by the original Markov motion, branches at rateβm_j with offspring law aʲP(A=a)/m_j, and each mark independently chooses a child uniformly. Unmarked particles retain the original branching law. The marked genealogical subtree is its skeleton.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normal distribution [definition]: The real probability distribution N(m,σ²) with density (2πσ²)⁻¹ᐟ²exp(−(x−m)²/(2σ²)) for σ>0; variance zero gives a point mass at m.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Operator norm of a bounded linear operator [definition]: For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Orthonormal basis of a Hilbert space [definition]: An orthonormal family whose linear span is dense in the Hilbert space.
- Orthonormal family in a Hilbert space [definition]: Vectors (e_i) with ⟨e_i,e_j⟩=0 for i≠j and ‖e_i‖=1.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Predictable stochastic process [definition]: A process measurable for the σ-algebra on time×sample space generated by adapted left-continuous processes. This formalizes choosing an integrand from information available just before the integration time.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Probability density relative to a measure [definition]: A nonnegative measurable function P with ∫P dμ=1; the measure P dμ is the corresponding probability measure.
- Probability space [definition]: A probability space is (Ω,𝔽,P), where 𝔽 is a σ-algebra on Ω and P is a measure on 𝔽 with P(Ω)=1.
- Product measure [construction]: For σ-finite measure spaces, the product measure on the product σ-algebra assigns a measurable rectangle A×B the value μ(A)ν(B); its completion also includes subsets of product-null sets.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Real-valued random variable [definition]: A real-valued random variable is a measurable map from a probability space to ℝ with its Borel σ-algebra.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Size-biased change of measure [definition]: Given a probability measure P and a nonnegative random variable W with 0<E_PW<∞, the W-biased measure is Q(A)=E_P[W·1_A]/E_PW; equivalently dQ/dP=W/E_PW.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Sobolev space H⁻¹ of a Euclidean domain [definition]: The continuous dual of H₀¹, with the Hilbert-space dual norm. A locally integrable function defines an element by pairing against test functions whenever that pairing extends continuously.
- Standard Brownian motion [definition]: A continuous process B_t starting at zero with independent increments and B_t−B_s distributed N(0,t−s) for 0≤s<t. A d-dimensional standard Brownian motion has d independent standard components.
- Stochastic process [definition]: A family (X_t) indexed by a specified time set, with each X_t a measurable map from a probability space into a specified measurable state space. Adaptation, joint measurability and path regularity are additional conditions when required.
- Stopping time for a filtration [definition]: A random time T taking values in the time index together with infinity, such that {T≤t}∈F_t for every t; by time t one can determine whether T has occurred.
- Strong Markov process [definition]: A Markov process whose conditional future after any finite stopping time, given the past through that time, has the transition law started from its state at that stopping time.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric matrix [definition]: A square matrix A equal to its transpose, so aᵢⱼ=aⱼᵢ.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Uniform convergence of functions into a metric space [definition]: A sequence fₙ:X→(Y,d) converges uniformly to f if for every ε>0, d(fₙ(x),f(x))<ε for every x∈X once n is sufficiently large independently of x.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- Weak Dirichlet Laplacian from H₀¹ to H⁻¹ [definition]: The operator −Δ:H₀¹→H⁻¹ defined by ⟨−Δu,v⟩=∫∇u·∇v. On a bounded domain it is an isomorphism when H₀¹ carries its gradient norm; its inverse solves the weak zero-boundary Poisson equation.
- Weak convergence of probability measures on a metric space [definition]: Measures μ_n converge weakly to μ if ∫f dμ_n→∫f dμ for every bounded continuous real function f on the space.
- Weak partial derivative of a locally integrable function [definition]: A locally integrable v is the ith weak derivative of u on an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ if ∫Ω u ∂ᵢφ=−∫Ω vφ for every smooth compactly supported test function φ.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

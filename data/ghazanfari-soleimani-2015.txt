REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (8)

- C*-completion associated to a C*-seminorm: For a C*-seminormγ on a complex *-algebraA, identifya,b whenγ(a−b)=0 and complete the resulting quotient algebra in the norm induced byγ. Its multiplication and involution extend continuously, making a possibly nonunitalC*-algebra.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for semi-inner-product C*-modules: For x,y in a semi-inner-product C*-module, |⟨x,y⟩|²≤∥⟨x,x⟩∥⟨y,y⟩ in the order on the coefficient C*-algebra.
- Hilbert C*-module: An inner-product C*-module complete in the norm induced by its C*-algebra-valued inner product.
- Idempotent element of an algebra: An element e satisfying e²=e.
- Positive linear functional on a C*-algebra: A complex linear map φ:A→ℂ satisfying φ(a*a)≥0 for every a∈A.
- Schwarz inequality for a C*-seminorm of an algebraic module form: For a semi-inner-product moduleX over a complex *-algebraA and aC*-seminormγ onA, γ(⟨x,y⟩)²≤γ(⟨x,x⟩)γ(⟨y,y⟩) for allx,y∈X.
- Spectral-radius Schwarz bound for positive functionals on Banach *-modules: For a semi-inner-product moduleX over a unital Banach *-algebra with continuous involution and a positive linear functionalφ, φ(⟨x,y⟩⟨y,x⟩)≤φ(⟨x,x⟩)r(⟨y,y⟩) for allx,y∈X.
- Trace-class operator on a Hilbert space: A bounded operator T for which |T|=(T*T)¹ᐟ² is a positive trace-class operator, equivalently its singular values are summable.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (27)

1. C*-algebra
   A complex *-algebra with a complete submultiplicative norm satisfying ∥a*a∥=∥a∥²; an identity is not required.
   Source: relies_on, §2 throughout
   Note: The coefficient algebra may be noncommutative and need not have a specified unit.

2. Positive element and order in a C*-algebra
   An element is positive if it has the form b*b. For self-adjoint a,b, write a≤b when b−a is positive.
   Source: relies_on, §§2–4
   Note: Algebra-valued inequalities use the positive cone, not a scalar pointwise order.

3. Positive square root in a C*-algebra
   Every positive element a of a C*-algebra has a unique positive element b with b²=a. For any a, its absolute value is |a|=(a*a)¹ᐟ².
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: The notation |x|=⟨x,x⟩¹ᐟ² and |a|=(a*a)¹ᐟ² uses the positive square root.

4. Right module over a complex algebra
   A complex vector space X with a bilinear action X×A→X satisfying (xa)b=x(ab); for a unital algebra the action is taken to satisfy x1=x.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: Multiplication of module vectors on the right is part of the structure.

5. Semi-inner-product C*-module
   A right module X over a C*-algebra A with an A-valued form ⟨x,y⟩, complex linear and A-linear in y, conjugate symmetric, additive in each argument, and satisfying ⟨x,x⟩≥0. Zero length need not imply x=0; this is not the nonsymmetric semi-inner product used in Banach-space geometry.
   Source: relies_on, §2 and§4
   Note: Positive semidefinite A-valued forms are the main setting; degeneracy is allowed.

6. Inner-product C*-module
   A semi-inner-product C*-module with ⟨x,x⟩=0 only for x=0. Its norm is ∥x∥=∥⟨x,x⟩∥¹ᐟ²; completeness is not assumed.
   Source: relies_on, §3
   Note: The zero-length implication is used for the e⟨e,e⟩=e identity.

7. Hilbert C*-module
   An inner-product C*-module complete in the norm induced by its C*-algebra-valued inner product.
   Source: relies_on, §2;§5 Example2
   Note: Complete modules provide examples, although the main inequalities do not need completeness.

8. Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for semi-inner-product C*-modules
   For x,y in a semi-inner-product C*-module, |⟨x,y⟩|²≤∥⟨x,x⟩∥⟨y,y⟩ in the order on the coefficient C*-algebra.
   Source: relies_on, (2.1)–(2.2);proofTheorem4
   Note: Imported explicitly fromLance[9,Proposition1.1]; applies to the centered form on Xⁿ.

9. Positive linear functional on a C*-algebra
   A complex linear map φ:A→ℂ satisfying φ(a*a)≥0 for every a∈A.
   Source: relies_on, (2.3)–(2.4)
   Note: The preliminary scalar and spectral-radius Schwarz variants use positive functionals.

10. Spectral radius in a complex Banach algebra
   For an element a of a complex Banach algebra, its spectral radius is sup{|λ|:λ belongs to the spectrum of a}, using the unitization when the algebra has no identity.
   Source: relies_on, (2.4)
   Note: The spectral radius is for algebra elements, distinct from the existing finite-matrix-only record.

11. Idempotent element of an algebra
   An element e satisfying e²=e.
   Source: relies_on, §3 Lemma1 andTheorem3
   Note: The normalization element has an idempotent inner product.

12. Complex Hilbert space
   A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
   Source: relies_on, §5
   Note: Bounded Hilbert-space operators furnish module examples.

13. Bounded linear operator
   A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
   Source: relies_on, §5
   Note: Operator-valued Fourier sums are applications of the inequalities.

14. Adjoint of a bounded Hilbert-space operator
   The adjoint of a bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space is the unique bounded operator T* satisfying ⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,T*y⟩ for every x,y.
   Source: relies_on, §5
   Note: The module inner product is X*Y on B(H₁,H₂).

19. Grüss inequality for bounded real functions
   If m≤f≤M and n≤g≤N almost everywhere on [a,b], with a<b, then the absolute difference between the average of fg and the product of the averages of f and g is at most (M−m)(N−n)/4. The constant 1/4 is optimal.
   Source: explains, Introduction(1.1)
   Note: Established background, explicitly attributed to earlier literature.

20. Vector-valued discrete Fourier transform
   For x₀,…,xₙ₋₁ in a complex vector space, its discrete Fourier transform has components ∑ₖ₌₀ⁿ⁻¹exp(−2πimk/n)xₖ, m=0,…,n−1. Sign and scalar normalization conventions may vary.
   Source: discusses, §5
   Note: The paper permits an arbitrary frequency step ω; the ordinary DFT is a specialization. Finite power-weighted sums called discrete Mellin transforms are retained in notes rather than a new generic transform concept.

21. Grüss inequality for finite families in semi-inner-product C*-modules
   Let pᵢ≥0 sum to one, and let xᵢ,yᵢ lie in a semi-inner-product C*-module with ∥xᵢ−a∥≤r and ∥yᵢ−b∥≤s. Then ∥∑pᵢ⟨xᵢ,yᵢ⟩−⟨∑pᵢxᵢ,∑pᵢyᵢ⟩∥≤rs; the constant one is optimal.
   Source: explains, Theorem4;Corollary1
   Note: Theorem4/Corollary1 re-explain the exact finite norm bound already inGhazanfari–Ghazanfari2014Corollary1. The2015A-valued refinement has its own precise concept.

22. C*-seminorm on a complex *-algebra
   A seminorm γ on a complex *-algebra satisfying γ(ab)≤γ(a)γ(b) and γ(a*a)=γ(a)². It can vanish on nonzero elements and does not imply completeness.
   Source: relies_on, §2,(2.5)
   Note: The preliminary Schwarz variant uses a C*-seminorm, which can be degenerate.

23. Positive linear functional on a complex *-algebra
   A complex linear map φ:A→ℂ satisfying φ(a*a)≥0 for all a∈A; neither a C*-norm on A nor continuity is assumed.
   Source: relies_on, §2,(2.3)–(2.4)
   Note: The scalarized form is stated for a complex *-algebra; continuity is not silently assumed.

24. Trace-class operator on a Hilbert space
   A bounded operator T for which |T|=(T*T)¹ᐟ² is a positive trace-class operator, equivalently its singular values are summable.
   Source: relies_on, §3,Example1
   Note: Understanding the trace-class example is necessary even though its Hilbert-module completeness claim is false in infinite dimension.

25. Semi-inner-product module over a complex *-algebra
   A complex rightA-moduleX with anA-valued sesquilinear form linear in the second variable, satisfying⟨x,ya⟩=⟨x,y⟩a,⟨x,y⟩*=⟨y,x⟩ and⟨x,x⟩ in the algebraic positive cone. Zero length need not implyx=0; noC*-norm onA is assumed.
   Source: relies_on, §2,(2.3)–(2.5)
   Note: These preliminaries temporarily allow arbitrary *-algebra coefficients, unlike the mainC*-module setting.

34. C*-algebra-valued Grüss bound after a module projection
   In an inner-productC*-module, let⟨e,e⟩ be idempotent. Forx,y∈X and anya,b∈A, |⟨x,y⟩−⟨x,e⟩⟨e,y⟩|²≤∥x−ea∥² |y−eb|² in the order ofA. Equivalently, taking positive square roots gives the corresponding unsquared bound.
   Source: explains, Lemma1 andTheorem3

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (60)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Algebraic positive cone of a complex *-algebra [definition]: The cone of finite sums∑a_i* a_i in a complex *-algebra. Writea≤b whenb−a is such a sum. No norm, completeness or spectral characterization is assumed.
- Associative algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity.
- Banach space [definition]: A real or complex normed vector space complete for the metric induced by its norm.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Bounded positive operator [definition]: A bounded self-adjoint operator A is positive, written A≥0, if ⟨Ax,x⟩≥0 for every vector x.
- Bounded self-adjoint operator [definition]: A bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space is self-adjoint if T=T*.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for complex inner-product spaces [theorem]: In a complex inner-product space, |⟨u,v⟩|²≤⟨u,u⟩⟨v,v⟩ for all u,v.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Completion of a metric space [definition]: A complete metric space containing an isometric dense copy of the given metric space. It is unique up to an isometry fixing that copy.
- Complex *-algebra, possibly nonunital [definition]: A complex vector space with associative bilinear multiplication and a conjugate-linear involution a↦a* satisfying (ab)*=b*a*. A multiplicative identity is not required.
- Complex Banach algebra, possibly nonunital [definition]: A complex Banach space with associative bilinear multiplication satisfying ∥ab∥≤∥a∥∥b∥. An identity is not required.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex inner-product space [definition]: A complex vector space with a positive definite Hermitian form ⟨x,y⟩, linear in x and conjugate-linear in y. Its induced norm is ∥x∥=√⟨x,x⟩.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Positive trace-class operator on a Hilbert space [definition]: A nonnegative bounded self-adjoint operator Q is trace class if ∑_k⟨Qe_k,e_k⟩<∞ for an orthonormal basis; the sum is independent of the basis and equals its trace.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Quotient vector space [definition]: For a subspace U⊆V, the cosets V/U form a vector space with addition and scalar multiplication induced from V.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Seminorm on a real or complex vector space [definition]: A nonnegative function p with p(ax)=|a|p(x) and p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y); p may vanish on nonzero vectors.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Spectrum of an element of a complex Banach algebra [definition]: For a in a complex Banach algebra A, σ(a) consists of complex λ for which λ1−a has no two-sided inverse, using the unitization if A has no identity.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Unitization of a complex Banach algebra [definition]: For a nonunital complex Banach algebra A, its unitization is A⊕ℂ with multiplication (a,λ)(b,μ)=(ab+λb+μa,λμ), identity (0,1), and norm ∥a∥+|λ|.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.

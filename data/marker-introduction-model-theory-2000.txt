LOCATED CONCEPTS (3)

1. First-order sentence in the language of rings
   A first-order formula in the language of rings is built from variables, the constants 0 and 1, the operations +, − and ·, equality, the connectives ¬, ∧, ∨ and quantifiers ∀, ∃ over elements; a sentence is a formula without free variables. Interpreting the symbols in a ring R makes each sentence true or false in R.
   Source: explains, §1, pp.16–17
   Note: Exposition; not a priority claim.

2. Vaught’s test for completeness of a first-order theory
   If a first-order theory T in a language L has no finite models and is κ-categorical for some infinite cardinal κ≥|L|, meaning any two models of cardinality κ are isomorphic, then T is complete: every sentence or its negation is a consequence of T.
   Source: explains, §2, Proposition 2.4, p.19
   Note: Statement and proof; not a priority claim.

3. Lefschetz principle for algebraically closed fields
   Every first-order sentence in the language of rings with rational coefficients has the same truth value in all algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero. Bounded finite-type algebraic data can be transported to C after choosing a finitely generated field of definition.
   Source: explains, §2, Corollary 2.5, pp.19–20
   Note: First sentence of the catalogue statement (completeness of ACF₀); not a priority claim.

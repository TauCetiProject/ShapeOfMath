REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (20)

- Banach contraction mapping theorem: Every map T:X→X on a nonempty complete metric space satisfying d(Tx,Ty)≤q d(x,y) for a fixed 0≤q<1 has a unique fixed point, and Tⁿx converges to it for every x.
- Brownian orthogonal decomposition of a local martingale: On a usual filtration carrying a d-dimensional Brownian motion W, every real càdlàg local martingale N has a unique decomposition N=N₀+∫Z_s·dW_s+M, where Z is predictable and locally square-integrable and M is a càdlàg local martingale starting at0 with [M,W^i]=0 for every i. Uniqueness of Z is modulo dt×dP.
- Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequalities for càdlàg local martingales at p≥1: For each p≥1 there are 0<c_p≤C_p<∞ such that every real càdlàg local martingale M starting at0 satisfies c_pE([M]_T^(p/2))≤E[sup_(t≤T)|M_t|^p]≤C_pE([M]_T^(p/2)) for stopping times T, with extended expectations.
- Comparison for càdlàg reflected Brownian BSDE solutions: Let two given càdlàg reflected Brownian BSDE solutions on a usual Brownian-carrying filtration have a common finite horizon, globally Lipschitz drivers g1,g2 and some p>1 integrability: the suprema of |Y| and K_T and the square roots of the Brownian and orthogonal-martingale quadratic variations lie in Lp. Use the convention Y_t=xi−∫_t^Tg ds−∫_t^TZ dW−(M_T−M_t)+K_T−K_t. If xi1≤xi2, S1≤S2 and g1(t,y,z)≥g2(t,y,z) almost everywhere for every y,z, then Y1≤Y2 indistinguishably.
- Convex power jump-remainder lower bound: For1<p<2 and reala,b withR=max(|a|,|b|)>0, |b|ᵖ−|a|ᵖ−p|a|^(p−1)sgn(a)(b−a)≥[p(p−1)/2](b−a)²R^(p−2), withsgn(0)=0. WhenR=0 bothsides are defined as0.
- Covariation stability under UT and ucp convergence: On a common usual filtered space let X^n,Y^n and X,Y be real càdlàg semimartingales. If each sequence X^n and Y^n satisfies UT and (X^n,Y^n)→(X,Y) jointly ucp, then [X^n,Y^n]→[X,Y] ucp.
- C² Itô formula with combined left and right jumps: LetX be optional and làdlàg withX⁺ a càdlàg semimartingale andX_(0−)=X_0. Forf∈C²(ℝ) putR_f(a,b)=f(b)−f(a)−f′(a)(b−a). Then f(X_t)=f(X_0)+∫_0^t f′(X_(s−))dX_s+(1/2)∫_0^t f″(X_(s−))d[X⁺]^c_s+Σ_(0≤s<t)R_f(X_(s−),X_(s+))+R_f(X_(t−),X_t), using the right-regularized integral increments.
- Doob’s Lp maximal inequality for càdlàg martingales: For 1<p<∞ and a càdlàg real martingale M on [0,T] with M_T∈Lᵖ, ‖sup_(t≤T)|M_t|‖_p≤p/(p−1)‖M_T‖_p.
- Fatou’s lemma: For nonnegative measurable functions f_n on a measure space, ∫liminf_n f_n≤liminf_n∫f_n, allowing∞.
- Girsanov theorem for cylindrical Wiener noise: If an adapted U-valued h satisfies E exp(½∫₀ᵀ‖h_s‖²ds)<∞, then R=exp(−∫₀ᵀ⟨h_s,dW_s⟩−½∫₀ᵀ‖h_s‖²ds) has mean one, and W_t+∫₀ᵗh_sds is cylindrical Wiener noise under R,dP.
- Hölder’s inequality: For measurable functions f_i and exponents p_i∈[1,∞] with ∑_i1/p_i=1, ∫|∏_if_i|dμ≤∏_i‖f_i‖_{p_i}.
- Kunita–Watanabe inequality for càdlàg covariation measures: For real càdlàg semimartingalesM,N and predictableH,K on[0,T], ∫₀ᵀ|H_sK_s| |d[M,N]|_s≤(∫₀ᵀH_s²d[M]_s)^(1/2)(∫₀ᵀK_s²d[N]_s)^(1/2) almost surely, whenever the two right-hand integrals are finite; |d[M,N]| is the total variation of the signed covariation measure.
- Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem: If measurable fₙ converge almost everywhere to f and |fₙ|≤g for an integrable g, then f is integrable and ∫|fₙ−f|→0; consequently their integrals converge.
- Linear reflected Brownian BSDE on a usual filtration: Let p>1 and let a usual filtration carry a d-dimensional Brownian motion W. Let xi∈Lp, an adapted càdlàg lower obstacle S satisfy sup S^+∈Lp and S_T≤xi, and a progressively measurable driver h satisfy ∫₀ᵀ|h_s|ds∈Lp. Then the reflected equation Y_t=xi−∫_t^Th_sds−∫_t^TZ_sdW_s−(M_T−M_t)+K_T−K_t has a unique càdlàg solution, with M orthogonal to W, K predictable nondecreasing, ∫(Y_-−S_-)dK=0, and sup|Y|, K_T, (∫|Z|²)^(1/2), [M]_T^(1/2) in Lp.
- Lp bound for the increasing parts of a strong supermartingale: Let p>1 and let X be a real strong optional supermartingale on a usual filtration over [0,T] with E sup_(t≤T)|X_t|^p<∞. In its Mertens decomposition X=X₀+M−A−I, with predictable càdlàg nondecreasing A and predictable left-continuous nondecreasing purely discontinuous I starting at0, one has E A_T^p+E I_T^p≤C_p E sup_(t≤T)|X_t|^p, with C_p depending only on p.
- Meyer’s Lp estimate for the increasing process of a supermartingale potential: For each 1<p<∞ there are positive constants c_p,C_p such that if X is a class-D supermartingale potential with natural predictable increasing process A, then c_p‖sup_tX_t‖_p≤‖A_∞‖_p≤C_p‖sup_tX_t‖_p, allowing infinite norms.
- Quasi-left-continuous filtration: A usual filtration is quasi-left-continuous if F_τ=F_(τ−) for every predictable stopping time τ, where F_(τ−) is generated by F₀ and the events A∩{t<τ} with A∈F_t.
- Snell representation of a càdlàg reflected semimartingale: On a usual filtration let Y=Y₀+N−A be a càdlàg class-D process, where N is a uniformly integrable martingale and A is predictable càdlàg nondecreasing with A₀=0. Let R be adapted càdlàg with its positive stopped values uniformly integrable, Y≥R on [0,T), and ∫₀ᵀ(Y_(s−)−R_(s−))dA_s=0, using R_T=Y_T. Then Y_t is the essential supremum of E[R_τ1_(τ<T)+Y_T1_(τ=T)|F_t] over τ∈[t,T] for which the reward is integrable; the same formula holds with extended conditional expectations for other τ.
- Stochastic exponential of a continuous local martingale: For a real continuous local martingale L with L₀=0, its stochastic exponential is the strictly positive process E(L)_t=exp(L_t−[L]_t/2), where [L] is quadratic variation. It is the solution of dE(L)=E(L)dL with initial value1.
- Young’s inequality for products: For a,b≥0 and p,q>1 with 1/p+1/q=1, ab≤aᵖ/p+bᑫ/q.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (31)

1. Quasi-left-continuous filtration
   A usual filtration is quasi-left-continuous if F_τ=F_(τ−) for every predictable stopping time τ, where F_(τ−) is generated by F₀ and the events A∩{t<τ} with A∈F_t.
   Source: relies_on, §1
   Note: The paper explicitly removes this earlier hypothesis; the comparison requires understanding predictable jumps.

2. Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequalities for càdlàg local martingales at p≥1
   For each p≥1 there are 0<c_p≤C_p<∞ such that every real càdlàg local martingale M starting at0 satisfies c_pE([M]_T^(p/2))≤E[sup_(t≤T)|M_t|^p]≤C_pE([M]_T^(p/2)) for stopping times T, with extended expectations.
   Source: relies_on, §2 proofs
   Note: The imported BDG theorem must allow jump martingales; the continuous version does not cover M.

3. Quadratic covariation of càdlàg semimartingales
   For real càdlàg semimartingales X,Y, their quadratic covariation is [X,Y]_t=X_tY_t−X₀Y₀−∫₀ᵗX_(s−)dY_s−∫₀ᵗY_(s−)dX_s. Its jumps satisfy Δ[X,Y]_t=ΔX_tΔY_t; [X]=[X,X] is quadratic variation.
   Source: relies_on, §1–§2
   Note: The optional cross-variation [M,K] is the central difficulty.

4. Stochastic integral of a predictable process against a semimartingale
   For a real semimartingale X and a predictable locally bounded integrand H, H·X is the canonical stochastic-integral extension of elementary predictable sums ∑H_i(X_(t∧t_(i+1))−X_(t∧t_i)), where H_i is measurable at time t_i; convergence is in probability uniformly on compact time intervals.
   Source: relies_on, §1; §2
   Note: General stochastic integrals with locally bounded predictable integrands are used.

5. Strong optional supermartingale
   An optional process X is a strong supermartingale if X_τ is integrable for every bounded stopping time τ and E[X_τ|F_σ]≤X_σ almost surely whenever σ≤τ are bounded stopping times.
   Source: relies_on, AppendixA
   Note: The increasing-process extension treats strong supermartingales, not just deterministic-time supermartingales.

6. Doob–Meyer decomposition of a càdlàg class-D supermartingale
   On a usual filtered probability space, a càdlàg supermartingale X of class D on [0,T] has a unique decomposition X=X₀+M−A, where M is a càdlàg uniformly integrable martingale starting at0 and A is predictable, càdlàg, nondecreasing and starts at0.
   Source: relies_on, §1 and §3.2
   Note: Decomposition and uniqueness are used repeatedly.

7. Mertens decomposition of a class-D strong optional supermartingale
   On a usual filtration, a class-D strong optional supermartingale X has a unique decomposition X=X₀+M−A−I, where M is a càdlàg uniformly integrable martingale, A is predictable càdlàg nondecreasing, and I is predictable left-continuous nondecreasing and purely discontinuous, all starting at0. Equivalently I=C_− for an adapted càdlàg purely discontinuous increasing C with C_(0−)=0.
   Source: relies_on, AppendixA, (A.1)
   Note: Right jumps require the Mertens decomposition.

8. Meyer’s Lp estimate for the increasing process of a supermartingale potential
   For each 1<p<∞ there are positive constants c_p,C_p such that if X is a class-D supermartingale potential with natural predictable increasing process A, then c_p‖sup_tX_t‖_p≤‖A_∞‖_p≤C_p‖sup_tX_t‖_p, allowing infinite norms.
   Source: relies_on, AppendixA, Step(i)
   Note: Meyer’s potential estimate is explicitly imported from[21], never attributed to this paper.

9. Doob’s Lp maximal inequality for càdlàg martingales
   For 1<p<∞ and a càdlàg real martingale M on [0,T] with M_T∈Lᵖ, ‖sup_(t≤T)|M_t|‖_p≤p/(p−1)‖M_T‖_p.
   Source: relies_on, Proposition3.2; AppendixA
   Note: The estimates use arbitrary p>1, not only L².

10. Martingale orthogonal to a Brownian motion
   A càdlàg local martingale M is orthogonal to a d-dimensional Brownian motion W if [M,W^i] is identically0 for every coordinate i. The Brownian continuity is part of this convention; orthogonality of two arbitrary jump martingales need not mean zero optional covariation.
   Source: relies_on, §1; Definitions2.1,3.1
   Note: An orthogonal martingale is necessary in non-Brownian-generated filtrations.

11. Hölder’s inequality
   For measurable functions f_i and exponents p_i∈[1,∞] with ∑_i1/p_i=1, ∫|∏_if_i|dμ≤∏_i‖f_i‖_{p_i}.
   Source: relies_on, §2–§3
   Note: Standard scalar inequality used throughout.

12. Young’s inequality for products
   For a,b≥0 and p,q>1 with 1/p+1/q=1, ab≤aᵖ/p+bᑫ/q.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: Standard scalar absorption estimates.

13. Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem
   If measurable fₙ converge almost everywhere to f and |fₙ|≤g for an integrable g, then f is integrable and ∫|fₙ−f|→0; consequently their integrals converge.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem3.1 proof
   Note: Used to pass limits of supermartingales.

14. Fatou’s lemma
   For nonnegative measurable functions f_n on a measure space, ∫liminf_n f_n≤liminf_n∫f_n, allowing∞.
   Source: relies_on, Proposition2.1; §2 proof
   Note: Used for localization limits.

   Source: explains, Definition3.1
   Note: Reflected BSDE vocabulary is recalled from earlier literature; terminal compatibility is explicit in the mathematical definition.

   Source: relies_on, §3.1 equation(3.4)

30. Lp bound for the increasing parts of a strong supermartingale

32. Banach contraction mapping theorem

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Adapted stochastic process [definition]: A process X_t is adapted to a filtration(F_t) if each X_t is F_t-measurable.
- Almost-everywhere convergence of measurable functions [definition]: Measurable functions fₙ converge almost everywhere to f if the pointwise limit fₙ(x)→f(x) holds outside a measurable null set.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Almost-sure convergence of real random variables [definition]: Xₙ→X almost surely means P({ω:limₙXₙ(ω)=X(ω)})=1.
- Banach space [definition]: A real or complex normed vector space complete for the metric induced by its norm.
- Banach-valued simple function [definition]: A measurable function with finitely many values in a Banach space, represented as a finite sum∑_ix_i1_{A_i} with measurable sets A_i.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.

- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Boundedness in probability of a random family [definition]: A family (X_i) of real random variables is bounded in probability if lim_(K→∞)sup_iP(|X_i|>K)=0. The definition also applies across different probability spaces by using each variable’s own probability.
- C^k map between Euclidean open sets [definition]: For a finite integer k≥0, a map between open Euclidean sets is C^k if all component partial derivatives of total order at most k exist and are continuous; order zero is the component itself, so C^0 means continuous. A map is C^∞ when it is C^k for every finite k.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Conditional expectation given a σ-algebra [definition]: For integrable X and a sub-σ-algebra G, E[X|G] is a G-measurable integrable random variable satisfying ∫_AE[X|G]dP=∫_AXdP for every A∈G, defined up to almost-sure equality.
- Continuous local martingale [definition]: A continuous adapted process M_t for which stopping times T_n↑∞ almost surely exist such that M_{t∧T_n} is a martingale for every n.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Continuous-time martingale [definition]: An integrable process M_t adapted to a filtration F_t, t≥0, with E[M_t|F_s]=M_s almost surely whenever s≤t. Right-continuity of paths is an additional regularity condition when needed.
- Continuous-time supermartingale [definition]: An integrable adapted process M on a filtered probability space is a supermartingale if E[M_t|F_s]≤M_s almost surely for every s≤t; the term refers to a continuous time parameter and does not require continuous sample paths.
- Convergence in probability [definition]: Random variables X_n converge in probability to X if P(|X_n−X|>ε)→0 for every ε>0; metric-valued variables use their metric in place of absolute difference.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Cylindrical Wiener process on a real Hilbert space [definition]: A family W_t(h),t≥0,h∈U, linear in h, centered jointly Gaussian and adapted, with E[W_t(h)W_s(k)]=min(t,s)⟨h,k⟩ and independent increments relative to the filtration. For each h it is a real Brownian motion scaled by ‖h‖. In infinite dimension the formal sum∑_kβ_k(t)e_k generally is not U-valued.
- Càdlàg path [definition]: A path from a real time interval into a metric space that is right-continuous and has a left limit at every positive time.
- Càdlàg semimartingale [definition]: A real adapted càdlàg process X is a semimartingale if it can locally be written as X=X₀+M+A, where M is a local martingale starting at0 and A is an adapted càdlàg process of locally finite variation starting at0.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Expectation of an integrable real random variable [definition]: If E abs(X)=∫abs(X)dP<∞, the expectation of X is EX=∫X dP.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Extended real line [definition]: The ordered set ℝ∪{−∞,+∞}, with−∞<r<+∞ for every real r.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Filtration of σ-algebras [definition]: An increasing family (F_t) of sub-σ-algebras on a fixed probability space, indexed by ordered time: F_s⊆F_t for s≤t.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Finite signed measure [definition]: A differenceµ=µ₁−µ₂ of two finite positive measures on the sameσ-algebra. It is real-valued and countably additive.
- Fixed point of a self-map [definition]: A fixed point of f:X→X is an x∈X satisfying f(x)=x. The fixed-point set is Fix(f)={x∈X:f(x)=x}.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Function of bounded variation on an interval [definition]: A real function f has bounded variation on [a,b] if sup∑_i|f(t_i)−f(t_{i−1})| over finite ordered partitions is finite. Vector-valued paths use the corresponding norm.
- Gaussian process [definition]: A real or vector-valued stochastic process whose every finite vector of coordinate evaluations has a multivariate normal law.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Hilbert–Schmidt operator [definition]: A bounded linear map B:U→H between Hilbert spaces is Hilbert–Schmidt if ∑_k‖Be_k‖²<∞ for an orthonormal basis(e_k) of U. The square root of this basis-independent sum is its Hilbert–Schmidt norm.
- Independent family of random variables [definition]: For distinct i₁,…,iₖ and Borel A₁,…,Aₖ, independence requires P(⋂ⱼ{Xᵢⱼ∈Aⱼ})=∏ⱼP(Xᵢⱼ∈Aⱼ).

- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.

- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.

- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.

- Local martingale [definition]: An adapted càdlàg process M for which stopping times T_n↑∞ almost surely exist such that each stopped process M_{t∧T_n} is a martingale.
- Làdlàg path [definition]: A path on a real time interval into a metric space is làdlàg if it has both left and right limits at every interior time; neither limit is required to equal the value at that time.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Multivariate normal distribution [definition]: A distribution of a real vector X for which every real linear combination of coordinates is normally distributed; it is specified by its mean vector and positive semidefinite covariance matrix.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.

- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.

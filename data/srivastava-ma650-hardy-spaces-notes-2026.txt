LOCATED CONCEPTS (4)

1. Fatou’s nontangential limit theorem for bounded holomorphic functions
   If f is bounded and holomorphic on the unit disk, then for almost every ζ on the unit circle f(z) converges as z→ζ inside every Stolz angle {z∈𝔻:|z−ζ|<α(1−|z|)}, α>1. In particular its radial limits exist almost everywhere and equal these nontangential limits.
   Source: explains, Corollary 5.27 and proof (p=∞), pp. 58–59
   Note: Selected proof or learning reference for cone task r2-053; not a priority claim.

2. Nontangential limit at a point of the unit circle
   A function F on the unit disk 𝔻 has nontangential limit L at ζ∈𝕋 if F(z)→L as z→ζ within each Stolz angle S_α(ζ)={z∈𝔻:|z−ζ|<α(1−|z|)}, α>1. Radial approach is the special case z=rζ.
   Source: explains, Definition 5.25, p. 54
   Note: Selected proof or learning reference for cone task r2-053; not a priority claim.

3. Fatou’s theorem on nontangential limits of Poisson integrals
   Let μ be a finite complex Borel measure on the unit circle with Lebesgue decomposition dμ=h dm+dμ_s, h∈L¹(m), μ_s⊥m. Then at every ζ where dμ/dm(ζ)=h(ζ) exists, hence for m-almost every ζ, the Poisson integral P[μ](z) tends to h(ζ) as z→ζ within every Stolz angle.
   Source: explains, Theorem 5.26, pp. 55–58
   Note: Selected proof or learning reference for cone task r2-053; not a priority claim.

4. Identification of Hardy Hᵖ functions with their boundary functions
   For 1≤p≤∞ and f∈Hᵖ(𝔻) there is a unique f̃∈Lᵖ(𝕋) with vanishing negative Fourier coefficients such that f(r·)=f̃∗P_r on 𝕋 for 0<r<1, so f is the Poisson integral of f̃; f(r·)→f̃ in Lᵖ as r↑1 (weak-* when p=∞), and ‖f‖_{Hᵖ}=‖f̃‖_p.
   Source: explains, Theorem 5.9, pp. 43–45
   Note: Selected proof or learning reference for cone task r2-053; not a priority claim.

REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (7)

- Dimension formula for sums of subspaces: For finite-dimensional subspaces U,W, dim(U+W)+dim(U∩W)=dim(U)+dim(W).
- Direct sum of vector spaces: The algebraic direct sum of a family of vector spaces consists of tuples with only finitely many nonzero coordinates, with coordinatewise operations.
- Nondegenerate bilinear form: A bilinear form b:V×V→F is nondegenerate if b(v,w)=0 for all w forces v=0, and b(v,w)=0 for all v forces w=0.
- Orthogonal complement for a bilinear form: For a bilinear form b on V and subspace U, its right orthogonal complement is U⊥={v∈V:b(u,v)=0 for every u∈U}. This definition does not assume a positive inner product.
- Separable algebraic field extension: An algebraic extension L/F is separable if the minimal polynomial over F of every element of L has distinct roots in an algebraic closure.
- Snake lemma for vector spaces: For a commuting diagram with exact rows 0→A→B→C→0 and 0→A′→B′→C′→0 and vertical maps a,b,c, there is a natural exact sequence 0→ker(a)→ker(b)→ker(c)→coker(a)→coker(b)→coker(c)→0.
- Subfield generated by a subset: For S⊆L in a field extension L/F, F(S) is the smallest subfield of L containing F and S.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (21)

1. Field extension
   An inclusion of one field into another.
   Source: relies_on, §1, p. 1
   Note: The ambient extension L/F is assumed throughout, not introduced here.

2. Dimension of a vector space
   The cardinality of a basis of the vector space; a finite basis with n vectors gives dimension n.
   Source: relies_on, §1 and §§2–4
   Note: Dimension comparisons drive all statements and proofs.

3. Finite-dimensional vector space
   A vector space having a finite basis.
   Source: relies_on, §1, Theorems 1–2
   Note: The factors S,T have finite positive dimension.

4. Quotient vector space
   For a subspace U⊆V, the cosets V/U form a vector space with addition and scalar multiplication induced from V.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1, pp. 3–4
   Note: Boundary dimensions are dimensions of quotient spaces.

5. Sum of vector subspaces
   For subspaces U and W of the same vector space, U+W={u+w:u∈U,w∈W}, the smallest subspace containing both.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1 and §§2–4
   Note: Sum/intersection calculations are used throughout.

6. Direct sum of vector spaces
   The algebraic direct sum of a family of vector spaces consists of tuples with only finitely many nonzero coordinates, with coordinatewise operations.
   Source: relies_on, §4, p. 14
   Note: Uses T=T_t⊕T′ to reduce the ambient field.

7. Snake lemma for vector spaces
   For a commuting diagram with exact rows 0→A→B→C→0 and 0→A′→B′→C′→0 and vertical maps a,b,c, there is a natural exact sequence 0→ker(a)→ker(b)→ker(c)→coker(a)→coker(b)→coker(c)→0.
   Source: relies_on, §2.1, proof of Lemma 5, p. 4
   Note: Explicitly invoked from Lang [13], Chapter III §9, to get the quotient-space exact sequence.

8. Dimension formula for sums of subspaces
   For finite-dimensional subspaces U,W, dim(U+W)+dim(U∩W)=dim(U)+dim(W).
   Source: relies_on, §2.1, Corollary 6 and Proposition 4
   Note: Dimension additivity and the sum/intersection formula underlie submodularity.

9. Nondegenerate bilinear form
   A bilinear form b:V×V→F is nondegenerate if b(v,w)=0 for all w forces v=0, and b(v,w)=0 for all v forces w=0.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2, p. 5
   Note: Uses the multiplication-compatible pairing σ(xy) induced by a nonzero linear functional.

10. Orthogonal complement for a bilinear form
   For a bilinear form b on V and subspace U, its right orthogonal complement is U⊥={v∈V:b(u,v)=0 for every u∈U}. This definition does not assume a positive inner product.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2 and §3
   Note: Uses orthogonal complements of subspaces for the chosen bilinear pairing, not Hilbert orthogonality.

11. Subfield generated by a subset
   For S⊆L in a field extension L/F, F(S) is the smallest subfield of L containing F and S.
   Source: relies_on, §2 opening and §4
   Note: Reduces first to L=F(S), then treats general L.

12. Separable algebraic field extension
   An algebraic extension L/F is separable if the minimal polynomial over F of every element of L has distinct roots in an algebraic closure.
   Source: relies_on, §1, Theorem 1
   Note: Needed to understand the predecessor hypothesis, not an assumption in the new proof.

13. Product of finite-dimensional subspaces of a field extension
   For a field extension L/F and finite-dimensional F-subspaces S,T⊆L, their product ST is the F-linear span of {st:s∈S,t∈T}. It is spanned by products of basis vectors of S and T.
   Source: explains, §1, opening paragraph
   Note: The multiplication-span operation is defined here; the same operation on arbitrary subspaces is used in §2 technical lemmas.

14. Stabilizer field of a finite-dimensional subspace
   For a nonzero finite-dimensional F-subspace V⊆L in L/F, its stabilizer field is H(V)={x∈L:xV⊆V}. It contains F; every nonzero x in H(V) satisfies xV=V.
   Source: explains, §1, following Theorem 1; §2.3 Lemma 12
   Note: Use xV⊆V to include zero. The printed equality shorthand xV=V denotes the nonzero stabilizers and must be supplemented by zero to get a field.

15. Hou–Leung–Xiang linear Kneser theorem
   Let L/F be a field extension in which every element algebraic over F is separable. For nonzero finite-dimensional F-subspaces S,T⊆L, dim_F(ST)≥dim_F S+dim_F T−dim_F H(ST), where H(ST) is the stabilizer field of ST.
   Source: explains, §1, Theorem 1 and following paragraph
   Note: Explicitly credited to Hou, Leung and Xiang [9]; the new result removes separability and strengthens uniformity.

16. Balandraud’s uniform stabilizer theorem for sumsets
   Fix a finite nonempty subset S of an abelian group. Either |S+T|≥|S|+|T|−1 for every finite nonempty T, or there is a finite nontrivial subgroup H, depending only on S, such that S+T+H=S+T for every T violating that bound.
   Source: explains, §1, Theorem 3 and pp. 2–3
   Note: Stated and explained as the additive prototype; it is not imported as a proof step.

17. Kneser’s addition theorem for finite sumsets
   For finite nonempty subsets A,B of an abelian group, |A+B|≥|A|+|B|−|H(A+B)|, where H(A+B) is the translation stabilizer of the sumset.
   Source: discusses, §1, pp. 1–3
   Note: Classical motivation, credited to Kneser [12]; the paper gives a field analogue, not an original sumset theorem.

18. Uniform subfield stabilizer theorem for subspace products
   Let L/F be any field extension and S⊆L a nonzero finite-dimensional F-subspace. Either dim_F(ST)≥dim_F S+dim_F T−1 for every nonzero finite-dimensional F-subspace T, or there is a subfield F⊊K⊆L, depending only on S, such that STK=ST for every T violating this bound. No separability assumption is required.
   Source: introduces, §1, Theorem 2; proof in §4, p. 14
   Note: The nonseparable extension is a consequence of this single stronger result. Paper-local saturation, dual X*=(XS)⊥, i-cells and i-kernels, and Theorem 13’s nested kernel-subfield chain are kept as proof notes, not assumed reader vocabulary.
   Origin evidence: The abstract and §1 explicitly identify removing the separability assumption as a solution of Hou’s conjecture [8], then state that Theorem 2 is stronger because one subfield K depends only on S and stabilizes every subcritical product ST. The earlier Hou–Leung–Xiang Theorem 2.4 (2002) has the separability hypothesis and a stabilizer depending on ST; checked that primary text. Balandraud’s predecessor concerns finite sumsets in abelian groups, not field subspaces. Thus this field-extension uniform theorem is an original contribution, not merely the new proof of the separable result.

19. Multiplicative saturation of a subspace
   For a finite-dimensional subspace S⊂L of a field extension L/F with1∈S and an F-subspace X, its S-saturation is {x∈L:xS⊂XS}, where XS is the F-span of products. X is S-saturated when it equals this subspace.
   Source: explains, §2.3
   Note: Definition priority relative to the earlier linear isoperimetric method is not inferred from the word introduces in the exposition. The subfield-chain statement explicitly includes H(S)=F. The source’s claim that F is clearly saturated needs this normalization. If H(S)>F, the main uniform-stabilizer conclusion is immediate with H(S), so the issue does not refute that main theorem.

20. Boundary-minimizing multiplicative linear kernel
   For fixed S and an attained finite value λ, a linear kernel is a finite-dimensional S-saturated subspace of minimum dimension among such subspaces satisfying dim(XS)−dimX=λ. In the source an i-kernel refers to the i-th increasing attained boundary value. This is unrelated to the kernel of a linear map.
   Source: explains, §3 definitions
   Note: Definition priority relative to the earlier linear isoperimetric method is not inferred from the word introduces in the exposition. The subfield-chain statement explicitly includes H(S)=F. The source’s claim that F is clearly saturated needs this normalization. If H(S)>F, the main uniform-stabilizer conclusion is immediate with H(S), so the issue does not refute that main theorem.

21. Subfield chain of multiplicative linear kernels
   Let1∈S⊂L generate L over F, with S finite-dimensional, S≠L and {x:xS⊂S}=F. Suppose some nonzero finite-dimensional T has ST≠L and dimST<dimS+dimT−1. List the positive attained saturated-subspace boundary values below dimS as λ₁<⋯<λ_n. The minimum-dimensional kernels containing1 are unique subfields F₁⊋⋯⊋F_n=F, and every finite-dimensional S-saturated X of boundary λ_i satisfies XF_i=X.
   Source: introduces, §3 Theorem13
   Note: Definition priority relative to the earlier linear isoperimetric method is not inferred from the word introduces in the exposition. The subfield-chain statement explicitly includes H(S)=F. The source’s claim that F is clearly saturated needs this normalization. If H(S)>F, the main uniform-stabilizer conclusion is immediate with H(S), so the issue does not refute that main theorem.
   Origin evidence: The introduction says it exports Balandraud’s saturation method to field extensions and introduces the linear kernels in§3; it identifies Theorem13 as its core structural result. These are the linear analogues, not an origin claim for Balandraud’s set-theoretic saturation.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (35)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Algebraic closure of a field [definition]: An algebraic extension of a field that is itself algebraically closed. This specifies an object, without including the existence or uniqueness-up-to-isomorphism theorem.
- Algebraic element of a field extension [definition]: An element α of L/F is algebraic over F if a nonzero polynomial in F[x] vanishes at α.
- Algebraically closed field [definition]: A field in which every nonconstant polynomial has a root.
- Basis of a vector space [definition]: A linearly independent family of vectors whose linear span is the whole vector space.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Cardinality of a set [definition]: The size of a set, with two sets having the same cardinality when there is a bijection between them. A set has finite cardinality n when it is in bijection with {1,…,n}; infinite cardinalities are distinguished by the same bijection criterion.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cokernel of a linear map [definition]: For a linear map f:V→W, its cokernel is the quotient W/im(f).
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Exact sequence of vector spaces [definition]: A sequence of linear maps is exact at a vector space if the image of the incoming map equals the kernel of the outgoing map; it is exact if this holds everywhere.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Image of a linear map [definition]: For a linear map f:V→W, its image is the subspace {f(v):v∈V} of W.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Kernel of a linear map [definition]: For a linear map f:V→W, its kernel is the subspace {v∈V:f(v)=0}.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Linearly independent family of vectors [definition]: A family of vectors is linearly independent if every finite linear combination equalling zero has all coefficients zero.
- Minimal polynomial of an algebraic element [definition]: The unique monic polynomial of least positive degree over F vanishing at an algebraic element α; it generates the ideal of all polynomials over F vanishing at α.
- Multiplicity of a polynomial root [definition]: The multiplicity of a root a of a polynomial P is the largest m≥1 for which (x−a)^m divides P. A root of multiplicity1 is simple.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Polynomial over a field [definition]: A formal finite sum a₀+a₁x+⋯+aₙxⁿ with coefficients in a field, with the usual coefficientwise addition and multiplication.
- Separable polynomial over a field [definition]: A polynomial having no repeated root in an algebraic closure of its coefficient field.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.

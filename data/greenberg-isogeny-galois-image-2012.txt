LOCATED CONCEPTS (5)

1. Isogeny invariance of an open elliptic Tate-image index
   Let E and E′ be isogenous over a number field F and geometrically non-CM, and let p be prime. Their closed images in Aut(T_pE) and Aut(T_pE′) have the same finite index in their respective integral automorphism groups; the same holds for the indices of their determinant-one images in SL₂(ℤ_p).
   Source: explains, §2.1 Prop. 2.1.1, §2.2–2.3 pp.5–8
   Note: Proof of isogeny invariance of the image index; priority not assessed.

2. Sylow image criterion from a nonsplit prime isogeny
   Let E/F be elliptic over a number field, p≥5, and0→Φ→E[p]→Ψ→0 a nonsplit G_F-module sequence with one-dimensional charactersφ,ψ. If E has no cyclic F-isogeny of degree p², φψ⁻¹ has order≥3, and p divides[F(μ_(p²)):F], then the p-adic Tate image contains a Sylow pro-p subgroup of GL₂(ℤ_p).
   Source: explains, §4.1 Prop. 4.1.1 pp.17–18
   Note: Statement and proof; priority not assessed.

3. Congruence core from a maximal cyclic square isogeny
   Let E/F be geometrically non-CM over a number field, p≥5, and suppose E has a cyclic F-isogeny of degree p² but no curve F-isogenous to E has one of degree p³. Suppose E[p] is nonsplit with kernel and quotient charactersφ,ψ, ord(φψ⁻¹)≥3, and p divides[F(μ_(p²)):F]. In a Tate basis with the cyclic p²-kernel first, the closed Tate image contains every matrix [[1+p²a,b],[p²c,1+p²d]] with a,b,c,d∈ℤ_p. Its index in GL₂(ℤ_p) has p-adic valuation1 or2.
   Source: explains, §5.1 Prop. 5.1.1 pp.22–24
   Note: Statement and proof (nonsplit type II case); priority not assessed.

4. Character ratio for a non-CM rational prime isogeny at p≥11
   Let E/ℚ be geometrically non-CM with a rational isogeny of prime degree p≥11. Ifφ andψ are the characters on its kernel and quotient in E[p], thenφψ⁻¹ has order at least3.
   Source: explains, §4.2 and Remark 4.2.1 pp.18–19
   Note: Argument for p≥11; priority not assessed.

5. Ordinary filtration from two local5-isogenies
   Let E/ℚ₅ have two distinct ℚ₅-rational cyclic subgroups of order5. Then E is potentially ordinary or potentially multiplicative. There is a G_ℚ₅-stable subgroup C⊂E[5^∞] isomorphic to ℚ₅/ℤ₅, and inertia acts on the quotient E[5^∞]/C through a group of order dividing4.
   Source: explains, §5.2 Lemmas 5.2.1–5.2.2 pp.24–25
   Note: Lemma attributed to Rubin–Silverberg with Greenberg’s proof; priority not assessed.

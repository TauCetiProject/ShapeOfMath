LOCATED CONCEPTS (8)

1. Riemann change of variables on a compact Jordan set
   Let S⊂U⊂R^n be compact and Jordan measurable, with U open. If g:U→R^n is injective and C¹ and detDg is nonzero onS, then for every Riemann-integrable f on g(S), ∫_(g(S))f(y)dy=∫_S f(g(x))|detDg(x)|dx.
   Source: explains, Theorem 10.7.2 and proof, pp. 134–137
   Note: Textbook statement and proof; not a priority claim.

2. Jordan measurable set in ℝⁿ
   A bounded set S⊆ℝⁿ is Jordan measurable if its indicator function χ_S is Riemann integrable on a closed box R containing S; this does not depend on R, and the Jordan content (volume) of S is V(S)=∫_R χ_S.
   Source: explains, §10.5.1, p. 123
   Note: Textbook definition; not a priority claim.

3. Riemann integral over a Jordan measurable set
   For a bounded Jordan measurable S⊆ℝⁿ, a bounded f:S→ℝ is Riemann integrable on S if its extension by zero to a closed box R containing S is Riemann integrable on R; then ∫_S f is the integral of that extension, independent of R.
   Source: explains, Definition 10.5.4, pp. 124–125
   Note: Textbook definition; not a priority claim.

4. Lebesgue outer measure on ℝⁿ
   For S⊆ℝⁿ, the Lebesgue outer measure m*(S)∈[0,∞] is the infimum of ∑_j V(R_j) over all sequences of open boxes R_j covering S, where V(R_j) is the product of the side lengths of R_j. A set S with m*(S)=0 is a Lebesgue-null set, or has measure zero.
   Source: explains, Definition 10.3.1, p. 108
   Note: Textbook definition; not a priority claim.

5. Jordan measurability of injective C¹ images
   If U⊆ℝⁿ is open, S⊆U is compact and Jordan measurable, and g:U→ℝⁿ is an injective C¹ map whose Jacobian determinant never vanishes on S, then g(S) is compact and Jordan measurable.
   Source: explains, Proposition 10.5.9, pp. 126–127
   Note: Textbook statement and proof.

6. Volume of the linear image of a box
   If R⊆ℝⁿ is a closed box and A:ℝⁿ→ℝⁿ is linear, then A(R) is Jordan measurable and its volume is |det A|·V(R).
   Source: explains, Proposition 10.7.1, p. 134
   Note: Statement; proof left as Exercise 10.7.1.

7. Linearity and almost-everywhere monotonicity of the Riemann integral over a Jordan set
   Let S⊆ℝⁿ be bounded and Jordan measurable and f,g Riemann integrable on S. Then f+g and αf (α∈ℝ) are Riemann integrable with ∫_S(f+g)=∫_Sf+∫_Sg and ∫_Sαf=α∫_Sf, and f≤g outside a Lebesgue-null set implies ∫_Sf≤∫_Sg; in particular functions equal almost everywhere have equal integrals.
   Source: explains, Proposition 10.5.6, p. 125
   Note: Statement; proof left as exercise.

8. Additivity of the Riemann integral over disjoint Jordan sets
   If A,B⊆ℝⁿ are disjoint bounded Jordan measurable sets and f:A∪B→ℝ is Riemann integrable on A and on B, then f is Riemann integrable on A∪B and ∫_(A∪B)f=∫_Af+∫_Bf.
   Source: explains, Proposition 10.5.7, p. 125
   Note: Statement; proof left as exercise.

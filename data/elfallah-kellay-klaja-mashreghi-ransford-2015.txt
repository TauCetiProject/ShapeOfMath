REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (14)

- Berezin transform on the unit disk: For an integrable weight ψ on the disk, Bψ(z)=∫D (1−|z|²)²|1−zw̄|⁻⁴ψ(w)dA(w), the average against the normalized Bergman kernel density.
- Diagonal uniqueness for a sesquiholomorphic kernel: If K(z,w) is holomorphic in z and antiholomorphic in w on a connected disk product, and K(z,z)=0 throughout the disk, then K is identically zero. Thus such a kernel is determined by its diagonal.
- Extreme-point criterion for the closed unit ball of H∞: A scalar Schur function b is non-extreme exactly when log(1−|b*|²) is integrable on the unit circle.
- Hilbert space structure on an operator range: For a bounded map A:H→K between Hilbert spaces, give Ran A the norm ∥y∥=inf{∥x∥:Ax=y}, equivalently transport the norm from (ker A)⊥ through A. This norm can differ from the ambient K norm.
- Inner–outer factorization in Hardy H²: Every nonzero H² function is the product of an inner function and an outer H² function; the factors are unique up to opposite unimodular constants.
- Inner–outer factorization in the Smirnov class: Every nonzero function in the Smirnov class on the disk is a product of an inner function and an outer Smirnov function, uniquely up to opposite unimodular constants.
- Local Douglas formula on the closed unit disk: For f∈H² and |ζ|≤1, Dζ(f)=∫_T |(f*(λ)−f(ζ))/(λ−ζ)|²dm(λ). At a boundary point ζ, f(ζ) means its radial limit; if that limit does not exist then Dζ(f)=∞. Both sides may be infinite.
- Minimum principle for superharmonic functions: A superharmonic function on a bounded domain with nonnegative lower boundary limits is nonnegative throughout the domain. An interior minimum forces it to be constant on that connected component.
- Partial isometry between Hilbert spaces: A bounded linear operator that preserves norms on the orthogonal complement of its kernel.
- Polynomial density in H(b) for non-extreme b: If b is a non-extreme scalar Schur function, analytic polynomials are dense in its de Branges–Rovnyak space H(b).
- Riesz decomposition for positive superharmonic functions on the disk: A positive superharmonic function on the unit disk decomposes uniquely into a Green potential of a positive locally finite measure and a positive harmonic function; the harmonic part has a positive boundary-measure Poisson representation.
- Sarason’s Toeplitz characterization of H(b) for non-extreme b: For a Pythagorean pair (b,a), f∈H² lies in H(b) exactly when T_{b̄}f=T_{ā}g for some g∈H². That g is unique and ∥f∥²_{H(b)}=∥f∥²_{H²}+∥g∥²_{H²}.
- Sarason’s analytic-function inclusion criterion for H(b): For a scalar Schur function b, the following are equivalent: b is non-extreme; H(b) contains every analytic polynomial; H(b) contains every function holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk.
- Smirnov maximum principle: If F belongs to the Smirnov class on the unit disk and its radial boundary values satisfy |F*|≤M almost everywhere for a finite M≥0, then F is bounded analytic and |F(z)|≤M throughout the disk.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (31)

1. Weighted holomorphic Dirichlet space
   For a nonnegative integrable weight ω on the unit disk, Dω consists of holomorphic f with Dω(f)=∫D|f′(z)|²ω(z)dA(z)<∞. When Dω⊆H², its Hilbert norm is ∥f∥²H²+Dω(f), with normalized area measure dA.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–2
   Note: Norm conventions include the full H² norm, not only |f(0)|².

2. Superharmonic function on a planar domain
   A lower semicontinuous function u:Ω→(−∞,∞], not identically∞ on a component, is superharmonic if its value at each center is at least its circle average whenever the closed disk lies in Ω.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: The positivity and mean-value/minimum principles control the weight.

3. Hardy space H² on the unit disk
   The holomorphic functions f(z)=∑ₙ≥0aₙzⁿ on the unit disk with ∑|aₙ|²<∞, normed by (∑|aₙ|²)¹ᐟ²; boundary values identify it with the nonnegative-frequency subspace of L² of the unit circle.
   Source: relies_on, §§2–6
   Note: Boundary L², Cauchy kernels, density and Toeplitz maps are used.

4. Hardy space H∞ on the unit disk
   The bounded holomorphic functions on the unit disk, with the supremum norm.
   Source: relies_on, §3
   Note: The parameter b lies in its closed unit ball.

5. Reproducing kernel Hilbert space
   A Hilbert space of scalar functions on a set X in which evaluation at every x∈X is a bounded linear functional; its reproducing kernel satisfies f(x)=⟨f,kₓ⟩.
   Source: relies_on, §3 and proofsTheorems4.1,5.1,6.2
   Note: Kernel polarization reduces isometric identification to scalar formulas.

6. Toeplitz operator on H² of the unit disk
   For ψ∈L∞ of the unit circle, Tψf=P₊(ψf), where P₊ is orthogonal projection from boundary L² onto H².
   Source: relies_on, §3,Theorem3.2
   Note: The non-extreme H(b) norm is characterized using adjoint Toeplitz operators.

7. de Branges–Rovnyak space H(b)
   For b holomorphic on the unit disk with ∥b∥∞≤1, H(b) is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space with kernel Kb(z,w)=(1−b(z)b(w)̄)/(1−zw̄). Equivalently it is the range of (I−TbTb*)¹ᐟ² with the induced range norm.
   Source: relies_on, §3
   Note: Explicitly credited to de Branges and Rovnyak[4,5]; not introduced here.

8. Outer function in Hardy H²
   A function a∈H² is outer if the linear span of a,z a,z²a,… is dense in H²; this is equivalent to the usual exponential Poisson-integral definition.
   Source: relies_on, §3,Theorem3.2 andproofTheorem4.2
   Note: Cyclicity of aH² is used for norm estimates.

9. Inner–outer factorization in Hardy H²
   Every nonzero H² function is the product of an inner function and an outer H² function; the factors are unique up to opposite unimodular constants.
   Source: relies_on, §§3,6
   Note: Inner factors and the Smirnov quotient are used.

10. Riesz decomposition for positive superharmonic functions on the disk
   A positive superharmonic function on the unit disk decomposes uniquely into a Green potential of a positive locally finite measure and a positive harmonic function; the harmonic part has a positive boundary-measure Poisson representation.
   Source: relies_on, §2 equation2.2
   Note: Imports the positive superharmonic potential/harmonic decomposition.

11. Bergman projection on the unit disk
   The orthogonal projection from area L² of the disk onto its holomorphic subspace; with normalized area its kernel is Qψ(z)=∫D ψ(w)/(1−zw̄)² dA(w), whenever the formula is meaningful.
   Source: relies_on, §6
   Note: The general-weight criterion uses Qω.

12. Berezin transform on the unit disk
   For an integrable weight ψ on the disk, Bψ(z)=∫D (1−|z|²)²|1−zw̄|⁻⁴ψ(w)dA(w), the average against the normalized Bergman kernel density.
   Source: relies_on, §6
   Note: The general-weight criterion compares the Berezin transform to |Qω|².

19. Isometric identification of superharmonic Dirichlet and de Branges–Rovnyak spaces
   Let μ be a nonzero finite positive measure on the closed unit disk, representing a superharmonic Dirichlet weight, and let b be non-extreme with associated outer a normalized by a(0)>0 and |a|²+|b|²=1 on the circle.    Source: introduces, Theorems4.1 and5.1
   Note: Forward identification and converse form one isometric classification statement.

20. Dilation inequality for superharmonic Dirichlet integrals
   For a positive superharmonic Dirichlet weight represented by a finite positive closed-disk measure μ, f∈Dμ and fᵣ(z)=f(rz), Dμ(fᵣ)≤2r/(1+r)Dμ(f) for 0≤r<1.
   Source: introduces, Theorem4.2
   Note: Improved bound for every superharmonic weight.
   Origin evidence: The introduction compares earlier harmonic-weight results and Aleman’s5/2 estimate for superharmonic weights; Theorem4.2 proves the displayed2r/(1+r) improvement by the new closed-disk point-mass identification, rather than claiming invention of the earlier dilation phenomenon.

37. Diagonal uniqueness for a sesquiholomorphic kernel
   If K(z,w) is holomorphic in z and antiholomorphic in w on a connected disk product, and K(z,z)=0 throughout the disk, then K is identically zero. Thus such a kernel is determined by its diagonal.
   Source: explains, ProofsTheorems4.1 and6.2, diagonal/polarization steps; full local power-series proof supplied here
   Note: The specified proof is examined here; this is not a claim that the theorem originated in this source.

38. Sarason’s analytic-function inclusion criterion for H(b)
   For a scalar Schur function b, the following are equivalent: b is non-extreme; H(b) contains every analytic polynomial; H(b) contains every function holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk.
   Source: explains, Theorem3.1(iii); curator-written coefficient/defect proof in009-proof-notes.txt, using the located Aleman–Malman identity
   Note: The specified proof is examined here; this is not a claim that the theorem originated in this source.

39. Outer function in the Smirnov class on the unit disk
   A holomorphic function of the form c exp(∫_T(ζ+z)/(ζ−z)u(ζ)dm(ζ)), where |c|=1, u is real and integrable on the circle, and m is normalized arc measure. No Hardy H² bound is imposed; the boundary log modulus is u almost everywhere.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem6.2(iii)
   Note: The Bergman projectionQω may be outer without lying inH²; anH²-only outer-function concept is insufficient.

40. Inner–outer factorization in the Smirnov class
   Every nonzero function in the Smirnov class on the disk is a product of an inner function and an outer Smirnov function, uniquely up to opposite unimodular constants.
   Source: relies_on, §3 andTheorem6.1
   Note: The proof compares the inner factors ofb and the unbounded quotientb/a.

41. Smirnov maximum principle
   If F belongs to the Smirnov class on the unit disk and its radial boundary values satisfy |F*|≤M almost everywhere for a finite M≥0, then F is bounded analytic and |F(z)|≤M throughout the disk.
   Source: relies_on, §3, normalization of a Smirnov quotient to a pair
   Note: The standard outer normalization must control an analytic quotient inside the disk, not only its boundary values.

42. Diagonal uniqueness for a sesquiholomorphic kernel
   If K(z,w) is holomorphic in z and antiholomorphic in w on a connected disk product, and K(z,z)=0 throughout the disk, then K is identically zero. Thus such a kernel is determined by its diagonal.
   Source: relies_on, Proofs4.1 and6.2
   Note: The proof reduces sesquiholomorphic kernel equalities to their diagonal values.

43. Sarason’s analytic-function inclusion criterion for H(b)
   For a scalar Schur function b, the following are equivalent: b is non-extreme; H(b) contains every analytic polynomial; H(b) contains every function holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed unit disk.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem3.1(iii),proofs5.1 and6.1
   Note: Containing all functions analytic across the closed disk is the actual imported criterion used to force non-extremality.

44. Isometric weighted Dirichlet–H(b) criterion for general weights
   If a nonzero nonnegative integrable disk weightω givesDω=H(b) with the Hardy-plus-Dirichlet norm, thenb is non-extreme with inner factorz up to phase. When∫ωdA=1, suchb exists exactly whenDω⊂H², polynomials are dense inDω, andQω is outer in the Smirnov sense with(1−|z|²)|Qω(z)|²=Bω(z). Then the associated Pythagorean quotient isb/a=czQω for|c|=1.
   Source: introduces, Theorems6.1–6.2
   Note: The necessary inner-factor restriction and the complete normalized-weight criterion are one coherent general-weight classification result.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (110)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Adjoint of a bounded Hilbert-space operator [definition]: The adjoint of a bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space is the unique bounded operator T* satisfying ⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,T*y⟩ for every x,y.
- Almost-everywhere convergence of measurable functions [definition]: Measurable functions fₙ converge almost everywhere to f if the pointwise limit fₙ(x)→f(x) holds outside a measurable null set.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Boundary of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The boundary of A is its closure with its interior removed.
- Bounded linear functional [definition]: A linear map from a real or complex normed vector space to its scalar field satisfying |ℓ(x)|≤C∥x∥ for one finite C and every x.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Bounded positive operator [definition]: A bounded self-adjoint operator A is positive, written A≥0, if ⟨Ax,x⟩≥0 for every vector x.
- Bounded self-adjoint operator [definition]: A bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space is self-adjoint if T=T*.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for complex inner-product spaces [theorem]: In a complex inner-product space, |⟨u,v⟩|²≤⟨u,u⟩⟨v,v⟩ for all u,v.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex Hilbert space [definition]: A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex derivative [definition]: For a complex-valued function on an open subset of C, f′(z) is the limit of (f(z+h)−f(z))/h as nonzero complex h→0, when it exists.
- Complex exponential function [definition]: The entire function exp(z)=∑ₙ₌₀∞zⁿ/n!; on imaginary arguments exp(it)=cos(t)+i sin(t).
- Complex inner-product space [definition]: A complex vector space with a positive definite Hermitian form ⟨x,y⟩, linear in x and conjugate-linear in y. Its induced norm is ∥x∥=√⟨x,x⟩.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Complex power series [definition]: A series ∑ₙ₌₀∞aₙ(z−z₀)ⁿ with complex coefficients, considered at points where its partial sums converge.
- Connected component of a topological space [definition]: A maximal connected subset, with connectedness understood in the subspace topology.
- Connected topological space [definition]: A topological space that is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex subset of a real vector space [definition]: A subset C of a real vector space is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and0≤t≤1. The vector space may be infinite-dimensional.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Extreme point of a convex set [definition]: A point x of a convex set is extreme if x=(1−t)y+tz with0<t<1 and y,z in the set implies y=z=x.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Fourier coefficient of an integrable circle function [definition]: For an integrable function g on the unit circle, its nth Fourier coefficient is ĝ(n)=(2π)⁻¹∫₀²π g(e^{it})e^{−int}dt, for n∈ℤ.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Green function of the unit disk [definition]: For z,ζ in the disk, G(z,ζ)=log|(1−ζ̄z)/(z−ζ)|, with value∞ at z=ζ; it is zero at the boundary in the z variable and gives the disk’s logarithmic potential kernel.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Harmonic function on a plane domain [definition]: A twice continuously differentiable real function u on an open Ω⊂ℝ² satisfying ∂₁²u+∂₂²u=0.
- Holomorphic function of one complex variable [definition]: A function on an open subset of ℂ is holomorphic if its complex derivative exists at every point of that subset.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Inner function on the unit disk [definition]: A bounded holomorphic function on the unit disk whose radial boundary values have modulus one almost everywhere on the unit circle.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Interior of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The interior of A is the union of all open subsets contained in A.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Kernel of a linear map [definition]: For a linear map f:V→W, its kernel is the subspace {v∈V:f(v)=0}.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.

- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Local Dirichlet integral at a closed-disk point [definition]: For holomorphic f on the unit disk and |ζ|≤1, put Dζ(f)=∫|f′(z)|²ωζ(z)dA(z), with normalized area. For |ζ|<1 use ωζ(z)=2G(z,ζ)/(1−|ζ|²); for |ζ|=1 use the Poisson kernel ωζ(z)=(1−|z|²)/|ζ−z|². The integral may be infinite.
- Lower semicontinuous extended-real function [definition]: A function u:X→(−∞,∞] on a topological space is lower semicontinuous if {x:u(x)>a} is open for every real a.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.

- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.

- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.

- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.

- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.

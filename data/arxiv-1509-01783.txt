REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (16)

- Compensation formula for Poisson random measures: If N has intensity dt·ν(dz), then for every nonnegative predictable H, E∫H(t,z)N(dt,dz)=E∫H(t,z)dtν(dz). The predictable hypothesis refers to a filtration in which future Poisson increments are independent of the past.
- C² Itô formula for a càdlàg semimartingale: For a real càdlàg semimartingaleA andf∈C²(ℝ), f(A_t)=f(A_0)+∫_(0,t]f′(A_(s−))dA_s+(1/2)∫_(0,t]f″(A_(s−))d[A]^c_s+Σ_(0<s≤t)[f(A_s)−f(A_(s−))−f′(A_(s−))ΔA_s], where[A]^c is the continuous part of the quadratic variation.
- Fatou’s lemma: For nonnegative measurable functions f_n on a measure space, ∫liminf_n f_n≤liminf_n∫f_n, allowing∞.
- Geometric Foster–Lyapunov condition for a continuous-time generator: For a specified extended generator A of a Markov process, a continuous function V≥1 in its declared domain satisfies a geometric drift condition if AV≤−kV+b1_C for constants k>0,b<∞ and a specified compact or small set C; the domain and small-set convention are part of the chosen route.
- Geometric ergodicity from a half-line Lyapunov drift and positive transitions: Let a nonexplosive càdlàg weak Feller strong Markov family on[0,∞), with usual filtration, have positive transition probabilities to every Lebesgue-positive Borel set at every positive time. Suppose a continuous V≥1, k>0, b<∞ and compact C satisfy that V(X_t)−V(X₀)−∫₀ᵗ[−kV(X_s)+b1_C(X_s)]ds is a local supermartingale from every initial state. Then there is a unique invariant probability pi with pi(V)<∞ and constants D,rho>0 such that ‖P_t(x,·)−pi‖V≤D V(x)e^(−rho t) for every x,t. No equality rho=k or special explicit prefactor is asserted.
- Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem: If measurable fₙ converge almost everywhere to f and |fₙ|≤g for an integrable g, then f is integrable and ∫|fₙ−f|→0; consequently their integrals converge.
- Local Doob–Meyer decomposition of a càdlàg supermartingale: On a usual filtration, a real càdlàg supermartingale X has a unique local decomposition X=X₀+N−A, where N is a càdlàg local martingale starting at0 and A is predictable càdlàg nondecreasing starting at0 and locally integrable. This is the local form; a uniformly integrable martingale conclusion requires the corresponding class-D/integrability hypotheses.
- Mollifier on Euclidean space: A nonnegative smooth compactly supported function j on ℝᵈ with integral one. Its scaled kernels j_ε(x)=ε⁻ᵈj(x/ε) produce smooth approximations by convolution.
- Optional sampling for a nonnegative càdlàg supermartingale: On a usual filtration, if X is a nonnegative càdlàg supermartingale and sigma≤tau are bounded stopping times, then E[X_tau|F_sigma]≤X_sigma almost surely. No class-D assumption is needed for this bounded-time nonnegative formulation.
- Ordered coupling of càdlàg half-line Markov paths: Let X,Y be time-homogeneous càdlàg Markov families on[0,∞) with transition semigroups P,Q, such that P_t(x,·)≤stQ_t(y,·) whenever x≤y and t≥0. For every x≤y there is a coupling of their whole path laws started at x,y with X_t≤Y_t for every t≥0 almost surely. In particular this holds for two ordered initial states of one stochastically monotone family, or when P is stochastically dominated by a monotone Q. This path-law assertion alone makes no claim that the coordinates are Markov or martingales in their common enlarged filtration.
- Poisson point process: A point process with intensity measure λ for which counts in disjoint measurable sets are independent and ξ(A) has Poisson law of mean λ(A) whenever λ(A)<∞.
- Positive transitions of a Lipschitz reflected half-line diffusion: Let the reflected half-line diffusion have globally Lipschitz drift g and diffusion coefficient sigma with inf sigma>0. For every t>0, initial x≥0 and Borel set B⊆[0,∞) of positive Lebesgue measure, P_x(X_t∈B)>0.
- Tanaka formula for the absolute value of a continuous semimartingale: For a real continuous semimartingale X, there is a continuous nonnegative nondecreasing process L^0 with L₀^0=0, increasing only when X=0, such that |X_t|=|X₀|+∫₀ᵗsgn(X_s)dX_s+L_t^0, with sgn(0)=0. This L^0 is the symmetric local time of X at0.
- Tower property of conditional expectation: For sub-σ-algebras F⊆G and integrable X, E[E[X|G]|F]=E[X|F]; also E[E[X|F]|G]=E[X|F].
- Two rank-based finite-activity Lévy particles: Two real particles are driven by the upper and lower coordinates of a two-dimensional Lévy process with finite jump measure, drift vector (g₊,g₋) and positive definite covariance: the particle currently ranked higher uses the upper driver and the lower-ranked particle uses the lower driver, with a declared rule resolving ties. The ranked gap is the difference of their ordered positions.
- Well-posedness of bounded-rate reflected jump-diffusions on the half-line: Suppose g and σ are globally Lipschitz on [0,∞), inf σ>0, and ν_x are finite positive Borel destination measures such that x↦∫f dν_x is continuous for every bounded continuous f and sup_xν_x([0,∞))<∞. Piecing out reflected diffusions with jump measure ν_x yields a unique-in-law nonexplosive weak Feller strong Markov family. Its extended generator on bounded C² test functions with f′(0)=0 is Af(x)=g(x)f′(x)+σ(x)²f″(x)/2+∫(f(y)−f(x))dν_x(y).

1. Finite-activity reflected jump-diffusion on the half-line
   A half-line reflected diffusion with coefficients g,σ is pieced together with jumps having a state-dependent finite destination measure ν_x: while at x, jumps occur at rate ν_x([0,∞)) and land with normalized law ν_x/ν_x([0,∞)), when this rate is positive. Bounded jump rates ensure no finite-time accumulation; the zero-rate case has no jumps.
   Source: relies_on, §2
   Note: The source works with the standard pieced-out reflected jump process; no origin claim.

2. Extended martingale generator of a Markov process
   A pair of Borel functions (f,Af) belongs to the extended martingale generator of a Markov process X when ∫₀ᵗ|Af(X_s)|ds is locally finite and f(X_t)−f(X₀)−∫₀ᵗAf(X_s)ds is a local martingale under every initial state, using the declared filtration and localization convention.
   Source: relies_on, §2–§3
   Note: Generator domain at the reflecting boundary is essential; e^(λx) is not in the ordinary Neumann domain.
The allowed reset kernel ν_x=δ₀ refutes C₀ preservation, so source reading occurrence must refer to the extended martingale generator. Preserve the existing C₀ concept and its UUID.

3. Geometric Foster–Lyapunov condition for a continuous-time generator
   For a specified extended generator A of a Markov process, a continuous function V≥1 in its declared domain satisfies a geometric drift condition if AV≤−kV+b1_C for constants k>0,b<∞ and a specified compact or small set C; the domain and small-set convention are part of the chosen route.
   Source: relies_on, §3
   Note: The proof of Theorem 3.2 uses a smoothed exponential Lyapunov function.

4. Usual stochastic order for finite measures on the real line
   For finite positive Borel measures μ and ν of equal mass on ℝ, μ≤_stν means μ([a,∞))≤ν([a,∞)) for every real a; for probabilities this is equivalently domination of expectations of bounded increasing measurable functions.
   Source: relies_on, §4–§5
   Note: Ordered destination measures and semigroup laws control coupling.

5. Stochastic domination of Markov semigroups on the half-line
   A Markov semigroup P_t on [0,∞) is stochastically dominated by Q_t if P_t(x,·)≤_stQ_t(x,·) for every x≥0 and t≥0.
   Source: relies_on, §5
   Note: The nonmonotone process is compared with an ordered upper process.

6. Stochastically monotone Markov semigroup on the half-line
   A Markov transition semigroup P_t on [0,∞) is stochastically monotone if P_t(x,·)≤_stP_t(y,·) whenever x≤y, for every t≥0.
   Source: relies_on, §4–§5
   Note: A pathwise ordered process is used for the convergence bound.

7. Weighted total variation norm of a signed measure
   For a measurable weight V≥1 and a signed measure μ, its weighted variation norm is ‖μ‖_V=sup{|∫f dμ|:f measurable, |f|≤V}, allowing infinity. The convention V=1 is the full total variation norm, twice the usual supremum-over-events distance for differences of probability measures.
   Source: relies_on, §1.6; §3–§7
   Note: Weighted variation is stronger than total variation and controls moments.

8. V-uniform ergodicity of a continuous-time Markov process
   A Markov semigroup P_t is V-uniformly ergodic with rate κ>0 if it has a unique invariant probability π and a finite constant D such that ‖P_t(x,·)−π‖_V≤DV(x)e^(−κt) for all x and t≥0, for a fixed measurable V≥1.
   Source: relies_on, §3–§7
   Note: This is the paper’s convergence criterion.

9. Strong Markov process
   A Markov process whose conditional future after any finite stopping time, given the past through that time, has the transition law started from its state at that stopping time.
   Source: relies_on, §2 and proof of Theorem 5.2
   Note: Post-coupling laws are identified using the strong Markov framework.

10. Fatou’s lemma
   For nonnegative measurable functions f_n on a measure space, ∫liminf_n f_n≤liminf_n∫f_n, allowing∞.
   Source: relies_on, Remark 3; proof of Theorems 4.3 and 5.2
   Note: Used to pass from nonnegative local supermartingales to supermartingales.

11. Two rank-based finite-activity Lévy particles
   Two real particles are driven by the upper and lower coordinates of a two-dimensional Lévy process with finite jump measure, drift vector (g₊,g₋) and positive definite covariance: the particle currently ranked higher uses the upper driver and the lower-ranked particle uses the lower driver, with a declared rule resolving ties. The ranked gap is the difference of their ordered positions.
   Source: relies_on, §7
   Note: Rank-based Lévy systems are inherited model vocabulary; the paper broadens correlations and jump laws.

12. Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem
   If measurable fₙ converge almost everywhere to f and |fₙ|≤g for an integrable g, then f is integrable and ∫|fₙ−f|→0; consequently their integrals converge.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem 7.2 proof
   Note: Exponential jump moments dominate the tails in the negative-drift argument.

13. Poisson point process
   A point process with intensity measure λ for which counts in disjoint measurable sets are independent and ξ(A) has Poisson law of mean λ(A) whenever λ(A)<∞.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma 4.2; §6
   Note: Shared jump times are Poisson when the total rate is constant.

14. Tower property of conditional expectation
   For sub-σ-algebras F⊆G and integrable X, E[E[X|G]|F]=E[X|F]; also E[E[X|F]|G]=E[X|F].
   Source: relies_on, §4–§5
   Note: The coupling proof uses conditional expectations.

15. Continuous-time supermartingale
   An integrable adapted process M on a filtered probability space is a supermartingale if E[M_t|F_s]≤M_s almost surely for every s≤t; the term refers to a continuous time parameter and does not require continuous sample paths.

- Quadratic covariation of càdlàg semimartingales [construction]: For real càdlàg semimartingales X,Y, their quadratic covariation is [X,Y]_t=X_tY_t−X₀Y₀−∫₀ᵗX_(s−)dY_s−∫₀ᵗY_(s−)dX_s. Its jumps satisfy Δ[X,Y]_t=ΔX_tΔY_t; [X]=[X,X] is quadratic variation.
- Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space [definition]: A Borel measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space that is finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets (each has measure equal to the infimum over open supersets) and inner regular on open sets (each has measure equal to the supremum over compact subsets). This is the Folland–Cohn convention; on σ-compact spaces it coincides with compact inner regularity on all Borel sets.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Real-valued random variable [definition]: A real-valued random variable is a measurable map from a probability space to ℝ with its Borel σ-algebra.
- Reflected Itô diffusion on the positive half-line [definition]: A continuous adapted process Y_t≥0 solves a reflected Itô equation if Y_t=Y₀+∫₀ᵗg(Y_s)ds+∫₀ᵗσ(Y_s)dW_s+L_t, where L is continuous, adapted, nondecreasing, starts at zero and can increase only when Y_t=0.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Standard Brownian motion [definition]: A continuous process B_t starting at zero with independent increments and B_t−B_s distributed N(0,t−s) for 0≤s<t. A d-dimensional standard Brownian motion has d independent standard components.
- Stochastic integral of a predictable process against a semimartingale [construction]: For a real semimartingale X and a predictable locally bounded integrand H, H·X is the canonical stochastic-integral extension of elementary predictable sums ∑H_i(X_(t∧t_(i+1))−X_(t∧t_i)), where H_i is measurable at time t_i; convergence is in probability uniformly on compact time intervals.
- Stochastic process [definition]: A family (X_t) indexed by a specified time set, with each X_t a measurable map from a probability space into a specified measurable state space. Adaptation, joint measurability and path regularity are additional conditions when required.
- Stopping time for a filtration [definition]: A random time T taking values in the time index together with infinity, such that {T≤t}∈F_t for every t; by time t one can determine whether T has occurred.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric matrix [definition]: A square matrix A equal to its transpose, so aᵢⱼ=aⱼᵢ.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Usual conditions for a filtration [definition]: A filtration on a probability space satisfies the usual conditions if it is right-continuous, F_t=⋂_(s>t)F_s, and F₀ contains every subset of every null set of the ambient probability space.
- Vague convergence of Radon measures [definition]: On a locally compact Hausdorff space, μ_n converge vaguely to μ if ∫f dμ_n→∫f dμ for every continuous compactly supported real function f.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Weak convergence of probability measures on a metric space [definition]: Measures μ_n converge weakly to μ if ∫f dμ_n→∫f dμ for every bounded continuous real function f on the space.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

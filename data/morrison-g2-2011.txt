REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (6)

- Braid-group action on a tensor power: For an object V in a braided monoidal category, σᵢ acts on V⊗n as the braiding c(V,V) on factors i,i+1 and the identity on all other factors.
- Centralizer algebra of a representation: For an H-module W, End_H(W) is the algebra of linear endomorphisms commuting with H; multiplication is composition.
- Euler measure of a surface with assigned corner angles: For a compact surface F with finitely many assigned boundary corner angles θᵢ, its Euler measure is e(F)=χ(F)−∑ᵢ(π−θᵢ)/(2π). Smooth portions of the boundary contribute no corner correction.
- Group algebra: For a group G and field k, k[G] consists of finite k-linear combinations of group elements, with multiplication extending the group law bilinearly.
- Kuperberg's G₂ spider equivalence theorem: Over ℂ(q), the G₂ spider is equivalent, as a braided tensor category, to the category whose objects are tensor powers of the seven-dimensional U_q(𝔤₂)-module and whose morphisms are intertwiners.
- Kuperberg's G₂ web-basis theorem: Over ℂ(q), each morphism space in the G₂ spider has a basis consisting of the non-elliptic webs.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (23)

1. Braid group
   Bₙ is generated by σ₁,…,σₙ₋₁, with σᵢσⱼ=σⱼσᵢ for abs(i−j)>1 and σᵢσᵢ₊₁σᵢ=σᵢ₊₁σᵢσᵢ₊₁.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

2. Group algebra
   For a group G and field k, k[G] consists of finite k-linear combinations of group elements, with multiplication extending the group law bilinearly.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

3. Quantum group U_q(𝔤₂)
   The Drinfeld–Jimbo quantized enveloping algebra of 𝔤₂; in the braid-generation theorem q is indeterminate and the coefficient field is ℂ(q).
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

4. Seven-dimensional fundamental representation of U_q(𝔤₂)
   The irreducible fundamental U_q(𝔤₂)-module of dimension seven, deforming the smallest nontrivial representation of 𝔤₂.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

5. Intertwiner of algebra representations
   A linear map T between modules over an algebra A such that T(av)=aT(v) for every a∈A and v in the source module.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

6. Tensor power of a Hopf-algebra representation
   The representation on V⊗n obtained by applying the iterated coproduct before acting on its n factors.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

7. Centralizer algebra of a representation
   For an H-module W, End_H(W) is the algebra of linear endomorphisms commuting with H; multiplication is composition.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

8. Universal R-matrix
   An invertible element R in a suitable completion of H⊗H that conjugates the coproduct to its opposite and satisfies the two coproduct compatibility identities; on modules, flip∘R gives a braiding.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

9. Braided monoidal category
   A monoidal category with natural isomorphisms c(X,Y):X⊗Y→Y⊗X satisfying the two hexagon identities.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

10. Braid-group action on a tensor power
   For an object V in a braided monoidal category, σᵢ acts on V⊗n as the braiding c(V,V) on factors i,i+1 and the identity on all other factors.
   Source: relies_on, §1, pp. 189–190

11. Kuperberg's G₂ spider
   Kuperberg’s G₂ spider T(G₂) is the ℂ(q)-linear monoidal category whose objects are natural numbers and whose morphisms n→m are ℂ(q)-linear combinations of G₂ webs in a rectangle with n bottom and m top boundary points, modulo Kuperberg’s local relations: a loop equals q¹⁰+q⁸+q²+1+q⁻²+q⁻⁸+q⁻¹⁰, a monogon vanishes, and bigons, triangles, squares and pentagons reduce to webs with fewer vertices. Composition is vertical stacking, tensor product is juxtaposition, and Kuperberg’s crossing formula, a combination of four webs, makes it braided. Here q is Morrison’s parameter; Kuperberg’s parameter is q².
   Source: explains, §1, pp. 189–190

12. G₂ web
   A compact one-dimensional subset of a closed disc or rectangle that near each point looks, up to homeomorphism, like an empty disc, an open arc or three arcs meeting at a trivalent vertex, and near the boundary like an empty half-disc or one arc ending on the boundary; closed loop components are allowed and webs are considered up to isotopy fixing the boundary. These planar trivalent graphs present the morphisms of Kuperberg’s G₂ spider.
   Source: explains, §1, pp. 189–190

13. Non-elliptic G₂ web
   A G₂ web with no closed loop components and with at least six edges on every internal face.
   Source: explains, §1, pp. 189–190

14. Kuperberg's G₂ web-basis theorem
   Over ℂ(q), each morphism space in the G₂ spider has a basis consisting of the non-elliptic webs.
   Source: relies_on, Theorems 1.1–1.2, p. 190

15. Kuperberg's G₂ spider equivalence theorem
   Over ℂ(q), the G₂ spider is equivalent, as a braided tensor category, to the category whose objects are tensor powers of the seven-dimensional U_q(𝔤₂)-module and whose morphisms are intertwiners.
   Source: relies_on, Theorems 1.1–1.2, p. 190

16. Braid generation of the G₂ tensor centralizer
   For the seven-dimensional U_q(𝔤₂)-module V over ℂ(q), the R-matrix representation induces a surjective algebra map ℂ(q)[Bₙ]→End_{U_q(𝔤₂)}(V⊗n) for every n.
   Source: explains, Theorem 2.1, pp. 191–195

17. Euler measure of a surface with assigned corner angles
   For a compact surface F with finitely many assigned boundary corner angles θᵢ, its Euler measure is e(F)=χ(F)−∑ᵢ(π−θᵢ)/(2π). Smooth portions of the boundary contribute no corner correction.
   Source: relies_on, Proof of Theorem 2.1, pp. 194–195
   Note: The proof assigns 2π/3 at trivalent corners and π/2 at boundary intersections. These choices are proof data, not part of the concept definition.

18. Strongly multiplicity-free highest-weight representation
   A finite-dimensional irreducible highest-weight representation of a complex semisimple Lie algebra or its generic Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum group, with one-dimensional weight spaces and weights totally ordered by the root order. The root order compares weights whose difference is a nonnegative integral combination of simple roots.
   Source: discusses, Remark after Theorem 2.1, p. 191

19. Lehrer–Zhang braid-generation theorem
   For a strongly multiplicity-free irreducible representation V of a generic Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum group, the braid-group image generates End_U(V⊗n) as an algebra for every n.
   Source: discusses, Remark after Theorem 2.1, p. 191

20. Tilting module for a quantum group
   A finite-dimensional module admitting both a filtration by Weyl modules and a filtration by dual Weyl modules.
   Source: discusses, §3, p. 195
   Note: These are objects in questions, not claimed equivalence theorems at roots of unity.

21. Karoubi envelope of a category
   The category obtained by formally splitting idempotent endomorphisms. Its objects are pairs (X,e) with e²=e; a map (X,e)→(Y,f) is a map a:X→Y with a=fae.
   Source: discusses, §3, p. 195
   Note: These are objects in questions, not claimed equivalence theorems at roots of unity.

22. Negligible morphism in a spherical linear category
   In a spherical k-linear category with End(1)=k, f:X→Y is negligible if tr(gf)=0 for every g:Y→X. Such morphisms form a tensor ideal.
   Source: discusses, §3, p. 195
   Note: These are objects in questions, not claimed equivalence theorems at roots of unity.

23. Semisimplification by negligible morphisms
   Quotient a spherical linear category by its negligible morphisms and take the Karoubi envelope. Under the usual finiteness and nondegeneracy hypotheses this gives a semisimple category; the construction does not guarantee semisimplicity for an arbitrary pivotal category.
   Source: discusses, §3, p. 195
   Note: These are objects in questions, not claimed equivalence theorems at roots of unity.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (107)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Abstract simplicial complex [definition]: An abstract simplicial complex on a vertex set V is a family of finite subsets of V closed under taking subsets and containing every singleton. Members are simplices, their subsets are faces, and a simplex with n+1 vertices has dimension n. An isomorphism is a vertex bijection preserving simplices in both directions.
- Associative algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity.
- Basis of a vector space [definition]: A linearly independent family of vectors whose linear span is the whole vector space.
- Bifunctor [definition]: A functor C×D→E from a product of two categories to another category.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Braided monoidal functor [definition]: A strong monoidal functor (F,J) between braided monoidal categories is braided if J_{Y,X}∘c_{F(X),F(Y)}=F(c_{X,Y})∘J_{X,Y} for all objects X,Y. A braided monoidal equivalence is a braided monoidal functor whose underlying functor is an equivalence of categories.
- Cardinality of a set [definition]: The size of a set, with two sets having the same cardinality when there is a bijection between them. A set has finite cardinality n when it is in bijection with {1,…,n}; infinite cardinalities are distinguished by the same bijection criterion.
- Cartan matrix of a root system [definition]: For a reduced finite crystallographic root system with an ordered simple system α₁,…,α_r, the Cartan matrix is the r×r integer matrix a_ij=⟨α_i∨,α_j⟩=2(α_i,α_j)/(α_i,α_i). It has a_ii=2 and a_ij≤0 for i≠j, d_i·a_ij is symmetric for d_i=(α_i,α_i)/2, and it determines the root system up to isomorphism. For G₂ it is (2 −3; −1 2) in this ordering; some authors use the transpose.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Category [definition]: Objects and morphisms with associative composition and identity morphisms.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact surface with boundary [definition]: A compact Hausdorff space in which every point has an open neighbourhood homeomorphic to an open subset of the closed half-plane {(x,y)∈ℝ²: y≥0}; points sent to the line y=0 form the boundary. Examples are the closed disc, the annulus and closed surfaces, whose boundary is empty.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Composition of functions [definition]: For f:A→B and g:B→C, the composite g∘f:A→C sends x to g(f(x)).
- Connected component of a topological space [definition]: A maximal connected subset, with connectedness understood in the subspace topology.
- Connected topological space [definition]: A topological space that is not the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets.
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex hull [definition]: The smallest convex set containing a given set of points.
- Convex subset of ℝᵈ [definition]: C⊆ℝᵈ is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and 0≤t≤1.
- Coroot of a root [definition]: In a finite root system with specified invariant inner product, the coroot associated to α is α∨=2α/(α,α), identified with the corresponding vector in the dual space via the inner product.
- Dimension of a vector space [definition]: The cardinality of a basis of the vector space; a finite basis with n vectors gives dimension n.
- Direct sum of vector spaces [definition]: The algebraic direct sum of a family of vector spaces consists of tuples with only finitely many nonzero coordinates, with coordinatewise operations.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum group U_q(𝔤) [construction]: For the Cartan matrix (a_ij) of a reduced root system, with d_i=(α_i,α_i)/2 and q_i=q^(d_i), U_q(𝔤) is the associative algebra over the rational-function field ℚ(q) (or ℂ(q)) generated by E_i,F_i,K_i^(±1) with K_iK_j=K_jK_i, K_iK_i^(−1)=1, K_iE_jK_i^(−1)=q_i^(a_ij)E_j, K_iF_jK_i^(−1)=q_i^(−a_ij)F_j, E_iF_j−F_jE_i=δ_ij(K_i−K_i^(−1))/(q_i−q_i^(−1)), and the quantum Serre relations ∑_(r=0)^(1−a_ij)(−1)^r[1−a_ij choose r]_(q_i)E_i^(1−a_ij−r)E_jE_i^r=0 and likewise for F, for i≠j, with Gaussian binomials built from quantum integers. It is a Hopf algebra with Δ(K_i)=K_i⊗K_i, Δ(E_i)=E_i⊗K_i+1⊗E_i, Δ(F_i)=F_i⊗1+K_i^(−1)⊗F_i. It deforms the universal enveloping algebra of the corresponding semisimple Lie algebra; normalizations of q and of the coproduct vary between authors.
- Eigenvalue of a linear endomorphism [definition]: A scalar λ for which a linear endomorphism T has a nonzero vector v with Tv=λv.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence of categories [definition]: A functor with an inverse up to natural isomorphism.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Euler characteristic of a finite triangulated space [definition]: For a finite simplicial complex K, χ(K)=∑_n(−1)ⁿs_n, where s_n is the number of n-simplices; in dimension two this is vertices − edges + triangles. A space homeomorphic to the geometric realization of K has Euler characteristic χ(K), independent of the chosen triangulation because it equals the alternating sum of the ranks of homology. For example, a closed disc has χ=1.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite crystallographic root system [definition]: A finite spanning set Φ of nonzero vectors in a real inner-product space, preserved by the orthogonal reflection s_α in each root α, and satisfying2(β,α)/(α,α)∈Z for all α,β∈Φ. Nonreduced systems are allowed; reduced means the only multiples of α in Φ are ±α. A simple system is a basis whose root expansions are all nonnegative or all nonpositive integer combinations.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Free Lie algebra on a set [construction]: For a set X and a field k, the free Lie algebra FL(X) is a Lie algebra with a map X→FL(X) such that every map from X to a Lie algebra 𝔤 extends uniquely to a Lie algebra homomorphism FL(X)→𝔤; it is spanned by iterated brackets of elements of X. The Lie algebra presented by generators X and relations R is the quotient of FL(X) by the ideal generated by R.
- Free associative algebra on a set [construction]: For a set X and a field k, the free algebra k⟨X⟩ has the finite words in X as a basis, with concatenation as multiplication and the empty word as identity; every map from X to a unital associative k-algebra extends uniquely to an algebra homomorphism. The algebra presented by generators X and relations R is k⟨X⟩ modulo the two-sided ideal generated by R, the smallest subspace containing R and closed under multiplication on both sides.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Functor [definition]: A map of categories preserving objects, morphisms, identities and composition.
- Fundamental weight of a root system [definition]: For a simple system α₁,…,α_r, the fundamental weights ω_i are the dual basis to the simple coroots: (ω_i,α_j∨)=δ_ij. They belong to the real root span; integrality for a specified algebraic group is a separate lattice condition.
- Geometric n-simplex [definition]: An n-simplex is conv(v₀,…,vₙ), where v₁−v₀,…,vₙ−v₀ are linearly independent. Its faces are the hulls of subsets of its vertices.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Hausdorff topological space [definition]: A topological space in which any two distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods.
- Homeomorphism [definition]: A bijection between topological spaces that is continuous and has a continuous inverse.
- Homomorphism of Lie algebras [definition]: A linear map f:𝔤→𝔥 between Lie algebras over the same field satisfying f([x,y])=[f(x),f(y)] for all x,y∈𝔤. An isomorphism is a bijective homomorphism.
- Homomorphism of algebras over a field [definition]: A linear map of unital associative algebras over the same field preserving multiplication and the multiplicative identity.
- Homotopy of continuous maps [definition]: A homotopy from continuous f₀:X→Y to f₁:X→Y is a continuous map F:X×[0,1]→Y with F(x,0)=f₀(x) and F(x,1)=f₁(x). A homotopy relative to A⊆X additionally keeps F(a,t) constant in t for each a∈A.
- Hopf algebra [definition]: A unital associative algebra H with coassociative coproduct Δ:H→H⊗H and counit ε:H→k that are algebra homomorphisms, and an antipode S satisfying m(S⊗id)Δ=m(id⊗S)Δ=ηε, where m and η are multiplication and unit.
- Ideal of a Lie algebra [definition]: A linear subspace 𝔥 of a Lie algebra 𝔤 with [x,y]∈𝔥 for all x∈𝔤 and y∈𝔥.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Isomorphism in a category [definition]: A morphism f:X→Y having an inverse g:Y→X, so gf=id_X and fg=id_Y.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lie algebra over a field [definition]: A vector space with a bilinear bracket [x,y] satisfying [x,x]=0 and [x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0.
- Lie algebra 𝔤₂ [definition]: The 14-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra of exceptional type G₂.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Linearly independent family of vectors [definition]: A family of vectors is linearly independent if every finite linear combination equalling zero has all coefficients zero.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Module over an associative algebra [definition]: A vector space on which the algebra acts linearly, compatibly with multiplication and the identity.
- Monoidal category [definition]: A category C with a tensor bifunctor C×C→C, a unit object, and natural associativity and unit isomorphisms satisfying the pentagon and triangle identities.
- Natural isomorphism [definition]: A natural transformation whose component at every object is an isomorphism.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Natural transformation [definition]: A family of maps between the values of two functors, commuting with every morphism in their source category.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Planar isotopy of tangle diagrams [definition]: A continuous deformation of embedded diagrams preserving their incidences, boundary components and marked data.
- Polynomial over a field [definition]: A formal finite sum a₀+a₁x+⋯+aₙxⁿ with coefficients in a field, with the usual coefficientwise addition and multiplication.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Product of categories [definition]: C×D has pairs of objects and pairs of morphisms, with identities and composition defined componentwise.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Quantum integer [definition]: For n≥1, [n]_q=qⁿ⁻¹+qⁿ⁻³+⋯+q¹⁻ⁿ, and [0]_q=0. Equivalently [n]_q=(qⁿ−q⁻ⁿ)/(q−q⁻¹) when the quotient is defined; the Laurent polynomial also specializes at q=±1.
- Quotient vector space [definition]: For a subspace U⊆V, the cosets V/U form a vector space with addition and scalar multiplication induced from V.
- Rational function over a field [definition]: An element of k(x), represented by a quotient p(x)/q(x) of polynomials over k with q≠0; two quotients are equal when their cross-products agree.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Representation of a Lie algebra [definition]: A representation of a Lie algebra 𝔤 over k on a vector space V is a Lie algebra homomorphism 𝔤→𝔤𝔩(V), where 𝔤𝔩(V) is the space of linear endomorphisms of V with bracket [A,B]=AB−BA. Equivalently V is a module over the universal enveloping algebra U(𝔤).
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Serre presentation of a semisimple Lie algebra from its Cartan matrix [construction]: For the Cartan matrix (a_ij) of a reduced root system R of rank r, 𝔤(R) is the complex Lie algebra generated by e_i,f_i,h_i (1≤i≤r) with relations [h_i,h_j]=0, [h_i,e_j]=a_ij e_j, [h_i,f_j]=−a_ij f_j, [e_i,f_j]=δ_ij h_i and the Serre relations (ad e_i)^(1−a_ij)e_j=0=(ad f_i)^(1−a_ij)f_j for i≠j, where (ad x)(y)=[x,y]. By Serre’s theorem 𝔤(R) is finite-dimensional with root system R, and it is simple when R is irreducible.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Simple Lie algebra [definition]: A Lie algebra 𝔤 is simple if it is not abelian (some bracket [x,y] is nonzero) and its only ideals are 0 and 𝔤.
- Simple module over an associative algebra [definition]: A module V over an associative algebra A is simple, or irreducible, if V≠0 and its only A-invariant subspaces are 0 and V.
- Strong monoidal functor [definition]: A functor equipped with coherent isomorphisms preserving tensor products and the tensor unit.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Subspace topology [definition]: For A⊆X, the topology whose open sets are A∩U for U open in X.
- Tensor equivalence [definition]: A strong monoidal functor whose underlying functor is an equivalence of categories.
- Tensor product of vector spaces [definition]: The vector space V⊗W with its universal bilinear map from V×W.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Type-1 highest-weight module of a Drinfeld–Jimbo quantum group [definition]: A U_q(𝔤)-module M is of type 1 if it is the direct sum of its weight spaces M_λ={m: K_i m=q_i^(⟨λ,α_i∨⟩)m for all i} over integral weights λ, the integer combinations of fundamental weights. A highest-weight vector is a nonzero weight vector annihilated by every E_i, and a highest-weight module is generated by one. For generic q and each dominant integral λ there is a unique finite-dimensional simple type-1 module L(λ) of highest weight λ, and these exhaust the finite-dimensional simple type-1 modules.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra [definition]: The unital associative algebra U(g) with a linear map i:g→U(g) satisfying i([x,y])=i(x)i(y)−i(y)i(x), universal for such maps from g into associative algebras. Its universal property does not include an unproved assertion of injectivity.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- k-linear category [definition]: A category whose morphism spaces are vector spaces over a field k and whose composition is k-bilinear. This convention does not require finite sums or a zero object.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.

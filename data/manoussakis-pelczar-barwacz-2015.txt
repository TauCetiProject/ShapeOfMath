REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (15)

- Banach space c₀ of null sequences: The scalar sequences tending to zero, with the supremum norm.
- Bessaga–Pełczyński selection principle: In a Banach space with a Schauder basis, every seminormalized weakly null sequence has a subsequence equivalent to a seminormalized block sequence; the block vectors can be chosen to approximate that subsequence with any prescribed summable positive error bounds.
- Block approximation of an infinite-dimensional subspace: LetX have a Schauder basis andM⊂X be an infinite-dimensional closed subspace. For any positive summable error boundsε_n, one can choose unit vectorsv_n∈M and successive finite block vectorsu_n with∥v_n−u_n∥<ε_n; the errors can be made small enough that(v_n) and(u_n) are equivalent basic sequences.
- Closed operator ideal of strictly singular maps: Strictly singular bounded maps between Banach spaces are closed under operator-norm limits, sums with the same domain and codomain, and composition on either side by bounded linear maps.
- Compact restriction of a strictly singular operator to a further block subspace: IfS:[x_n]→Y is strictly singular on a block subspace of a Banach space with basis, then[x_n] contains a further normalized block sequence(u_k) such thatS|[u_k] is compact.
- Diagonal bounded operator relative to a Schauder basis: A bounded linear operator D satisfying Deₙ=λₙeₙ for a fixed Schauder basis (eₙ).
- Diagonal-free operator on a fixed block subspace: A bounded operatorT:[x_n]→X, with(x_n) a fixed block sequence of a specified ambient Schauder basis(e_i), is diagonal-free when supp_e(Tx_n)∩supp_e(x_n)=∅ for every n. This depends on the chosen ambient basis and block sequence.
- Existence of arbitrarily long ℓ₁ averages in Gowers’s unconditional space: For every block subspace ofX_U, every integer n≥1 and every c>1, there is an ℓ₁ⁿ block average with constantc in that subspace.
- Gowers–Maurey rapidly increasing sequence of ℓ₁ averages: Fix ε>0, ε′=min(ε,1), f(t)=log₂(t+1), and M(t)=2^(36t²)−1. A finite successive sequencex₁,…,x_N is rapidly increasing with constant1+ε when∥x_k∥=1 and x_k is a sum of n_k successive vectors of norms≤(1+ε)/n_k, with n₁≥4(1+ε)log(2)M(N/ε′)/ε′ and(ε′/2)√f(n_k)≥|supp(x_(k−1))| for k≥2. This is the original quantitative convention for these spaces.
- Gowers’s disjoint-support separation estimates: InX_U, let(u_n),(v_n) be seminormalized block sequences with all supports of theu_n disjoint from all supports of thev_n, and letv_n be ℓ₁ⁿ averages with a common constant. There are successive finite setsJ_k and common coefficientsa_n with∥∑_{J_k}a_nu_n∥=1 and∥∑_{J_k}a_nv_n∥→0. Also, for any basis subsequence(e_(i_n)) with infinite complement there are normalized successive combinationsw_k=∑_{J_k}a_ne_(i_n) whose equidistributed copies∑_{J_k}a_ne_(j_n) have norms tending to0 uniformly over increasing sequences(j_n) disjoint from{i_n}.
- Kutzarova–Lin ℓ₁ spreading model in Schlumprecht’s space: Schlumprecht’s spaceS contains a seminormalized block sequence generating a spreading model equivalent to the unit vector basis ofℓ₁.
- Schlumprecht spreading model of Gowers’s unconditional basis: The unit vector basis ofX_U generates the unit vector basis ofSchlumprecht’s spaceS as a spreading model: for every finite scalar pattern(a_i), its iterated asymptotic norm along increasing basis indices is∥∑a_ie_i∥_S.
- Schlumprecht’s strong-domination and spreading-model criterion: Let (e_i) be a suppression1-unconditional,1-subsymmetric normalized basis with ∥∑_{i=1}^m e_i∥→∞, and let (y_i) a normalized basic weakly null sequence. The following are equivalent: some subsequence of (y_i) is strongly dominated by (e_i); some subsequence has a spreading model strongly dominated by (e_i); some subsequence (z_i) satisfies ∥∑a_i z_i∥≤sup_k δ_k max_{i₁<⋯<i_k}∥∑_{j=1}^k a_{i_j}e_j∥ for finitely supported a and positive δ_k↓0. Equivalently, one may use lengths ℓ_k and require k≤i₁<⋯<i_{ℓ_k} in the bound.
- Subsymmetric Schauder basis: An unconditional Schauder basis equivalent to each of its subsequences.
- ℓ¹ strong domination criterion for weakly null basic sequences: A normalized basic weakly null sequence has a subsequence strongly dominated by the ℓ¹ unit vector basis exactly when it has a subsequence whose spreading model is not equivalent to the ℓ¹ unit vector basis.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (37)

1. Banach space
   A real or complex normed vector space complete for the metric induced by its norm.
   Source: relies_on, Introduction and§§1–2
   Note: The closed spans and operator domains are Banach spaces.

2. Schauder basis of a Banach space
   A sequence (eₙ) in a Banach space for which every vector x has a unique expansion x=∑ₙaₙeₙ converging in norm.
   Source: relies_on, Notationbefore§1
   Note: Unique coordinate expansions define supports and diagonal maps.

3. Unconditional Schauder basis
   A Schauder basis for which each vector’s expansion converges unconditionally, equivalently converges to the same vector after every permutation of its terms.
   Source: relies_on, §1 throughout
   Note: Coordinate projections and bounded diagonal multipliers use unconditionality.

4. Block sequence relative to a Schauder basis
   A sequence of nonzero finitely supported vectors xₙ such that max supp(xₙ)<min supp(xₙ₊₁); its closed linear span is a block subspace.
   Source: relies_on, Notationbefore§1;§2
   Note: Block supports, block subspaces and averages are used throughout.

5. Strictly singular bounded operator
   A bounded linear operator whose restriction to no infinite-dimensional closed subspace is an isomorphism onto its image, equivalently is bounded below on no such subspace.
   Source: relies_on, Introduction;Proposition1.2
   Note: Strict singularity is the relevant operator-ideal obstruction.

6. Diagonal bounded operator relative to a Schauder basis
   A bounded linear operator D satisfying Deₙ=λₙeₙ for a fixed Schauder basis (eₙ).
   Source: relies_on, Introduction;§§1–2
   Note: The claimed decomposition uses a diagonal operator on the whole ambient space.

7. Tight-by-support Schauder basis
   A Schauder basis is tight by support if no two infinite-dimensional closed subspaces with disjoint coordinate supports are isomorphic as Banach spaces.
   Source: relies_on, Definition1.1
   Note: The definition is explicitly credited toFerenczi–Rosendal[5].

8. Weak convergence in a Banach space
   A sequence xₙ converges weakly to x if ℓ(xₙ)→ℓ(x) for every bounded linear functional ℓ on the space.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.3
   Note: The sequence is weakly null; a block sequence is a relevant special case.

27. Schlumprecht’s strong-domination and spreading-model criterion
   Let (e_i) be a suppression1-unconditional,1-subsymmetric normalized basis with ∥∑_{i=1}^m e_i∥→∞, and let (y_i) a normalized basic weakly null sequence. The following are equivalent: some subsequence of (y_i) is strongly dominated by (e_i); some subsequence has a spreading model strongly dominated by (e_i); some subsequence (z_i) satisfies ∥∑a_i z_i∥≤sup_k δ_k max_{i₁<⋯<i_k}∥∑_{j=1}^k a_{i_j}e_j∥ for finitely supported a and positive δ_k↓0.    For every block subspaceY ofX_U, any boundedT:Y→X_U hasT=D|Y+S, withD a bounded diagonal operator onX_U andS strictly singular. In particular every bounded operator diagonal-free relative to a fixed block basis ofY is strictly singular.
   Source: introduces, Proposition2.7
   Note: Original reusable result; predecessor setting checked.

39. Block approximation of an infinite-dimensional subspace

41. Coordinate support relative to a Schauder basis
   For x=∑a_i e_i in a Banach space with a specified Schauder basis, supp_e(x)={i:a_i≠0}. The support of a subspace is the union of its vectors’ coordinate supports. These supports need not be finite.
   Source: relies_on, Notation before§1
   Note: The supports of vectors, subspaces and operator images need not be finite.

42. Diagonal-free operator on a fixed block subspace

43. Gowers–Maurey rapidly increasing sequence of ℓ₁ averages
   Fix ε>0, ε′=min(ε,1), f(t)=log₂(t+1), and M(t)=2^(36t²)−1. A finite successive sequencex₁,…,x_N is rapidly increasing with constant1+ε when∥x_k∥=1 and x_k is a sum of n_k successive vectors of norms≤(1+ε)/n_k, with n₁≥4(1+ε)log(2)M(N/ε′)/ε′ and(ε′/2)√f(n_k)≥|supp(x_(k−1))| for k≥2. This is the original quantitative convention for these spaces.
   Source: relies_on, §2,p.6 andFact2.2 proof sketch
   Note: The paper uses the established RIS vocabulary but only describes its growth informally; the original exact convention is supplied here.

44. Existence of arbitrarily long ℓ₁ averages in Gowers’s unconditional space
   For every block subspace ofX_U, every integer n≥1 and every c>1, there is an ℓ₁ⁿ block average with constantc in that subspace.
   Source: relies_on, Fact2.1 and proofProposition2.7
   Note: Long averages exist in every block subspace and detect strict singularity.

45. Gowers’s disjoint-support separation estimates
   InX_U, let(u_n),(v_n) be seminormalized block sequences with all supports of theu_n disjoint from all supports of thev_n, and letv_n be ℓ₁ⁿ averages with a common constant. There are successive finite setsJ_k and common coefficientsa_n with∥∑_{J_k}a_nu_n∥=1 and∥∑_{J_k}a_nv_n∥→0. Also, for any basis subsequence(e_(i_n)) with infinite complement there are normalized successive combinationsw_k=∑_{J_k}a_ne_(i_n) whose equidistributed copies∑_{J_k}a_ne_(j_n) have norms tending to0 uniformly over increasing sequences(j_n) disjoint from{i_n}.
   Source: relies_on, Fact2.2(a),(b);Claims3–4 andProposition2.7
   Note: These exact canonical support estimates are imported, not merely familiarity with the nameX_U.

46. Schlumprecht spreading model of Gowers’s unconditional basis
   The unit vector basis ofX_U generates the unit vector basis ofSchlumprecht’s spaceS as a spreading model: for every finite scalar pattern(a_i), its iterated asymptotic norm along increasing basis indices is∥∑a_ie_i∥_S.
   Source: relies_on, Fact2.4;proofTheorem2.5

47. Kutzarova–Lin ℓ₁ spreading model in Schlumprecht’s space
   Schlumprecht’s spaceS contains a seminormalized block sequence generating a spreading model equivalent to the unit vector basis ofℓ₁.

48. Bessaga–Pełczyński selection principle
   In a Banach space with a Schauder basis, every seminormalized weakly null sequence has a subsequence equivalent to a seminormalized block sequence; the block vectors can be chosen to approximate that subsequence with any prescribed summable positive error bounds.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem1.3;small-perturbation passages inProposition2.7
   Note: The uncited standard gliding-hump selection is actual background.

49. Compact restriction of a strictly singular operator to a further block subspace
   IfS:[x_n]→Y is strictly singular on a block subspace of a Banach space with basis, then[x_n] contains a further normalized block sequence(u_k) such thatS|[u_k] is compact.
   Source: relies_on, ProofTheorem1.4,first sentence
   Note: Passing from a strictly singular map to a compact further restriction is actual uncited input.

50. Closed operator ideal of strictly singular maps
   Strictly singular bounded maps between Banach spaces are closed under operator-norm limits, sums with the same domain and codomain, and composition on either side by bounded linear maps.
   Source: relies_on, Proposition1.2,Lemma1.6,Theorem1.4 andProposition2.7
   Note: Compositions, sums and restrictions of strictly singular maps are repeatedly used.

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (80)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Bounded linear functional [definition]: A linear map from a real or complex normed vector space to its scalar field satisfying |ℓ(x)|≤C∥x∥ for one finite C and every x.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact linear operator [definition]: A bounded linear operator between normed spaces that maps every bounded subset to a relatively compact subset.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Completion of a metric space [definition]: A complete metric space containing an isometric dense copy of the given metric space. It is unique up to an isometry fixing that copy.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Counting measure [definition]: The measure on all subsets of a set that assigns a finite set its cardinality and an infinite set the value∞.
- Dense subset of a metric space [definition]: A subset A of a metric space X is dense if every open ball centered in X with positive radius meets A.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Equidistributed finitely supported basis vectors [definition]: Two finitely supported vectors relative to specified ordered bases are equidistributed when the increasing bijection between their nonzero coordinate supports preserves every coefficient. This means the same ordered coefficient pattern, not equality of probability laws.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Finitely supported scalar sequence [definition]: A scalar sequence with only finitely many nonzero terms; these sequences form the vector space c₀₀.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Isomorphism of Banach spaces [definition]: A bounded linear bijection between Banach spaces whose inverse is bounded.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Norming family of linear functionals [definition]: A family F of linear functionals on a normed space X is norming when ∥x∥=sup{|f(x)|:f∈F} for every x∈X.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Operator norm of a bounded linear operator [definition]: For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Relatively compact subset of a topological space [definition]: A subset whose closure is compact.
- Seminormalized sequence in a normed space [definition]: A sequence(x_n) for which0<inf_n∥x_n∥≤sup_n∥x_n∥<∞.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Suppression 1-unconditional, 1-subsymmetric normalized basis [definition]: A normalized Schauder basis whose coordinate-suppression projections have norm at most one and whose subsequences are isometrically equivalent to the whole basis.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

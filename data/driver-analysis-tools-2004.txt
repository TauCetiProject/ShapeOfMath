LOCATED CONCEPTS (9)

1. Completeness of continuous Banach-valued function spaces
   For a compact metric space K and Banach space X, the continuous maps K→X form a Banach space for the supremum norm.
   Source: explains, Lemma 7.3 and Remark 7.6, pp. 57–58
   Note: Selected proof exposition; Remark 7.6 states the Banach-valued case with the same proof.

2. Density of compactly supported continuous functions in Radon Lᵖ
   If μ is a Radon measure on a locally compact second-countable Hausdorff space and1≤p<∞, then compactly supported continuous functions are dense in Lᵖ(μ).
   Source: explains, Theorem 28.22 and Proposition 28.23, pp. 550–551
   Note: Selected proof exposition; not an origin claim.

3. Regularity of locally finite Borel measures on σ-compact locally compact spaces
   On a locally compact Hausdorff space in which every open set is σ-compact, in particular a second-countable one, every Borel measure finite on compact sets is outer regular by open sets and inner regular by compact sets on every Borel set.
   Source: explains, Theorem 28.22, p. 550
   Note: Statement and proof via Riesz–Markov; not an origin claim.

4. Urysohn lemma for locally compact Hausdorff spaces
   If K is a compact subset of an open set U in a locally compact Hausdorff space X, there is a continuous f:X→[0,1] with f=1 on K whose support is compact and contained in U.
   Source: explains, Lemma 15.8
   Note: Statement located; proof not inspected.

5. Inner regularity of finite Borel measures on the real line
   For a finite Borel measureρ onℝ and a Borel setA, ρ(A)=sup{ρ(K):K⊆A compact}.
   Source: explains, Corollary 33.11 and proof, pp. 679–680
   Note: Stated for measures finite on compacts on open subsets of ℝⁿ; the root is the finite case with n=1. Not an origin claim.

6. Closed–open regularity of finite Borel measures on metric spaces
   For a finite Borel measure μ on a metric space X and every Borel set A⊆X, μ(A)=sup{μ(F):F⊆A closed}=inf{μ(U):U⊇A open}; equivalently, for each ε>0 there are F closed and U open with F⊆A⊆U and μ(U∖F)<ε.
   Source: explains, Exercises 33.1–33.5, pp. 678–679
   Note: Set as exercises (monotone-class argument; closed sets are decreasing intersections of open neighbourhoods in a metric space).

7. Sard theorem for a smooth scalar function
   For a smooth functionf:Ω⊂ℝⁿ→ℝ, the set of critical valuesf({x:Df(x)=0}) has Lebesgue measure zero.
   Source: explains, §20.6, Theorem 20.33 and proof (Milnor’s route), pp. 376–380
   Note: General Sard theorem; scalar case of the hard case m>n.

8. Radon measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space
   A Borel measure on a locally compact Hausdorff space that is finite on compact sets, outer regular on Borel sets (each has measure equal to the infimum over open supersets) and inner regular on open sets (each has measure equal to the supremum over compact subsets). This is the Folland–Cohn convention; on σ-compact spaces it coincides with compact inner regularity on all Borel sets.
   Source: explains, Definitions 28.12–28.13, pp. 545–546
   Note: Defines Radon measures on a locally compact Hausdorff space as K-finite, outer regular on Borel sets and inner regular on open sets (Folland convention). Exposition; not an origin claim.

9. Uniqueness of Radon measures from continuous tests
   Two nonnegative Radon Borel measures on a locally compact Hausdorff space are equal if they give the same integral to every real continuous compactly supported function.
   Source: explains, Theorem 28.16, injectivity part of the proof, p. 547
   Note: Proves injectivity of μ↦I_μ on Radon measures, i.e. uniqueness from Cᶜ integrals, for arbitrary locally compact Hausdorff X. Exposition; not an origin claim.

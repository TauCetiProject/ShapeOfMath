REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (14)

- Aldous–Diaconis optimal strong stationary time theorem: For an irreducible aperiodic finite Markov chain with positive stationary law, there exists a strong stationary time T with P(T>n)=sep(Law(X_n),π) for every n; any strong stationary time has tail at least this separation distance.
- Chebyshev's inequality: If EX²<∞ and t>0, then P(abs(X−EX)≥t)≤Var(X)/t².
- Convergence to equilibrium for finite irreducible aperiodic Markov chains: Every finite irreducible aperiodic Markov chain has a unique strictly positive stationary law π, and Pⁿ(x,y)→π(y) for every pair of states, hence in total variation from every initial law.
- Generator of a finite continuous-time Markov chain: The real matrix Q with Q(x,y)=lim_{h↓0}(P_h(x,y)−1_{x=y})/h, nonnegative off-diagonal entries and zero row sums; off-diagonal entries are jump rates.
- Griffeath’s maximal coupling theorem for Markov chains: Finite Markov chains with the same transition kernel can be coupled so that their coalescence time T satisfies P(T>n)=d_TV(Law(X_n),Law(Y_n)) for every n. Such a coupling need not be co-adapted.
- Interlacing of consecutive orthogonal-polynomial zeros: For n≥1 let p₀,…,p_n be monic orthogonal polynomials with positive norms for a positive measure having at least n+1 support points and the required finite moments. The next polynomial p_{n+1}=(x−a_n)p_n−b_np_{n−1}, where a_n=∫xp_n²/‖p_n‖² and b_n=‖p_n‖²/‖p_{n−1}‖², has simple real zeros strictly interlacing those of p_n. If the measure has exactly n+1 distinct support points with positive masses, p_{n+1} is their monic vanishing polynomial, even though its measure norm is zero.
- Karlin–McGregor spectral representation for a finite birth–death chain: For an irreducible birth–death transition matrix P on{0,…,N}, stationary lawπ, and recurrence polynomials φ₀=1 with Pφ(λ)=λφ(λ), there is a probability measureν on its real eigenvalues in[−1,1] such that ∫φ_xφ_y,dν=1_{x=y}π(0)/π(x) and Pⁿ(x,y)=π(y)/π(0)∫λⁿφ_x(λ)φ_y(λ)dν(λ), n≥0.
- Killed transition matrix of a finite Markov chain: For a set A, retain the submatrix P_{Aᶜ,Aᶜ}; missing row mass records killing when the chain enters A. Its powers give survival transition probabilities before hitting A.
- Matrix exponential: For a finite square real or complex matrix A, exp(A)=∑_{n≥0}Aⁿ/n!, with convergence in any matrix norm.
- Perron–Frobenius theorem for nonnegative matrices: A nonnegative real matrix has a nonnegative eigenvalue equal to its spectral radius. If it is irreducible, that eigenvalue is simple and has a strictly positive eigenvector, unique up to scalar.
- Riemann integral: A bounded f:[a,b]→ℝ has Riemann integral I if all tagged Riemann sums approach I as the maximum length of the partition intervals tends to zero.
- Spectral gap of a reversible finite Markov chain: For an irreducible reversible transition matrix with eigenvalues 1=λ₀>λ₁≥⋯, its spectral gap is 1−λ₁; the absolute spectral gap instead uses max_{i≥1}|λ_i|.
- Stochastically monotone Markov kernel: On a partially ordered finite state space, P is monotone if Pf is increasing whenever f is increasing, equivalently P(x,·) is stochastically dominated by P(y,·) whenever x≤y.
- Total variation cutoff for a family of finite Markov chains: For chosen initial laws, a family has cutoff at t_N if d_N(ct_N)→1 for every 0<c<1 and d_N(ct_N)→0 for every c>1, where d_N is distance to stationarity.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (42)

1. Time-homogeneous Markov chain on a finite state space
   A sequence of random states in a finite set whose conditional next-state distribution given its whole past depends only on the present state, through one fixed stochastic transition matrix.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–4

2. Stationary distribution of a finite Markov chain
   A probability row vector π satisfying πP=π for the chain’s transition matrix P.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–4

3. Irreducible finite Markov chain
   A finite Markov chain is irreducible if for any states x,y some number of steps n has Pⁿ(x,y)>0.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–4

4. Aperiodic finite Markov chain
   A state has period equal to the gcd of its possible positive return times. An irreducible chain is aperiodic when this common period is one.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–4

5. Reversible finite Markov chain
   A finite Markov chain with a positive stationary distribution π satisfying detailed balance; under π its finite path laws are invariant under reversal of time.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–4

6. Detailed balance for a finite Markov chain
   A positive probability vector π satisfies detailed balance for P if π(x)P(x,y)=π(y)P(y,x) for every pair of states.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–4

7. Total variation distance between finite probability distributions
   For probability mass functions p,q on the same finite set, d_TV(p,q)=½∑_ω|p(ω)−q(ω)|=sup_A|p(A)−q(A)|.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–3

8. Randomized stopping time
   A stopping time after the process filtration has been enlarged by auxiliary randomization independent of the original process.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–3

9. Hitting time of a set for a Markov chain
   For A in the state space, T_A=inf{t≥0:X_t∈A}, with infinity if A is never visited. The starting state or initial law is specified separately.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–3

10. Strong stationary time of a Markov chain
   A randomized stopping time T such that X_T has the stationary distribution and is independent of T.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–3

11. Maximal coupling of finite probability distributions
   A coupling (X,Y) of p and q with P(X≠Y)=d_TV(p,q), attaining the smallest possible disagreement probability.
   Source: relies_on, §§1–3

12. Griffeath’s maximal coupling theorem for Markov chains
   Finite Markov chains with the same transition kernel can be coupled so that their coalescence time T satisfies P(T>n)=d_TV(Law(X_n),Law(Y_n)) for every n. Such a coupling need not be co-adapted.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.1–2.3
   Note: The source explicitly credits these earlier constructions toGriffeath,Lindvall andAldous–Diaconis; its reformulation is not a new theorem identity.

13. Aldous–Diaconis optimal strong stationary time theorem
   For an irreducible aperiodic finite Markov chain with positive stationary law, there exists a strong stationary time T with P(T>n)=sep(Law(X_n),π) for every n; any strong stationary time has tail at least this separation distance.
   Source: relies_on, §§2.1–2.3
   Note: The source explicitly credits these earlier constructions toGriffeath,Lindvall andAldous–Diaconis; its reformulation is not a new theorem identity.

14. Finite birth-and-death chain
   A finite Markov chain on {0,…,N} that can move only to neighboring states or remain at its current state; continuous-time versions have jump rates only between neighbors.
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3–4.3 andAppendix5.1

15. Stochastically monotone Markov kernel
   On a partially ordered finite state space, P is monotone if Pf is increasing whenever f is increasing, equivalently P(x,·) is stochastically dominated by P(y,·) whenever x≤y.
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3–4.3 andAppendix5.1

16. Spectral gap of a reversible finite Markov chain
   For an irreducible reversible transition matrix with eigenvalues 1=λ₀>λ₁≥⋯, its spectral gap is 1−λ₁; the absolute spectral gap instead uses max_{i≥1}|λ_i|.
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3–4.3 andAppendix5.1

17. Killed transition matrix of a finite Markov chain
   For a set A, retain the submatrix P_{Aᶜ,Aᶜ}; missing row mass records killing when the chain enters A. Its powers give survival transition probabilities before hitting A.
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3–4.3 andAppendix5.1

18. Eigenvalue of a linear endomorphism
   A scalar λ for which a linear endomorphism T has a nonzero vector v with Tv=λv.
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3–4.3 andAppendix5.1

19. Perron–Frobenius theorem for nonnegative matrices
   A nonnegative real matrix has a nonnegative eigenvalue equal to its spectral radius. If it is irreducible, that eigenvalue is simple and has a strictly positive eigenvector, unique up to scalar.
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3–4.3 andAppendix5.1

20. Time-homogeneous continuous-time Markov chain on a finite set
   A right-continuous jump process on a finite state space whose future conditional law depends only on its current state and elapsed time. Its transition matrices form a semigroup.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2 andAppendix5.2

21. Generator of a finite continuous-time Markov chain
   The real matrix Q with Q(x,y)=lim_{h↓0}(P_h(x,y)−1_{x=y})/h, nonnegative off-diagonal entries and zero row sums; off-diagonal entries are jump rates.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2 andAppendix5.2

22. Matrix exponential
   For a finite square real or complex matrix A, exp(A)=∑_{n≥0}Aⁿ/n!, with convergence in any matrix norm.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2 andAppendix5.2

23. Derivative of a real function
   The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2 andAppendix5.2

24. Riemann integral
   A bounded f:[a,b]→ℝ has Riemann integral I if all tagged Riemann sums approach I as the maximum length of the partition intervals tends to zero.
   Source: relies_on, §2.2 andAppendix5.2

25. Sequence of orthogonal polynomials
   A sequence of real polynomials of successive degrees, orthogonal under ∫p(x)q(x)dμ(x) for a specified positive measure with the necessary finite moments.
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3,4.1,4.3 andAppendix5.1

26. Expectation of an integrable real random variable
   If E abs(X)=∫abs(X)dP<∞, the expectation of X is EX=∫X dP.
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3,4.1,4.3 andAppendix5.1

27. Variance of a real random variable
   If EX²<∞, then Var(X)=E[(X−EX)²].
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3,4.1,4.3 andAppendix5.1

28. Chebyshev's inequality
   If EX²<∞ and t>0, then P(abs(X−EX)≥t)≤Var(X)/t².
   Source: relies_on, §§3.3,4.1,4.3 andAppendix5.1

29. Separation distance from a positive finite distribution
   For a probability mass function p and a strictly positive π, sep(p,π)=max_x(1−p(x)/π(x)). It is generally not symmetric.
   Source: explains, §1

30. Total variation mixing time
   For a finite chain and chosen initial law, t_mix(ε)=inf{t:d_TV(Law(X_t),π)≤ε}. Worst-case mixing time takes the maximum over starting states.
   Source: explains, §1

31. Total variation cutoff for a family of finite Markov chains
   For chosen initial laws, a family has cutoff at t_N if d_N(ct_N)→1 for every 0<c<1 and d_N(ct_N)→0 for every c>1, where d_N is distance to stationarity.
   Source: explains, §1

32. Cutoff window in total variation
   A scale w_N=o(t_N) is a cutoff window if lim_{γ→∞}liminf_N d_N(t_N−γw_N)=1 and lim_{γ→∞}limsup_N d_N(t_N+γw_N)=0, with integer times rounded in discrete time.
   Source: explains, §1

33. Gilbert–Shannon–Reeds riffle shuffle
   Split a deck at a binomially chosen position and interleave the two packets uniformly while preserving each packet’s internal order. Repeating gives a Markov chain on permutations.
   Source: explains, §§3.2,4.3.5–4.3.6

- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Uniform distribution on the unit interval [definition]: The real probability law on [0,1] whose probability of an interval [a,b]⊆[0,1] is b−a.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

1. Kirszbraun extension theorem for Hilbert spaces
   EveryL-Lipschitz map from a subset of a real Hilbert space to a real Hilbert space extends to the whole domain Hilbert space with the same Lipschitz constant. Complex Hilbert spaces may be viewed as real for this metric assertion.
   Source: explains, §4, Theorem 4.1 and proof via Theorem 2.3, Proposition 2.5, Theorem 4.3, Corollaries 4.4–4.5, pp. 13–14
   Note: Exposition of Valentine’s Hilbert-space proof; not a priority claim.

2. Kirszbraun extension theorem for Euclidean spaces
   Every L-Lipschitz map from a subset of ℝᵐ to ℝⁿ, both with Euclidean norms, extends to an L-Lipschitz map on all of ℝᵐ. Equivalently (Kirszbraun’s intersection property), if uᵢ∈ℝᵐ and vᵢ∈ℝⁿ (i≤k) satisfy ‖vᵢ−vⱼ‖≤‖uᵢ−uⱼ‖, then every x∈ℝᵐ has some y∈ℝⁿ with ‖y−vᵢ‖≤‖x−uᵢ‖ for all i.
   Source: explains, Theorems 1.4, 2.7 and 2.8, pp. 3, 7
   Note: Statement and equivalent intersection-property forms, credited to Kirszbraun 1934; not a priority claim.

3. Finite intersection property for closed bounded convex sets in a Hilbert space
   If a nonempty family of closed bounded convex subsets of a Hilbert space has the finite intersection property, meaning every finite subfamily has nonempty intersection, then the whole family has nonempty intersection.
   Source: explains, Lemma 4.2 and Theorem 4.3, pp. 13–14
   Note: Proof following Šmulian-type arguments credited in the text; not a priority claim.

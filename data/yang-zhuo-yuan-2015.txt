REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (18)

- Almost orthogonality of dyadically dilated Schwartz kernels: If φ,ψ∈S(ℝⁿ) both have vanishing moments below the integer L≥0 and φⱼ(x)=2ʲⁿφ(2ʲx), ψₖ(x)=2ᵏⁿψ(2ᵏx), then for every M>0, |φⱼ*ψₖ(x)|≤C2^{−L|j−k|}2^{n min(j,k)}(1+2^min(j,k)|x|)⁻ᴹ. For j≥k only φ needs the moment condition; for k≥j only ψ does. Constants may depend on the fixed kernels, L,M,n, but not j,k,x.
- Calderón reproducing formula for an inhomogeneous dyadic resolution: Let φ₀,ψ₀ be fixed Schwartz kernels and, for j≥1, let φ_j,ψ_j be the standard dyadic dilations of fixed Schwartz kernels with annular Fourier support, with φ̂₀ψ̂₀+∑_{j≥1}φ̂_jψ̂_j=1 and the high-frequency terms supported in dyadic annuli. Then f=ψ₀*φ₀*f+∑_{j≥1}ψ_j*φ_j*f in S′ for every tempered distribution f.
- Characteristic-function norm estimates in variable-exponent Lebesgue spaces: Let 0<p⁻≤p⁺<∞ and p be globally log-Hölder. For a cube Q, ∥χ_Q∥_{p(·)}≈|Q|^{1/p(x)} for |Q|≤1 and x∈Q, and ≈|Q|^{1/p∞} for |Q|≥1. If Q₁⊂Q₂, their norm ratio lies between constant multiples of (|Q₁|/|Q₂|)^{1/p⁻} and (|Q₁|/|Q₂|)^{1/p⁺}. If p⁻>1, ∥χ_Q∥_{p(·)}∥χ_Q∥_{p′(·)}≈|Q|.
- Doubling cube gauge: A positive measurable function φ(x,r) on ℝⁿ×(0,∞), viewed as a weight of the cube centered at x with side r, is doubling if φ(x,2r) is comparable to φ(x,r), uniformly in x,r. Write c̃₁⁻¹≤φ(x,r)/φ(x,2r)≤c₁; neither chosen constant must be at least one.
- Dual norm formula in variable-exponent Lebesgue spaces: For measurable p with 1<p⁻≤p⁺<∞ and p′=p/(p−1), the associate norm sup{∫|fg|:∥g∥_{p′(·)}≤1} lies between ∥f∥_{p(·)} and 2∥f∥_{p(·)}. This formula is valid also for a measurable f whose norm is initially allowed to be infinite.
- Embedding of variable-exponent Lebesgue spaces with a common limit at infinity: If globally log-Hölder exponents p₁,p₂ have positive finite lower and upper bounds, p₁≤p₂, and the same limit p∞ at infinity, then L^{p₂(·)}(ℝⁿ) continuously embeds into L^{p₁(·)}(ℝⁿ).
- Frazier–Jawerth φ-transform: A discrete analysis map sends a tempered distribution to its scale-normalized convolutions with admissible band-limited Schwartz functions sampled at dyadic cube corners; a dual family reconstructs it by summing the associated atoms.
- Hyperplane trace operator for a function space: The restriction map f(x₁,…,x_n)↦f(x₁,…,x_{n−1},0), initially on smooth functions; a trace on a larger function space is a continuous extension of this map when such an extension exists.
- Hölder inequality in variable-exponent Lebesgue spaces: For measurable p with 1<p⁻≤p⁺<∞ and 1/p(x)+1/p′(x)=1, ∫|fg|≤2∥f∥_{p(·)}∥g∥_{p′(·)}.
- Littlewood–Paley characterization of variable-exponent Lebesgue spaces: For globally log-Hölder p with 1<p⁻≤p⁺<∞ and a fixed admissible inhomogeneous Schwartz resolution, L^{p(·)}(ℝⁿ) consists exactly of the tempered distributions with finite ∥(∑_{j≥0}|ϕⱼ*f|²)^{1/2}∥_{p(·)}, and this square-function norm is equivalent to the Luxemburg norm.
- Locally comparable cube gauge: A positive measurable cube gauge φ(x,r) is locally comparable if some C≥1 satisfies C⁻¹≤φ(x,r)/φ(y,r)≤C whenever |x−y|≤r. It compares cubes of the same size at nearby centers.
- Log-Hölder extension from a coordinate hyperplane: Every bounded globally log-Hölder real function g on ℝⁿ⁻¹ extends to a globally log-Hölder function on ℝⁿ with the same limit at infinity and values between inf g and sup g. For any prescribed A>0, the extension may be chosen equal to g(x′) throughout the strip |t|≤A.
- Minkowski inequality for Lᵖ norms: For 1≤p≤∞ and f,g∈Lᵖ of a measure space, ∥f+g∥_p≤∥f∥_p+∥g∥_p.
- Peetre maximal estimate by dyadic local means: Fix an admissible inhomogeneous Schwartz resolution (Φⱼ), a>0, r>0 and an integer N≥a. For f∈S′ and ℓ≥0, [sup_z |Φℓ*f(x+z)|/(1+2^ℓ|z|)^a]^r≤C∑_{v≥0}2⁻ᵛᴺʳ2⁽ᵛ⁺ℓ⁾ⁿ∫|Φᵥ₊ℓ*f(y)|^r/(1+2^ℓ|x−y|)^{ar}dy. The constant depends on the fixed resolution and parameters, not f,ℓ,x; an infinite right side is allowed.
- Peetre submean inequality for band-limited functions: For r>0 and m>n there is C such that every tempered distribution g with Fourier support in {|ξ|≤2^{ν+1}}, ν≥0, satisfies |g(x)|≤C[ηᵥ,ₘ*|g|^r(x)]^{1/r}, where ηᵥ,ₘ(x)=2^{νn}(1+2^ν|x|)⁻ᵐ. The band-limited distribution is represented by a smooth function; C is independent of g,ν,x.
- Power triangle inequality in variable-exponent Lebesgue spaces: For a bounded positive measurable exponent p and 0<r≤min(1,p⁻), the Luxemburg quasi-norm satisfies ∥∑ᵢfᵢ∥_{p(·)}^r≤∑ᵢ∥fᵢ∥_{p(·)}^r for every finite family; the analogous countable inequality holds whenever the sum of the pointwise absolute values defines a measurable function.
- Smooth molecule for dyadic function-space decompositions: A smooth molecule associated to a dyadic cube has prescribed vanishing moments and scale-normalized derivative bounds with polynomial decay away from that cube, instead of compact support.
- Vector-valued convolution inequality in variable Lebesgue spaces: If p,q are globally log-Hölder with 1<p⁻≤p⁺<∞ and 1<q⁻≤q⁺<∞, then for m>n the kernels ηⱼ,m(x)=2ʲⁿ(1+2ʲ|x|)⁻ᵐ satisfy ∥(ηⱼ,m*fⱼ)ⱼ∥_{L^{p(·)}(ℓ^{q(·)})}≤C∥(fⱼ)ⱼ∥_{L^{p(·)}(ℓ^{q(·)})}.

LOCATED CONCEPTS (40)

1. Tempered distribution
   A continuous linear functional on the Schwartz space S(ℝⁿ).
   Source: relies_on, Definition1.4;§§2–4
   Note: The spaces and reconstructions are in S′, not merely pointwise functions.

2. Schwartz space
   The space S(ℝⁿ) of smooth functions φ for which supₓ|xᵅ∂ᵝφ(x)| is finite for every pair of multi-indices α,β, with the topology of these seminorms.
   Source: relies_on, §1 beforeDefinition1.4
   Note: Admissible frequency-localized kernels and test functions.

3. Fourier transform on Euclidean space
   For an integrable f on ℝⁿ, its Fourier transform is f̂(ξ)=∫f(x)e⁻ⁱˣ·ξ dx; extend it to Schwartz functions and tempered distributions by duality.
   Source: relies_on, §1 equations1.2–1.3;§2
   Note: The dyadic partition and reconstruction use Fourier supports.

4. Convolution on Euclidean space
   For functions with an appropriate integrability condition, (f*g)(x)=∫f(x−y)g(y)dy; convolution of a tempered distribution with a Schwartz function is defined by duality.
   Source: relies_on, Definition1.4 and§2
   Note: All dyadic pieces are convolution operators.

5. Variable-exponent Lebesgue space
   For measurable p:ℝⁿ→(0,∞) with 0<ess inf p≤ess sup p<∞, L^{p(·)} consists of measurable functions of finite Luxemburg quasi-norm inf{λ>0:∫|f(x)/λ|^{p(x)}dx≤1}, modulo equality almost everywhere.
   Source: relies_on, §1 pp.4–5
   Note: The Luxemburg quasi-norm permits p⁻<1; not restricted to the Banach range.

6. Mixed variable Lebesgue–sequence space L^{p(·)}(ℓ^{q(·)})
   A sequence of measurable functions (fⱼ) has quasi-norm ∥(∑ⱼ|fⱼ(·)|^{q(·)})^{1/q(·)}∥_{L^{p(·)}}; functions are identified almost everywhere.
   Source: relies_on, §1 p.3; Lemma2.9
   Note: The pointwise variable sequence exponent is essential.

7. Log-Hölder continuous variable exponent
   An exponent p is locally log-Hölder if |p(x)−p(y)|≤C/log(e+1/|x−y|); global log-Hölder continuity additionally requires |p(x)−p∞|≤C/log(e+|x|) for a constant p∞.
   Source: relies_on, §1 equation1.1
   Note: Local and global regularity hypotheses must be distinguished.

8. Dyadic cube in Euclidean space
   A half-open cube 2⁻ʲ([0,1)ⁿ+k), with j∈ℤ and k∈ℤⁿ.
   Source: relies_on, §1 p.6
   Note: Scale/location indexing and cube-wise supremum.

9. Vector-valued convolution inequality in variable Lebesgue spaces
   If p,q are globally log-Hölder with 1<p⁻≤p⁺<∞ and 1<q⁻≤q⁺<∞, then for m>n the kernels ηⱼ,m(x)=2ʲⁿ(1+2ʲ|x|)⁻ᵐ satisfy ∥(ηⱼ,m*fⱼ)ⱼ∥_{L^{p(·)}(ℓ^{q(·)})}≤C∥(fⱼ)ⱼ∥_{L^{p(·)}(ℓ^{q(·)})}.
   Source: relies_on, Lemma2.9
   Note: Explicitly imported as Diening–Hästö–Roudenko[20] Theorem3.2; not new here.

10. Peetre maximal function of a band-limited function
   For scale j and a>0, the Peetre maximal function is f*_{j,a}(x)=sup_y |f(y)|/(1+2ʲ|x−y|)^a, used for functions localized to a frequency band of size 2ʲ.
   Source: relies_on, Theorem3.11
   Note: Equivalent maximal-function characterization.

11. Smooth atom for dyadic function-space decompositions
   A smooth atom associated to a dyadic cube Q is supported in a fixed dilation of Q, obeys scale-normalized derivative bounds up to a specified order, and has vanishing polynomial moments up to a specified order.
   Source: relies_on, Definition3.6;Theorem3.8
   Note: Scale normalization, support and moment conditions.

12. Smooth molecule for dyadic function-space decompositions
   A smooth molecule associated to a dyadic cube has prescribed vanishing moments and scale-normalized derivative bounds with polynomial decay away from that cube, instead of compact support.
   Source: relies_on, Definition3.6;Theorem3.8
   Note: Decaying molecules yield bounded synthesis.

13. Frazier–Jawerth φ-transform
   A discrete analysis map sends a tempered distribution to its scale-normalized convolutions with admissible band-limited Schwartz functions sampled at dyadic cube corners; a dual family reconstructs it by summing the associated atoms.
   Source: relies_on, §2 beforeTheorem2.3
   Note: Classical transform is credited to Frazier–Jawerth[27]; the new result concerns its boundedness for the new scale.

14. Variable-exponent Lebesgue space
   For measurable p:ℝⁿ→(0,∞) with 0<ess inf p≤ess sup p<∞, L^{p(·)} consists of measurable functions of finite Luxemburg quasi-norm inf{λ>0:∫|f(x)/λ|^{p(x)}dx≤1}, modulo equality almost everywhere.
   Source: explains, §1 pp.4–5
   Note: Recalls the Luxemburg construction and attributes the older variable-exponent literature.

15. Log-Hölder continuous variable exponent
   An exponent p is locally log-Hölder if |p(x)−p(y)|≤C/log(e+1/|x−y|); global log-Hölder continuity additionally requires |p(x)−p∞|≤C/log(e+|x|) for a constant p∞.
   Source: explains, §1 p.5
   Note: The continuity conventions are explicitly recalled.

16. Triebel–Lizorkin-type space with variable exponents and cube weight
   For bounded positive exponents p,q with globally log-Hölder reciprocals, bounded locally log-Hölder smoothness s, and a positive doubling/locally comparable cube weight φ, F^{s(·),φ}_{p(·),q(·)} consists of tempered distributions with finite sup_P φ(P)⁻¹∥(∑_{j≥max(0,−log₂ℓ(P))}(2^{js(·)}|ϕⱼ*f|)^{q(·)})^{1/q(·)}∥_{L^{p(·)}(P)}. Here P ranges over dyadic cubes and the admissible Schwartz family includes a low-frequency term at j=0.
   Source: introduces, Definition1.4 and§1 contribution discussion
   Note: New combined variable-smoothness/integrability/cube-weight scale.
   Origin evidence: The introduction explicitly distinguishes the earlier constant-exponent type spaces[76,77,80] and earlier variable-exponent unweighted spaces[20,35], and announces the combined F^{s(·),φ}_{p(·),q(·)} scale introduced inDefinition1.4.

17. φ-transform characterization of variable-exponent Triebel–Lizorkin-type spaces
   For the admissible p,q,s,φ defining F^{s(·),φ}_{p(·),q(·)}, the Frazier–Jawerth analysis and synthesis maps are bounded between this function space and its matching dyadic coefficient space, and analysis followed by synthesis reconstructs the distribution: TψSϕ=id. In particular the function-space quasi-norm is independent, up to equivalence, of the admissible Schwartz pair.
   Source: introduces, Theorem2.3 andCorollary2.4
   Note: Main bounded analysis/reconstruction theorem for the new scale.
   Origin evidence: §2 states this as the characterization for the newly defined scale; the paper credits the transform itself toFrazier–Jawerth, and the proof extends earlier estimates using the imported variable convolution bound. ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (87)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Compact topological space [definition]: A topological space in which every open cover has a finite subcover.
- Compactly supported smooth test function on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A smooth function on an open Ω⊂ℝⁿ whose support is a compact subset of Ω; these form C_c^∞(Ω).
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complex exponential function [definition]: The entire function exp(z)=∑ₙ₌₀∞zⁿ/n!; on imaginary arguments exp(it)=cos(t)+i sin(t).
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Complex power series [definition]: A series ∑ₙ₌₀∞aₙ(z−z₀)ⁿ with complex coefficients, considered at points where its partial sums converge.
- Composition of functions [definition]: For f:A→B and g:B→C, the composite g∘f:A→C sends x to g(f(x)).
- Continuous map between topological spaces [definition]: A map is continuous if the preimage of every open subset of its target is open in its domain.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex subset of a real vector space [definition]: A subset C of a real vector space is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and0≤t≤1. The vector space may be infinite-dimensional.
- Derivative of a real function [definition]: The derivative f′(x) is the finite limit of (f(x+h)−f(x))/h as h→0, when it exists. Higher derivatives are defined by iteration.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Distribution on an open Euclidean set [definition]: A continuous linear functional on C_c^∞(Ω), where test functions on each fixed compact support converge with all derivatives uniformly. It need not extend to a tempered distribution.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Integer [definition]: An integer is an element of the ordered ring ℤ, obtained by adjoining additive inverses to the natural numbers.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Lebesgue measure on Euclidean space [definition]: The completion of the Borel measure on ℝⁿ assigning each rectangular box the product of its side lengths.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Locally convex topological vector space [definition]: A real or complex topological vector space whose zero has a neighborhood base of convex sets; equivalently its topology can be generated by seminorms.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Quasi-normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space with a positive definite absolutely homogeneous function q satisfying q(x+y)≤C(q(x)+q(y)) for a fixed C≥1.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real polynomial in finitely many variables [definition]: A finite real linear combination of monomials x₁^{a₁}⋯x_d^{a_d} with nonnegative integer exponents.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Seminorm on a real or complex vector space [definition]: A nonnegative function p with p(ax)=|a|p(x) and p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y); p may vanish on nonzero vectors.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Test-function topology on C_c^∞ of an open Euclidean set [definition]: The finest locally convex topology making every inclusion D_K→C_c^∞(Ω) continuous, where D_K consists of smooth functions supported in a fixed compact K⊂Ω and has the seminorms max_{|α|≤m}sup|∂^αφ|.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Topological vector space over ℝ or ℂ [definition]: A real or complex vector space with a topology for which vector addition and scalar multiplication are continuous.
- Topology generated by a family of seminorms [definition]: For seminorms (p_i) on a vector space, a neighborhood base at zero consists of finite intersections of sets {x:p_i(x)<ε}; neighborhoods at other points are translates.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Vanishing polynomial moments of a Euclidean function [definition]: An integrable function with the required weighted integrability has vanishing moments of order below L if ∫xᵅf(x)dx=0 for every multi-index α with |α|<L. Here L is a nonnegative integer; L=0 imposes no moment conditions.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

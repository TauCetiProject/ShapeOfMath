REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (26)

- Centered subGaussian real random variable: A centered real random variableX is subGaussian if someK≥0 satisfiesEexp(tX)≤exp(K²t²/2) for every realt.
- Expected Euclidean norm of a standard Gaussian vector: For a standard Gaussian vectorg∈ℝ^m, E‖g‖₂=√2Γ((m+1)/2)/Γ(m/2) and m/√(m+1)≤E‖g‖₂≤√m.
- Expected norm of a Gaussian rank-one Rademacher sum: For independent standard complex Gaussian vectorsa_i∈ℂⁿ and independent Rademacherε_i, ifm≥cn then E‖m^(−1/2)∑_(i=1)^mε_ia_ia_i*‖_(op)≤C√n for universalc,C>0.
- Expected norm of a bounded isotropic rank-one Rademacher sum: FixK≥1. Let independent randoma_i∈ℂⁿ satisfy‖a_i‖₂²≤Kn almost surely and‖Ea_ia_i*‖_(op)≤K. For independent Rademacherε_i andm≥2nlogn,n≥2, E‖m^(−1/2)∑ε_ia_ia_i*‖_(op)≤C_K√(nlog(2n)), whereC_K depends only onK.
- Expected operator norm of a standard real Gaussian matrix: IfG is an m×n real matrix with independentN(0,1) entries, then E‖G‖_(op)≤√m+√n.
- Extreme singular values of a rectangular real Gaussian matrix: For an m×n matrixA,1≤n≤m, with independent real standard normal entries andt>0, P{√m−√n−t≤σ_min(A)≤σ_max(A)≤√m+√n+t}≥1−2exp(−t²/2).
- Frobenius robust rank null space property: A linear matrix measurement map A has the Frobenius robust rank null space property of order r with constants0<ρ<1 and τ>0 if, for every matrix M, ‖M_r‖₂≤ρ‖M_c‖₁/√r+τ‖A(M)‖_(ℓ²), where M_r is its singular-value rank-r truncation and M_c its tail.
- Gordon escape through a spherical mesh: LetA be an m×d real matrix with independent standard normal entries,T⊆S^(d−1) nonempty, andw(T)=E sup_(x∈T)⟨g_d,x⟩. For everyt>0, P{inf_(x∈T)‖Ax‖₂≥E‖g_m‖₂−w(T)−t}≥1−exp(−t²/2).
- Helstrom formula for distinguishing two states with equal priors: For density matricesX,Y and priorprobabilities1/2 each, the largest probability of correctly identifying the state using any finite POVM is1/2+‖X−Y‖₁/4. Equivalently max_(0≤E≤I)|tr(E(X−Y))|=‖X−Y‖₁/2.
- Latała fourth-moment bound for an independent-entry matrix: For a real random matrixX with independent mean-zero entries and finite fourth moments, E‖X‖_(op)≤C[max_i(∑_jEX_ij²)^(1/2)+max_j(∑_iEX_ij²)^(1/2)+(∑_(i,j)EX_ij⁴)^(1/4)], whereC is universal.
- Matrix Bernstein inequality for a self-adjoint sum: LetX_k be independent mean-zero self-adjoint n×n random matrices with‖X_k‖_(op)≤R almost surely. Setv=‖∑EX_k²‖_(op). Then for everyt>0, P{‖∑X_k‖_(op)≥t}≤2n exp[−t²/(2(v+Rt/3))].
- Mendelson small-ball lower bound for independent linear measurements: Letφ_i be independent copies of an integrable random vectorφ∈ℝ^d, and letE be a nonempty bounded deterministic subset ofℝ^d. DefineQ_ξ=inf_(u∈E)P(|⟨φ,u⟩|≥ξ) andW_m=E sup_(u∈E)⟨m^(−1/2)∑ε_iφ_i,u⟩ with independent Rademacherε_i. Forξ>0,t≥0, with probability at least1−exp(−2t²), inf_(u∈E)(∑|⟨φ_i,u⟩|²)^(1/2)≥ξ√mQ_(2ξ)−2W_m−ξt.
- Nuclear singular-value perturbation inequality: For complex matrices X,Y of equal size with singular values in decreasing order, ∑_j|σ_j(X)−σ_j(Y)|≤‖X−Y‖₁.
- Null space characterization of uniform nuclear-norm exact recovery: For a linear matrix measurement map A, every matrix X of rank at most r is the unique nuclear-norm minimizer with data A(X) if and only if ‖M_r‖₁<‖M_c‖₁ for every nonzero M∈kerA.
- Paley–Zygmund inequality: For nonnegative X with 0<E[X²]<∞ and 0<θ<1, P(X>θEX)≥(1−θ)²(EX)²/E[X²].
- Phase retrieval from quadratic measurements: Phase retrieval asks to recover a real or complex vector x from intensities |a_j*x|². In the complex case x is identifiable only up to multiplication by a scalar of modulus1; lifting X=xx* turns these observations into linear rank-one matrix measurements tr(a_ja_j*X).
- Positive semidefinite matrix square root: For a positive semidefinite complex matrix W=Udiag(λ_j)U*, its positive square root is W^(1/2)=Udiag(√λ_j)U*; this does not depend on the chosen orthonormal eigenbasis.
- Positive-semidefinite nullspace obstruction for Gaussian symmetric measurements: LetA(X)=(tr(A_iX))_(i=1)^m with independentA_i∼GOE(n) in Frobenius normalization andd=n(n+1)/2. For0<ε<1 andm≤(1−ε)d/2, with probability at least1−4exp(−ε²d/32), kerA contains a nonzero positive semidefinite realmatrixΔ. Consequently every noiseless positive-semidefinite feasibility set through anyX≥0 contains the unboundedrayX+tΔ,t≥0.
- Rademacher desymmetrization for independent centered matrices: For independent mean-zero integrable random matricesX_i and independent Rademacher signsε_i, E‖∑ε_iX_i‖_(op)≤2E‖∑X_i‖_(op), whenever the right side is finite.
- Rank restricted isometry property: A linear matrix measurement map A has the rank-r restricted isometry property with constant δ∈[0,1) if (1−δ)‖X‖_F²≤‖A(X)‖_(ℓ²)²≤(1+δ)‖X‖_F² for every matrix X of rank at most r.
- Rank-restricted informational completeness: A finite POVM is rank-r informationally complete if every two distinct density matrices X,Z of rank at most r have tr(E_jX)≠tr(E_jZ) for some POVM element E_j.
- Singular-value product bound in operator norm: For compatible real or complex matricesA,X,B and every singular-value indexj, σ_j(AXB)≤‖A‖_(op)‖B‖_(op)σ_j(X). For invertibleA,B this also givesσ_j(X)≤‖A⁻¹‖_(op)‖B⁻¹‖_(op)σ_j(AXB).
- Small-ball bound for Gaussian or exact four-design quadratic measurements: LetZ be Hermitian with‖Z‖_F=1. Leta either be a standard complex Gaussian vector or have the law[n(n+1)]^(1/4)w, wherew is drawn from an exact weighted complex projective4-design. For0≤ξ≤1, P(|a*Za|≥ξ)≥(1−ξ²)²/24.
- Small-ball bound for trace-accurate or norm-constrained operator-accurate four-designs: Letw be drawn from a weighted approximate complex projective4-design andseta=[n(n+1)]^(1/4)w. Ifθ₁≤1/4, then for every HermitianZ with‖Z‖_F=1 and0≤ξ≤1/√2, P(|a*Za|≥ξ)≥(1−2ξ²)²/192. The same bound holds underθ_∞≤1/(16s²),s≥1, provided also‖Z‖₁≤2√s.
- Spectral condition number of an invertible matrix: For an invertible square real or complex matrix W, its spectral condition number is κ(W)=σ_max(W)/σ_min(W)=‖W‖_(op)‖W⁻¹‖_(op).
- Trace Hölder bound for nuclear and operator norms: For real or complex matricesA,B of equal size, |tr(A*B)|≤‖A‖₁‖B‖_(op).

ADDITIONAL PREREQUISITES (122)

- Abelian group [definition]: A group whose operation is commutative.
- Almost-everywhere property on a measure space [definition]: A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails is contained in a measurable set of measure zero.
- Basis of a vector space [definition]: A linearly independent family of vectors whose linear span is the whole vector space.
- Bijection between sets [definition]: A function for which each element of the codomain is the image of exactly one element of the domain.
- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Binomial coefficient [definition]: For integers 0≤k≤n, binom(n,k) counts the k-element subsets of an n-element set and equals n!/(k!(n−k)!).
- Borel σ-algebra of a topological space [definition]: The σ-algebra generated by the open subsets of the space.
- Borel σ-algebra on the real line [definition]: The σ-algebra on ℝ generated by the open intervals.
- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Cardinality of a set [definition]: The size of a set, with two sets having the same cardinality when there is a bijection between them. A set has finite cardinality n when it is in bijection with {1,…,n}; infinite cardinalities are distinguished by the same bijection criterion.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for complex inner-product spaces [theorem]: In a complex inner-product space, |⟨u,v⟩|²≤⟨u,u⟩⟨v,v⟩ for all u,v.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.
- Complement of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, its complement Ω∖A consists of the elements of Ω that do not belong to A.
- Complete measure [definition]: A measure is complete if every subset of a measurable null set is measurable and has measure zero.
- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex Hilbert space [definition]: A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.
- Complex inner-product space [definition]: A complex vector space with a positive definite Hermitian form ⟨x,y⟩, linear in x and conjugate-linear in y. Its induced norm is ∥x∥=√⟨x,x⟩.
- Complex number [definition]: A complex number is a pair of real numbers, written a+bi. Addition is componentwise and multiplication is (a+bi)(c+di)=(ac−bd)+(ad+bc)i, so i²=−1.
- Convergence of a sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) converges to x∈X if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,x)<ε whenever n≥N.
- Convergent real series [definition]: The series ∑ₙ≥1aₙ converges to S if its sequence of partial sums converges to S.
- Convex function on a convex subset of a real vector space [definition]: A function f:C→R on a convex subset of a real vector space satisfying f((1−t)x+ty)≤(1−t)f(x)+tf(y) for x,y∈C and 0≤t≤1. Unlike the separate interval-convex-function identity, the domain may be infinite-dimensional.
- Convex subset of a real vector space [definition]: A subset C of a real vector space is convex if (1−t)x+ty∈C whenever x,y∈C and0≤t≤1. The vector space may be infinite-dimensional.
- Dimension of a vector space [definition]: The cardinality of a basis of the vector space; a finite basis with n vectors gives dimension n.
- Disjoint sets [definition]: Two sets are disjoint when they have no common element; a family is pairwise disjoint when every two distinct members are disjoint.
- Eigenvalue of a linear endomorphism [definition]: A scalar λ for which a linear endomorphism T has a nonzero vector v with Tv=λv.
- Equivalence class [definition]: For an equivalence relation ~ on X, [x]={y∈X:y~x}; the quotient set X/~ consists of these classes.
- Equivalence relation [definition]: A reflexive, symmetric and transitive binary relation.
- Essential supremum of a measurable real function [definition]: The infimum of real a for which f≤a almost everywhere, allowing infinite values when no finite bound exists.
- Euclidean space ℝᵈ [definition]: For d≥1, ℝᵈ consists of d-tuples of real numbers with distance dist(x,y)=(∑ᵢ(xᵢ−yᵢ)²)¹ᐟ².
- Expectation of an integrable real random variable [definition]: If E abs(X)=∫abs(X)dP<∞, the expectation of X is EX=∫X dP.
- Extended nonnegative real numbers [definition]: The nonnegative real numbers together with a greatest element ∞. Sums involving ∞ equal ∞, and 0·∞ is taken to be 0 when integrating simple functions.
- Factorial [definition]: For n≥1, n!=1·2···n, and 0!=1.
- Field [definition]: A commutative ring in which 1≠0 and every nonzero element is invertible.
- Finite set [definition]: A set X is finite if it is bijective with {1,…,n} for some natural number n≥0; its cardinality is n.
- Finite-dimensional vector space [definition]: A vector space having a finite basis.
- Frobenius norm of a real or complex matrix [definition]: The Frobenius norm of A is (∑_{i,j}|a_{ij}|²)^{1/2}.
- Function between sets [definition]: An assignment taking each element of a specified domain set to exactly one element of a specified codomain set.
- Gamma function on the positive real axis [definition]: For s>0, Γ(s)=∫₀∞e^(−t)t^(s−1)dt, with the integral understood as an improper real integral.
- Gaussian orthogonal ensemble in Frobenius normalization [construction]: GOE(n) here is the law of a real symmetric n×n matrix whose diagonal entries are independentN(0,1) and whose upper offdiagonal entries are independentN(0,1/2), independent also of the diagonal. Equivalently its coordinates in a real orthonormal Frobenius basis are independent standard normals.
- Group [definition]: A set with an associative binary operation, an identity element, and a two-sided inverse for each element.
- Hermitian complex matrix [definition]: A square complex matrix A is Hermitian if its conjugate transpose A* equals A; equivalently A_(ij)=overline(A_(ji)) for every i,j.
- Improper Riemann integral [definition]: An improper Riemann integral over an open endpoint or an unbounded interval is a limit of integrals over compact subintervals; with two improper endpoints both limits must exist separately.
- Independent family of random variables [definition]: For distinct i₁,…,iₖ and Borel A₁,…,Aₖ, independence requires P(⋂ⱼ{Xᵢⱼ∈Aⱼ})=∏ⱼP(Xᵢⱼ∈Aⱼ).
- Indicator function of a subset [definition]: For A⊆Ω, the real function 1_A is 1 on A and 0 on its complement.
- Intersection of a family of sets [definition]: Inside a specified ambient set, the elements belonging to every member of the family.
- Invertible square matrix [definition]: A square matrix A over a field is invertible if there is a square matrix B with AB=BA=I; this B is denoted A⁻¹.
- Kernel of a linear map [definition]: For a linear map f:V→W, its kernel is the subspace {v∈V:f(v)=0}.
- Least upper bound in a partially ordered set [definition]: For A⊆P, its least upper bound is an upper bound s satisfying s≤b for every upper bound b of A, when such an s exists.
- Lebesgue Lᵖ space [definition]: For a measure space and 1≤p<∞, Lᵖ consists of measurable scalar functions modulo equality almost everywhere with ∫|f|ᵖ<∞, normed by (∫|f|ᵖ)¹ᐟᵖ. For p=∞ use the essential-supremum norm.
- Lebesgue integral [definition]: For measurable f≥0, ∫f dμ is the supremum of integrals of nonnegative simple functions below f. For signed f with ∫abs(f)dμ<∞, define ∫f=∫f⁺−∫f⁻.
- Lebesgue measure on Euclidean space [definition]: The completion of the Borel measure on ℝⁿ assigning each rectangular box the product of its side lengths.
- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear matrix measurement map [construction]: For fixed matrices A₁,…,A_m of the same size as X, a linear matrix measurement map sends X to (tr(XA_j*))_(j=1)^m. On Hermitian matrices with Hermitian A_j, this takes real values; noisy data have the form b=A(X)+w.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Linearly independent family of vectors [definition]: A family of vectors is linearly independent if every finite linear combination equalling zero has all coefficients zero.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Multivariate normal distribution [definition]: A distribution of a real vector X for which every real linear combination of coordinates is normally distributed; it is specified by its mean vector and positive semidefinite covariance matrix.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normal distribution [definition]: The real probability distribution N(m,σ²) with density (2πσ²)⁻¹ᐟ²exp(−(x−m)²/(2σ²)) for σ>0; variance zero gives a point mass at m.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Operator norm of a bounded linear operator [definition]: For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Orthogonal invariance of the standard Gaussian distribution [theorem]: If X has n independent N(0,1) coordinates and U is a real orthogonal n×n matrix, then UX has the same distribution as X.
- Orthonormal family in a Hilbert space [definition]: Vectors (e_i) with ⟨e_i,e_j⟩=0 for i≠j and ‖e_i‖=1.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Positive semidefinite complex matrix [definition]: A Hermitian n×n complex matrix A is positive semidefinite if z*Az≥0 for every z∈ℂⁿ.
- Positive semidefinite real matrix [definition]: A symmetric real matrix A such that xᵀAx≥0 for every real column vector x.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Probability space [definition]: A probability space is (Ω,𝔽,P), where 𝔽 is a σ-algebra on Ω and P is a measure on 𝔽 with P(Ω)=1.
- Pushforward measure [definition]: For a measurable map f:X→Y and measure μ on X, the pushforward f_*μ on Y satisfies f_*μ(B)=μ(f⁻¹(B)).
- Rank of a linear map [definition]: The dimension of the image of a linear map.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real orthogonal matrix [definition]: A real square matrix U with UᵀU=I, equivalently a linear map preserving the Euclidean inner product.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Real-valued random variable [definition]: A real-valued random variable is a measurable map from a probability space to ℝ with its Borel σ-algebra.
- Riemann integral [definition]: A bounded f:[a,b]→ℝ has Riemann integral I if all tagged Riemann sums approach I as the maximum length of the partition intervals tends to zero.
- Schatten norm of a finite matrix [definition]: For a real or complex matrix M with singular values σ_j and1≤p<∞, its Schatten p-norm is ‖M‖_p=(∑σ_j^p)^(1/p); ‖M‖_∞=maxσ_j is the operator norm. The p=1 case is the nuclear norm and p=2 is the Frobenius norm.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Singular values of a complex matrix [construction]: For a complex m×n matrix M, its singular values σ₁≥⋯≥σ_q≥0, q=min(m,n), are the nonnegative square roots of the eigenvalues of M*M, with the additional forced zero eigenvalues omitted when n>m.
- Singular-value rank truncation [construction]: For an SVD M=∑_(j=1)^qσ_j u_jv_j*, choose M_r=∑_(j≤r)σ_j u_jv_j* and M_c=M−M_r. Repeated singular values can make the choice nonunique, while the singular values and Schatten norms of the head and tail are well defined.
- Spectral theorem for a Hermitian complex matrix [theorem]: Every Hermitian complex matrix A has a decomposition A=U diag(λ₁,…,λₙ)U* with U unitary and all λⱼ real.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Symmetric matrix [definition]: A square matrix A equal to its transpose, so aᵢⱼ=aⱼᵢ.
- Tagged Riemann sum [definition]: For a partition a=x₀<⋯<x_n=b and tags t_i∈[x_{i−1},x_i], the Riemann sum of f is ∑ᵢf(t_i)(x_i−x_{i−1}); the partition mesh is maxᵢ(x_i−x_{i−1}).
- Tensor product of vector spaces [definition]: The vector space V⊗W with its universal bilinear map from V×W.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Trace of a square matrix [definition]: The sum of the diagonal entries of a square matrix.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Unitary operator on a complex Hilbert space [definition]: A surjective complex linear map preserving the inner product.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.

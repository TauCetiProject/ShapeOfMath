REQUIRED CONCEPT FRONTIER (32)

- Aubin–Lions compactness lemma: Let X₀↪X↪X₁ be Banach spaces, the first embedding compact and the second continuous. For1<p<∞ and1<q≤∞, a set bounded in Lᵖ(0,T;X₀) whose weak time derivatives are bounded in Lᑫ(0,T;X₁) is relatively compact in Lᵖ(0,T;X).
- Closed partial order on a topological space: A partial order whose graph {(x,y):x≤y} is closed in the product topology. For metric spaces this is equivalent to preservation of the relation under limits of convergent pairs.
- Comparison for the periodic heat equation with two reflecting walls: For fixed H²(S¹) walls h₁<h₂ and Lipschitz f, two reflected stochastic heat solutions driven by the same additive space-time white noise preserve pointwise order of their initial states. The order holds simultaneously for all nonnegative times on the pathwise continuous version.
- Continuity of the stochastic heat convolution on the circle: For space-time white noise on S¹, Z_t=∫₀ᵗe^{(t−s)∂ₓ²}dW_s has a version jointly continuous in(t,x) on every compact time interval times S¹, starting from0. Thus t↦Z_t is continuous with values in C(S¹) in the supremum norm.
- Cépa’s well-posedness theorem for multivalued Skorohod equations: Let η be proper lower semicontinuous convex onℝᵈ with int(dom∂η) nonempty and b globally Lipschitz. For every x∈closure(dom∂η) and continuous w with w₀=0, X=x+w+∫₀^.b(X_s)ds−K has a unique solution in the multivalued Skorohod sense. The map(x,w)↦X is continuous for uniform convergence on compact time intervals. Brownian inputs therefore define a continuous random dynamical system on closure(dom∂η).
- Hairer’s total-variation mixing theorem for additive fractional SDEs: Let dX_t=b(X_t)dt+dB_t^H onℝ with H∈(0,1),H≠½ and b∈C¹. Suppose (b(x)−b(y))(x−y)≤min(C−c|x−y|²,C|x−y|²), c>0, and |b(x)|+|b′(x)|≤C(1+|x|)^N. If H>½ also require b′ bounded. Then for each deterministic x the law of X_t^x converges in total variation to a common probability measure μ, the one-point marginal of the unique stationary solution adapted to the two-sided noise past.
- Integral Grönwall inequality: If u is nonnegative and continuous on [0,T], a≥0, b≥0 is integrable, and u(t)≤a+∫₀ᵗb(s)u(s)ds for every t, then u(t)≤a exp(∫₀ᵗb(s)ds) for every t∈[0,T].
- Invariant random probability measure for a random dynamical system: A probability kernel ω↦π_ω on the state space satisfying (φ_t(ω,·))_*π_ω=π_{θ_tω} almost surely for each t. Equivalently P(dω)π_ω(dx) is invariant under the skew-product evolution.
- Inverse-potential comparison for a forced porous-medium equation: Let u₁,u₂ be bounded variational solutions of ∂_tu=Δ(|u|^{m−1}u)+cu+F on a bounded smooth domain, with m>1, zero Dirichlet conditions and the same smooth forcing F. For R=(−Δ_D)⁻¹, Ru₁(0)≤Ru₂(0) almost everywhere implies Ru₁(t)≤Ru₂(t) for every t≥0.
- Itô’s formula for Brownian diffusions: If X solves dX=b dt+σ dB and f is C¹ in time and C² in space, then df(t,X_t)=(∂_tf+b·∇f+½tr(σσᵀD²f))dt+∇f·σdB, with the usual localization hypotheses.
- Markov’s inequality for nonnegative random variables: If Y is a nonnegative integrable real random variable and t>0, then P(Y≥t)≤E[Y]/t.
- Mixing of a dissipative one-dimensional additive Brownian SDE: If b is locally Lipschitz onℝ and xb(x)≤C−cx² for c>0, the solution of dX=b(X)dt+dW is nonexplosive, has unique invariant density proportional to exp(2∫₀^x b(s)ds), and its transition laws converge to that density in total variation from every starting point.
- Mixing of bounded one-dimensional convex stochastic inclusions: Let η:ℝ→(−∞,∞] be proper lower semicontinuous convex with bounded domain and int(dom∂η) nonempty, and let b∈C² have bounded derivatives. The solution of dX+∂η(X)dt∋b(X)dt+dW has a unique invariant probability, with density proportional to exp(2∫_{x₀}^x b(s)ds−2η(x)) on int(domη), and its laws converge in total variation from every state in closure(dom∂η).
- Normal cone of a closed convex set: For a closed convex D⊂ℝᵈ and x∈D, the outward normal cone is N_D(x)={v:⟨v,y−x⟩≤0 for every y∈D}; it is empty outside D. It equals the convex subdifferential of the extended-real indicator I_D.
- Normality of the positive-distribution order on H⁻¹: On a bounded smooth domain, H⁻¹=(H₀¹)* with its gradient-dual norm has a normal positive-distribution cone. More precisely, if h≥0 and−h≤w≤h then ‖w‖_{H⁻¹}≤‖h‖_{H⁻¹}; hence the norm diameter of[f,g] is at most‖g−f‖_{H⁻¹}.
- Normally reflected diffusion in a convex domain: A continuous process in a closed convex domain solving an SDE plus an inward normal finite-variation correction supported on boundary times, keeping the process inside the domain.
- Order interval in a partially ordered set: For f≤g, the order interval [f,g] consists of all x satisfying f≤x≤g.
- Order-preserving random dynamical system: A random dynamical system on a partially ordered state space such that x≤y implies φ_t(ω,x)≤φ_t(ω,y) for all t≥0 and every noise realization.
- Portmanteau theorem for probability measures: For probability measures on a metric space, weak convergence μ_n⇒μ is equivalent to limsup μ_n(F)≤μ(F) for every closed F, and equivalently to liminf μ_n(G)≥μ(G) for every open G.
- Positive-distribution order preservation for additive stochastic porous media: For the zero-Dirichlet additive stochastic porous-medium equation with m>1 and trace-class H⁻¹ noise, solutions driven by the same noise preserve the ordinary positive-distribution order: x≤y implies X_t^x≤X_t^y for every t≥0 on the continuous cocycle version.
- Prokhorov’s theorem on Polish spaces: A family of probability measures on a Polish space is relatively compact for weak convergence if and only if it is tight; in particular every tight sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence.
- Random continuous tests for stable convergence of probability kernels: If probability kernels µ_n on a Polish space converge stably to µ, then E∫F(ω,x)µ_n(ω,dx)→E∫F(ω,x)µ(ω,dx) for every jointly measurable F continuous in x with E sup_x|F(ω,x)|<∞. Stable convergence means convergence first for tests a(ω)f(x), with a bounded measurable and f bounded continuous.
- Regular conditional distribution for a Polish-valued random variable: If X takes values in a Polish spaceE and G⊆F is a sub-σ-algebra, there is a G-measurable probability kernel K(ω,·) with∫f,dK(ω)=E[f(X)|G](ω) almost surely for every bounded Borel f. It is unique up to a common null set. Equivalently a probability measure on a product with Polish second factor disintegrates into its first marginal and such conditional probabilities.
- Sobolev embedding W²,ᵖ into continuous functions: On a bounded Lipschitz domain Ω⊂ℝᵈ, if1<p<∞ and2p>d, every W²,ᵖ function has a continuous representative on the closure, with sup norm bounded by C‖u‖_{W²,ᵖ}.
- Stable compactness of random probability kernels with tight mean laws: Let K_n be probability kernels on one probability space with Polish targetE. If their mean probability measuresν_n(A)=E[K_n(ω,A)] form a tight family, then some subsequence converges stably to a probability kernelK on the same base space.
- Strong mixing of nondegenerate additive stochastic porous media: On a bounded smooth domain, consider dX=[Δ(|X|^{m−1}X)+cX]dt+dW with m>1,c∈ℝ, zero Dirichlet conditions and covariance Q trace class onH⁻¹. Put p=m+1,V=Lᵖ,H=H⁻¹. If Q¹ᐟ² is injective and ‖u‖ᵖ_V≥a‖u‖^σ_{Q¹ᐟ²}‖u‖^{p−σ}_H for all nonzero u∈V, for some a>0,σ≥2,σ>p−2, then its transition laws converge in total variation to a unique invariant probability measure. This includes the weak one-point-law mixing required for synchronization.
- Total-variation mixing for the periodic stochastic heat equation with two walls: With strictly separated H²(S¹) walls, globally Lipschitz drift and unit additive space-time white noise, the reflected heat equation has a unique invariant probability on its continuous state space, and transition laws converge to it in total variation from every initial state between the walls.
- Variational Itô formula for the Hilbert norm: Let V⊂H⊂V* be a separable Gelfand triple, p>1, X₀∈L²(Ω;H), Y∈L^{p/(p−1)}(dt⊗P;V*) progressive and B predictable with E∫‖B‖²_HS<∞. If X_t=X₀+∫₀ᵗY_sds+∫₀ᵗB_sdW_s has an Lᵖ(dt⊗P;V) version, then it has continuous H paths, E sup_{t≤T}‖X_t‖²_H<∞, and ‖X_t‖²_H=‖X₀‖²_H+∫₀ᵗ(2⟨Y_s,X_s⟩+‖B_s‖²_HS)ds+2∫₀ᵗ⟨X_s,B_sdW_s⟩_H.
- Weak subsequences of bounded Lᵖ sequences: For1<p<∞ on a bounded Euclidean domain with Lebesgue measure, every norm-bounded sequence in Lᵖ has a weakly convergent subsequence with limit f∈Lᵖ and ‖f‖_p≤liminf‖f_n‖_p along that subsequence.
- Weak synchronization by noise: Existence of a weak point attractor consisting almost surely of one random point. Its invariance is φ_t(ω,a(ω))=a(θ_tω), and every deterministic initial state converges to it in pullback probability.
- White-noise random dynamical system: A filtered random dynamical system whose noise σ-algebras generated by cocycle increments on disjoint time intervals are independent, and transform with the time shifts of the base flow.
- W²,ᵖ regularity of the Dirichlet Poisson problem: On a bounded C¹,¹ domain and2≤p<∞, for f∈Lᵖ the weak solution u∈H₀¹ of−Δu=f belongs to W²,ᵖ and satisfies‖u‖_{W²,ᵖ}≤C‖f‖_{Lᵖ}.

1. Polish space
   A topological space admitting a complete separable metric that induces its topology.
   Source: relies_on, §2, Proposition2.4 and Theorem2.6

2. Closed partial order on a topological space
   A partial order whose graph {(x,y):x≤y} is closed in the product topology. For metric spaces this is equivalent to preservation of the relation under limits of convergent pairs.
   Source: relies_on, §2, Proposition2.4 and Theorem2.6

3. Order interval in a partially ordered set
   For f≤g, the order interval [f,g] consists of all x satisfying f≤x≤g.
   Source: relies_on, §2, Proposition2.4 and Theorem2.6

4. Weak convergence of probability measures on a metric space
   Measures μ_n converge weakly to μ if ∫f dμ_n→∫f dμ for every bounded continuous real function f on the space.
   Source: relies_on, §2, Proposition2.4 and Theorem2.6

5. Tight family of probability measures
   A family of probability measures on a topological space is tight if for every ε>0 some compact K has μ(K)≥1−ε for every member μ of the family.
   Source: relies_on, §2, Proposition2.4 and Theorem2.6

6. Prokhorov’s theorem on Polish spaces
   A family of probability measures on a Polish space is relatively compact for weak convergence if and only if it is tight; in particular every tight sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence.
   Source: relies_on, §2, Proposition2.4 and Theorem2.6

7. Portmanteau theorem for probability measures
   For probability measures on a metric space, weak convergence μ_n⇒μ is equivalent to limsup μ_n(F)≤μ(F) for every closed F, and equivalently to liminf μ_n(G)≥μ(G) for every open G.
   Source: relies_on, §2, Proposition2.4 and Theorem2.6

- Banach-valued simple function [definition]: A measurable function with finitely many values in a Banach space, represented as a finite sum∑_ix_i1_{A_i} with measurable sets A_i.

- Bilinear map [definition]: A map of two vector-space variables that is linear in each variable separately.
- Binary operation on a set [definition]: A function S×S→S.
- Binary relation on a set [definition]: A subset R⊆S×S; writing xRy means (x,y)∈R.
- Bochner integral [definition]: For a strongly measurable Banach-valued f with ∫‖f‖<∞, its integral is the norm limit of the integrals of simple approximants converging in the integral of their norm difference; simple functions integrate by∑x_iμ(A_i).

- Bounded linear operator [definition]: A linear map T:X→Y between normed vector spaces is bounded if ∥Tx∥≤C∥x∥ for one finite constant C and every x∈X.
- Bounded positive operator [definition]: A bounded self-adjoint operator A is positive, written A≥0, if ⟨Ax,x⟩≥0 for every vector x.
- Bounded self-adjoint operator [definition]: A bounded operator T on a complex Hilbert space is self-adjoint if T=T*.
- Cartesian product of sets [definition]: A×B is the set of ordered pairs (a,b) with a∈A and b∈B.
- Cauchy sequence in a metric space [definition]: A sequence (xₙ) in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if for every ε>0 there is N such that d(xₙ,xₘ)<ε whenever n,m≥N.
- Cauchy–Schwarz inequality for complex inner-product spaces [theorem]: In a complex inner-product space, |⟨u,v⟩|²≤⟨u,u⟩⟨v,v⟩ for all u,v.
- Closure of a subset of a topological space [definition]: The closure of A is the intersection of all closed subsets containing A; a set is closed when its complement is open.
- Commutative ring with identity [definition]: An abelian group under addition, together with an associative commutative multiplication with identity, distributing over addition. The zero ring is allowed.

- Complete metric space [definition]: A metric space is complete if every Cauchy sequence in it converges to a point of that same space.
- Complex Hilbert space [definition]: A complex inner-product space complete in the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥ induced by its inner product.
- Complex conjugation [definition]: The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is z̄=a−bi.

- Limit of a function between metric spaces [definition]: For f defined near an accumulation point a of a metric space, lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<d(x,a)<δ implies d(f(x),L)<ε.
- Limit of a real function at a point [definition]: The relation lim_{x→a}f(x)=L means that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ in the domain implies |f(x)−L|<ε.
- Limit of a real sequence [definition]: A sequence (aₙ) converges to L∈ℝ if for every ε>0 there is N such that n≥N implies abs(aₙ−L)<ε.
- Linear combination of vectors [definition]: A finite sum a₁v₁+⋯+aₙvₙ of vectors multiplied by scalars from their vector space’s ground field; the empty sum is zero.
- Linear map between vector spaces [definition]: A map between vector spaces over the same field preserving addition and scalar multiplication.
- Linear span of a set of vectors [definition]: The subspace consisting of all finite linear combinations of a specified set of vectors.
- Lipschitz domain in Euclidean space [definition]: An open connected Euclidean set whose boundary is locally the graph of a Lipschitz function, with the domain locally on one side.
- Lipschitz map between metric spaces [definition]: A map f:(X,d)→(Y,e) is Lipschitz if e(f(x),f(y))≤L d(x,y) for one finite constant L and all x,y∈X.
- Locally convex topological vector space [definition]: A real or complex topological vector space whose zero has a neighborhood base of convex sets; equivalently its topology can be generated by seminorms.
- Lower semicontinuous extended-real function [definition]: A function u:X→(−∞,∞] on a topological space is lower semicontinuous if {x:u(x)>a} is open for every real a.
- Markov transition semigroup [definition]: A family of transition kernels P_t(x,A), t≥0, with P₀(x,·)=δ_x and P_{s+t}(x,A)=∫P_t(y,A)P_s(x,dy). Sub-Markov semigroups may lose mass at a cemetery state.
- Matrix over a ring [definition]: A rectangular array of elements of a ring; addition is entrywise and matrix multiplication uses row-by-column sums.
- Measurable map between measurable spaces [definition]: A function for which the preimage of each measurable subset of the codomain is measurable in the domain.
- Measurable simple real function [definition]: A measurable real function with finite range. It can be written as a finite sum ∑ a_i 1_{A_i} with measurable level sets A_i.
- Measurable space [definition]: A set equipped with a specified σ-algebra of subsets.
- Measure [definition]: A measure μ maps a σ-algebra to [0,∞], sends ∅ to 0, and satisfies μ(⋃ₙAₙ)=∑ₙμ(Aₙ) for every pairwise disjoint sequence of measurable sets.
- Measure-preserving measurable flow [definition]: A jointly measurable family θ_t of probability-preserving maps, indexed by real t, with θ₀=id and θ_{s+t}=θ_s∘θ_t.
- Metric induced by a norm [construction]: The metric induced by a norm on a real or complex vector space is d(x,y)=‖x−y‖.
- Metric space [definition]: A set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) such that d(x,y)=0 exactly when x=y, d(x,y)=d(y,x), and d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).
- Modulus of a complex number [definition]: For z=a+bi, its modulus is the nonnegative real number |z|=√(a²+b²).
- Multi-index for Euclidean partial derivatives [definition]: An n-tuple α=(α₁,…,α_n) of nonnegative integers, with |α|=∑α_i; Dᵅ denotes taking α_i partial derivatives in coordinate i.
- Multivalued Skorohod problem for a convex subdifferential [construction]: For proper lower semicontinuous convex η:ℝᵈ→(−∞,∞], continuous w with w₀=0 and x∈closure(dom∂η), find continuous X and K, K₀=0, K locally of bounded variation, with X=x+w−K in closure(dom∂η) and∫_sᵗ⟨X_r−a,dK_r−a*dr⟩≥0 for every(a,a*)∈graph∂η and s≤t. For an indicator of a closed convex set, this is normal reflection.
- Multivariate normal distribution [definition]: A distribution of a real vector X for which every real linear combination of coordinates is normally distributed; it is specified by its mean vector and positive semidefinite covariance matrix.
- Natural logarithm [definition]: The inverse ln:(0,∞)→ℝ of the real exponential function.
- Natural number [definition]: One of 0, 1, 2, …; natural numbers count finite collections.
- Nonnegative real square root [definition]: For t≥0, √t is the unique real number y≥0 satisfying y²=t.
- Normal distribution [definition]: The real probability distribution N(m,σ²) with density (2πσ²)⁻¹ᐟ²exp(−(x−m)²/(2σ²)) for σ>0; variance zero gives a point mass at m.
- Normed vector space [definition]: A real or complex vector space X with a function ∥·∥:X→[0,∞) that is positive except at zero, absolutely homogeneous, and subadditive.
- Open interval in the real line [definition]: For real a<b, the set (a,b)={x∈ℝ:a<x<b}.
- Operator norm of a bounded linear operator [definition]: For a bounded linear map T:X→Y between normed spaces, ∥T∥=sup{∥Tx∥:∥x∥≤1}.
- Ordered field [definition]: A field with a total order preserved by addition, in which the product of two positive elements is positive.
- Ordered pair [definition]: A pair (x,y) with distinguished first and second entries; (x,y)=(x′,y′) exactly when x=x′ and y=y′.
- Orthonormal basis of a Hilbert space [definition]: An orthonormal family whose linear span is dense in the Hilbert space.
- Orthonormal family in a Hilbert space [definition]: Vectors (e_i) with ⟨e_i,e_j⟩=0 for i≠j and ‖e_i‖=1.
- Partial derivative in Euclidean coordinates [definition]: The ith partial derivative of f at x is the derivative at zero of t↦f(x+t e_i), with all other coordinates fixed; vector-valued maps are differentiated componentwise.
- Partial order [definition]: A reflexive, antisymmetric and transitive binary relation, usually written ≤.
- Positive semidefinite real matrix [definition]: A symmetric real matrix A such that xᵀAx≥0 for every real column vector x.
- Predictable stochastic process [definition]: A process measurable for the σ-algebra on time×sample space generated by adapted left-continuous processes. This formalizes choosing an integrand from information available just before the integration time.
- Preimage of a subset under a function [definition]: For f:A→B and E⊆B, f⁻¹(E)={x∈A:f(x)∈E}.
- Probability kernel between measurable spaces [definition]: A probability kernel from(S,Σ) to(E,ℰ) assigns a probability measure K(s,·) onE to each s∈S, with s↦K(s,A) Σ-measurable for every A∈ℰ. A random probability measure is such a kernel with a probability space as its domain.
- Probability space [definition]: A probability space is (Ω,𝔽,P), where 𝔽 is a σ-algebra on Ω and P is a measure on 𝔽 with P(Ω)=1.
- Product measure [construction]: For σ-finite measure spaces, the product measure on the product σ-algebra assigns a measurable rectangle A×B the value μ(A)ν(B); its completion also includes subsets of product-null sets.
- Product topology [definition]: On a Cartesian product of topological spaces, the topology with basic open sets restricting finitely many coordinates to open subsets and leaving all other coordinates unrestricted.
- Progressively measurable stochastic process [definition]: A process X_t is progressively measurable for a filtration(F_t) if for every T≥0, (t,ω)↦X_t(ω) on[0,T]×Ω is measurable for B([0,T])⊗F_T.
- Proper convex extended-real functional [definition]: A function R:V→(−∞,∞] on a real vector space is proper if it is finite somewhere and is convex if R((1−t)x+ty)≤(1−t)R(x)+tR(y) for 0≤t≤1.
- Random closed set in a Polish space [definition]: A nonempty closed-set-valued map A(ω) for which ω↦d(x,A(ω)) is measurable for every fixed x. It is a random compact set if its values are compact almost surely.
- Real Hilbert space [definition]: A real inner-product space complete for its induced norm metric.
- Real exponential function [definition]: The real function exp(x)=∑_{n≥0}xⁿ/n!, whose series converges for every real x.
- Real inner-product space [definition]: A real vector space with a symmetric bilinear form ⟨x,y⟩ that is positive on ⟨x,x⟩ for x≠0; its induced norm is √⟨x,x⟩.
- Real number [definition]: A real number is an element of a complete ordered field ℝ; completeness means every nonempty subset bounded above has a least upper bound.
- Real power of a positive real number [definition]: For x>0 and p∈ℝ, define xᵖ=exp(p log x). For p>0 extend by 0ᵖ=0.
- Real sequence [definition]: A real sequence is a function a:ℕ₊→ℝ, written (aₙ)ₙ≥1.
- Real sine function [definition]: The sine function is defined by the everywhere convergent power series sin(x)=∑ₖ≥0(−1)ᵏx²ᵏ⁺¹/(2k+1)!.
- Real-valued random variable [definition]: A real-valued random variable is a measurable map from a probability space to ℝ with its Borel σ-algebra.
- Reflexive Banach space [definition]: A Banach space X for which the canonical isometric map X→X**, sending x to the functional ℓ↦ℓ(x), is onto.
- Relatively compact subset of a topological space [definition]: A subset whose closure is compact.
- Riemann–Stieltjes integral [construction]: For continuous real f and a real bounded-variation function g on[a,b], ∫_a^bf,dg is the common limit of sums∑f(ξ_i)(g(t_i)−g(t_{i−1})) as the partition mesh tends to zero, with ξ_i∈[t_{i−1},t_i]. Vector pairings are defined by summing the component integrals.
- Seminorm on a real or complex vector space [definition]: A nonnegative function p with p(ax)=|a|p(x) and p(x+y)≤p(x)+p(y); p may vanish on nonzero vectors.
- Separable metric space [definition]: A metric space having a countable dense subset.
- Sequence in a set [definition]: A function from the natural numbers, or the positive natural numbers, to that set.
- Set [definition]: A collection of distinct elements, regarded as one object.
- Smooth map between open Euclidean sets [definition]: A map on an open subset of ℝⁿ is smooth if all of its component partial derivatives of every order exist and are continuous.
- Sobolev space Wᵏ,ᵖ of a Euclidean domain [definition]: For integer k≥0 and1≤p≤∞, Wᵏ,ᵖ(Ω) consists of Lᵖ functions whose distributional partial derivatives Dᵅ for all |α|≤k also lie in Lᵖ. A norm combines the finitely many derivative Lᵖ norms.
- Stable convergence of random probability kernels [definition]: For probability kernels K_n,K on a fixed probability space(Ω,F,P) with Polish targetE, K_n converges stably toK if E[a(ω)∫f(x)K_n(ω,dx)]→E[a(ω)∫f(x)K(ω,dx)] for every bounded F-measurable a and every bounded continuous f onE. The base probabilityP stays fixed.
- Standard Brownian motion [definition]: A continuous process B_t starting at zero with independent increments and B_t−B_s distributed N(0,t−s) for 0≤s<t. A d-dimensional standard Brownian motion has d independent standard components.
- Stochastic process [definition]: A family (X_t) indexed by a specified time set, with each X_t a measurable map from a probability space into a specified measurable state space. Adaptation, joint measurability and path regularity are additional conditions when required.
- Strongly continuous contraction semigroup [definition]: Bounded operators T_t on a Banach space satisfy T₀=I,T_{s+t}=T_sT_t,‖T_t‖≤1 and T_tx→x in norm as t↓0 for every x.
- Strongly measurable Banach-valued function [definition]: A function that is an almost-everywhere pointwise norm limit of Banach-valued simple functions.
- Subset [definition]: A⊆B means every element of A is an element of B.
- Subspace topology [definition]: For A⊆X, the topology whose open sets are A∩U for U open in X.
- Sum of a nonnegative series [definition]: For aₙ≥0, define ∑ₙ≥1aₙ as the supremum of its finite partial sums; the value may be infinite.
- Support of a function [definition]: The closure in its domain of the set on which a function is nonzero.
- Symmetric matrix [definition]: A square matrix A equal to its transpose, so aᵢⱼ=aⱼᵢ.
- Test-function topology on C_c^∞ of an open Euclidean set [definition]: The finest locally convex topology making every inclusion D_K→C_c^∞(Ω) continuous, where D_K consists of smooth functions supported in a fixed compact K⊂Ω and has the seminorms max_{|α|≤m}sup|∂^αφ|.
- Topological space [definition]: A set X with a family τ of subsets, called open sets, containing ∅ and X and closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- Topological vector space over ℝ or ℂ [definition]: A real or complex vector space with a topology for which vector addition and scalar multiplication are continuous.
- Topology generated by a family of seminorms [definition]: For seminorms (p_i) on a vector space, a neighborhood base at zero consists of finite intersections of sets {x:p_i(x)<ε}; neighborhoods at other points are translates.
- Total order [definition]: A partial order in which any two elements are comparable.
- Uniform convergence of functions into a metric space [definition]: A sequence fₙ:X→(Y,d) converges uniformly to f if for every ε>0, d(fₙ(x),f(x))<ε for every x∈X once n is sufficiently large independently of x.
- Union of a family of sets [definition]: The set of elements belonging to at least one member of the family.
- Unit circle [definition]: The subspace S¹={(x,y)∈ℝ²:x²+y²=1}, often identified with a2π-periodic angular coordinate and equipped with arc-length measure.
- Vector space [definition]: An abelian group with scalar multiplication by a field satisfying a(v+w)=av+aw, (a+b)v=av+bv, (ab)v=a(bv), and 1v=v.
- Vector subspace [definition]: A subset of a vector space containing zero and closed under addition and scalar multiplication.
- Weak convergence in a Banach space [definition]: A sequence xₙ converges weakly to x if ℓ(xₙ)→ℓ(x) for every bounded linear functional ℓ on the space.
- Weak partial derivative of a locally integrable function [definition]: A locally integrable v is the ith weak derivative of u on an open Ω⊆ℝⁿ if ∫Ω u ∂ᵢφ=−∫Ω vφ for every smooth compactly supported test function φ.
- π [definition]: π is the least positive zero of the real sine function and equals the circumference-to-diameter ratio of a Euclidean circle.
- σ-algebra [definition]: A σ-algebra on Ω contains Ω and is closed under complements in Ω and countable unions.
- σ-algebra generated by a family of subsets [definition]: The smallest σ-algebra containing that family, equivalently the intersection of all σ-algebras on the ambient set that contain it.
- σ-finite measure [definition]: A measure whose whole underlying space is a countable union of measurable sets of finite measure.

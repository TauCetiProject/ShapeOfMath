LOCATED CONCEPTS (19)

1. Comparison theorem for Markov chain drift inequalities
   Let (X_k) be a Markov chain with natural filtration (F_k), let Z_k≥0 be adapted, and let f_k,s_k≥0 be measurable functions with E[Z_{k+1}|F_k]≤Z_k−f_k(X_k)+s_k(X_k) for every k. Then for every stopping time τ and initial state x, E_x[∑_{k=0}^{τ−1}f_k(X_k)]≤Z_0(x)+E_x[∑_{k=0}^{τ−1}s_k(X_k)].
   Source: explains, Proposition 11.3.2, p. 269
   Note: Statement and proof via Dynkin’s formula. Not a priority claim.

2. Existence of small sets for a ψ-irreducible kernel
   If a Markov kernel P on a countably generated measurable space is ψ-irreducible, then every measurable set A with ψ(A)>0 contains a small set C with ψ(C)>0: for some m≥1 and nonzero measure ν with ν(C)>0, P^m(x,B)≥ν(B) for all x∈C and measurable B. The result goes back to Jain and Jamison and to Orey.
   Source: explains, Theorems 5.2.1–5.2.2, pp. 110–112
   Note: Proof following Orey; the result is attributed to Jain–Jamison and Orey. Not a priority claim.

3. Atom of a Markov chain
   For a Markov kernel P on a measurable space, a measurable set α is an atom if all its points have the same transition law: there is a probability measure ν with P(x,·)=ν for every x∈α. For a ψ-irreducible chain, an atom with ψ(α)>0 is called accessible.
   Source: explains, Chapter 5, definition of atoms, p. 103
   Note: Definition of atoms and accessible atoms. Not a priority claim.

4. f-geometrically regular set of a Markov chain
   For a ψ-irreducible Markov chain and a measurable f≥1, a measurable set A is f-geometrically regular if for every measurable B of positive maximal irreducibility measure there is r>1 with sup_{x∈A}E_x[∑_{k=0}^{τ_B−1}f(X_k)r^k]<∞, where τ_B=inf{n≥1:X_n∈B} is the first return time to B. For f=1 such a set is called geometrically regular.
   Source: explains, Section 15.2.1, p. 368
   Note: Definition of f-Kendall and f-geometrically regular sets. Not a priority claim.

5. m-skeleton of a Markov chain
   For an integer m≥1, the m-skeleton of a Markov chain with kernel P is the Markov chain with kernel Pᵐ, the m-fold iterate of P; observing a chain (X_n) at the times 0,m,2m,… gives the m-skeleton (X_{mn}).
   Source: explains, Section 5.5.1, p. 123
   Note: The m-skeleton as the K_{δ_m}-chain with kernel Pᵐ. Not a priority claim.

6. Positive recurrent ψ-irreducible Markov chain
   A ψ-irreducible Markov chain is recurrent if ∑_{n≥1}Pⁿ(x,A)=∞ for every state x and every measurable set A of positive maximal irreducibility measure. It is positive recurrent if it is recurrent and admits an invariant probability measure.
   Source: explains, Chapters 8 and 10
   Note: Recurrence and positivity of ψ-irreducible chains. Not a priority claim.

7. Cyclic decomposition of a ψ-irreducible Markov chain
   Let P be ψ-irreducible with ψ maximal, and let C be a ν_M-small set with ψ(C)>0 and ν_M(C)>0. Let d be the greatest common divisor of E_C={n≥1: Pⁿ(x,·)≥δ_nν_M on C for some δ_n>0}. Then there are disjoint measurable D_1,…,D_d with P(x,D_{i+1})=1 for x∈D_i (indices mod d) and ψ-null complement of their union; d is the largest length of such a cycle, independent of C. Hence the chain is aperiodic exactly when d=1.
   Source: explains, Theorem 5.4.4, pp. 120-121
   Note: Statement and proof. Not a priority claim.

8. Recurrence transfer from uniform future hitting probabilities
   For a time-homogeneous Markov chainX and measurable setsB,C, suppose someβ>0 satisfiesP_x(X_s∈C for somes≥1)≥β for everyx∈B. Then almost surely, ifX visitsB infinitelyoften, it also visitsC infinitelyoften.
   Source: explains, Theorem 9.1.3, pp. 207-208
   Note: Statement and martingale proof. Not a priority claim.

9. Regenerative decomposition for a Markov chain with an atom
   Let a positive Harris recurrent Markov chain with invariant probability π have an accessible atom α with first return time τ_α. Put a_λ(n)=P_λ(τ_α=n), u(n)=Pⁿ(α,α) and t_f(n)=E_α[f(X_n)1{τ_α≥n}]. Then for every initial law λ, f≥0 and n≥1, |E_λf(X_n)−π(f)|≤E_λ[f(X_n)1{τ_α≥n}]+(|a_λ∗u−π(α)|∗t_f)(n)+π(α)∑_{j>n}t_f(j), where ∗ is convolution of sequences on the positive integers.
   Source: explains, Theorem 13.2.5, pp. 324-325
   Note: Statement and proof. Not a priority claim.

10. Geometric convergence at a Kendall atom
   If a ψ-irreducible aperiodic Markov chain has an accessible atom α that is a Kendall atom, E_α[κ^{τ_α}]<∞ for some κ>1 with τ_α the first return time, then the chain has an invariant probability π and there are r>1 and R<∞ with |Pⁿ(α,α)−π(α)|≤Rr^{−n} for every n≥0.
   Source: explains, Proposition 15.1.2, p. 364
   Note: Stated as a consequence of Kendall’s renewal theorem (Theorem 15.1.1). Not a priority claim.

11. Return-time representation of the invariant measure of a recurrent chain
   For a recurrent ψ-irreducible Markov chain on a countably generated state space, the invariant measure π is unique up to constant multiples and equivalent to a maximal irreducibility measure, and for every set A of positive maximal irreducibility measure and measurable B, π(B)=∫_Aπ(dy)E_y[∑_{k=1}^{τ_A}1_B(X_k)]=∫_Aπ(dy)E_y[∑_{k=0}^{τ_A−1}1_B(X_k)], where τ_A is the first return time to A.
   Source: explains, Theorem 10.4.9, p. 250
   Note: Statement and proof. Not a priority claim.

12. Petite sets are small for aperiodic chains
   For a ψ-irreducible aperiodic Markov kernel P, every petite set C is small: there are m≥1, ε>0 and a probability ν with P^m(x,·)≥εν for every x∈C.
   Source: explains, Theorem 5.5.7, pp. 127-128
   Note: Statement and proof. Not a priority claim.

13. f-norm ergodic theorem for Markov chains
   For a ψ-irreducible aperiodic Markov chain and measurable f≥1, the following are equivalent: (i) the chain is positive recurrent with invariant probability π and π(f)<∞; (ii) some petite set C has sup_{x∈C}E_x[∑_{n=0}^{τ_C−1}f(X_n)]<∞; (iii) some petite C, b<∞ and nonnegative V, finite at some point, satisfy PV≤V−f+b1_C. Then {V<∞} is absorbing and full, and ‖P^n(x,·)−π‖_f→0 for every x in it.
   Source: explains, Theorem 14.0.1, pp. 334-335; Sections 14.1-14.3
   Note: Statement; proof assembled from Theorems 14.1.1-14.3.5. Not a priority claim.

14. Drift characterization of geometric regularity of a Markov chain
   For a ψ-irreducible Markov chain: if an everywhere finite V≥1, a petite set C, β>0 and b<∞ satisfy PV≤(1−β)V+b1_C, then for every B with ψ(B)>0 there are r>1 and c(B)<∞ with E_x[∑_{k=1}^{τ_B}V(X_k)r^k]≤c(B)V(x) for all x. Conversely, if for some petite C and κ>1 the quantity E_x[∑_{k=0}^{τ_C−1}f(X_k)κ^k] is finite for every x and bounded on C, with f≥1, then such a drift inequality holds for some everywhere finite V≥f.
   Source: explains, Theorem 15.3.3, p. 377
   Note: Statement and proof. Not a priority claim.

15. Skeleton inheritance of geometric drift
   For a ψ-irreducible aperiodic Markov chain, if V≥1, a petite set C, β>0 and b<∞ satisfy PV≤(1−β)V+b1_C, then for every m≥1 the m-skeleton P^m satisfies P^mV≤ρV+b′1_{C′} with ρ<1, b′<∞ and a set C′ petite for P^m. Consequently a set that is f-geometrically regular for the chain is f-geometrically regular for every skeleton.
   Source: explains, Theorem 15.3.4, p. 378
   Note: Statement and proof. Not a priority claim.

16. Harris recurrence from almost sure return to a petite set
   A ψ-irreducible Markov chain is Harris recurrent if some petite set C is reached almost surely from every initial state, that is P_x(τ_C<∞)=1 for every x.
   Source: explains, Proposition 9.1.7 (ii), pp. 210-211
   Note: Statement and proof. Not a priority claim.

17. Geometric ergodic theorem for a Kendall atom
   Let an aperiodic ψ-irreducible Markov chain with invariant probability π have an accessible atom α (all states of α have the same transition law) that is f-Kendall of rate κ>1 for a measurable f≥1, i.e. E_α[∑_{k=0}^{τ_α−1}f(X_k)κ^k]<∞ with τ_α the first return time. Then the set S where U(x)=E_x[∑_{k=1}^{τ_α}f(X_k)κ^k] is finite is absorbing and full, and there are r>1 and R<∞ with ∑_n r^n‖P^n(x,·)−π‖_f≤R·U(x) for every x∈S. The proof uses Kendall's renewal theorem and the regenerative decomposition.
   Source: explains, Theorem 15.1.4, p. 365
   Note: Statement and proof. Not a priority claim.

18. Nummelin splitting of a Markov chain
   Given a one-step minorization P(x,·)≥δ1_C(x)ν(·) with δ∈(0,1] and a probability ν, the split chain lives on X×{0,1}: at a visit to x∈C an independent coin with success probability δ is tossed; on success the next state is drawn from ν, otherwise from (P(x,·)−δν)/(1−δ), and outside C from P(x,·). Its first coordinate is a copy of the original chain, and C×{1} is an atom of the split chain, accessible when the original chain is ψ-irreducible and ψ(C)>0.
   Source: explains, Section 5.1, pp. 105-108
   Note: Construction of the split chain. Not a priority claim (Nummelin 1978; Athreya–Ney 1978).

19. Geometric regularity of petite Kendall sets
   For a ψ-irreducible Markov chain and measurable f≥1, a set C is petite and f-Kendall, meaning sup_{x∈C}E_x[∑_{k=0}^{τ_C−1}f(X_k)κ^k]<∞ for some κ>1, if and only if ψ(C)>0 and C is f-geometrically regular: for every B with ψ(B)>0 there is r>1 with sup_{x∈C}E_x[∑_{k=0}^{τ_B−1}f(X_k)r^k]<∞. Here τ denotes first return times.
   Source: explains, Theorem 15.2.1, pp. 369-371
   Note: Statement and geometric-trials proof. Not a priority claim.
